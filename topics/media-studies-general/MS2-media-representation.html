Lesson 2: Core Concepts: media representation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on media representation. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Media representation is how media products portray people, groups, places, events, and ideas — representations are always constructed, never a simple reflection of reality.
Key Fact: Selection and omission: media producers choose what to include and exclude, which shapes how the subject is represented. What is left out is as important as what is included.
Key Fact: Stereotypes are simplified, widely recognised representations of groups — e.g. the 'dumb blonde', the 'action hero', the 'grumpy old man'. They reduce complex identities to a few traits.
Key Fact: Dominant representations are the most common and widely accepted portrayals in society. Alternative representations challenge or subvert these norms.
Key Fact: Gender representation: women have historically been underrepresented and stereotyped (damsel in distress, sex object, nagging wife). The Bechdel Test measures whether women talk to each other about something other than men.
Key Fact: Laura Mulvey's 'male gaze' theory argues that mainstream media represents women from a heterosexual male perspective, objectifying them for male pleasure.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate how representations of gender have changed in contemporary media products. Refer to your CSPs. [15 marks] <div class="

Representations of gender in contemporary media have shifted significantly from traditional stereotypes, though change is uneven across different media forms. In the 'This Girl Can' campaign (BBC, 2017), women are represented as active, powerful, and engaged in sport for enjoyment rather than appearance. This directly challenges the 'male gaze' identified by Mulvey, where women are positioned as objects for male visual pleasure. The campaign shows women of different body types, ages, and ethnicities, rejecting the dominant representation of women in sports media as slim, young, and conventionally attractive. However, traditional representations persist in other areas. Video game characters still frequently sexualise women (e.g. Lara Croft's original design), and female characters in action films are often still defined by their appearance. The Bechdel Test continues to be failed by many mainstream films. In men's representation, there has been some diversification. The 'new man' representation shows men as emotionally articulate and nurturing, as seen in some contemporary advertising.
