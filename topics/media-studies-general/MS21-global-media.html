Lesson 2: Core Concepts: global media

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on global media. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Global media refers to media products and companies that operate across national boundaries, distributing content to worldwide audiences.
Key Fact: Cultural imperialism: the theory that Western (particularly American) media dominates global markets, overwhelming local cultures and imposing Western values. Hollywood films and American TV are distributed worldwide.
Key Fact: Glocalisation: global media products are adapted for local markets — e.g. McDonald's menus varying by country, or Netflix commissioning local-language content (Squid Game, Money Heist).
Key Fact: Transnational media companies: corporations that operate across multiple countries — e.g. Disney, Netflix, Sony. They produce and distribute content globally, but adapt strategies for local markets.
Key Fact: Media flows: media content flows in multiple directions, not just from West to East. Korean pop (K-pop), Bollywood films, and Latin American telenovelas are consumed globally.
Key Fact: The success of non-Western media challenges cultural imperialism theory. Parasite (South Korea) won Best Picture at the Oscars; Squid Game was Netflix's most-watched series.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the impact of globalisation on media industries and audiences. [15 marks] <div class="

Globalisation has profoundly reshaped media industries and audiences, creating both opportunities and concerns. For industries, globalisation enables massive scale. Transnational corporations like Disney and Netflix produce and distribute content worldwide, achieving economies of scale that national industries cannot match. Streaming platforms commission local content for global distribution — Squid Game (South Korea) became Netflix's most-watched series, demonstrating that local content can achieve global reach. However, the dominance of Western conglomerates raises cultural imperialism concerns. Hollywood's global reach means American values, lifestyles, and perspectives are disproportionately represented worldwide. Local industries may struggle to compete, potentially reducing cultural diversity. For audiences, globalisation increases access to diverse content. Streaming services offer content from multiple countries, subtitled and available globally. Audiences can discover Korean dramas, Spanish thrillers, and Indian films that traditional distribution would never have reached them.
