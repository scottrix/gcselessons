Lesson 2: Core Concepts: media language

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on media language. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Media language refers to the ways in which media products communicate meaning to audiences through visual, audio, written, and technical codes.
Key Fact: Denotation is the literal or surface meaning of a sign (what you see). Connotation is the deeper or associated meaning (what it suggests). For example, a red rose denotes a flower but connotes love and romance.
Key Fact: Technical codes include camerawork (close-up, wide shot, low angle), editing (cross-cutting, montage, pace), sound (diegetic, non-diegetic, sound bridge), and mise-en-scene (setting, costume, lighting, props).
Key Fact: Symbolic codes convey meaning through objects, colours, and visual elements that carry cultural significance — e.g. a wedding ring symbolises commitment, dark lighting connotes danger.
Key Fact: Written codes include headlines, captions, taglines, and dialogue — the language choices that shape meaning and audience response.
Key Fact: Genre conventions are the shared features audiences expect from a particular type of media product — e.g. horror films use low-key lighting, eerie music, and isolated settings.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Analyse how media language creates meaning in one of your CSPs. [15 marks] <div class="

In the advert for 'This Girl Can' (BBC, 2017), media language is used to challenge conventional representations of women in sports media. The visual code shows a close-up of a woman mid-exercise, sweating and smiling. The denotation is a woman exercising; the connotation is that fitness is for enjoyment and empowerment, not just appearance. This subverts the conventional media language of women's fitness adverts, which typically use long shots emphasising slim bodies and polished appearance. The bold, sans-serif typography of 'This Girl Can' uses an informal, confident register. The word 'can' is an active verb that connotes ability and determination, contrasting with the passive representation of women in many media products. The colour palette uses bright, energetic colours (pinks, oranges) that connote vitality and energy rather than traditional feminine delicacy. The tagline 'I'm building my strengths and you can see it' anchors the image with a direct, first-person statement that connotes empowerment and visibility. Exam Tips: Always use specific examples from CSPs when discussing media language | Identify the code FIRST, then explain what it connotes — don't just describe what you see | Use the correct terminology: 'connote' not 'mean', 'signifier' not 'sign'
Common Errors: ✗ Denotation and connotation mean the same thing. ✓ Denotation is the literal meaning (what you see); connotation is the suggested meaning (what it implies culturally). ✗ Every audience reads a media text the same way. ✓ Media texts are polysemic — different audiences can interpret the same product differently based on their cultural background, age, gender, and experiences. ✗ Semiotics only applies to images. ✓ Semiotics applies to all signs — images, words, sounds, objects, and colours all function as signs with signifiers and signifieds.
