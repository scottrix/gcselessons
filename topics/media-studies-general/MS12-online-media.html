Lesson 2: Core Concepts: online, social & participatory media

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on online, social & participatory media. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Online and social media encompass websites, social networking platforms, blogs, vlogs, wikis, and apps — media products that exist on the internet and often enable audience participation.
Key Fact: Websites use conventions: homepage, navigation menu, banner, content sections, hyperlinks, multimedia (images, video, audio), and calls to action.
Key Fact: Social media platforms (Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter, YouTube, Snapchat) enable user-generated content, social networking, and algorithmic content distribution.
Key Fact: Prosumer culture: audiences are no longer just consumers — they produce, share, and remix content. This democratises media but also creates challenges with misinformation and quality.
Key Fact: Algorithms determine what content users see on social media, based on engagement data. This creates filter bubbles (echo chambers) where users see content that reinforces their views.
Key Fact: Viral content: media that spreads rapidly through sharing on social networks. Virality is driven by emotional appeal, relatability, humour, and shareability rather than quality or importance.

Social media has fundamentally transformed the relationship between media audiences and industries, creating both opportunities and challenges. For audiences, social media has empowered participation. Prosumers create and share content, challenging the traditional producer-audience hierarchy. Jenkins' participatory culture shows fans as active creators, not passive consumers. Audiences can engage directly with producers, access diverse content, and build communities around shared interests. However, algorithms shape what audiences see. Filter bubbles created by personalisation can narrow perspectives and polarise debate. The attention economy rewards engaging content over accurate content, enabling misinformation. The viral spread of content prioritises emotional impact over truth. For industries, social media has created new business models. Influencer marketing has become a major revenue stream. Brands can target audiences with unprecedented precision using data analytics. Streaming services use social media for distribution and promotion. But industries have also lost control.
