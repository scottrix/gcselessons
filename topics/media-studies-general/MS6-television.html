Lesson 2: Core Concepts: television

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on television. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Television is one of the most influential media forms, combining visual, audio, and written codes to tell stories and communicate ideas to mass and niche audiences.
Key Fact: Traditional broadcast TV used scheduling to target audiences — primetime (7-10pm) for mass audiences, late night for niche content, daytime for specific demographics.
Key Fact: Streaming services (Netflix, Amazon Prime, Disney+) have disrupted traditional broadcasting — audiences can watch what they want, when they want, on any device.
Key Fact: TV drama conventions: ensemble casts, multiple storylines, cliffhangers, series arcs, episodic and serial structures. Quality TV / 'peak TV' features cinematic production values.
Key Fact: Narrative structures: linear (chronological), non-linear (flashbacks, flash-forwards), open (unresolved), closed (resolved), single-strand, multi-strand.
Key Fact: Todorov's narrative theory: equilibrium → disruption → recognition → attempt to repair → new equilibrium. Many TV dramas follow this pattern within episodes and across series.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Analyse how television conventions are used to engage audiences in one of your TV CSPs. [15 marks] <div class="

Contemporary TV drama uses a range of conventions to engage audiences, combining traditional narrative techniques with innovations driven by streaming. Enigma codes are central to audience engagement — unanswered questions drive viewers to keep watching. In many TV dramas, each episode ends with a cliffhanger that creates narrative suspense, while longer series arcs develop mysteries across multiple episodes. Barthes argued that enigma codes are the 'engine' of narrative, and TV drama relies heavily on them. Multi-strand narratives engage audiences by interweaving different storylines, creating complexity that rewards sustained viewing. Streaming services have amplified this convention — series designed for binge-watching can develop more complex narratives across an entire season, since audiences watch episodes in quick succession rather than waiting a week. Character-driven narratives are a key convention of 'quality TV'. Complex, morally ambiguous protagonists engage audiences emotionally and intellectually — viewers debate characters' motivations and decisions on social media, extending engagement beyond the screen. Visual conventions including cinematic camerawork, production

Exam Tips: Always name specific TV CSPs and their institutional contexts | Use narrative theorists (Todorov, Barthes, Levi-Strauss) to structure analysis | Discuss how streaming has changed TV conventions and audience behaviour
Common Errors: ✗ Streaming has completely replaced broadcast television. ✓ Streaming has disrupted broadcasting but not replaced it. Live events (sport, news), reality TV, and scheduled programming still attract large audiences. Broadcast and streaming coexist with different strengths. ✗ Todorov's theory applies to every TV programme. ✓ Todorov's theory is a useful framework but doesn't fit all narratives. Some TV deliberately disrupts equilibrium without resolution, and postmodern narratives may reject the structure entirely. ✗ All TV drama follows the same conventions. ✓ While TV dramas share some conventions (character arcs, cliffhangers), they vary significantly across sub-genres.
