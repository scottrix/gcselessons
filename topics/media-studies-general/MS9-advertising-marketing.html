Lesson 2: Core Concepts: advertising & marketing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on advertising & marketing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Advertising is a paid form of persuasive communication that aims to promote products, services, or ideas to target audiences through various media platforms.
Key Fact: Marketing encompasses the broader strategy of identifying, anticipating, and satisfying customer requirements profitably — advertising is one tool within the marketing mix.
Key Fact: Persuasive techniques include: emotional appeal, fear, humour, celebrity endorsement, bandwagon (everyone's doing it), testimonials, rhetorical questions, and superlatives ('the best').
Key Fact: AIDA model: Attention (grab the audience), Interest (hold it), Desire (create want), Action (prompt purchase). Most adverts follow this structure.
Key Fact: Print advertising conventions: headline, visual image, body copy, logo, slogan, call to action. Layout follows the Z-pattern (how eyes scan a page).
Key Fact: Digital advertising: social media ads, influencer marketing, programmatic advertising (automated buying), native advertising (content that looks like editorial), and retargeting.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Analyse how one of your advertising CSPs targets and appeals to its audience. [15 marks] <div class="

Advertising uses a range of media language techniques to target and appeal to specific audiences, guided by the AIDA model and informed by detailed audience profiling. The visual codes of the advert are designed to attract the target audience's attention. The choice of imagery — whether featuring aspirational lifestyles, relatable people, or striking visuals — is determined by psychographic profiling. For example, adverts targeting 'aspirers' (psychographic group) use images of success, luxury, and social status to create desire. The written codes reinforce the visual message. Headlines use direct address ('You deserve this') to create personal connection, while body copy emphasises benefits over features. Slogans create memorable brand associations through repetition and linguistic devices (alliteration, rhyme, puns). Celebrity endorsement leverages the star's cultural meaning (Dyer's star theory) to transfer positive associations to the product. The celebrity's star image — carefully constructed through media appearances — carries connotations that the brand wishes to associate with.
