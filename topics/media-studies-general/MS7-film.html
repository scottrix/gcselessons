Lesson 2: Core Concepts: film

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on film. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Film is a major global media form that combines visual, audio, and narrative codes to tell stories and create experiences for cinema and home audiences.
Key Fact: Film genres include: action, horror, comedy, sci-fi, romance, thriller, documentary, animation. Genres set audience expectations and help with marketing and distribution.
Key Fact: Genre hybrids blend elements from multiple genres — e.g. romantic comedy, sci-fi horror. Postmodern films may deliberately mix or parody genre conventions.
Key Fact: Richard Dyer's star theory: film stars are constructed images, not real people. Stars function as cultural symbols — e.g. Dwayne Johnson represents strength and reliability.
Key Fact: Hollywood dominance: the 'big five' studios (Disney, Warner Bros, Universal, Paramount, Sony) control most global film production and distribution. This dominance has economic and cultural implications.
Key Fact: Independent cinema: films made outside the major studio system, often with lower budgets, more creative freedom, and challenging or experimental content.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the dominance of Hollywood in global film production. [12 marks] <div class="

Hollywood dominates global film production through the 'big five' studios, which control production, distribution, and marketing worldwide. This dominance gives Hollywood enormous economic power — blockbusters with budgets over $200m can saturate global markets with marketing campaigns that independent and national cinemas cannot match. Hollywood's dominance has cultural implications. Its products promote American values, lifestyles, and perspectives globally, leading to concerns about cultural imperialism. Local film industries struggle to compete for audiences and screen time, potentially reducing cultural diversity in cinema. However, Hollywood's dominance is not unchallenged. Bollywood produces more films annually than Hollywood, Nollywood (Nigeria) is one of the world's largest film industries, and Korean cinema has achieved global recognition (Parasite winning Best Picture at the Oscars). Streaming platforms have increased access to non-Hollywood content. Hollywood itself is becoming more international — films are made for global rather than just American audiences, featuring diverse casts and international settings. The Marvel franchise is designed to appeal across cultures.

Exam Tips: Always identify the genre of any film CSP and its conventions | Use star theory when discussing actors and their function in marketing | Discuss the economic context — budget, studio, distribution model
Common Errors: ✗ Hollywood films are always American. ✓ Hollywood is dominated by American studios but produces films globally. Many 'Hollywood' films are shot in the UK, Canada, or other countries, and the studios are increasingly international in ownership and content. ✗ Independent films are always better than blockbusters. ✓ Independents offer more creative freedom and diversity, but quality varies. Blockbusters can be well-crafted and culturally significant. The distinction is about production context, not quality. ✗ CGI has made practical effects completely unnecessary. ✓ Many filmmakers still use practical effects alongside CGI, arguing they look more authentic.
