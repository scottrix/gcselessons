Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the road to war

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the road to war. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: On 23 August 1939, Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union signed a non-aggression pact in Moscow. The public agreement promised that neither side would attack the other for 10 years. A secret protocol divided eastern Europe into spheres of influence: Germany would get western Poland, and the USSR would get eastern Poland, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, and Bessarabia. This pact shocked the world - the two ideological enemies had made a deal.
Key Fact: At 4:45 am on 1 September 1939, the German battleship Schleswig-Holstein opened fire on the Polish garrison at Westerplatte in Danzig. Over 1.5 million German troops, with 2,500 tanks and 1,900 aircraft, invaded Poland from the north, south, and west. The attack used the "Blitzkrieg" (lightning war) tactic of rapid armoured advances supported by overwhelming air power.
Key Fact: On 3 September 1939, Britain declared war on Germany at 11:15 am, followed by France at 5:00 pm. Chamberlain broadcast to the nation: "This country is at war with Germany." The Second World War had begun. However, the declaration came two days after the invasion - there had been delays while Britain and France made last-minute attempts to negotiate.
Key Fact: Appeasement was abandoned because Hitler's actions after Munich proved he could not be trusted and his aims were conquest, not self-determination. The seizure of Czechoslovakia (March 1939) was the decisive moment that turned British opinion against further concessions.
Key Fact: The outbreak of WWII in September 1939 was the final proof that the League of Nations had failed in its primary purpose of preventing war. The League continued to exist in Geneva until it was formally dissolved in April 1946 and replaced by the United Nations, but it had been irrelevant as a peacekeeping body since 1936.

Lesson 3: Application: the road to war

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: History Exam Tips — The Road to War. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why the Nazi-Soviet Pact was important. (4 marks)

Answer: The Nazi-Soviet Pact was important because it allowed Hitler to invade Poland without fear of a two-front war. The pact guaranteed Soviet neutrality and included a secret protocol dividing Poland between Germany and the USSR. Without the pact, Hitler might have faced Soviet opposition; with it, he had a free hand in the west. It also undermined the British guarantee to Poland, which had assumed Soviet support.

Q2: How important was the Nazi-Soviet Pact in causing the outbreak of war in 1939? (8 marks)

Answer: The Nazi-Soviet Pact was crucial as the immediate trigger for war. It gave Hitler the confidence to invade Poland by guaranteeing Soviet neutrality and removing the threat of a two-front war. Without the pact, Hitler might have delayed invasion, giving more time for Anglo-French-Soviet negotiations. However, the underlying causes of war were deeper: Hitler's aggressive foreign policy, the failure of appeasement, and the weakness of the League of Nations. The pact was the final piece in the jigsaw, but without Hitler's determination to conquer, no pact would have led to war.

Q3: "Appeasement was the main reason war broke out in 1939." How far do you agree? (12 marks)

Answer: Appeasement was a major reason for the outbreak of war because it encouraged Hitler's aggression. Each concession (Rhineland 1936, Anschluss 1938, Sudetenland 1938) showed Hitler that Britain and France would not resist, making him increasingly bold. The Munich Agreement gave Hitler Czech military assets and proved he could not be trusted. However, other factors were also important: Hitler was determined on war regardless of concessions; the League of Nations had already failed; the Treaty of Versailles created grievances that made German revisionism popular; and the Nazi-Soviet Pact gave Hitler the immediate confidence to invade. Appeasement enabled Hitler but it did not create his aggressive aims - he would have pursued them anyway.

Q4: Explain why Britain and France declared war on Germany on 3 September 1939. (8 marks)

Answer: Britain and France declared war on 3 September 1939 because Germany invaded Poland on 1 September despite the British guarantee of 31 March 1939. The guarantee promised to defend Polish independence; when Hitler attacked, Britain and France had to honour it or lose all credibility. Hitler ignored their ultimatum to withdraw. The declaration of war was the end of appeasement - Chamberlain finally recognised that Hitler could not be negotiated with and that force was the only remaining option.

Q5: "The Treaty of Versailles made the Second World War inevitable." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: The Treaty of Versailles did NOT make war inevitable, but it created the conditions that made war possible. It generated German resentment that Hitler exploited; the "stab-in-the-back" myth undermined the Weimar Republic; reparations and territorial losses created economic hardship; and the military restrictions were seen as humiliating. However, war was not inevitable: the Dawes Plan and Locarno Pact showed that reconciliation was possible; the Weimar Republic survived for 14 years; and many Germans accepted the Treaty in practice. It was the specific combination of the Great Depression, Hitler's rise, Western weakness, and the failure of the League that made war happen. Versailles created grievances, but it was Hitler who turned those grievances into war. Without Hitler, a revised Versailles might have been achieved peacefully.
