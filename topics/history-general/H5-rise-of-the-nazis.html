Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the rise of the nazis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the rise of the nazis. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: On 29 October 1929, the American stock market crashed (Wall Street Crash). American banks recalled loans from Germany, causing economic collapse. By 1932, 6 million Germans were unemployed (over 30% of the workforce), and industrial production fell by 58% from its 1929 level.
Key Fact: In the 1928 election, the Nazis won just 2.6% of the vote (12 seats). By July 1932, they were the largest party with 37.3% of the vote (230 seats). This extraordinary rise was driven by the economic crisis and the failure of mainstream parties to solve it.
Key Fact: The Nazis were NOT primarily a working-class party. Their strongest support came from the Protestant middle class, farmers, and small-town voters. The working class largely continued to support the SPD or KPD. However, the Nazis did make some inroads into the working class through the SA, which offered unemployed men food, comradeship and a sense of purpose.
Key Fact: Joseph Goebbels was appointed Nazi Party propaganda leader in 1929. He controlled the Nazi message with extraordinary skill, using every available medium to reach the German people. He said: "The essence of propaganda consists in winning people over to an idea so sincerely, so vitally, that in the end they succumb to it and can never escape."
Key Fact: The SA (Brownshirts or Stormtroopers) was the Nazi paramilitary force, founded in 1921 and led by Ernst Röhm. By 1932, the SA had over 400,000 members - many of them unemployed men attracted by the uniform, comradeship, and regular meals.
Key Fact: Hitler was appointed Chancellor on 30 January 1933 by President Hindenburg, not because he won a majority but because a group of conservative politicians believed they could control him. Franz von Papen famously claimed: "We've hired him" and "In two months, we'll have pushed Hitler so far into a corner that he'll squeak."

Q: Explain why the Wall Street Crash helped the Nazis. (4 marks)

Answer: The Wall Street Crash caused American banks to recall loans from Germany, triggering economic collapse. By 1932, 6 million were unemployed. The Weimar government could not solve the crisis, and Brüning governed by decree, undermining democracy. Desperate Germans turned to extremist parties - the Nazis promised "Bread and Work" and someone to blame (Versailles, Jews, Communists).

Lesson 3: Application: the rise of the nazis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: key terms from the rise of the nazis. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why the Wall Street Crash helped the Nazis. (4 marks)

Answer: The Wall Street Crash caused American banks to recall loans from Germany, triggering economic collapse. By 1932, 6 million were unemployed. The Weimar government could not solve the crisis, and Brüning governed by decree, undermining democracy. Desperate Germans turned to extremist parties - the Nazis promised "Bread and Work" and someone to blame (Versailles, Jews, Communists).

Q2: How important was propaganda in the rise of the Nazis? (8 marks)

Answer: Propaganda was very important: Goebbels used rallies, posters, newspapers and radio to create the Hitler Myth and target different groups with tailored messages ("Bread and Work" for the unemployed, anti-Communism for the middle class). The Nuremberg rallies showed power and discipline. However, propaganda alone could not have succeeded without the economic crisis creating the conditions for extremism. Propaganda was most effective because it gave people already desperate for solutions a compelling narrative and a strong leader to believe in.

Q3: "The Nazis rose to power because of the economic crisis." How far do you agree? (12 marks)

Answer: The economic crisis was the essential trigger: unemployment rose to 6 million by 1932, the government cut welfare, and democratic politicians seemed incapable of solving the crisis. The Nazi vote rose from 2.6% (1928) to 37.3% (July 1932) directly because of economic collapse. However, other factors were also important: the weaknesses of the Weimar Constitution (Article 48, PR) meant democratic government had already failed; Hitler's personal charisma and Goebbels' propaganda made the Nazi message compelling; the SA created disorder that made democracy look weak; and the conservative elites' backroom deal was necessary for Hitler to actually gain power. The economic crisis was the most important factor but it was not sufficient on its own.

Q4: Explain why Hitler was appointed Chancellor in January 1933. (8 marks)

Answer: Hitler was appointed Chancellor because: the Nazis were the largest party in the Reichstag (196 seats after November 1932); no stable democratic government could be formed; conservative elites (von Papen, Hugenberg) believed they could control Hitler in a coalition where only 3 of 12 ministers were Nazis; they feared civil war between Nazis and Communists; industrialists supported the deal; and Hindenburg was persuaded by his son Oskar and von Papen. Schleicher's government had collapsed, leaving no alternative.

Q5: "The conservative elites were most responsible for Hitler's appointment as Chancellor." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: The conservative elites were crucial because without their deal, Hitler could never have become Chancellor legally - the Nazis never won a majority and Hitler had already been refused the Chancellorship in August 1932. Von Papen's machinations, Hugenberg's media support, and Oskar von Hindenburg's influence on his father were all essential. However, the elites only made the deal because of broader circumstances: the economic crisis had made the Nazis the largest party; democratic government had failed; and Hitler's leadership, propaganda and the SA had built Nazi strength. The elites' miscalculation was the final step, but it depended on all the factors that had made the Nazis powerful enough to be worth including in government. The elites' failure was not creating the Nazis but underestimating them.
