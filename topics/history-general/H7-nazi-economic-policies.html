Lesson 2: Core Concepts: nazi economic policies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on nazi economic policies. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: When Hitler became Chancellor in January 1933, over 6 million Germans were unemployed - nearly 30% of the workforce. By 1939, official unemployment was under 500,000. The Nazis claimed to have solved the unemployment crisis, but the reality was more complex.
Key Fact: The Nazis launched massive public works programmes, most famously the autobahn (motorway) network. Between 1933 and 1938, over 3,000 km of autobahn were built. However, the autobahn programme was originally proposed by the Weimar government - the Nazis simply took credit for it.
Key Fact: Rearmament was the single biggest factor in reducing unemployment. Government spending on arms rose from 3.5 billion marks in 1933 to 30 billion marks by 1939. By 1939, 40% of government expenditure was on the military.
Key Fact: The National Labour Service (RAD) was made compulsory for all men aged 18-25 in June 1935. They had to serve 6 months doing manual labour - building roads, draining marshes, planting forests, and working on farms. They wore military-style uniforms and lived in camps.
Key Fact: The Kraft durch Freude (KdF) programme was set up in November 1933 as part of the German Labour Front (DAF). It provided leisure activities for workers - cruises, holidays, trips to the theatre, and sport. By 1938, over 10 million people had taken KdF holidays.
Key Fact: The Beauty of Labour division of the DAF was established in 1934 to improve working conditions. It pressured employers to provide better facilities: washrooms, canteens, rest areas, and sports facilities. Between 1936-1939, it claimed to have improved 60,000 workplaces.

Lesson 3: Application: nazi economic policies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: key terms from nazi economic policies. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two ways the Nazis reduced unemployment. (4 marks)

Answer: Rearmament: conscription from March 1935 removed young men from the workforce, and arms factories employed millions. The RAD (National Labour Service): made compulsory in June 1935, it placed young men in work camps doing manual labour, removing them from unemployment figures.

Q2: How important was rearmament in reducing German unemployment? (8 marks)

Answer: Rearmament was the most important factor in reducing unemployment. Government spending on arms rose from 3.5 billion marks (1933) to 30 billion marks (1939). Conscription removed hundreds of thousands of young men from the workforce. Arms factories employed millions directly and in supply industries. By 1939, 40% of government spending was military. However, other factors also contributed: public works (autobahns) created some jobs; the RAD removed young men from statistics; and the removal of Jews and women from the workforce also lowered official figures. Rearmament was the single biggest factor but it was not the only one.

Q3: "Nazi economic policies benefited German workers." How far do you agree? (12 marks)

Answer: Nazi economic policies benefited workers in some ways: unemployment fell dramatically; KdF provided affordable leisure; Beauty of Labour improved conditions; and the sense of national recovery gave hope and pride. However, workers also lost fundamental rights: trade unions were abolished; the right to strike was removed; wages remained low in real terms; working hours increased from 43 to 49 per week; labour books restricted job changes; and the economy was geared towards war, not welfare. The KdF car scheme took workers' savings without delivering cars. Overall, workers gained employment but lost freedom and representation - the benefits were real but limited, while the losses were fundamental.

Q4: Explain the purpose and impact of the Strength Through Joy programme. (8 marks)

Answer: The Strength Through Joy (KdF) programme provided subsidised leisure activities for workers: cruises (180,000 participants in 1938), package holidays, theatre tickets (43 million sold by 1939), and sports. Its purpose was to improve worker satisfaction and loyalty to the regime, and to show that the Nazi state valued workers. However, it also served as propaganda - cruises were supervised and promoted Nazi ideology. The KdF car (Volkswagen) scheme took savings from workers but delivered no cars before the war. KdF improved workers' leisure but did not address their real concerns about wages, hours, and rights.

Q5: "The Nazis created an economic miracle." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: The Nazis did NOT create an economic miracle. Unemployment fell from 6 million to under 500,000, but this was achieved through: rearmament (the biggest factor), removing people from statistics (women, Jews, political opponents), compulsory RAD service, and conscription. Workers lost rights - no unions, no strikes, restricted job mobility. Real wages barely improved. The economy was built on unsustainable military spending; by 1939, Germany faced a balance of payments crisis and raw material shortages. The KdF and Beauty of Labour were propaganda designed to mask the reality of workers' lost rights. The apparent miracle was achieved by preparing for war, not by creating genuine prosperity.
