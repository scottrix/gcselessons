Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the weimar republic

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the weimar republic. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: In October 1918, Germany was facing defeat in World War I. The Kaiser's government transformed into a parliamentary democracy to secure better peace terms. However, the revolution went further than intended when sailors mutinied at Kiel on 3 November 1918.
Key Fact: The Weimar Constitution, approved on 11 August 1919, created the most democratic system Germany had ever known - but it contained crucial weaknesses that opponents exploited.
Key Fact: Friedrich Ebert, leader of the SPD, became the first President of the Weimar Republic in February 1919. He governed using Article 48 136 times during his presidency (1919-1925), often to deal with political crises - setting a dangerous precedent for future use.
Key Fact: In January 1919, the Communist Spartacist League (led by Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht) attempted to overthrow the Weimar government in Berlin. The government used the Freikorps (right-wing ex-soldiers) to crush the uprising. Luxemburg and Liebknecht were murdered on 15 January 1919.
Key Fact: In March 1920, right-wing Freikorps units under Wolfgang Kapp attempted to seize control of Berlin. The army (Reichswehr) refused to fire on the Freikorps. The government survived only through a general strike called by trade unions, which paralysed Berlin and forced Kapp to flee.
Key Fact: Between 1919 and 1922, right-wing extremists committed 354 political murders in Germany. Left-wing extremists committed 22. However, right-wing assassins received an average sentence of 4 months, while left-wing assassins received an average of 15 years. This showed the justice system was biased towards the right and against the Republic.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe two weaknesses of the Weimar Constitution. (4 marks)

Answer: Article 48 allowed the President to rule by decree in an emergency, but "emergency" was never defined, so it could be abused. Proportional representation meant that no single party could win a majority, leading to unstable coalition governments that frequently collapsed.

Lesson 3: Application: the weimar republic

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: History Exam Tips — The Weimar Republic. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe two weaknesses of the Weimar Constitution. (4 marks)

Answer: Article 48 allowed the President to rule by decree in an emergency, but "emergency" was never defined, so it could be abused. Proportional representation meant that no single party could win a majority, leading to unstable coalition governments that frequently collapsed.

Q2: Explain why the Spartacist Uprising failed. (8 marks)

Answer: The Spartacist Uprising failed because: it lacked popular support - most Berlin workers did not actively join the revolt; the Spartacists failed to seize key infrastructure like communications and transport; the government called in the Freikorps who were heavily armed and ruthless; the SPD had control of the state machinery and could mobilise force against the Communists. Luxemburg and Liebknecht were also poor strategists who hesitated at crucial moments.

Q3: How far was the Weimar Republic threatened by right-wing opposition in the years 1919-1923? (12 marks)

Answer: Right-wing threats were very serious: the Kapp Putsch briefly seized Berlin in March 1920; right-wing extremists murdered Erzberger (1921) and Rathenau (1922); the Munich Putsch in 1923 showed the growing Nazi threat. The army was unreliable - it refused to fire on the Freikorps during the Kapp Putsch. The judiciary was biased, giving light sentences to right-wing killers. However, left-wing threats were also serious (Spartacists, Bavarian Soviet Republic), and the Republic survived both. The Republic's biggest weakness was that its own institutions (army, courts, civil service) were often sympathetic to its right-wing opponents.

Q4: "The Weimar Constitution was doomed from the start." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: The Constitution had significant flaws: Article 48 gave the President emergency powers that Hitler exploited; PR produced fragmented parliaments and weak coalitions; the President had too much power over the Chancellor. However, the Constitution was not inherently doomed - it functioned reasonably well during the Stresemann era (1924-1929). The real problems were external: the Versailles Treaty undermined the Republic's legitimacy, economic crises destroyed confidence, and the Republic's own institutions (army, judiciary) were hostile. The Constitution was a contributing factor but not the sole cause of the Republic's failure.

Q5: Explain why the Kapp Putsch was significant for the Weimar Republic. (8 marks)

Answer: The Kapp Putsch was significant because: it showed the Weimar government could not rely on the army to defend democracy (Reichswehr refused to fire on Freikorps); it demonstrated that the Republic depended on the working class (general strike saved the government); it revealed the biased justice system - right-wing conspirators were barely punished; and it set a pattern where right-wing threats to the Republic were treated leniently while left-wing revolts were crushed with maximum force.
