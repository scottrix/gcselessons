Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the treaty of versailles

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the treaty of versailles. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Treaty of Versailles was signed on 28 June 1919 in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles, exactly 5 years after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand. It was primarily negotiated by three leaders: Wilson (USA), Clemenceau (France), and Lloyd George (Britain). Their conflicting aims made a balanced settlement almost impossible.
Key Fact: The Treaty of Versailles had five main categories: War Guilt, Reparations, Military restrictions, Territorial losses, and the League of Nations. Germans called it the "Diktat" (dictated peace) because they were not allowed to negotiate the terms.
Key Fact: Germany lost 13% of its European territory, 10% of its population, and all of its overseas empire. The "Polish Corridor" was particularly resented because it separated East Prussia from the rest of Germany and placed millions of ethnic Germans under Polish rule.
Key Fact: Germans called the Treaty a "Diktat" (dictated peace) because they were given no opportunity to negotiate. The German delegation was shown the terms on 7 May 1919 and told to sign within 15 days. When they proposed modifications, the Allies rejected all significant changes. The German government initially refused to sign, but agreed after the Allies threatened to invade.
Key Fact: The League of Nations was created as part of the Treaty of Versailles. Wilson's Fourteenth Point called for "a general association of nations" to maintain peace. The League's Covenant (rules) was incorporated into all the peace treaties. However, the US Senate refused to ratify the Treaty, meaning America never joined the League - undermining it from the start.

Lesson 3: Application: the treaty of versailles

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: key terms from the treaty of versailles. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two reasons why Clemenceau wanted a harsh treaty. (4 marks)

Answer: France had suffered the most in WWI: 1.3 million dead and northern France devastated by German occupation and fighting. Clemenceau feared future German attacks and wanted to permanently weaken Germany so it could never invade again. He also wanted to recover Alsace-Lorraine (lost in 1871) and gain massive reparations to rebuild France's destroyed regions.

Q2: How far were the Big Three satisfied with the Treaty of Versailles? (8 marks)

Answer: Clemenceau was partially satisfied: he gained Alsace-Lorraine, reparations, and military restrictions on Germany. However, he wanted even harsher terms - an independent Rhineland and the permanent breakup of Germany. Lloyd George was partially satisfied: he gained German colonies and naval restrictions, but worried the Treaty was too harsh and would cause future problems. Wilson was the least satisfied: the League of Nations was created, but the Senate refused to let America join; many of his Fourteen Points were ignored; the Treaty was far harsher than his idealistic vision.

Q3: "The Treaty of Versailles was completely unfair to Germany." How far do you agree? (12 marks)

Answer: The Treaty was unfair in several respects: Germany was not allowed to negotiate (a "Diktat"); Article 231 assigned total war guilt, which was historically inaccurate; reparations of £6.6 billion were economically crippling; self-determination was not applied to Germans in the Polish Corridor; and the military restrictions left Germany vulnerable. However, some aspects were fair: Alsace-Lorraine was rightfully returned to France; Germany had itself imposed harsh terms on Russia at Brest-Litovsk; the damage caused by Germany justified compensation; and some territorial losses gave independence to peoples who wanted it. The Treaty was not "completely" unfair, but it was harsh enough to create lasting resentment.

Q4: Explain why Germans hated the Treaty of Versailles. (8 marks)

Answer: Germans hated the Treaty because: (1) Article 231 forced them to accept total war guilt, which they saw as a lie; (2) reparations of £6.6 billion seemed impossible to pay; (3) military restrictions to 100,000 men were humiliating for a proud nation; (4) territorial losses including the Polish Corridor separated German territory and placed Germans under foreign rule; (5) it was a "Diktat" - they could not negotiate; (6) they had expected a fair peace based on Wilson's Fourteen Points but received a punitive settlement; (7) the loss of all colonies and the Saar's coal mines damaged the economy. Every aspect of the Treaty was resented.

Q5: "The Treaty of Versailles made another war inevitable." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: The Treaty made another war more likely but not inevitable. It created the conditions for future conflict: German resentment fuelled revisionism; the "stab-in-the-back" myth undermined the Weimar Republic; economic hardship from reparations destabilised German democracy; the Polish Corridor and Sudetenland created ongoing disputes. However, war was not inevitable: the Dawes Plan (1924) and Locarno Pact (1925) showed that reconciliation was possible; the League of Nations provided a framework for resolving disputes; and many Germans accepted the Treaty in practice. It was the Wall Street Crash (1929), Hitler's rise (1933), and the policy of appeasement that actually caused WWII - the Treaty created grievances that Hitler exploited, but other paths were possible. The Treaty made war possible but not inevitable.
