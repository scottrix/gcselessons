Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the spanish armada

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the spanish armada. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Context: The Spanish Armada of 1588 was the culmination of decades of growing tension between England and Spain. Philip II of Spain, the most powerful monarch in Europe, was motivated by a combination of religious, political, and economic grievances against Elizabeth I. The execution of Mary Queen of Scots in February 1587 gave Philip the final justification he needed.
Weaknesses of the Armada: Despite its size, the Armada had significant weaknesses: (1) the original commander, the Marquis of Santa Cruz, died in February 1588 and was replaced by Medina Sidonia, who had no naval experience; (2) Drake's raid on Cadiz (1587) had destroyed supplies and delayed the fleet; (3) many of the ships were armed merchant vessels, not purpose-built warships; (4) the Spanish ships were designed for boarding and close combat, not long-range gunnery; (5) the plan required perfect coordination between the Armada and Parma's army, which was extremely difficult in an era before radio communication.
1. Better Tactics and Ships: The English ships were smaller, faster, and more manoeuvrable than the Spanish galleons. They could sail closer to the wind and choose their position in battle. English guns had longer range and could fire faster - the English used a "stand-off" tactic, pounding the Spanish from a distance, while the Spanish were designed for close boarding actions. The English fired approximately 2,000 rounds at Gravelines, compared to approximately 750 from the Spanish.
2. Better Commanders: The English were led by Lord Howard of Effingham (Lord High Admiral), with experienced seamen like Sir Francis Drake, Sir John Hawkins, and Martin Frobisher under his command. They understood naval warfare and adapted their tactics to the situation. The Spanish commander, the Duke of Medina Sidonia, had no naval experience and had begged Philip not to appoint him.
3. The Weather: The famous "Protestant Wind" blew the Spanish fleet north after Gravelines and caused devastating storms during the return voyage around Scotland and Ireland. More Spanish ships were destroyed by storms than by English guns. The wind consistently favoured the English, allowing them to keep the weather gauge (upwind position) during the Channel battles.
4. Strategic Errors by Spain: Philip's plan required perfect coordination between the Armada and Parma's army - but this was impossible without modern communications. Parma's troops were blockaded in port by Dutch ships and could not join the Armada. The Armada was forced to wait at Calais, giving the English the chance to use fire ships.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Give two reasons why Philip II of Spain wanted to invade England. (4 marks)

Answer: Two reasons: (1) Religion - Philip was a devout Catholic who wanted to overthrow Elizabeth's Protestant settlement and restore Catholicism in England; (2) English support for the Dutch rebels - Elizabeth sent money and troops to help the Dutch Protestants fighting against Spanish rule in the Netherlands, which was a direct threat to Spanish power. Other reasons include Drake's raids on Spanish ships and Mary's execution.

Lesson 3: Application: the spanish armada

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Key Context, Weaknesses of the Armada, 1. Better Tactics and Ships. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Give two reasons why Philip II of Spain wanted to invade England. (4 marks)

Answer: Two reasons: (1) Religion - Philip was a devout Catholic who wanted to overthrow Elizabeth's Protestant settlement and restore Catholicism in England; (2) English support for the Dutch rebels - Elizabeth sent money and troops to help the Dutch Protestants fighting against Spanish rule in the Netherlands, which was a direct threat to Spanish power. Other reasons include Drake's raids on Spanish ships and Mary's execution.

Q2: Describe the key events of the Armada campaign from 19 July to 29 July 1588. (4 marks)

Answer: Key events: 19 July - Armada spotted off the Lizard, beacons lit; 21 July - English fleet engaged the Armada in the Channel, using longer-range guns; 27 July - Armada anchored at Calais to wait for Parma's army; 28 July - English sent fire ships into the anchored Spanish fleet, causing panic and scattering the formation; 29 July - Battle of Gravelines, the English attacked the scattered Spanish ships, destroying five and damaging many more; the wind drove the Armada north-east.

Q3: Explain why the fire ships were a turning point in the Armada campaign. (4 marks)

Answer: The fire ships were a turning point because they broke the Armada's tight defensive crescent formation, which had protected the fleet during the Channel battles. When the Spanish panicked and cut their anchor cables, they scattered and could not regroup. This made the Armada vulnerable to the English attack at the Battle of Gravelines the next day. Without the fire ships, the Armada might have maintained its formation and successfully linked up with Parma's army.

Q4: "The weather was the most important reason for England's victory over the Armada." How far do you agree? (8 marks)

Answer: A balanced answer should discuss: the weather (the "Protestant Wind" scattered the fleet and caused devastating losses on the return voyage - more ships were lost to storms than to English guns); better English ships and tactics (faster, more manoeuvrable, longer-range guns, stand-off tactics); better commanders (Howard, Drake, Hawkins vs the inexperienced Medina Sidonia); Spanish strategic errors (poor coordination with Parma). Argue that the weather was important but the English tactical advantages were equally or more significant.

Q5: "The defeat of the Spanish Armada was the most important event of Elizabeth's reign." How far do you agree? (16 marks + 4 SPaG)

Answer: A balanced answer should discuss: the Armada (it saved England from invasion, boosted Elizabeth's prestige, and established English naval power); other important events such as the Religious Settlement 1559 (provided 45 years of stability), the execution of Mary Queen of Scots (removed the Catholic figurehead), the defeat of the Catholic plots, the exploration and cultural achievements. Argue either that the Armada was the defining moment of the reign or that the Religious Settlement was more important because it had longer-lasting consequences.

Q6: Explain the weaknesses of Philip II's invasion plan. (4 marks)

Answer: Weaknesses of Philip's plan: it required the Armada to meet Parma's army at a specific time and place, which was impossible without modern communications; Parma's troops were blockaded in port by Dutch ships and could not join the Armada; the Spanish commander Medina Sidonia had no naval experience; many Spanish ships were armed merchant vessels, not purpose-built warships; the Spanish were designed for boarding actions but the English used long-range gunnery; there was no fallback plan if the rendezvous failed.
