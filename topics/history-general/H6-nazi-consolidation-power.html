Lesson 2: Core Concepts: nazi consolidation of power

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on nazi consolidation of power. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: On 27 February 1933, the Reichstag building in Berlin was set on fire. Marinus van der Lubbe, a Dutch Communist, was found at the scene and arrested. Hitler used the fire to persuade Hindenburg to issue the Decree for the Protection of the People and State on 28 February 1933, which suspended all civil liberties.
Key Fact: In the March 1933 election, the Nazis won 43.9% of the vote (288 seats). This was not a majority, but combined with the DNVP's 8% (52 seats), Hitler had a majority in the Reichstag. The election was held in an atmosphere of terror - SA thugs patrolled the streets, opposition meetings were banned, and the Communist Party was effectively illegal.
Key Fact: The Enabling Act (Law to Remedy the Distress of the People and the Reich), passed on 23 March 1933, gave Hitler the power to make laws without the Reichstag for four years. This effectively made Hitler a legal dictator. It required a two-thirds majority, which Hitler achieved by arresting Communists and intimidating the remaining deputies.
Key Fact: On 14 July 1933, the Law Against the Formation of New Parties made the NSDAP the only legal political party in Germany. Attempting to form or maintain another party became a criminal offence. Germany was now a one-party state.
Key Fact: On 29-30 June 1934, Hitler ordered the SS to murder the leadership of the SA, including its head Ernst Röhm. At least 85 people were killed, including former Chancellor von Schleicher and other conservative critics. The SA's power was broken and the SS (under Himmler) became the dominant force in Nazi terror.
Key Fact: By August 1934, Hitler had achieved total power: the Reichstag was powerless; all other parties were banned; trade unions were abolished; the SA was neutered; the army had sworn personal loyalty; and he combined the roles of President and Chancellor. Germany was now a totalitarian dictatorship.

Q: Explain how the Reichstag Fire helped Hitler consolidate power. (4 marks)

Answer: The Reichstag Fire (27 February 1933) allowed Hitler to persuade Hindenburg to issue the Reichstag Fire Decree (28 February), which suspended civil liberties including freedom of speech, assembly, and the press. This gave the Nazis legal authority to arrest 4,000 Communists, ban opposition meetings, and use SA violence freely. It created an atmosphere of fear before the March 1933 election, helping the Nazis win 43.9% of the vote.

Lesson 3: Application: nazi consolidation of power

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain how the Reichstag Fire helped Hitler consolidate power. (4 marks)

Answer: The Reichstag Fire (27 February 1933) allowed Hitler to persuade Hindenburg to issue the Reichstag Fire Decree (28 February), which suspended civil liberties including freedom of speech, assembly, and the press. This gave the Nazis legal authority to arrest 4,000 Communists, ban opposition meetings, and use SA violence freely. It created an atmosphere of fear before the March 1933 election, helping the Nazis win 43.9% of the vote.

Q2: How important was the Enabling Act in the establishment of the Nazi dictatorship? (8 marks)

Answer: The Enabling Act (23 March 1933) was critically important because it gave Hitler the power to make laws without the Reichstag for four years. This single law destroyed the Weimar Constitution and created the legal basis for dictatorship. Without it, Hitler could not have banned parties, abolished trade unions, or created the one-party state. However, it was not sufficient on its own - the Reichstag Fire Decree had already suspended civil liberties, and the Night of the Long Knives was needed to eliminate internal threats. The Enabling Act was the legal cornerstone of the dictatorship but required other measures to make it complete.

Q3: "The Night of the Long Knives was the most important event in Hitler's consolidation of power." How far do you agree? (12 marks)

Answer: The Night of the Long Knives was very important because: it destroyed the SA as a rival power base; it secured the army's support for Hitler (crucial for becoming Fuhrer); it eliminated potential opposition within the Nazi movement itself; and it showed that Hitler ruled by terror. However, other events were equally or more important: the Reichstag Fire gave Hitler emergency powers; the Enabling Act gave him legal dictatorship; and the banning of parties eliminated all organised opposition. The Night of the Long Knives was essential for securing the army's backing, but without the Enabling Act, Hitler could not have consolidated power at all.

Q4: Explain why Hitler was able to ban all political parties by July 1933. (8 marks)

Answer: Hitler was able to ban all parties by July 1933 because: the Reichstag Fire Decree (28 February) suspended civil liberties, allowing the arrest of opponents; the Enabling Act (23 March) gave Hitler the power to make laws without the Reichstag; the KPD was effectively destroyed first (March 1933); trade unions were abolished on 2 May, removing workers' ability to organise; the SPD was formally banned on 22 June; and remaining parties "voluntarily" dissolved under pressure. The Law Against New Parties (14 July) completed the process. Each step was made possible by the powers gained from the previous one.

Q5: "Hitler consolidated power through legal means rather than violence." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: Hitler used a combination of legal and violent methods. Legal methods included: the Reichstag Fire Decree (signed by Hindenburg); the Enabling Act (passed by the Reichstag); laws banning parties and trade unions; and the plebiscite of August 1934. These gave the dictatorship a veneer of legality. However, violence was equally important: the SA terrorised voters and opponents; the Reichstag Fire was exploited to justify emergency powers; the Night of the Long Knives used murder to eliminate threats; and the constant threat of arrest by the SS/Gestapo kept the population compliant. Hitler's genius was using legal methods to authorise violence - the Decree and Enabling Act made SA and SS violence state-sanctioned. Both were essential; neither alone would have been sufficient.
