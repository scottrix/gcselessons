Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the golden years: stresemann

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the golden years: stresemann. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Gustav Stresemann served as Chancellor for just 102 days (August-November 1923) but remained Foreign Secretary from August 1923 until his death on 3 October 1929. He was the dominant figure in German politics during the "Golden Years" and was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1926 alongside French Foreign Minister Aristide Briand.
Key Fact: The Dawes Plan, negotiated by American banker Charles Dawes, restructured Germany's reparations payments. Annual payments were reduced from £50 million to a starting figure of £10 million, rising to £25 million over 5 years. Crucially, it also arranged American loans of 800 million gold marks to Germany.
Key Fact: The Locarno Pact was signed on 16 October 1925 in Locarno, Switzerland. Germany, France and Belgium agreed to guarantee their mutual western borders as set by Versailles. This was hailed as the beginning of a new era of peace - the "Locarno Spring" or "Locarno Honeymoon."
Key Fact: The Weimar Republic (1919-1933) was one of the most creative periods in German cultural history. Berlin became one of the most exciting and cosmopolitan cities in Europe, known for its art, cinema, architecture, and nightlife.
Key Judgement: The "Golden Years" were golden mainly for the urban middle class and big business. Farmers, agricultural workers, and those on fixed incomes continued to struggle. The recovery was built on fragile foundations - American loans that could be withdrawn at any time. When the Wall Street Crash happened in October 1929, just a month after Stresemann's death, the entire recovery collapsed.

Q1: Explain two ways Stresemann improved Germany's international position. (4 marks)

Answer: Through the Locarno Pact (October 1925), which improved relations with France and Britain by guaranteeing western borders, and by joining the League of Nations (September 1926) as a permanent Council member, which restored Germany to equal status in international affairs. Both achievements showed that the policy of "fulfilment" was working.

Q2: How far did the Dawes Plan solve Germany's economic problems? (8 marks)

Answer: The Dawes Plan helped by reducing annual reparations from £50m to a starting £10m, arranging 800 million gold marks in American loans, and leading to French withdrawal from the Ruhr. Industrial output recovered and employment rose. However, it did not solve all problems: Germany became dependent on American loans; reparations were still being paid; unemployment never fell below 1 million; and farmers continued to struggle. The Plan addressed symptoms but not the underlying fragility of depending on foreign credit.

Q3: "The Locarno Pact was Stresemann's greatest achievement." How far do you agree? (12 marks)

Answer: The Locarno Pact was a great achievement: it normalised relations with France, guaranteed western borders, led to Germany joining the League of Nations, and created the "Locarno Spring" of international goodwill. However, the Dawes Plan was arguably equally important because it provided the economic foundation for recovery. The Young Plan (1929) actually reduced total reparations. Locarno's weakness was that Germany refused to guarantee eastern borders, leaving the door open for future revision. Overall, Locarno was important diplomatically but the economic measures were equally crucial.

Q4: Explain why Weimar culture was controversial. (8 marks)

Answer: Weimar culture was controversial because it challenged traditional values. Bauhaus and New Objectivity art were seen as ugly and "un-German" by conservatives. Cabaret, jazz and liberal attitudes towards sexuality offended traditional moral values. The "New Woman" threatened the traditional family model. Remarque's anti-war novel offended nationalists. Conservatives called it "cultural Bolshevism." The Nazis later condemned it as "degenerate art." The cultural divide between progressive cities and conservative rural areas showed that Weimar society was deeply split.

Q5: "The years 1924-1929 were truly golden for the Weimar Republic." How far do you agree? (16 marks)

Answer: The years 1924-1929 were golden in some respects: the economy recovered, Germany regained international respect, unemployment fell, and culture flourished. However, the recovery was fragile: it depended on American loans; unemployment never fell below 1 million; farmers suffered throughout; coalition governments remained unstable; extremist parties (Nazis 2.6% in 1928, Communists growing) never disappeared; and the cultural flowering divided rather than united society. The death of Stresemann in October 1929 and the Wall Street Crash a month later showed how shallow the recovery was. The "Golden Years" were golden for urban middle classes and big business, but not for all Germans.
