Lesson 2: Core Concepts: hypothesis formulation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on hypothesis formulation. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A hypothesis is a testable statement that predicts the outcome of an investigation.
Key Fact: A good hypothesis is specific, measurable and can be tested using data.
Key Fact: The null hypothesis (H₀) states there is no difference, no effect or no relationship — it is the default position.
Key Fact: The alternative hypothesis (H₁) states there is a difference, an effect or a relationship.
Key Fact: Statistical tests aim to find evidence to reject the null hypothesis in favour of the alternative.
Key Fact: An independent variable is the variable you change or categorise; a dependent variable is the one you measure.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A student wants to investigate whether people who exercise regularly have a lower resting heart rate. Write a suitable hypothesis and identify the variables. <div class="

Hypothesis: People who exercise regularly have a lower resting heart rate than those who do not exercise regularly. Independent variable: Whether the person exercises regularly (categorised as 'exercises regularly' or 'does not exercise regularly'). Dependent variable: Resting heart rate (measured in beats per minute). The hypothesis is testable because both variables can be measured: exercise habits can be categorised and resting heart rate can be recorded. Data from both groups can then be compared using an appropriate diagram and summary statistic.

Exam Tips: Always write your hypothesis before collecting any data — never change it to fit results. | Use precise comparative language: 'greater than', 'less than', 'there is a relationship between'. | Make sure your hypothesis mentions both variables clearly. | In the exam, you may be asked to write or evaluate a hypothesis — check it is specific and testable. | Know the difference between null and alternative hypotheses even if you do not use H₀/H₁ notation.
Common Errors: ✗ Writing a hypothesis as a question ✓ Writing a hypothesis as a clear statement, e.g. 'Students who revise more score higher.' ✗ Using vague language like 'might affect' in a hypothesis ✓ Using definite comparative language, e.g. 'Students who sleep 8+ hours perform better than those who sleep less.' ✗ Confusing independent and dependent variables ✓ Identifying the independent variable as what you change/categorise and the dependent variable as what you measure. ✗ Writing a hypothesis that cannot be tested with data ✓ Ensuring the hypothesis refers to measurable variables that can be investigated quantitatively or qualitatively.
