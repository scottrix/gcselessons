Lesson 2: Core Concepts: statistical enquiry cycle

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Statistical Enquiry Cycle (SEC) is a structured approach to carrying out a statistical investigation.
Key Fact: The five/six stages are: Plan, Collect, Process, Present, Interpret and Evaluate.
Key Fact: Some specifications list five stages (combining Interpret and Evaluate); others list six.
Key Fact: Plan: Define the hypothesis, identify variables, decide on methods and sample.
Key Fact: Collect: Gather data using your chosen method — survey, experiment, observation or secondary sources.
Key Fact: Process: Clean, organise and summarise the data — calculate statistics, group data, deal with anomalies.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: statistical enquiry cycle

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A student investigates whether taller people have larger shoe sizes. They collect heights and shoe sizes from 30 classmates, calculate the mean for each group, draw a scatter graph and conclude the hypothesis is supported. Identify the stage for each action. <div class="

1. 'They collect heights and shoe sizes from 30 classmates' — Collect stage: gathering raw data. 2. 'Calculate the mean for each group' — Process stage: summarising the data using a statistic. 3. 'Draw a scatter graph' — Present stage: displaying the data visually. 4. 'Conclude the hypothesis is supported' — Interpret stage: drawing a conclusion and relating it to the hypothesis. The student should also carry out the Evaluate stage, assessing whether 30 classmates is a representative sample, whether there may be bias (e.g. all same age), and whether the method could be improved.

Exam Tips: Learn the stages in order — exam questions often ask you to name or sequence them. | When asked which stage an action belongs to, think about what the action achieves. | Calculating statistics is 'Process'; drawing a chart is 'Present'; writing a conclusion is 'Interpret'. | Evaluation questions are common — always comment on bias, sample size and method. | Remember the cycle can be iterative — evaluation leads to improvement and re-planning.
Common Errors: ✗ Confusing the Process and Present stages ✓ Process involves calculating summary statistics and cleaning data; Present involves drawing charts and diagrams. ✗ Thinking the cycle is strictly one-way ✓ The cycle is iterative — evaluation often leads back to planning improvements. ✗ Putting 'drawing conclusions' in the Present stage ✓ Drawing conclusions is part of the Interpret stage; Present is for creating visual displays only. ✗ Forgetting to link conclusions back to the hypothesis in the Interpret stage ✓ Always relate your findings explicitly to the original hypothesis, stating whether the data supports it.
