Lesson 2: Core Concepts: index numbers &amp; population estimation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on index numbers &amp; population estimation. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: An index number measures how a quantity changes relative to a base period; the base value is always 100
Key Fact: Index number = (current value ÷ base value) × 100; an index of 120 means a 20% increase from the base period
Key Fact: RPI (Retail Prices Index) measures the change in cost of a basket of goods and services including mortgage interest payments
Key Fact: CPI (Consumer Prices Index) is similar to RPI but excludes mortgage interest and housing costs; CPI is used for the government’s inflation target
Key Fact: GDP (Gross Domestic Product) measures the total value of goods and services produced in a country; GDP per capita = GDP ÷ population
Key Fact: A price relative = (current price ÷ base price) × 100; it shows the change in price for a single item

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A health researcher compares two towns. Town X has a crude death rate of 14 per 1000. Town Y has a crude death rate of 9 per 1000. After standardising for age, Town X has a standardised rate of 10 per 1000 and Town Y has a standardised rate of 11 per 1000. Explain these results. <div class="

The crude rates suggest Town X has a higher death rate (14 vs 9 per 1000), which might lead to the conclusion that Town X is less healthy. However, after standardising for age, Town Y actually has the higher standardised death rate (11 vs 10 per 1000). This reversal happens because Town X likely has a larger proportion of older residents. Older people are more likely to die, so Town X’s crude rate is inflated by its age structure. Once we adjust for this by applying the same standard age distribution to both towns, the true picture emerges: Town Y has slightly worse health outcomes when age is accounted for. This demonstrates why standardised rates are essential for fair comparisons between populations with different age structures.

Exam Tips: Always state the base year when giving an index number, and show the calculation as (current/base) × 100 | For weighted index questions, set out a clear table with columns for item, price relative, and weight before calculating | When discussing RPI vs CPI, mention that CPI excludes mortgage interest payments and is the government’s preferred measure | In capture-recapture questions, list the assumptions and explain how violating each one would affect the estimate | For crude vs standardised rate questions, always explain that crude rates can be misleading due to different population structures
Common Errors: ✗ Saying an index of 130 means prices are 130% higher ✓ An index of 130 means a 30% increase from the base period (index of 100) ✗ Using a simple average instead of a weighted average when items have different importance ✓ Multiply each price relative by its weight, sum, then divide by the sum of weights ✗ Forgetting to check capture-recapture assumptions before calculating ✓ Always state assumptions: closed population, no lost marks, equal chance of capture ✗ Confusing CPI and RPI ✓ CPI excludes housing costs like mortgage interest; RPI includes them. CPI is used for the inflation target.
