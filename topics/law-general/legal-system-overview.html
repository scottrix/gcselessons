Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the english legal system

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the english legal system. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The English legal system is based on common law (judge-made law) and statute law (Acts of Parliament)
Key Fact: Parliament is the supreme law-making body — parliamentary sovereignty means no Parliament can bind its successors
Key Fact: The court hierarchy ensures that lower courts follow the decisions of higher courts (doctrine of precedent)
Key Fact: The Supreme Court is the highest court in the UK for civil and criminal matters
Key Fact: Legal personnel include judges, magistrates, solicitors, barristers and legal executives
Key Fact: Judges are independent — they are not controlled by the government and must decide cases impartially

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How far does the English legal system provide equal access to justice? (15 marks) <div class="

The English legal system aims to provide equal access to justice but falls short in practice, particularly for those who cannot afford legal representation. Legal aid exists to help those on low incomes, ensuring that the most serious criminal charges and some civil matters can be funded. The availability of duty solicitors at police stations and Magistrates' Courts provides immediate access to advice for anyone accused of a crime. The small claims track in civil courts allows individuals to represent themselves without expensive lawyers. Conditional fee agreements (no win, no fee) provide access to civil justice for those who cannot pay upfront. However, legal aid has been severely cut since LASPO 2012, removing funding for most civil cases including family law, housing and debt. This has created 'deserts' where no legal aid lawyers are available. The complexity of the legal system disadvantages those without representation — litigants in person often struggle to navigate procedures and present their cases effectively. Exam Tips: Know the difference between common law and statute law — both are sources of law | Parliamentary sovereignty is a key concept — be able to explain and evaluate it | The court hierarchy is essential knowledge — know which courts hear which cases | Use specific examples of legal personnel and their roles | Legal aid is often examined as a contemporary issue — know its scope and limitations
Common Errors: ✗ Confusing common law with statute law ✓ Common law = judge-made through court decisions; statute law = made by Parliament through Acts ✗ Saying Parliament is controlled by the courts ✓ Parliamentary sovereignty means courts cannot override Acts of Parliament — courts interpret and apply them ✗ Thinking magistrates are professional judges ✓ Magistrates are unpaid volunteers — they are not legally qualified (though they have legal advisers) ✗ Describing the rule of law as just 'following rules' ✓ The rule of law means everyone, including government, is subject to law applied equally — it limits arbitrary power
