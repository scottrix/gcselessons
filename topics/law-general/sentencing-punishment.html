Lesson 2: Core Concepts: sentencing &amp; punishment

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The five main aims of sentencing are: retribution (punishing the offender for what they have done), deterrence (discouraging the offender or others from reoffending), rehabilitation (reforming the offender to prevent future crime), incapacitation (protecting the public by removing dangerous offenders) and reparation (making the offender compensate the victim or society)
Key Fact: Deterrence is divided into individual deterrence (discouraging the specific offender) and general deterrence (discouraging others from committing similar offences)
Key Fact: The Sentencing Council issues guidelines that courts must follow unless it would be contrary to the interests of justice to do so, ensuring consistency in sentencing
Key Fact: Custodial sentences include: detention for offenders under 18, imprisonment for adult offenders, and life sentences for the most serious offences including murder (which carries a mandatory life sentence)
Key Fact: Community orders under the Sentencing Act 2020 can include requirements such as unpaid work, curfew, drug rehabilitation, attendance at specified activities and prohibitions on travel
Key Fact: Fines are the most common sentence in the Magistrates' Court; they must be based on the offender's ability to pay and the seriousness of the offence

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How far do custodial sentences achieve the aims of sentencing? (15 marks) <div class="

Custodial sentences achieve some aims of sentencing more effectively than others. They are most effective at incapacitation, removing dangerous offenders from society and preventing them from causing further harm during their imprisonment. They also serve retribution by imposing a severe punishment that reflects the seriousness of the offence and satisfies society's expectation that serious wrongdoing is punished. General deterrence may be partially achieved, as the knowledge that serious offences lead to imprisonment may discourage some potential offenders. However, custodial sentences are much less effective at rehabilitation. The prison environment can entrench criminal behaviour through association with other offenders, and limited access to education, treatment and training means many prisoners are not reformed. Recidivism rates are high — around 48% of adults reoffend within one year of release, suggesting imprisonment fails to rehabilitate. Individual deterrence is also questionable, as many offenders reoffend after release. Exam Tips: When discussing aims of sentencing, always define the aim and give a specific example of how it works in practice | Use the terms 'aggravating' and 'mitigating' factors correctly — not 'factors that make it worse/better' | When evaluating a sentence, consider which aims of sentencing it serves and whether it achieves them effectively | Remember that murder carries a mandatory life sentence — the judge has no discretion on this, only on the minimum term (tariff) | For young offenders, always emphasise rehabilitation as the primary aim and detention as a last resort
