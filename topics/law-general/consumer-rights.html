Lesson 2: Core Concepts: consumer rights

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on consumer rights. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Consumer Rights Act 2015 is the main law protecting consumers in contracts with businesses
Key Fact: Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described — if not, the consumer has rights to reject, repair or replacement
Key Fact: For faulty goods, the consumer has 30 days to reject for a full refund; after 30 days, they can request repair or replacement
Key Fact: If a repair or replacement fails, the consumer is entitled to a refund (which may be reduced for use)
Key Fact: Services must be performed with reasonable care and skill, within a reasonable time and at a reasonable price
Key Fact: Digital content (downloads, apps, games) must also be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described under the 2015 Act

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How effectively does the Consumer Rights Act 2015 protect consumers? (15 marks) <div class="

The Consumer Rights Act 2015 provides the strongest consumer protection framework in English legal history, but enforcement gaps and practical barriers mean that protection is not always effective in reality. The Act consolidates and modernises consumer law into a single, accessible statute, making rights clearer for both consumers and traders. The three implied terms for goods — satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described — set clear minimum standards that traders must meet. The short-term right to reject within 30 days gives consumers a powerful remedy that the old law did not provide. The inclusion of digital content is a significant modernisation, recognising that consumers buy apps, downloads and games that were previously unprotected. Unfair terms provisions prevent traders from imposing one-sided conditions, and the transparency requirement ensures that key terms are brought to the consumer's attention. However, the Act has significant limitations. Its protections depend on consumers knowing their rights — research consistently shows low awareness of consumer rights, particularly among vulnerable groups.
