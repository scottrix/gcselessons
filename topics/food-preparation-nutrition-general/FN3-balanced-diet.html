Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the balanced diet & eatwell guide

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the balanced diet & eatwell guide. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Eatwell Guide (2016) divides food into five groups: fruit and vegetables (at least 5-a-day), starchy carbohydrates (base of each meal), dairy and alternatives (moderate amounts), proteins (beans, pulses, fish, eggs, meat), and oils and spreads (small amounts).
Key Fact: The Eatwell Guide applies to the overall diet, not individual meals. It shows the proportions of each food group that should be consumed over a day or week.
Key Fact: Dietary Reference Values (DRVs) are estimates of the energy and nutrient requirements for different population groups. They include EAR (Estimated Average Requirement), RNI (Reference Nutrient Intake), and LRNI (Lower Reference Nutrient Intake).
Key Fact: RNI is the amount of a nutrient sufficient for almost all individuals in a population group. If intake falls below LRNI, deficiency is likely.
Key Fact: Energy requirements vary by age, sex, activity level, and life stage. Adolescent boys need about 2,200-2,800 kcal/day; adolescent girls about 1,800-2,200 kcal/day.
Key Fact: Nutritional needs at different life stages: infants (rapid growth, need high energy and nutrient density), teenagers (growth spurts, increased calcium and iron needs), elderly (reduced energy needs but same micronutrient needs, vitamin D and B12 concerns).

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Plan a nutritionally balanced day's meals for a 15-year-old vegetarian girl. Justify your choices. [12 marks] <div class="

A nutritionally balanced day for a 15-year-old vegetarian girl must meet increased needs for iron, calcium, and protein during adolescence while addressing the limitations of a vegetarian diet. Breakfast: Porridge made with fortified plant milk, topped with berries and seeds. Porridge provides slow-release energy from complex carbohydrates. Fortified plant milk supplies calcium and vitamin D. Berries provide vitamin C (which enhances iron absorption). Seeds add omega-3 fatty acids. Lunch: Wholegrain pitta with hummus, roasted vegetables, and a side salad. The wholegrain pitta provides fibre and B vitamins. Hummus provides protein and iron from chickpeas. The salad vegetables add vitamins and fibre. Adding a glass of orange juice enhances iron absorption from the plant-based meal. Dinner: Tofu stir-fry with mixed vegetables and brown rice. Tofu provides HBV protein and calcium (if set with calcium salts). Mixed vegetables provide a range of vitamins and minerals. Brown rice adds fibre and B vitamins. Common Errors: ✗ The Eatwell Guide applies to every single meal. ✓ The Eatwell Guide shows proportions for the overall diet, not individual meals. A meal doesn't need to include all five groups — it's the balance across a day or week that matters. ✗ Eating the RNI of a nutrient guarantees you won't be deficient. ✓ RNI is sufficient for almost all individuals (97.5% of the population), but individual requirements vary. Some people may need more than the RNI due to health conditions, activity levels, or absorption issues. ✗ A balanced diet means eating equal amounts of each food group. ✓ A balanced diet means eating the correct proportions of each food group — not equal amounts.
