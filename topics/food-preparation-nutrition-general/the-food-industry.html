Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the food industry

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the food industry. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The food industry encompasses all stages from farm to fork: production, processing, manufacturing, distribution, retail, and food service.
Key Fact: Primary production: farming (arable, pastoral, mixed), fishing, and aquaculture (fish farming).
Key Fact: Food manufacturing transforms raw ingredients into processed products; the UK food manufacturing sector is the largest manufacturing sector by employment.
Key Fact: Distribution includes transport (road, rail, sea, air), cold chain logistics (refrigerated transport and storage), and warehousing.
Key Fact: Retail: supermarkets dominate UK food retail (the 'Big Four' are Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons); also discounters (Aldi, Lidl), convenience stores, and online shopping.
Key Fact: Food service includes: restaurants, takeaways, school and hospital catering, workplace canteens, and the growing meal delivery sector.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe the journey of food from farm to fork, explaining the role of each stage in the supply chain. Discuss one environmental impact of the food industry. (8 marks) <div class="

Food travels through several stages from farm to fork. Primary production involves farming (arable crops, livestock, dairy) and fishing or aquaculture, where raw food materials are produced. Processing and manufacturing transforms these raw materials: milling wheat into flour, pasteurising milk, and manufacturing products like bread, cheese, and ready meals. This stage adds value, extends shelf life, and creates convenience products. Distribution and logistics transport food from manufacturers to retailers using road, rail, and sea freight. The cold chain — refrigerated transport and storage — ensures perishable foods remain safe throughout. Retail includes supermarkets, convenience stores, and increasingly online platforms that sell food to consumers. Food service (restaurants, takeaways, catering) prepares and serves food directly. Each stage adds cost, processing, and environmental impact. A major environmental impact is greenhouse gas emissions: livestock farming produces methane, a gas 25 times more potent than CO2; food transport and cold chain logistics consume fossil fuels; and food processing factories use significant energy. Common Errors: ✗ The food industry only includes farming and food manufacturing. ✓ The food industry covers all stages from farm to fork: primary production, processing, manufacturing, distribution, retail, food service, and waste management. ✗ Supermarket dominance is always bad for consumers. ✓ Supermarkets offer low prices through economies of scale and wide choice, benefiting consumers. However, concerns include reduced choice of independent shops, pressure on suppliers to lower prices, and promotion of unhealthy processed foods. ✗ Food prices are set by the government. ✓ Food prices are largely determined by market forces (supply, demand, production costs, global trade).
