Lesson 2: Core Concepts: food preparation skills

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on food preparation skills. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Mise en place means 'everything in its place' — preparing and organising all ingredients and equipment before cooking begins, ensuring efficient and safe food preparation.
Key Fact: The bridge grip is used to hold food securely when cutting: form a bridge with thumb and fingers over the food, guiding the knife underneath. Used for cutting foods in half.
Key Fact: The claw grip is used for slicing and chopping: curl fingers inward like a claw, with fingertips behind the knuckles, guiding the knife while keeping fingers safe.
Key Fact: Knife types and uses: chef's knife (general chopping), paring knife (small precision work), bread knife (serrated for bread), filleting knife (flexible for fish), carving knife (slicing cooked meat).
Key Fact: Weighing methods: digital scales (most accurate), balance scales, measuring spoons (level for dry, heaped for some ingredients), measuring jugs for liquids.
Key Fact: Standard measuring: 1 teaspoon = 5ml, 1 tablespoon = 15ml, 1 cup = 240ml; use level measures unless recipe specifies 'heaped' or 'rounded'.

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on food preparation skills from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Demonstrate knowledge of specific knife grips (bridge and claw) — these are frequently tested practical skills. | When asked about weighing and measuring, emphasise accuracy and explain what happens if measurements are wrong. | Safety questions require specific practices, not vague answers: name the grip, describe the technique, explain the precaution. | Use French culinary terms where relevant (mise en place) to show technical knowledge.
Common Errors: ✗ You hold food with your flat hand when chopping. ✓ You should use the claw grip: curl your fingers inward with fingertips behind the knuckles, guiding the knife safely. A flat hand exposes fingertips to the blade. ✗ A dull knife is safer than a sharp knife. ✓ A sharp knife is safer because it requires less force, gives better control, and is less likely to slip.
