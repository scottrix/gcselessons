Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: NEA Task 2 is a food preparation assessment worth 35% of the total GCSE grade (AQA 8585). It is completed in controlled conditions over approximately 20 hours.
Key Fact: Task 2 requires students to plan, prepare, cook, and present a menu of three dishes (plus accompaniments) in response to a set brief, typically within a 3-hour practical session.
Key Fact: The brief specifies a context and target group, e.g. 'Plan and prepare a menu for a teenager following a vegetarian diet' or 'Produce a menu for a family on a low budget'.
Key Fact: Task 2 portfolio must include: research into the brief, analysis of the task, menu planning and justification, nutritional analysis, time plan, costings, practical skills evidence, and evaluation.
Key Fact: High-scoring menus demonstrate a wide range of skills: at least one complex skill per dish (e.g. making pastry from scratch, filleting fish, bread-making, sauce-making from a roux).
Key Fact: The time plan (production schedule) is critical: it lists every task in chronological order with timings, oven temperatures, and key checkpoints for the 3-hour session.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response You have been set the following NEA Task 2 brief: 'Plan and prepare three dishes suitable for a vegetarian teenager, ensuring the menu is nutritionally balanced.' Explain how you would approach this task, including your choice of dishes with justification, key skills, and how you would ensure nutritional balance. (8 marks) <div class="

I would begin by researching the nutritional needs of vegetarian teenagers, who require adequate protein (for growth), iron (to replace that normally obtained from red meat), calcium (for bone development), vitamin B12 (from eggs and dairy), and vitamin C (to enhance iron absorption from plant sources). My three-dish menu would be: Starter — roasted red pepper and goat's cheese tart with a homemade shortcrust pastry case (complex skill: pastry-making from scratch; provides protein and calcium from goat's cheese, vitamin C from peppers). Main course — chickpea and spinach curry with basmati rice and homemade naan bread (complex skills: bread-making and spice blending; provides iron from spinach and chickpeas, protein from chickpeas, complex carbohydrates from rice and bread). Dessert — mango and pistachio yoghurt panna cotta with a berry coulis (skill: gelatine setting and coulis-making; provides calcium from yoghurt, vitamins from fruit, and healthy fats from pistachios). This menu is nutritionally balanced because it provides protein from multiple sources (goat's cheese, chickpeas, yoghurt), iron from spinach and chickpeas, calcium from cheese and yoghurt, and vitamin C from peppe

Exam Tips: Plan a menu that shows the WIDEST range of skills possible — include at least one complex skill per dish. | Your time plan must be realistic: allow time for preparation, cooking, washing up between dishes, and final presentation. | Always justify dish choices by linking them back to the brief: nutritional needs, target group, budget, skills demonstrated. | The evaluation should be honest and reflective — identifying what went wrong and how you would fix it earns more marks than claiming everything was perfect.
