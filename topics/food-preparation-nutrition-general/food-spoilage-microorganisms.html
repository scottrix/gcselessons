Lesson 2: Core Concepts: food spoilage & microorganisms

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on food spoilage & microorganisms. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Food spoilage is caused by microorganisms (bacteria, yeasts, moulds) breaking down food.
Key Fact: Bacteria are the most common cause of food spoilage and food-borne illness.
Key Fact: Yeasts cause fermentation, producing alcohol and carbon dioxide — useful in bread-making but spoilage in other foods.
Key Fact: Moulds grow as visible fuzzy patches and can produce harmful mycotoxins.
Key Fact: The conditions microorganisms need to grow are summarised by FATTOM: Food, Acidity, Temperature, Time, Oxygen, Moisture.
Key Fact: The danger zone for bacterial growth is 5°C to 63°C; bacteria multiply fastest at around 37°C.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: food spoilage & microorganisms

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on food spoilage & microorganisms from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to FATTOM when explaining how to prevent microbial growth — examiners look for this acronym. | Distinguish clearly between spoilage (food becomes unpalatable) and food poisoning (food causes illness) — they are not the same. | When asked about storage, always state the specific temperature: fridge at 0–5°C, freezer at −18°C or below. | Use scientific vocabulary: say 'microorganisms' or 'pathogenic bacteria' rather than 'germs' or 'bugs'.
Common Errors: ✗ Freezing kills all bacteria in food. ✓ Freezing stops bacterial growth but does not kill them; bacteria survive in a dormant state and can multiply again when thawed. ✗ All bacteria cause food spoilage. ✓ Many bacteria are harmless or even beneficial (e.g. in yoghurt production). Only some bacteria cause spoilage or food poisoning. ✗ If food looks and smells fine, it is safe to eat. ✓ Pathogenic bacteria may not produce any visible signs of spoilage — food can look and smell fine but still cause food poisoning. ✗ Mouldy food is safe to eat if you cut the mould off. ✓ Moulds can produce mycotoxins that spread through the food. Soft foods with mould should be discarded entirely.
