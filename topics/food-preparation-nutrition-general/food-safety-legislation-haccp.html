Lesson 2: Core Concepts: food safety legislation & haccp

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on food safety legislation & haccp. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Food Safety Act 1990 is the key UK legislation: it requires food businesses to ensure food is safe, not harmful to health, and labelled accurately.
Key Fact: Under the Food Safety Act, it is an offence to sell food that is unfit for human consumption, or to describe food falsely (e.g. mislabelling).
Key Fact: Environmental Health Officers (EHOs) enforce food safety law: they inspect premises, take food samples, issue improvement notices, and can close businesses that pose a health risk.
Key Fact: EHOs inspect food businesses unannounced and assess hygiene based on: food handling, physical condition of premises, and confidence in management.
Key Fact: The Food Hygiene Rating Scheme (FHRS) gives businesses a rating from 0 (urgent improvement) to 5 (very good), displayed as a sticker.
Key Fact: HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) is a systematic method for identifying and controlling food safety hazards.

Exam Tips: Learn the 7 HACCP principles in order — examiners often ask you to list or apply them. | Always give three types of hazard (biological, chemical, physical) with specific examples when discussing HACCP. | When asked about legislation, name the specific Act (Food Safety Act 1990) and state its key requirements. | For due diligence, explain it is a legal defence that requires documented evidence of all reasonable precautions.
Common Errors: ✗ HACCP stands for Health And Catering Control Plan. ✓ HACCP stands for Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points — it is a risk management system, not a general health plan. ✗ A hazard and a risk are the same thing. ✓ A hazard is something with the potential to cause harm (e.g. raw chicken). A risk is the likelihood that the harm will actually occur (e.g. high risk if cross-contamination is not controlled). ✗ The Food Safety Act only applies to restaurants and food manufacturers. ✓ The Food Safety Act 1990 applies to all food businesses, including school canteens, market stalls, food banks, and any organisation that prepares or sells food. ✗ EHOs must give notice before inspecting a food
