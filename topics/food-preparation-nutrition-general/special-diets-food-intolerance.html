Lesson 2: Core Concepts: special diets & food intolerance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on special diets & food intolerance. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A food allergy is an immune system reaction to a specific food protein; symptoms can be severe and include anaphylaxis (a life-threatening reaction).
Key Fact: A food intolerance is a non-immune adverse reaction to food; symptoms are usually less severe and include bloating, diarrhoea, and stomach cramps.
Key Fact: Coeliac disease is an autoimmune condition where gluten damages the small intestine lining, preventing nutrient absorption. Sufferers must follow a strict gluten-free diet.
Key Fact: Lactose intolerance is the inability to digest lactose (milk sugar) due to a lack of the enzyme lactase; symptoms include bloating and diarrhoea after consuming dairy.
Key Fact: Common food allergies include: peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, soy, wheat, celery, and sesame.
Key Fact: Vegetarians do not eat meat, poultry, or fish; types include lacto-ovo (eat dairy and eggs), lacto (eat dairy), and ovo (eat eggs).

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain the difference between a food allergy and a food intolerance. Describe how you would adapt a cheese and pasta bake for someone with coeliac disease and someone who is lactose intolerant. (8 marks) <div class="

A food allergy involves the immune system reacting to a specific food protein. Reactions can be immediate and severe, including swelling, difficulty breathing, and anaphylaxis (a life-threatening condition treated with adrenaline). Even trace amounts of the allergen can trigger a reaction. A food intolerance does not involve the immune system; it is a difficulty digesting certain foods, causing symptoms such as bloating, stomach cramps, and diarrhoea. Intolerances are generally less dangerous but can significantly affect quality of life. To adapt a cheese and pasta bake for someone with coeliac disease, I would replace wheat-based pasta with gluten-free pasta (made from rice, corn, or quinoa flour) and use a gluten-free flour blend to thicken the cheese sauce, adding xanthan gum to replace gluten's binding properties. I would also check that all other ingredients (stock, mustard, breadcrumb topping) are gluten-free, as even small amounts of gluten can cause damage. For someone with lactose intolerance, I would replace the cow's milk in the sauce with lactose-free milk or a plant-based alternative such as oat milk, and substitute the cheese with lactose-free cheese or a small amount

Exam Tips: Always distinguish between allergy (immune, potentially fatal) and intolerance (non-immune, usually digestive) — this is a very common question. | When adapting recipes, name the specific substitute ingredient AND explain why it works (e.g. 'gluten-free flour blend plus xanthan gum to replace the binding properties of gluten'). | For vegan diets, always mention the key nutrients at risk: B12, iron, calcium, and omega-3 fatty acids. | Learn the symptoms of coeliac disease, lactose intolerance, and anaphylaxis — they are frequently tested.
