Lesson 2: Core Concepts: factors affecting food choice

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on factors affecting food choice. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Food choice is influenced by multiple interacting factors: social, economic, cultural, religious, ethical, psychological, and health-related.
Key Fact: Social factors include: family meals and traditions, peer pressure, media and advertising, and social status (e.g. eating out as a social activity).
Key Fact: Economic factors include: income level, food prices, availability of affordable fresh produce, and access to shops (food deserts).
Key Fact: Cultural factors: different cultures have distinct food traditions, staple foods, flavour preferences, and cooking methods (e.g. rice in Asian cuisine, pasta in Italian cuisine).
Key Fact: Religious factors: Halal and Haram in Islam, Kosher in Judaism, beef avoidance in Hinduism, vegetarianism in some Buddhist traditions.
Key Fact: Ethical factors: animal welfare concerns, fair trade, sustainability, food miles, and environmental impact of food production.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Discuss how social, economic, and cultural factors influence the food choices people make. (8 marks) <div class="

Social factors influence food choice through family traditions (e.g. Sunday roast as a family meal), peer pressure (teenagers choosing trendy or convenience foods), and media influence (advertising and social media promoting certain products). Social occasions such as celebrations and festivals also determine what foods are eaten. Economic factors significantly affect choice: people on low incomes may be unable to afford fresh fruit, vegetables, and quality proteins, and instead rely on cheaper processed foods that are often higher in fat, sugar, and salt. Living in a food desert — where affordable fresh food is hard to access — further limits choice. Cooking skills, time, and kitchen equipment also vary with income. Cultural factors shape food choice through established food traditions and staple foods: rice and soy sauce are staples in many Asian cultures, while pasta and olive oil are central to Italian cuisine. Cultural norms also determine flavour preferences, cooking methods, and meal structures.
