Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Write 4 sentences in Spanish about question forms and modal verbs.

Answer: Four sentences using topic vocabulary, with correct adjective agreement, articles and verb conjugation. Include at least two different tenses for Higher tier.

Q2: Write a paragraph (40-60 words) giving your opinion on question forms and modal verbs.

Answer: A structured paragraph with: introduction (en mi opinión...), opinion with reason (creo que... porque...), a different viewpoint (sin embargo...), and a conclusion (en conclusión...). Use connecting words and varied vocabulary.

Q3: Translate into Spanish: "I think question forms and modal verbs is important because..."

Answer: "Creo que [topic] es importante porque..." — ensure adjective agreement (masculine/feminine), correct verb form, and give a specific reason.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Writing task: Write about question words, inverted question marks, interrogative forms, modal verbs (poder, deber, querer, saber). <div class="

A grade 9 answer will: use at least three tenses accurately; include complex structures (subordinate clauses, pronouns, negation); show precise grammar (agreement, conjugation, accents); express and justify opinions; include cultural knowledge; use a wide range of vocabulary; be well-organised with connecting words (sin embargo, además, por lo tanto, en conclusión). Mark scheme: Content (relevance and detail) + Quality of language (accuracy and range) = total marks

Exam Tips: Use a range of tenses (present, preterite, imperfect, future) to access higher marks | Include opinions with reasons (creo que... porque...) | Check adjective agreement (gender and number) before finishing | Use complex structures: negation (no...nada, no...nunca), pronouns (lo, la, los, las), conjunctions (sin embargo, además, por eso) | Include cultural references to Spanish-speaking countries | For writing: plan your response, check verb conjugations, re-read for errors
Common Errors: ✗ Forgetting adjective agreement — writing "ella es inteligent" instead of "ella es inteligente" ✓ All Spanish adjectives must agree in gender and number with the noun they describe: inteligente works for both, but many add -a for feminine (alto/alta, bueno/buena). ✗ Using the wrong tense — preterite for descriptions instead of imperfect ✓ Use the imperfect for ongoing past actions, descriptions and habits (era, tenía, hacía). Use the preterite for completed single events (fue, tuvo, hizo). ✗ Confusing ser and estar — both mean "to be" but are used differently ✓ Ser is for permanent characteristics, identity, time (es grande, es español).
