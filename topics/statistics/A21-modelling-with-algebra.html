Lesson 3: Application: modelling with algebra

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Mathematical modelling. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: I think of a number, double it, then subtract 5. The result is 17. Find the number.

Answer: 11

Q2: A rectangle has sides x and 2x - 1. The perimeter is 32 cm. Find x.

Answer: x = 5.5 cm

Q3: Tickets cost £12 for adults and £8 for children. A group of 10 people pays £96. How many adults?

Answer: 4 adults (and 6 children)

Q4: Sarah is 3 times as old as her brother. In 12 years, she will be twice his age. How old is Sarah now?

Answer: Sarah is 36, brother is 12

Q5: A phone contract costs £20 per month plus 5p per text. Write a formula for monthly cost C with t texts.

Answer: C = 20 + 0.05t

Q6: Three consecutive numbers add to 78. What are they?

Answer: 25, 26, 27

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: modelling with algebra

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Extended Answer 6 marks: A school trip costs £200 per adult and £120 per child. The total cost for a group is £2800. There are 20 people in total. (a) Write two equations. (b) How many adults and children are in the group? (c) If 2 more adults join, what is the new total cost? <div class="

(a) a + c = 20 and 200a + 120c = 2800 (b) From (1): c = 20 - a. Substitute: 200a + 120(20 - a) = 2800 → 200a + 2400 - 120a = 2800 → 80a = 400 → a = 5, c = 15 5 adults and 15 children (c) New cost = 7 × 200 + 15 × 120 = 1400 + 1800 = £3200 Mark scheme: (a) 2 marks. (b) 2 marks. (c) 2 marks.

Exam Tips: Always define your variables (let x = ...) | Read the question carefully - what do you need to find? | Check your answer makes sense in context | State your final answer clearly with units | Verify by substituting back into the problem
Common Errors: Watch Out! 1. Wrong: "5 more than x" written as 5x Correct: "5 more than x" means x + 5 (5x means 5 times x) 2. Wrong: Forgetting units in the final answer Correct: Always state units (cm, £, kg, etc.) and interpret the answer in context 3. Wrong: Ignoring whether the answer is realistic (e.g., a negative number of people) Correct: Check your answer makes sense in context — you can't have -5 children
AO3 - Reasoning & Interpretation: Reasoning and Interpretation A gardener has 40 m of fencing to make a rectangular enclosure. The length is 4 m more than the width. (a) Write an expression for the perimeter. (b) Find the dimensions. (c) If the gardener wants the area to exceed 80 m², will this enclosure work? Answers: (a) Let width = w. Length = w + 4. Perimeter = 2(w + w + 4) = 4w + 8. (b) 4w + 8 = 40 → w = 8 m, length = 12 m. (c) Area = 8 × 12 = 96 m² > 80 m². Yes, it works.
