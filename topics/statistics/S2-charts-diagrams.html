S2: Charts and Diagrams

Foundation Higher All Boards

Construct and interpret a range of statistical diagrams including bar charts, pie charts, histograms, frequency polygons, and cumulative frequency graphs.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 4
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Construct and interpret bar charts (including compound/dual bar charts)
  • Construct and interpret pie charts, calculating angles from frequencies
  • Construct and interpret histograms with equal and unequal class widths (Higher: frequency density)
  • Construct and interpret frequency polygons
  • Construct and interpret cumulative frequency graphs (ogives) and find median, quartiles, IQR
  • Choose appropriate diagrams for different data types

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of discrete vs continuous data
  • Ability to calculate percentages and angles (360° in a circle)
  • Plotting points on axes
  • Understanding of frequency tables

Materials & Equipment

  • Graph paper / squared paper
  • Protractors (for pie charts)
  • Rulers, sharp pencils
  • Coloured pencils for compound charts
  • Spreadsheet software (optional, for checking)

Lesson 1: Bar Charts and Pie Charts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Interpretation

Show a misleading bar chart (non-zero baseline, 3D distortion). Ask: "What's wrong? How does it mislead?" Discuss importance of accurate representation.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Bar charts: For categorical/discrete data. Gaps between bars. Bars same width. Label axes clearly. Title. Scale starts at 0.
• Compound/dual bar chart: Compare two sets side by side (e.g. boys/girls). Use key/legend.
• Stacked bar chart: Show part-to-whole. Harder to compare parts.

Pie charts: For categorical data showing proportions. Angle = (frequency / total) × 360°.
Steps: 1) Total frequency. 2) Angle per item = 360° / total. 3) Multiply each frequency by angle per item. 4) Draw circle, measure angles with protractor. 5) Label sectors or use key.

Choosing: Bar chart for comparing categories; pie chart for showing proportions of a whole. Pie charts poor for many categories or similar-sized sectors.
Worked Example: Pie Chart

Favourite fruit: Apple 12, Banana 8, Orange 6, Other 4. Total = 30.
Angle per person = 360° / 30 = 12°.
Apple: 12 × 12° = 144°. Banana: 8 × 12° = 96°. Orange: 6 × 12° = 72°. Other: 4 × 12° = 48°. Check: 144+96+72+48 = 360° ✓

Pie chart angle = (category frequency ÷ total frequency) × 360°
Check: sum of all angles = 360°

Common Misconception

"Pie charts and bar charts are interchangeable."

Bar charts show frequency/magnitude for comparison. Pie charts show proportion of a whole. Use bar charts when comparing exact values; pie charts when emphasising share of total.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide pre-drawn circles with centre marked. Use angle calculation template. Give frequency tables with totals already calculated.
Stretch:
Critique real-world pie charts (e.g. from newspapers) — 3D effects, too many slices, missing labels. Create a compound bar chart comparing two years of data.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Match Data to Chart

Give 4 data sets and 4 chart types. Students match and justify: "This is nominal data with 6 categories — pie chart ok but bar chart better for comparison."

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Constructs accurate bar/pie charts with correct labels, scales, angles. Chooses appropriately.
  • Getting there: Constructs charts but makes scaling errors or forgets labels/keys.
  • Not yet: Cannot calculate pie chart angles. Reteach proportion: part/whole × 360°.

Lesson 2: Histograms and Frequency Polygons

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Bar Chart vs Histogram

Show a bar chart and a histogram side by side. Ask: "What's different? When do you use each?" Key: bars touch for continuous data; area represents frequency.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Histograms: For continuous grouped data. No gaps between bars. Area of bar = frequency.
• Equal class widths: Height = frequency (or frequency density = frequency/class width, which equals frequency when width=1).
• Unequal class widths (Higher): Height = frequency density = frequency / class width. Area = frequency density × class width = frequency.

Frequency polygons: Plot points at (midpoint of class, frequency). Join with straight lines. Extend to x-axis at start/end (midpoints of adjacent empty classes).
Can overlay two polygons for comparison.

Key difference: Histogram shows distribution shape via area; frequency polygon shows shape via line — better for comparing multiple distributions on same axes.
Worked Example: Histogram (Unequal Widths)

Height (cm) | Frequency | Class width | Frequency density
140–150 | 8 | 10 | 8/10 = 0.8
150–160 | 15 | 10 | 15/10 = 1.5
160–170 | 22 | 10 | 22/10 = 2.2
170–180 | 18 | 10 | 18/10 = 1.8
180–200 | 7 | 20 | 7/20 = 0.35
Plot bars with heights = frequency density.

Frequency density = Frequency ÷ Class width
Frequency = Frequency density × Class width
Area of bar = Frequency

Common Misconception

"The height of a histogram bar is always the frequency."

Only true when class widths are equal. With unequal widths, height = frequency density. Area represents frequency.

Differentiation

Support:
Start with equal class widths only. Provide table template with columns for class, frequency, width, frequency density. Use pre-drawn axes.
Stretch:
Given a histogram with unequal widths, estimate the frequency in a given range (e.g. 165–175 cm). Work backwards: given histogram, reconstruct frequency table.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Sketch the Shape

Describe distributions: "symmetrical", "positively skewed", "negatively skewed", "bimodal", "uniform". Students sketch histogram and frequency polygon for each.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates frequency density correctly, draws accurate histograms (unequal widths), constructs frequency polygons.
  • Getting there: Manages equal widths but confuses frequency/density for unequal widths.
  • Not yet: Draws bars with height = frequency regardless of class width. Reteach area principle.

Lesson 3: Cumulative Frequency Graphs

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Running Total

Give a frequency table. Ask students to calculate cumulative frequencies. "What does 'cumulative' mean? How is it different from frequency?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Cumulative frequency: Running total of frequencies. Always increases (or stays same).
Cumulative frequency graph (ogive): Plot points at (upper class boundary, cumulative frequency). Join with smooth curve (or straight lines).
Axes: x-axis = variable (upper boundaries); y-axis = cumulative frequency. Start at (lowest boundary, 0).

Reading from graph:
• Median = value at cumulative frequency = total/2 (50th percentile)
• Lower quartile (Q1) = value at cumulative frequency = total/4 (25th percentile)
• Upper quartile (Q3) = value at cumulative frequency = 3×total/4 (75th percentile)
• Interquartile range (IQR) = Q3 − Q1
• Percentiles = value at cumulative frequency = (p/100) × total

Comparison: Overlay two curves on same axes to compare distributions.
Worked Example: Cumulative Frequency

Height (cm) | Freq | Cum. Freq | Upper boundary
140–150 | 8 | 8 | 150
150–160 | 15 | 23 | 160
160–170 | 22 | 45 | 170
170–180 | 18 | 63 | 180
180–200 | 7 | 70 | 200
Plot: (150,8), (160,23), (170,45), (180,63), (200,70).
Total = 70. Median at 35th value ≈ 164 cm. Q1 at 17.5th ≈ 155 cm. Q3 at 52.5th ≈ 174 cm. IQR = 19 cm.

Median position = (n + 1) / 2 (approx n/2 for grouped data)
Q1 position = (n + 1) / 4
Q3 position = 3(n + 1) / 4
IQR = Q3 − Q1

Common Misconception

"Plot cumulative frequency at the midpoint of the class."

Plot at the upper class boundary. The cumulative frequency includes all values up to the end of that class.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide table with cumulative frequency column to complete. Pre-drawn axes with scales. Use rulers to read horizontal values from curve.
Stretch:
Given two cumulative frequency curves, compare medians and IQRs. "Which distribution has greater spread? Which has higher median? What does this mean in context?"

Plenary (5 minutes)

Box Plot Link

Sketch a box plot from the cumulative frequency graph values (min, Q1, median, Q3, max). Connect the two representations.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Plots cumulative frequency correctly at upper boundaries, reads median/quartiles accurately, calculates IQR.
  • Getting there: Plots correctly but reads values from wrong horizontal position or confuses Q1/Q3 positions.
  • Not yet: Plots at midpoints instead of upper boundaries. Reteach why upper boundary.

Lesson 4: Choosing and Comparing Diagrams

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Diagram Audit

Show 4 diagrams of the same data: bar chart, pie chart, histogram, frequency polygon. "Which is best? Why? What does each emphasise/hide?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Decision framework:
• Data type: categorical → bar chart or pie chart; continuous → histogram, frequency polygon, cumulative frequency
• Purpose: compare categories → bar chart; show proportions → pie chart; show distribution shape → histogram/frequency polygon; find median/quartiles → cumulative frequency
• Audience: non-specialist → simpler charts; specialist → histograms, cumulative frequency
• Number of data sets: one → any; two+ → dual bar chart, overlaid frequency polygons, overlaid cumulative frequency curves

Misleading diagrams to avoid: Non-zero baseline, 3D effects, distorted areas, truncated axes, inconsistent scales, missing labels.
Decision Practice

1. "Compare boys' and girls' favourite subjects" → dual bar chart (categorical, two groups).
2. "Show distribution of 200 reaction times" → histogram (continuous, shape).
3. "Find median and IQR of test scores" → cumulative frequency graph.
4. "Show market share of 5 phone brands" → pie chart (proportions of whole).
5. "Compare height distributions of Year 7 and Year 11" → overlaid frequency polygons.

Common Misconception

"Histograms and bar charts are the same thing."

Bar charts: categorical data, gaps between bars, height = frequency. Histograms: continuous grouped data, no gaps, area = frequency (height = frequency density if unequal widths).

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a flowchart: "Is data categorical? → Yes → Compare or proportion? → No → Continuous → Shape or summary?"
Stretch:
Given a dataset, produce 3 different charts. Write a report: what each shows, what each hides, which you'd publish and why.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam Question Practice

"A teacher wants to compare the distribution of test scores for two classes. Which diagram is most appropriate? Justify your choice." (Answer: frequency polygons on same axes — easy to compare shapes).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Selects appropriate diagram for context, justifies choice, identifies misleading features.
  • Getting there: Knows standard uses but struggles to justify for unfamiliar contexts.
  • Not yet: Chooses pie chart for everything. Reteach data types and purposes.

Homework Suggestions

  • Collect real data (e.g. car colours in a car park) and create bar chart, pie chart, and frequency table.
  • Given a frequency table with unequal class widths, draw the histogram and frequency polygon.
  • Draw a cumulative frequency graph from grouped data and find median, Q1, Q3, IQR.
  • Find a misleading chart in media/news. Annotate what makes it misleading and redraw correctly.
