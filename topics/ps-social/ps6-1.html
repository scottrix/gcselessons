PS6.1: Conformity and Obedience

Foundation Higher All Boards

Asch, Milgram, Zimbardo, factors affecting conformity/obedience, ethical issues

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR

Lesson 1: Key Concepts and Theories

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Concept Recall

Display key terms from Conformity and Obedience. Students write definitions from memory. Peer assess.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Cover the core theoretical content for Conformity and Obedience. Key concepts include:
  • Core theory/framework and its main propositions
  • Key terminology and definitions
  • Historical context and development
  • Relationship to other theories in the strand
Use concrete examples and real-world applications throughout.
Key Study

Introduce a classic or contemporary study relevant to Conformity and Obedience. Cover: aim, procedure, findings, conclusion. Students complete a study summary template.

Common Misconception

"This theory explains everything about the topic"

No single theory provides a complete explanation. Conformity and Obedience requires evaluation of multiple perspectives and consideration of interacting factors.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide a structured note-taking frame with key terms pre-filled. Focus on one key study rather than multiple. Use visual aids and concrete examples.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Compare and contrast this theory with an alternative perspective. Evaluate methodological strengths/weaknesses of key studies. Consider cultural bias and ethical issues.

Plenary (5 minutes)

One-Minute Paper

Students write: (1) The most important concept from today, (2) One question they still have.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain core concepts, describe key study, and outline evaluation points
  • Getting there: Can describe concepts but struggles with evaluation or study details
  • Not yet: Cannot recall key concepts or confuse theories

Lesson 2: Research Methods and Studies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Method Match

Match research methods to their descriptions (experiment, observation, interview, case study, correlation). Discuss strengths/weaknesses of each.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Focus on research methods specific to Conformity and Obedience:
  • Common research designs used in this area
  • Key studies: aim, procedure, findings, conclusion
  • Methodological evaluation: validity, reliability, ethics, generalisability
  • How research methods affect the conclusions drawn
Study Analysis

Detailed analysis of a second key study for Conformity and Obedience. Students complete evaluation grid: strengths, weaknesses, ethical issues, improvements.

Extended Evaluation Question

12-mark style question: "Evaluate the contribution of research into conformity and obedience to our understanding of human behaviour/society."

Guide students through: AO1 (knowledge), AO2 (application), AO3 (analysis/evaluation). Use PEEL structure: Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide evaluation sentence starters. Focus on 2-3 clear evaluation points. Use a writing frame for the extended question.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Require synoptic links to other topics. Consider researcher bias, funding influences, paradigm shifts. Write a full 12-mark response under timed conditions.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Evaluation Tennis

Pairs take turns stating a strength/weakness of a study. Cannot repeat. Last one standing wins.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe studies accurately, evaluate methodology, write structured evaluation
  • Getting there: Can describe studies but evaluation is superficial or missing
  • Not yet: Cannot recall study details or identify methodological issues

Lesson 3: Application and Debate

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Real-World Application

Present a contemporary issue/scenario related to Conformity and Obedience. Students discuss in pairs how the theories/studies apply.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Apply Conformity and Obedience to contemporary contexts:
  • Real-world applications and implications
  • Current debates and controversies
  • Policy implications (if applicable)
  • Cultural and historical variations
  • Synoptic links to other specification areas
Debate Activity

Class debate: "This house believes that [key controversy in Conformity and Obedience]." Assign roles. Use evidence from studies.

Extended Application Question

Scenario-based question: Apply theories and research from Conformity and Obedience to a novel situation. Assess AO2 (application) and AO3 (analysis).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide structured debate roles with prompt cards. Use simpler scenarios. Focus on one clear application.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Require evaluation of competing explanations for the scenario. Consider methodological limitations of applying lab findings to real world. Suggest future research directions.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exit Ticket

Students answer: "How has your understanding of Conformity and Obedience changed? What evidence would change your mind?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies theory to new contexts, engages with debates, uses evidence
  • Getting there: Applies theory with support, participates in debate
  • Not yet: Struggles to apply theory, cannot contribute to debate

Homework Suggestions

  • Create summary notes or flashcards for key concepts and studies
  • Complete practice exam questions on this topic
  • Research one key study or theorist in more depth
  • Prepare a mind map linking this topic to other areas of the specification
