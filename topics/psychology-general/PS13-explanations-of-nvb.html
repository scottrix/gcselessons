Lesson 2: Core Concepts: explanations of non-verbal behaviour

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on explanations of non-verbal behaviour. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Evolutionary explanations: NVC evolved because it helped survival. Darwin argued that facial expressions evolved from useful actions (e.g. baring teeth in anger originally prepared for biting). Universal expressions suggest an evolutionary origin shared across cultures.
Key Fact: Innate explanations: NVC is biologically programmed. Evidence: Ekman's universal facial expressions; blind individuals show similar facial expressions despite never seeing them (Matsumoto & Hwang); infants smile and cry without learning.
Key Fact: Cultural explanations: NVC is learned through socialisation. Evidence: display rules vary across cultures; specific gestures (emblems) have different meanings in different cultures; proxemics vary by culture (Latin American cultures use less space than Northern European).
Key Fact: Yuki's study (2007): compared how American and Japanese participants interpreted emotional expressions using eyes vs mouths. Japanese participants relied more on eyes (cultural emphasis on reading subtle eye cues in a collectivist culture), while Americans relied more on mouths. This demonstrates that NVC interpretation is culturally shaped.
Key Fact: Interactionist view: NVC has innate foundations (universal expressions) but is shaped by cultural learning (display rules, gestures, proxemics). The evolutionary basis provides a starting point; culture refines and modifies expression.
Psychology Exam Tips: When evaluating NVC explanations, always consider both nature and nurture. No single explanation fully accounts for the evidence - the interactionist position (innate basis + cultural modification) is most supported.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Compare evolutionary and cultural explanations of non-verbal behaviour.

Answer: Evolutionary: NVC evolved for survival (e.g. expressions signal intent, eye contact signals threat). Supported by universality and blind individuals showing expressions. Cultural: NVC is learned through socialisation. Supported by cultural differences in display rules, gestures, and proxemics. Both contribute - NVC has an innate basis modified by cultural learning.

Lesson 3: Application: explanations of non-verbal behaviour

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Psychology Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Compare evolutionary and cultural explanations of non-verbal behaviour.

Answer: Evolutionary: NVC evolved for survival (e.g. expressions signal intent, eye contact signals threat). Supported by universality and blind individuals showing expressions. Cultural: NVC is learned through socialisation. Supported by cultural differences in display rules, gestures, and proxemics. Both contribute - NVC has an innate basis modified by cultural learning.

Q2: Describe Yuki's study on cultural differences in interpreting facial expressions and evaluate its methodology.

Answer: Yuki (2007): American and Japanese participants judged emotions from eyes vs mouths. Americans relied more on mouths (expressive, open culture); Japanese relied more on eyes (subtle cues matter in collectivist culture). Evaluation: controlled methodology, but used static images rather than real interaction; samples were university students (limited generalisability); demonstrates cultural shaping of NVC interpretation.

Q3: Discuss whether non-verbal behaviour is primarily innate or learned.

Answer: Innate evidence: universal facial expressions (Ekman); blind individuals show same expressions; infants express emotions without learning. Learned evidence: display rules vary; gestures differ by culture; Yuki's study shows interpretation is cultural. Conclusion: NVC is primarily innate in its basic forms but extensively shaped by cultural learning. An interactionist position best explains the evidence.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate whether non-verbal behaviour is better explained by evolutionary or cultural factors. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: outline evolutionary explanations (universal expressions, survival value, blind individuals); outline cultural explanations (display rules, gestures, proxemics, Yuki's study); evaluate evolutionary (strong universality evidence but cannot explain cultural variation); evaluate cultural (explains variation but cannot explain universal expressions in blind individuals); conclude: NVC has innate evolutionary foundations that are shaped by cultural learning - neither explanation alone is sufficient. The interactionist position provides the most complete account.

Exam Tips: Evolutionary = NVC evolved for survival. Cultural = NVC is learned through socialisation. | Innate evidence: universal expressions + blind people + infants. Cultural evidence: display rules + gestures + proxemics. | Yuki: Japanese read EYES. Americans read MOUTHS. Culture shapes interpretation.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Facial expressions are entirely learned, which is why they differ across all cultures. Correct: While display rules and specific gestures differ across cultures, the six basic facial expressions (happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust) are recognised universally, including in remote tribes with minimal Western contact (Ekman's Fore tribe study). This strongly suggests a biological foundation. Culture modifies HOW and WHEN expressions are displayed, but the underlying expressions are innate.
