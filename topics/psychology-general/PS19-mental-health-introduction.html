Lesson 2: Core Concepts: mental health: an introduction

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on mental health: an introduction. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Mental health: a state of wellbeing where a person can cope with normal stresses, work productively, and contribute to their community (WHO definition). Mental health exists on a continuum - it is not simply 'healthy' or 'ill'.
Key Fact: Mental illness: a condition that affects thinking, feeling or behaviour, causing significant distress. The medical model views mental illness as a disease with biological causes (chemical imbalances, genetics, brain damage) treatable with medication.
Key Fact: The biopsychosocial model (Engel): mental illness results from biological factors (genes, neurotransmitters), psychological factors (cognitions, trauma, learned behaviour), and social factors (poverty, discrimination, social isolation). This is more comprehensive than the medical model alone.
Key Fact: Cultural issues in diagnosis: different cultures express and interpret symptoms differently. Culture-bound syndromes (e.g. ataque de nervios in Latin American cultures) are not recognised in Western diagnostic systems. Ethnocentric bias means Western diagnostic criteria may not apply globally.
Key Fact: Stigma and mental health: stigma (negative stereotypes) prevents people seeking help. The medical model can reduce blame (it's an illness, not a personal failing) but can also increase stigma (labelled as 'mentally ill'). Anti-stigma campaigns (e.g. Time to Change) aim to normalise mental health problems.
Psychology Exam Tips: When evaluating mental health models, consider: is the model reductionist? Does it lead to effective treatment? Does it account for individual and cultural differences? Always use a biopsychosocial approach for comprehensive evaluation.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Compare the medical model with the biopsychosocial model of mental illness.

Answer: Medical model: mental illness is a disease with biological causes (genetics, neurotransmitters, brain abnormalities). Treatment: medication (drugs). Biopsychosocial model: mental illness results from biological, psychological and social factors interacting. Treatment: combination of medication, therapy and social support. The biopsychosocial model is more comprehensive but harder to implement.

Lesson 3: Application: mental health: an introduction

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Psychology Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Compare the medical model with the biopsychosocial model of mental illness.

Answer: Medical model: mental illness is a disease with biological causes (genetics, neurotransmitters, brain abnormalities). Treatment: medication (drugs). Biopsychosocial model: mental illness results from biological, psychological and social factors interacting. Treatment: combination of medication, therapy and social support. The biopsychosocial model is more comprehensive but harder to implement.

Q2: Explain how cultural factors can affect the diagnosis of mental health problems.

Answer: Cultural factors: symptoms vary by culture (e.g. depression expressed as physical pain in some Asian cultures); culture-bound syndromes don't fit Western diagnoses; diagnostic criteria (DSM-5, ICD-11) are Western-developed and may not generalise; language barriers affect diagnosis; stigma varies across cultures. Result: people from minority cultures may be misdiagnosed or under-diagnosed.

Q3: Evaluate whether the medical model is the best approach to understanding mental illness.

Answer: Medical model strengths: scientific basis (brain chemistry evidence), effective drug treatments (SSRIs for depression), reduces blame (illness not personal failure). Limitations: reductionist (ignores social and psychological causes), over-reliance on medication (side effects, doesn't address root causes), deterministic (implies individuals cannot change). The biopsychosocial model is more comprehensive but the medical model provides effective short-term treatment.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: mental health: an introduction

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate whether the medical model or the biopsychosocial model provides a better understanding of mental health problems. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: define both models; present medical model strengths (scientific evidence, effective drug treatments, reduces blame) and limitations (reductionist, ignores social/psychological factors, over-relies on medication); present biopsychosocial strengths (comprehensive, considers multiple causes, combined treatment) and limitations (harder to implement, less precise, research is less controlled); conclude: the medical model is useful for acute treatment and biological understanding, but the biopsychosocial model provides a more complete explanation. For effective long-term treatment, an integrated approach using both models is best.

Exam Tips: Medical model = BIOLOGICAL causes only (drugs treat it). Biopsychosocial = BIO + PSYCHO + SOCIAL (combined treatment). | Culture affects diagnosis: different cultures express symptoms differently. Western diagnostic systems may not generalise. | Mental health is a CONTINUUM, not a simple healthy/ill divide.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Mental illness is simply a chemical imbalance in the brain, so medication alone is the best treatment. Correct: While neurotransmitter imbalances are associated with many mental health conditions, the relationship is correlational not necessarily causal. The diathesis-stress model suggests biological vulnerability interacts with psychological and social factors. Medication alone treats symptoms but doesn't address underlying causes (e.g. trauma, social isolation, unhelpful thinking patterns). Combined treatment (medication + therapy + social support) is generally more effective than medication alone.
