Lesson 2: Core Concepts: culture & communication

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on culture & communication. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Individualist cultures (e.g. USA, UK) prioritise personal goals, independence and individual rights. Collectivist cultures (e.g. Japan, China) prioritise group harmony, interdependence and social obligations. These values affect communication styles, conformity and self-concept.
Key Fact: High-context cultures (e.g. Japan, Arab nations) rely heavily on NVC, context and shared understanding - much is left unsaid. Low-context cultures (e.g. Germany, USA) rely on explicit, direct verbal communication - meaning is in the words.
Key Fact: Cultural differences in conformity: collectivist cultures show higher conformity rates (Smith & Bond's meta-analysis). This reflects different values - conformity is seen positively as maintaining group harmony in collectivist cultures, but negatively as weakness in individualist cultures.
Key Fact: Culture and self-concept: independent self-construal (individualist - self is separate from others) vs interdependent self-construal (collectivist - self is defined through relationships). Markus & Kitayama showed these different self-views affect cognition, emotion and motivation.
Key Fact: Ethnocentrism: judging other cultures by the standards of your own. Much early psychological research was conducted in Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic (WEIRD) societies and may not generalise to other cultures. Henrich et al. (2010) showed WEIRD participants are not representative of humanity.
Psychology Exam Tips: When evaluating cross-cultural research, always consider: is the comparison fair? Are measures culturally appropriate? Are researchers being ethnocentric? Have findings been replicated across diverse cultures?

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the difference between individualist and collectivist cultures and how this affects communication.

Answer: Individualist cultures value independence and direct communication; collectivist cultures value interdependence and indirect communication. In individualist cultures, speaking directly is valued; in collectivist cultures, preserving harmony and avoiding confrontation is prioritised. These differences affect workplace, social and family communication.

Lesson 3: Application: culture & communication

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Psychology Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain the difference between individualist and collectivist cultures and how this affects communication.

Answer: Individualist cultures value independence and direct communication; collectivist cultures value interdependence and indirect communication. In individualist cultures, speaking directly is valued; in collectivist cultures, preserving harmony and avoiding confrontation is prioritised. These differences affect workplace, social and family communication.

Q2: Describe how culture influences conformity, referring to Smith & Bond's meta-analysis.

Answer: Smith & Bond's meta-analysis found that conformity rates were higher in collectivist cultures (e.g. 37% in Japan vs 25% in the US for Asch-type tasks). This is because conformity serves different functions: in collectivist cultures it maintains group harmony (positive); in individualist cultures it is seen as weakness (negative). The meaning of conformity is culturally constructed.

Q3: Evaluate whether psychological research based on WEIRD samples can be generalised to all cultures.

Answer: WEIRD samples: 96% of psychology study participants are from Western countries (Arnett, 2008). Henrich et al. (2010) showed WEIRD participants are outliers on many psychological measures. This means findings may not generalise. However: some findings (e.g. universal facial expressions) DO replicate across cultures; biological processes are shared. Conclusion: WEIRD bias is a real problem but does not invalidate all findings - researchers must test across cultures.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: culture & communication

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the importance of considering culture in psychological research, discussing the limitations of WEIRD samples. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: define WEIRD and explain the problem (unrepresentative samples); describe Henrich et al.'s findings (WEIRD outliers on many measures); explain how culture affects key findings (conformity, self-concept, communication); evaluate the counter-argument (some findings are universal; biological processes are shared); conclude: cultural bias is a significant limitation in psychology, and researchers must test across cultures. However, the solution is not to dismiss Western research but to supplement it with cross-cultural replication. Culture matters but so do universal human processes.

Exam Tips: Individualist = ME first (independence, direct). Collectivist = US first (interdependence, indirect). | High-context = read between the lines. Low-context = say what you mean. | WEIRD = Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic. Most psych participants are WEIRD - this is a bias problem.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: People from collectivist cultures are always more conformist and less individualistic than people from individualist cultures. Correct: While collectivist cultures show higher conformity on average, there is huge variation within cultures. Urban, younger people in collectivist cultures may be more individualistic than rural, older people. Globalisation is also reducing cultural differences. The individualist-collectivist distinction is a useful generalisation but oversimplifies the complexity within each culture.
