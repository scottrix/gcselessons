Lesson 2: Core Concepts: vocabulary and word choice

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on vocabulary and word choice. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Point: Your vocabulary choices directly affect your AO6 mark (up to 16 marks). A wide, ambitious vocabulary demonstrates control and precision - but only when used appropriately.
Connotation Matters: "Cheap" and "inexpensive" have similar meanings but different connotations. "Cheap" suggests poor quality; "inexpensive" suggests good value. Choose the word with the right emotional shade.
Common Repeat Offenders: said, went, looked, got, nice, good, thing, something, very, really
Warning: Don't use words you don't fully understand. Misused ambitious vocabulary is worse than correctly used simple vocabulary.
Creative Writing Register: For narrative and descriptive writing, aim for accessible but varied vocabulary. Too formal feels stiff; too informal feels unpolished.
Examiner's Tip: Vocabulary is judged on range AND appropriateness. A wide vocabulary used incorrectly scores lower than a narrower vocabulary used effectively. Quality and precision matter more than quantity.

Lesson 3: Application: vocabulary and word choice

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Key Point, Connotation Matters, Common Repeat Offenders. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Replace "very happy" with a single, more precise word.

Answer: Ecstatic, overjoyed, elated, jubilant, delighted (any of these or similar).

Q2: What is the difference in connotation between "cheap" and "inexpensive"?

Answer: "Cheap" has negative connotations of poor quality. "Inexpensive" has neutral/positive connotations of good value. Same basic meaning, different emotional impact.

Q3: Rewrite "She walked slowly across the room" using a powerful verb instead of walked + slowly.

Answer: She crept/ambled/trudged/meandered/strolled across the room (any precise verb that shows slow movement).

Q4: Why should you avoid using words you don't fully understand?

Answer: Misused ambitious vocabulary is obvious to examiners and loses marks. It shows lack of control. Better to use simpler vocabulary correctly than ambitious vocabulary incorrectly.

Q5: Give three alternatives to "said" for angry dialogue.

Answer: Snapped, growled, hissed, retorted, barked, snarled (any three of these or similar).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: vocabulary and word choice

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on vocabulary and word choice from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Build vocabulary gradually - learn a few new words each week | Practice using new words in sentences before the exam | Read widely to absorb vocabulary naturally | Check you understand a word's meaning AND connotations | Don't force ambitious vocabulary - use it where it fits | Proofread for repeated words and find alternatives | Consider register - match formality to task and audience | Focus on verbs - they drive your writing
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often use ambitious vocabulary they don't fully understand. Wrong: "The melancholy pizza was delicious" — using 'melancholy' where it doesn't fit. Correct: Use ambitious vocabulary only where its meaning and connotation match the context — "A melancholy silence settled over the room." Students often rely on "very" and "really" to intensify words. Wrong: "She was very sad and really tired and very hungry." Correct: Replace weak intensifier + adjective with a single precise word — "She was devastated, exhausted, and ravenous." Students often use thesaurus words that sound unnatural together. Wrong: "The puissant entity perambulated the vicinage" — stuffed with thesaurus fin
