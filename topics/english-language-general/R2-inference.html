Lesson 2: Core Concepts: inference & deduction

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on inference & deduction. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: Inference means using evidence from the text AND your own reasoning to work out meanings that are NOT directly stated. You "read between the lines."
PEE Structure: P oint - State your inference E vidence - Quote from the text E xplain - Show how the evidence proves your point
Inference Exam Strategy: Always use the PEE structure - Point, Evidence, Explanation. Your point should state the inferred meaning clearly, your evidence must be a short quotation, and your explanation must show HOW the evidence leads to your inference. For AQA Paper 1 Q2 and Paper 2 Q3, spend 10-12 minutes. Examiners reward specific, well-supported inferences over vague guesses. Always ask yourself: "What does this word/phrase suggest, and how do I know?"

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 8 marks: Look in detail at lines 10-22. How does the writer use language here to suggest the character's state of mind? You should include your own inference about what the language suggests. <div class="

The writer suggests the character is deeply anxious and on edge. This is established through the description of her "fingers drumming an irregular rhythm on the tabletop," which implies restless nervous energy she cannot control. The adjective "irregular" is particularly revealing - it suggests her anxiety is unpredictable and overwhelming, preventing her from maintaining composure. Furthermore, the detail that she "jumped at the sound of the clock striking" reveals hyper-vigilance; her body is in a state of such heightened alert that even an expected sound triggers a startled reaction. This physical response implies she is anticipating something alarming, creating the impression of a character consumed by apprehension. Mark scheme: Level 4 (7-8 marks) requires perceptive inference with detailed explanation of effects, supported by precise quotations. The response must go beyond literal meaning and show how language choices create specific implied meanings.

Exam Tips: Always support inferences with quotations | Use PEE structure for clear, structured answers | Focus on words with strong connotations | Be specific - avoid vague emotional words | Consider the wider context, not just isolated words | Trust your instincts but verify with evidence
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make an inference but forget to support it with a quotation from the text. Wrong: "The character is angry." Correct: "The character is angry, shown by the way she 'slammed the door and stormed upstairs,' where 'stormed' suggests uncontrolled rage." Students often make inferences that are too vague or generic, missing the specific emotion or meaning. Wrong: "She feels bad." Correct: "She feels humiliated and resentful." Students often over-interpret, making claims the text cannot reasonably support. Wrong: "The dark room means the character has depression."
