Lesson 2: Core Concepts: evaluating texts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on evaluating texts. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: Evaluation means making judgements about a text and supporting your opinions with evidence. You assess how successfully the writer achieves their purpose.
Step 1: Decide your overall opinion - agree, disagree, or partially agree Step 2: Find evidence that supports your view Step 3: Analyse how the writer achieves the effect Step 4: Consider alternative viewpoints if appropriate Step 5: Reach a conclusion
Examiner's Tip: This is worth 20 marks - the most on the paper. Spend about 20-25 minutes on it. Make sure you give a clear opinion and back it up with detailed analysis of the text.
Evaluation Exam Strategy: This is AQA Paper 1 Q4 - the highest-mark question at 20 marks. Spend 20-25 minutes on it. Start by deciding your position: agree, disagree, or partially agree. Then build your argument with 3-4 PEEL paragraphs, each one analysing a different method the writer uses. Include a counter-argument or concession to show balanced thinking. End with a clear conclusion. Examiners reward confident, well-supported judgements - don't sit on the fence, but do show you've considered the text fully.

Lesson 3: Application: evaluating texts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Step 1, Examiner&#x27;s Tip, Evaluation Exam Strategy. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What does "evaluate" mean in the context of English Language?

Answer: To make a judgement about how effective or successful a text is, supported by evidence and analysis.

Q2: How should you structure an evaluation response?

Answer: Start with your opinion, use PEEL paragraphs with evidence and analysis, consider alternative views, and reach a conclusion.

Q3: Why is it important to use evidence in evaluation?

Answer: Evidence proves your opinion is based on the text rather than just personal preference - it shows you can support your ideas.

Q4: What phrases can you use to show partial agreement?

Answer: "To some extent...", "While I agree that..., I also think...", "However, this could also be seen as..."

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: evaluating texts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on evaluating texts from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Start by deciding your overall opinion | Make your judgement clear from the start | Use "I agree/disagree" language confidently | Support every point with specific evidence | Analyse language AND structure where relevant | Consider both sides for a balanced argument | This is a high-mark question - manage your time well
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often retell the story instead of evaluating it. Wrong: "First the character goes to the house, then she looks through the window, then she goes inside." Correct: "The writer effectively builds tension by delaying the character's entry to the house, making the reader share her apprehension." Students often give an opinion without analysing the writer's methods. Wrong: "I agree the writer creates fear because it is very scary." Correct: "I agree the writer creates fear. The use of short, fragmented sentences such as 'Footsteps. Closer. Now.' accelerates the pace, replicating the character's racing heartbeat."
