Lesson 2: Core Concepts: grammar and syntax

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on grammar and syntax. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

AO6 Requirement: Use a range of vocabulary and sentence structures for clarity, purpose and effect. Good grammar ensures your writing is clear, professional and achieves its purpose.
Watch out for: Words between subject and verb can confuse agreement. "The box of chocolates is on the table" - the subject is 'box' (singular), not 'chocolates'.
AO6 Tip: Vary your sentence structures for effect. Use simple sentences for impact, complex for detail, compound for flow.
Exam Tip: Using subject terminology accurately shows sophistication in language analysis. Know the difference between nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs at minimum.
Grammar Exam Strategy: Grammatical accuracy is essential for AO6 (16 marks). Even a few persistent errors can cap your mark at Level 2. Most important rule: Check subject-verb agreement and tense consistency. These are the errors examiners notice most. Read each sentence and ask: "Does the verb match the subject? Is the tense consistent?" During writing: Choose your tense before you start (past or present for narrative) and stick to it. Be especially careful with dialogue — characters may speak in present tense while narration is in past. Proofreading (last 5 minutes): Read each sentence individually, checking for: subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, correct pronoun use (I vs me), and absence of "should of" / "could of" errors. Mark allocation: AO6 Level 4 (13-16 marks) requires grammatically accurate writing. Level 2 (5-8 marks) describes "frequent errors." Just 3-4 grammar mistakes can drop you an entire level. What examiners look for: Consistent grammatical accuracy, especially with agreement and tense. They also look for sophisticated grammatical choices — varied clause structures and purposeful use of active and passive voice.

Lesson 3: Application: grammar and syntax

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: AO6 Requirement, Watch out for, AO6 Tip. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Identify the error: "The group of students are studying hard."

Answer: "are" should be "is" - the subject is "group" (singular), not "students"

Q2: Rewrite using active voice: "The window was broken by the ball."

Answer: The ball broke the window.

Q3: What type of sentence is this? "When the rain stopped, the children ran outside."

Answer: Complex sentence - main clause ("the children ran outside") + subordinate clause ("when the rain stopped")

Q4: Correct the tense inconsistency: "She walks into the room and saw the mess."

Answer: She walked into the room and saw the mess. (or: She walks into the room and sees the mess.)

Q5: Complete: "Neither the teacher nor the students ___ (is/are) responsible."

Answer: are - with "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearest subject ("students")

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on grammar and syntax from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Read through your work to check for tense consistency | Use a mix of simple, compound and complex sentences | Active voice is usually clearer and more engaging | Check subject-verb agreement when there are words between them | Know your grammar terminology for language analysis | Proofread for common errors like "could of" and "should of"
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often shift tense without realising, especially in narrative writing. Wrong: "She walked into the room and sees the letter on the table. She picks it up and read it slowly." Correct: Maintain consistent tense — "She walked into the room and saw the letter on the table. She picked it up and read it slowly." Choose past or present tense before you start and stick to it. Students often use "of" instead of "have" after modal verbs. Wrong: "I should of revised more. They must of known about the test."
