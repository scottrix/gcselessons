Lesson 2: Core Concepts: structure analysis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on structure analysis. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: Structure analysis examines how a text is organised - the order of ideas, paragraphing, sentence variety, and narrative techniques used to shape meaning.
Openings: How does the text begin? What effect does the opening have? - In media res (starting in the middle of the action) - Setting the scene - Creating mystery or intrigue
Endings: How does the text conclude? What is the final impression? - Resolution or closure - Cliffhanger or ambiguity - Reflective circular ending
Narrative Perspective: Who is telling the story? - First person (I, we) - personal, subjective - Third person (he, she, they) - more detached - Omniscient narrator - knows everything
Beginning: How does the text start? What does the writer focus on? Middle: How does it develop? What shifts occur? End: How does it conclude? What's the final effect?
Examiner's Tip: This question often causes confusion with language analysis. Focus on WHERE things are placed and WHY - not what words mean. Think about the "journey" through the text.
ItemDetail
Analysing language instead of structureFocus on organisation, not word choice
Listing structural features without effectAlways explain the effect of structural choices
Ignoring the whole text structureConsider beginning, middle and end
Forgetting sentence-level analysisComment on sentence variety and length
Not using structural terminologyUse terms like "focus", "shift", "perspective"

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: What is the difference between language and structure analysis?

Answer: Language analysis looks at word choice and literary devices; structure analysis looks at how the text is organised and sequenced.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Lesson 3: Application: structure analysis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Openings, Endings, Narrative Perspective. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What is the difference between language and structure analysis?

Answer: Language analysis looks at word choice and literary devices; structure analysis looks at how the text is organised and sequenced.

Q2: Why might a writer use a short paragraph?

Answer: To create impact, drama, tension, or to draw attention to a key moment or idea.

Q3: What does "in media res" mean?

Answer: Starting in the middle of the action - a narrative technique that creates immediate engagement.

Q4: How can sentence length affect the pacing of a text?

Answer: Short sentences speed up pacing and create tension; long sentences slow pacing and allow for detailed description.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: structure analysis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on structure analysis from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Trace the reader's journey through the text | Comment on beginnings, middles and endings | Look for shifts in focus, time, or perspective | Use structural terminology: focus, shift, perspective, sequence | Consider paragraph and sentence length | Don't analyse language - that's a different question!
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often analyse language when the question asks for structure. Wrong: "The word 'dark' creates a spooky atmosphere." Correct: "The writer places the description of the dark room at the opening to immediately establish a claustrophobic focus, before shifting to the wider landscape." Students often list structural features without explaining their effect. Wrong: "The writer uses short sentences and long paragraphs." Correct: "The single short sentence 'She stopped.' interrupts the flowing description, creating a sudden halt that mirrors the character's realisation." Students often ignore the overall shape of the text and focus on isolated moments. Wrong: Making separate point
