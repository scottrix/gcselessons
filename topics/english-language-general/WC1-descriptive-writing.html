Lesson 2: Core Concepts: descriptive writing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on descriptive writing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: Descriptive writing creates a vivid picture in the reader's mind using sensory details, figurative language, and careful word choices. It focuses on capturing a moment, place, or person rather than telling a story.
Golden Rule: Don't just describe what you see. Include sounds, smells, textures, and even tastes to bring your writing to life.
Core Principle: Instead of telling the reader what to feel or think, show them through specific details that allow them to experience it themselves.
Structure Options: Wide to narrow: Establish setting, then zoom to specific details Narrow to wide: Start with detail, then pull back to reveal context Pan across: Move through space like a camera panning
Balance is Key: Mix macro and micro description. Macro establishes context; micro creates intimacy and authenticity.
Examiner's Tip: The best descriptive writing makes the examiner FEEL something. Focus on creating atmosphere rather than describing everything you can think of.
TermMeaningExample
It was scary.My heart hammered against my ribs. Sweat prickled on my palms.
She was nervous.She twisted the ring on her finger, her gaze flicking constantly to the door.
The room was messy.Clothes spilled from the wardrobe like a fabric avalanche. Books lay open, spines cracked, pages scattered.
He was angry.His jaw tightened. A vein pulsed at his temple. His knuckles whitened around the pen.
The weather was bad.Rain lashed the windows. Thunder growled in the distance. The sky bruised purple with approaching storm.

Lesson 3: Application: descriptive writing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Golden Rule, Core Principle, Structure Options. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What is the difference between descriptive writing and narrative writing?

Answer: Descriptive writing paints a picture of a moment or place, focusing on sensory details and atmosphere. Narrative writing tells a story with a plot, characters, and events unfolding over time.

Q2: Name three techniques for creating "show, don't tell" writing.

Answer: Use sensory details, describe physical actions and reactions, include specific observations, use figurative language, focus on concrete details rather than abstract labels.

Q3: Why is it important to include senses beyond just sight?

Answer: Sound, smell, touch, and taste create a more immersive experience. They trigger stronger emotional responses and memories in readers, making the description more vivid and memorable.

Q4: What is the "zoom technique" and how does it help structure description?

Answer: The zoom technique moves from wide view (macro) to close-up detail (micro), or vice versa. It gives structure and logical flow to description, helping readers visualise the scene progressively.

Q5: Rewrite this telling sentence to show instead: "The old house was creepy."

Answer: Example: "Windows stared like hollow eyes from the crumbling facade. Ivy clawed up the rotting walls. Inside, floorboards groaned beneath invisible feet, and the air tasted of mould and forgotten memories."

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: descriptive writing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on descriptive writing from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Read the image or prompt carefully - use it as inspiration, not just description | Plan your structure: viewpoint, spatial movement, key moments | Use a clear viewpoint - either first person "I" or third person observer | Open with impact - grab attention from the first line | Balance sensory details - don't just describe visuals | Vary sentence length for rhythm and effect | End strongly - a final image or impression that lingers | Leave time to check spelling and punctuation
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often write a story instead of a description. Wrong: Telling events in order — "I walked into the room, then I saw a ghost, then I ran away." Correct: Capturing a single moment in vivid detail — "The room held its breath. Dust motes hung suspended in the thin light. In the corner, something shifted." Students often list adjectives to describe things. Wrong: "The old, dark, scary, creepy, abandoned house stood on the hill." Correct: Weave description through action and senses — "The house clung to the hillside, its windows staring hollow, the smell of rot seeping from its cracked walls."
