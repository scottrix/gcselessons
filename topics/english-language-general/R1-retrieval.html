Lesson 2: Core Concepts: retrieval & literal comprehension

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on retrieval & literal comprehension. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: Retrieval means finding information that is directly stated in the text. You don't need to guess or interpret - the answer is right there in black and white.
Top Tips: Write in your own words where possible (but keep close to the text) You can use bullet points or numbered lists Each point should be a complete idea Don't repeat the same point in different words
Examiner's Tip: This is one of the easiest questions on the paper. Don't overthink it - just find four things and write them down. Aim to complete this in about 5 minutes.
Retrieval Exam Strategy: This is the easiest question on the paper - don't overcomplicate it. For AQA Paper 1 Q1 (4 marks), spend no more than 5 minutes. Read the question carefully, note any line references, scan for keywords, then write four clear points. For AQA Paper 2 Q1 (true/false), go back to the text and verify each option before choosing. Examiners mark this question generously - they want to give you full marks. Just find the information and state it clearly.
TermMeaningExample
Using inference when retrieval is asked forYou're guessing instead of finding what's statedFind the exact information in the text
Quoting too muchYou lose marks for copying whole sectionsSelect only the relevant words
Not answering all parts of the questionYou might miss easy marksCheck you've listed the right number of points
Repeating the same pointYou won't get marks for saying the same thing twiceMake each point different
Analysing language when asked to retrieveThat's a different question typeJust find and state the facts

Lesson 3: Application: retrieval & literal comprehension

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: retrieval & literal comprehension

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 4 marks: Look in detail at lines 1-8. List four things you learn about the setting from this part of the source. <div class="

The setting is a remote coastal village, described as "perched on the edge of the cliff" which suggests it is dangerously close to the sea below. The houses are old and weather-worn, with "whitewashed walls peeling in the salt wind," indicating they have long been exposed to harsh coastal conditions. There is a single narrow road leading into the village, implying it is isolated and difficult to reach. The harbour below contains only "a handful of fishing boats," revealing that fishing is a key but small-scale industry in the community. Mark scheme: 1 mark for each valid point identified from the specified lines. Points must be distinct and accurately reflect information stated in the text. Own words or close paraphrase accepted.

Exam Tips: Read the question carefully and check line references | Don't analyse language - just find facts | Use bullet points for clarity | Check you have the right number of points | Keep answers concise - don't waffle | This is usually the easiest question - get it done quickly!
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often try to infer when the question asks for retrieval, reading between the lines instead of finding what is directly stated. Wrong: "The character probably feels lonely because she lives alone." Correct: "The character lives alone in a 'small flat on the outskirts of town.'" Students often copy huge chunks of text instead of selecting the key detail. Wrong: Quoting an entire paragraph to make one point. Correct: Extracting the specific detail and rephrasing in your own words. Students often make the same point twice using different wording. Wrong: "It was raining heavily" and "There was a downpour." Correct: Making each point about a different detail from the text.
