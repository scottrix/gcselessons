Lesson 2: Core Concepts: reviews

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on reviews. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: A review evaluates a product, service, or creative work, providing a judgment supported by evidence to help readers make informed decisions.
Include: Title and type of work/product Key details (author, director, release date) A brief overview of what it's about An engaging hook to interest readers
Key Elements: Discuss specific aspects (acting, directing, plot, etc.) Use examples to illustrate your points Balance positive and negative aspects Use new paragraphs for different aspects
Include: Clear overall judgment Who would enjoy this Star rating or recommendation Final memorable comment
Useful Phrases: "While the pacing occasionally drags..." "On the positive side..." "However, this is balanced by..." "For all its flaws..."
Top Tip: Be honest but fair. A review that's overly negative or gushing without substance won't be taken seriously. Balance your judgment with specific evidence.
TermMeaningExample
Descriptive languagePaint a picture for readers"Visually stunning, with sweeping cinematography"
Clear judgmentsDon't be vague - be direct"This is exceptional" not "It's quite good"
Specific examplesBack up every claim"The restaurant's signature dish..."
Appropriate toneMatch the publication styleFormal for broadsheets, casual for blogs
Engaging voiceMake it enjoyable to readUse wit, personality, and flair
Just summarising the plotA review needs evaluation, not just descriptionAlways add your judgment and evidence
Being too vague"It was good" means nothingUse specific examples to support opinions
Major spoilersRuins the experience for readersWarn readers or avoid key plot reveals

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: What are the four key elements of an effective review?

Answer: Judgment (your opinion), Evidence (specific examples), Balanced view (pros and cons), Recommendation (who should see/read/use it).

Lesson 3: Application: reviews

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Include, Key Elements, Include. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What are the four key elements of an effective review?

Answer: Judgment (your opinion), Evidence (specific examples), Balanced view (pros and cons), Recommendation (who should see/read/use it).

Q2: Why should you avoid major spoilers in a film or book review?

Answer: Spoilers ruin the experience for readers who haven't seen the film or read the book. You want to inform their decision, not reveal the ending.

Q3: Write an introduction for a review of a restaurant you've visited.

Answer: Your introduction should include: the restaurant name, location/cuisine type, and an engaging hook that hints at your overall judgment.

Q4: What's the difference between a summary and a review?

Answer: A summary describes what happens. A review evaluates how good it is, with judgment, evidence, and recommendations.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: reviews

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on reviews from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Start with a hook that engages your reader | State your judgment clearly and early | Support every opinion with specific examples | Balance positive and negative points | Avoid major spoilers in film and book reviews | End with a clear recommendation | Consider a star rating or similar summary
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often just summarise the plot instead of evaluating it. Wrong: "The film is about a boy called Harry who goes to a school for wizards and has adventures." Correct: Evaluate, don't just summarise — "While the premise of a young wizard discovering his powers is familiar, the execution is anything but. The school setting is brought to life with such rich, atmospheric detail that even the most fantastical elements feel grounded and believable." Students often make vague judgments without evidence.
