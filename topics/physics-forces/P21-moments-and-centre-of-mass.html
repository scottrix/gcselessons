P21: Moments and Centre of Mass

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understanding moments (turning effects of forces), the principle of moments, and the concept of centre of mass.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define a moment and use the equation moment = force × perpendicular distance
  • State and apply the principle of moments
  • Explain the concept of centre of mass
  • Describe how to find the centre of mass of a lamina
  • Apply moments to problems involving levers and balanced objects

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of forces and Newton's Laws (P17)
  • Ability to rearrange equations

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Ruler
  • Ruler balanced on a pencil (lever demonstration)
  • Card, scissors, and thread (for centre of mass practical)

Lesson 1: Moments

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Door Opening

Ask students to open a door near the hinge, then near the handle. "Which is easier? Why?" Discuss how the position of the force affects how easy it is to turn the door.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Moment is the turning effect of a force about a pivot.
Moment = Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot
- Measured in newton-metres (N·m)
- The further from the pivot, the greater the turning effect
- The larger the force, the greater the turning effect

Principle of Moments:
For an object in rotational equilibrium (not turning):
Sum of clockwise moments = Sum of anticlockwise moments

Levers:
- A lever amplifies a small force to produce a larger turning effect
- Effort × Effort arm = Load × Load arm
- Examples: see-saw, crowbar, wheelbarrow, scissors, bottle opener

Centre of mass:
The centre of mass is the point through which the entire weight of an object can be considered to act. For a uniform symmetrical object, the centre of mass is at its geometric centre.

Stability:
- An object is more stable if its centre of mass is low and its base is wide
- Racing cars have low centres of gravity for stability at high speeds
- An object topples when its centre of mass is no longer above its base
Worked Example

A uniform beam of length 4 m and weight 200 N is supported at its centre. A 300 N weight hangs 1 m from the left end. Where should a 100 N weight be placed to balance the beam?
Pivot at centre (2 m from each end).
Anticlockwise moment: 300 × (2 − 1) = 300 N·m
Clockwise moment needed: 300 N·m
Distance for 100 N weight: 300 ÷ 100 = 3 m from the pivot → 1 m from the right end

Key Equations:
Moment = Force × perpendicular distance (M = Fd)
Principle of Moments: Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments
Common Misconception: Students think the total weight of the beam acts at the pivot, not at the centre of mass. For a uniform beam, the weight acts at its centre (midpoint), regardless of where the pivot is.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a see-saw diagram with forces labelled. Students identify clockwise and anticlockwise moments and write the equation for equilibrium.
Stretch:
A uniform beam of length 6 m and weight 300 N is pivoted 2 m from one end. A 500 N weight hangs at the far end from the pivot. Calculate the force needed at the other end to balance the beam.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Calculation

A force of 50 N is applied 0.3 m from a pivot. What is the moment? (Answer: 15 N·m)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can calculate moments and apply the principle of moments to solve equilibrium problems
  • Getting there: Can calculate moments but struggles with multi-force equilibrium problems
  • Not yet: Cannot calculate moments or understand the principle of moments

Lesson 2: Centre of Mass and Stability

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Balance Challenge

Balance a ruler on your finger. Where is the balance point? "Is it always in the middle? What about if the ruler is not uniform?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Finding the centre of mass of a lamina (2D shape):
1. Cut out the shape from card
2. Hang it from a pin so it can swing freely
3. Hang a plumb line (weight on string) from the same pin
4. Draw the vertical line along the plumb line
5. Repeat from a different point on the edge
6. The centre of mass is where the lines cross

Centre of mass and stability:
- An object is stable when its centre of mass is above its base of support
- The lower the centre of mass, the more stable the object
- The wider the base, the more stable the object
- An object topples when its centre of mass moves outside its base

Examples:
- Racing cars: low centre of gravity (engine low in the chassis)
- Rugby players: lower centre of mass by bending knees when tackled
- Double-decker buses: designed with low centre of gravity to prevent tipping
- Leaning tower of Pisa: stable because its centre of mass is still above its base
Worked Example

A uniform rod AB of length 6 m and weight 120 N is pivoted at point C, 2 m from end A. A weight W hangs from end B. The rod is in equilibrium.
Anticlockwise moment about C: 120 × 1 (centre of mass is 1 m to the left of C) = 120 N·m
Clockwise moment about C: W × 4 (end B is 4 m to the right of C)
120 = 4W → W = 30 N

Key Principle:
An object topples when its centre of mass moves outside its base of support.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a cut-out shape and a pin. Students find the centre of mass practically by hanging from two points and drawing lines.
Stretch:
Explain why a rucksack is more comfortable when the load is close to the back and high up, using ideas about centre of mass and stability.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

"Explain why a footballer bends their knees when being tackled, using ideas about centre of mass and stability." Students write a 3-mark answer.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can find the centre of mass practically, explain stability, and solve moments problems involving centre of mass
  • Getting there: Can explain stability but cannot find centre of mass practically
  • Not yet: Cannot link centre of mass to stability or solve moments problems

Homework Suggestions

  • Find the centre of mass of a cardboard cut-out of your hand by the plumb line method
  • Calculate the moments about a pivot for a see-saw with three people of different weights at different distances
  • Research why Formula 1 cars have a minimum weight requirement and low centre of gravity
  • Explain how a mobile (hanging sculpture) achieves balance, using the principle of moments
