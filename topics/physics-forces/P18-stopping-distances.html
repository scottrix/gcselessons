P18: Stopping Distances

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understanding the factors that affect stopping distances, including thinking distance and braking distance, and their implications for road safety.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define stopping distance, thinking distance, and braking distance
  • Explain factors that affect thinking distance and braking distance
  • Calculate stopping distances using kinetic energy and work done
  • Evaluate the effects of drugs, alcohol, and speed on stopping distance

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of Newton's Laws (P17)
  • Knowledge of kinetic energy (P1)
  • Understanding of force and acceleration (P16)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Ruler and graph paper

Lesson 1: Thinking and Braking Distance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Reaction Test

Hold a ruler above a student's open hand and drop it without warning. Measure how far it falls before they catch it. Calculate reaction time: t = √(2s/g). Typical value: 0.2-0.3 s.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Stopping distance = Thinking distance + Braking distance

Thinking distance: the distance the vehicle travels during the driver's reaction time. - Typical reaction time: 0.2–0.7 seconds - Affected by: tiredness, drugs/alcohol, distractions (phone), age, speed - Thinking distance = speed × reaction time

Braking distance: the distance the vehicle travels while the brakes are being applied. - Affected by: speed (proportional to v²), brake condition, road surface, tyre condition, vehicle mass - Friction between tyres and road provides the braking force

Stopping distances (approximate): - 30 mph: 23 m (thinking: 9 m + braking: 14 m) - 50 mph: 53 m (thinking: 15 m + braking: 38 m) - 70 mph: 96 m (thinking: 21 m + braking: 75 m)
Worked Example

A car travels at 20 m/s. The driver's reaction time is 0.5 s and brakes provide deceleration of 8 m/s².
(a) Thinking distance = 20 × 0.5 = 10 m
(b) Braking distance = v² ÷ 2a = 400 ÷ 16 = 25 m
(c) Stopping distance = 10 + 25 = 35 m

Key Equations:
Thinking distance = speed × reaction time
Braking distance = v² ÷ 2a
Stopping distance = Thinking distance + Braking distance
Common Misconception: Students think doubling speed doubles stopping distance. It more than doubles it — thinking distance doubles but braking distance quadruples (KE ∝ v²).

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a table with pre-calculated thinking distances. Students calculate braking and total stopping distances.
Stretch:
Calculate stopping distance for a lorry (10 000 kg, 25 m/s, deceleration 5 m/s², reaction time 0.6 s) and compare with a car (1000 kg) at the same speed.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Speed Effect

Calculate stopping distances for 20, 30, 40, and 50 m/s. Plot a graph. Describe the shape.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can calculate stopping distances and explain all factors affecting them
  • Getting there: Can calculate but cannot explain all factors
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish between thinking and braking distance

Lesson 2: Road Safety and Energy

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Speed Comparison

At 30 mph, stopping distance is 23 m. At 60 mph, it is 93 m. "Why does doubling speed more than quadruple stopping distance?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Energy approach:
KE = ½mv² must be reduced to zero by work done by brakes.
½mv² = F × d, so d = ½mv² ÷ F
This shows braking distance is proportional to v².

Effects on stopping distance:
- Alcohol/drugs: increase reaction time (thinking distance increases). Cannabis: reaction time increases ~20%.
- Speed: doubles thinking distance, quadruples braking distance.
- Wet road: braking distance roughly doubles. Icy road: increases ~10×.
- Worn tyres/brakes: reduced grip and braking force → longer braking distance.
Worked Example

A 1200 kg car at 25 m/s brakes with 6000 N force.
(a) KE = ½ × 1200 × 25² = 375 000 J
(b) Braking distance = 375 000 ÷ 6000 = 62.5 m
(c) Reaction time 0.4 s: thinking distance = 10 m
(d) Total = 72.5 m

d = ½mv² ÷ F (braking distance from energy)

Differentiation

Support:
Provide scenarios where students identify which factor affects thinking or braking distance, and whether it increases or decreases.
Stretch:
Compare the braking distances of a car and a lorry (same speed, different mass) when the braking force is proportional to weight.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

"Explain why drink-driving is dangerous, using ideas about reaction time, thinking distance, and braking distance." Students write a 3-mark answer.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can use the energy approach and evaluate all factors affecting stopping distance
  • Getting there: Can explain factors but cannot perform energy calculations
  • Not yet: Cannot link energy, force, and stopping distance

Homework Suggestions

  • Calculate stopping distances for 10, 20, 30, 40 m/s and plot stopping distance against speed squared
  • Research the effect of mobile phone use on reaction time and write a road safety report
  • Calculate the kinetic energy of a 1500 kg car at 70 mph and determine the braking force needed to stop in 50 m
  • Explain why speed limits are lower in wet conditions, using physics terminology
