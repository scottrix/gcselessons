P16: Motion and Acceleration

Foundation Higher All Boards

Using the equations of motion (SUVAT) to solve problems involving constant acceleration, including free fall under gravity.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • State the four equations of motion for constant acceleration
  • Select the correct equation for a given problem
  • Solve problems using SUVAT equations
  • Describe motion under gravity (free fall)
  • Interpret and draw velocity-time graphs for accelerated motion

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of scalar and vector quantities (P15)
  • Knowledge of speed, velocity, and acceleration
  • Ability to rearrange equations

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Ruler
  • Stopwatch
  • Toy car and ramp (for acceleration investigations)

Lesson 1: The Equations of Motion

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

What Do You Know?

A car accelerates from rest to 30 m/s in 10 seconds. Ask: "Can you calculate the acceleration? What other quantities could you find?" Students attempt and share their methods.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
For objects moving with constant (uniform) acceleration, we use four equations. The variables are:
- u = initial velocity (m/s)
- v = final velocity (m/s)
- a = acceleration (m/s²)
- t = time (s)
- s = displacement (m)

The four SUVAT equations:
1. v = u + at
2. s = ½(u + v)t
3. s = ut + ½at²
4. v² = u² + 2as

How to choose the right equation:
Look at what you know and what you need to find. Choose the equation that contains those quantities (and no unknowns).

Key points:
- These equations only work for constant acceleration
- Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 9.8 m/s² (≈ 10 m/s² for simplicity)
- When falling freely: a = g = 9.8 m/s² (downward)
- When thrown upward: initial velocity is positive, a = −9.8 m/s² (gravity opposes motion)
- At the highest point: v = 0 (momentarily stationary)
Worked Example

A ball is thrown upward with initial velocity 20 m/s. Calculate: (a) the time to reach the highest point, (b) the maximum height, (c) the total time to return to the hand.

At highest point: v = 0, u = 20, a = −9.8
(a) v = u + at → 0 = 20 + (−9.8)t → t = 20/9.8 = 2.04 s
(b) v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 400 + 2(−9.8)s → s = 400/19.6 = 20.4 m
(c) Total time = 2 × 2.04 = 4.08 s (symmetry of free fall)

The Four SUVAT Equations:
v = u + at
s = ½(u + v)t
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

g = 9.8 m/s² (acceleration due to gravity)
Common Misconception: Students think a ball thrown upward has zero velocity during its flight. It only has zero velocity at the highest point. It has upward velocity on the way up and downward velocity on the way down.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a flowchart showing which equation to use for each scenario. Students match equations to problems.
Stretch:
A stone is dropped from a cliff 80 m high. Calculate: (a) the time to hit the sea, (b) the velocity on impact. (Ignore air resistance.)

Plenary (5 minutes)

Equation Selection

Students identify which SUVAT equation they would use for five different scenarios (without solving).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can select and apply the correct SUVAT equation for any constant acceleration problem
  • Getting there: Can use v = u + at and s = ut + ½at² but struggles with v² = u² + 2as
  • Not yet: Cannot select the correct equation or make errors with signs

Lesson 2: Free Fall and Gravity

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Drop Test

Drop two objects of different masses (e.g., a pen and a piece of paper) from the same height. Ask: "Which hits the ground first? Why?" If the paper is crumpled into a ball, both fall at the same rate. Discuss.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Free fall is motion under the influence of gravity alone (no air resistance).

Key facts about gravity:
- All objects in free fall accelerate at the same rate regardless of mass (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- Galileo demonstrated this by dropping objects from the Tower of Pisa
- On Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 m/s²
- On the Moon, g ≈ 1.6 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth's)
- g decreases with altitude (weaker gravitational field further from Earth)

Terminal velocity:
- In reality, air resistance increases as an object falls faster
- At terminal velocity, air resistance = weight, so net force = 0
- The object continues at constant velocity (no further acceleration)
- Terminal velocity depends on shape, size, and mass

Examples of terminal velocity:
- Skydiver: ~55 m/s (about 200 km/h) belly-down
- Bowling ball: ~30 m/s
- Feather: ~1 m/s (very low terminal velocity due to low mass and high air resistance)

Velocity-time graph for free fall with air resistance:
- Initially steep gradient (high acceleration)
- Gradient decreases (acceleration decreases as air resistance increases)
- Eventually horizontal (terminal velocity reached, zero acceleration)
Worked Example

A skydiver jumps from a plane at 2000 m. She reaches terminal velocity of 55 m/s after 15 seconds.
(a) Distance fallen in first 15 s (assuming constant acceleration — approximate):
s = ½ × 55 × 15 = 412.5 m
(b) Height above ground after 15 s: 2000 − 412.5 = 1587.5 m
(c) Time to fall remaining 1587.5 m at 55 m/s: t = 1587.5 ÷ 55 = 28.9 s
(d) Total time: 15 + 28.9 ≈ 44 s

Key Equation:
s = ½(u + v)t (for average velocity during acceleration)

Terminal velocity: when air resistance = weight, acceleration = 0

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a velocity-time graph for a skydiver with sections already labelled. Students explain what is happening at each section.
Stretch:
Calculate the time for a ball to fall from a 45 m building. Then calculate how long it takes to fall the last 5 m. Compare with the first 5 m. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

"A ball is dropped from the top of a building. It takes 3.0 s to reach the ground. Calculate the height of the building and the velocity just before impact." (g = 10 m/s²)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can solve free fall problems and explain terminal velocity using force arguments
  • Getting there: Can solve simple free fall problems but cannot explain terminal velocity
  • Not yet: Cannot apply SUVAT equations to free fall or understand the concept of g

Lesson 3: Investigating Acceleration

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Predict the Gradient

Show a velocity-time graph with a straight line through the origin. Ask: "What does the gradient of this graph represent? What would a steeper line mean?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Required Practical: Investigate the acceleration of a toy car down a ramp.

Method:
1. Set up a ramp at a small angle
2. Place a toy car at the top of the ramp
3. Release the car and measure the time to travel between two points
4. Repeat with different ramp angles or different starting positions
5. Measure distance between the two points

Calculating acceleration from the experiment:
- Measure time t for the car to travel distance s from rest (u = 0)
- Use s = ½at² to calculate acceleration: a = 2s ÷ t²

Analysing results:
- Plot a graph of s against t² for different distances
- The gradient = ½a (since s = ½at² when u = 0)
- Calculate acceleration from the gradient

Reducing errors:
- Use a light gate and data logger for accurate timing
- Repeat each measurement 3 times and calculate a mean
- Ensure the car starts from rest each time
- Use a consistent release method (not pushing the car)
Worked Example

A toy car rolls down a ramp from rest. At 0.5 m from the start, the time is 1.0 s. At 2.0 m from the start, the time is 2.0 s.
(a) a₁ = 2s/t² = 2 × 0.5/1² = 1.0 m/s²
(b) a₂ = 2s/t² = 2 × 2.0/2² = 1.0 m/s²
Acceleration is the same in both cases (as expected — same ramp angle).

Key Equation:
a = 2s ÷ t² (when u = 0)

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a results table with column headings. Students record measurements and calculate acceleration step by step.
Stretch:
Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the acceleration if the timing uncertainty is ±0.1 s and the distance uncertainty is ±1 cm. Discuss which measurement has the larger impact on accuracy.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Graph Analysis

Students plot s against t² from their results and calculate acceleration from the gradient.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can plan and carry out the experiment, calculate acceleration, and plot/analyse graphs
  • Getting there: Can follow the method and calculate acceleration but cannot analyse graphs
  • Not yet: Cannot plan the experiment or use the equation a = 2s/t²

Homework Suggestions

  • Solve 10 SUVAT problems: five with gravity, five horizontal motion
  • Calculate how long it takes for an object to fall from 100 m (g = 9.8 m/s²). Then calculate how far it falls in the first second.
  • Research the acceleration due to gravity on other planets (Mars, Jupiter, Moon). Compare to Earth.
  • Draw a velocity-time graph for a ball thrown upward at 15 m/s, including the descent back to the starting point
