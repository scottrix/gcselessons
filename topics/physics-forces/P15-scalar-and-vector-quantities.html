P15: Scalar and Vector Quantities

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understanding the difference between scalar and vector quantities, and calculating speed, velocity, and acceleration.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish between scalar and vector quantities
  • Give examples of common scalar and vector quantities
  • Calculate speed, velocity, and acceleration
  • Interpret distance-time and velocity-time graphs
  • Use vector diagrams to represent motion

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of motion from KS3
  • Ability to rearrange equations
  • Basic graph interpretation skills

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Ruler
  • Stopwatch (for speed investigations)
  • Graph paper

Lesson 1: Scalars and Vectors

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Journey Map

Show a map with two routes from A to B: one direct (5 km east) and one longer route (3 km north then 4 km east). Ask: "Which route covers more distance? Which has the greater displacement?" Discuss the difference.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Scalar quantities have magnitude (size) only:
- Distance (m), Speed (m/s), Time (s), Mass (kg), Energy (J), Temperature (°C), Power (W)

Vector quantities have both magnitude and direction:
- Displacement (m), Velocity (m/s), Acceleration (m/s²), Force (N), Momentum (kg·m/s)

Key pairs:
- Distance ↔ Displacement (scalar ↔ vector)
- Speed ↔ Velocity (scalar ↔ vector)

Why does direction matter?
- A car travelling at 30 m/s east is not the same as one travelling at 30 m/s west
- If two cars travel at 30 m/s in opposite directions, their relative velocity is 60 m/s
- Forces in opposite directions can cancel out (vectors)

Speed vs Velocity:
- Speed = distance ÷ time (scalar — no direction)
- Velocity = displacement ÷ time (vector — includes direction)
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time
- Average velocity = total displacement ÷ total time

Acceleration:
- Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time taken
- a = (v − u) ÷ t
- Units: m/s² (metres per second squared)
- Acceleration can be positive (speeding up) or negative (slowing down / deceleration)
Worked Example

A car travels from London to Manchester (200 km north) and then back to London.
(a) Total distance = 200 + 200 = 400 km
(b) Total displacement = 0 (car is back where it started)
(c) Average speed = 400 ÷ 4 = 100 km/h (if journey took 4 hours)
(d) Average velocity = 0 ÷ 4 = 0 km/h

Key Equations:
Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
Acceleration = (v − u) ÷ t

v = final velocity (m/s)
u = initial velocity (m/s)
t = time (s)
Common Misconception: Students often use "speed" and "velocity" interchangeably. Speed is a scalar (just a number), velocity is a vector (number + direction). An object moving in a circle at constant speed is constantly changing direction, so its velocity is changing.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a table with quantities listed. Students classify each as scalar or vector.
Stretch:
A car travels 6 km north, then 8 km east in 20 minutes. Calculate: (a) total distance, (b) magnitude of displacement, (c) average speed, (d) average velocity. (Answer: displacement = 10 km, using Pythagoras)

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Classification

Students sort these into scalar and vector: weight, mass, temperature, force, time, energy, acceleration, speed.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can classify quantities as scalar/vector and calculate speed, velocity, and acceleration
  • Getting there: Can classify quantities but makes errors in calculations or graph interpretation
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish between scalar and vector quantities

Lesson 2: Graphs of Motion

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Graph Reading

Show a distance-time graph of a car journey with straight lines at different gradients and flat sections. Ask: "What is the car doing at each section of the graph?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Distance-time graphs:
- Gradient = speed (steeper gradient = faster)
- Horizontal line = stationary (not moving)
- Curved line = changing speed (acceleration or deceleration)
- Straight diagonal line = constant speed

Velocity-time graphs:
- Gradient = acceleration (steeper = greater acceleration)
- Horizontal line = constant velocity (zero acceleration)
- Negative gradient = deceleration
- Area under the graph = displacement (distance travelled in a given direction)
- Positive area = forward motion, negative area = backward motion

Interpreting gradients:
- Speed (from distance-time graph) = vertical change ÷ horizontal change
- Acceleration (from velocity-time graph) = vertical change ÷ horizontal change

Area under velocity-time graph:
- For a rectangle: area = velocity × time = displacement
- For a triangle: area = ½ × base × height = ½ × time × velocity
- For complex shapes: split into rectangles and triangles, calculate each area
Worked Example

A velocity-time graph shows a car accelerating from 0 to 20 m/s in 10 s, then travelling at constant velocity for 20 s, then decelerating to 0 in 5 s.
(a) Acceleration during first 10 s: a = (20 − 0) ÷ 10 = 2 m/s²
(b) Displacement during acceleration: area = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100 m
(c) Displacement during constant velocity: area = 20 × 20 = 400 m
(d) Displacement during deceleration: area = ½ × 5 × 20 = 50 m
(e) Total displacement = 100 + 400 + 50 = 550 m

Key Relationships:
Distance-time graph: gradient = speed
Velocity-time graph: gradient = acceleration
Velocity-time graph: area under curve = displacement

Differentiation

Support:
Provide graph templates with grid lines. Students calculate gradients and areas step by step with guidance.
Stretch:
Sketch a velocity-time graph for a car that: accelerates from rest at 3 m/s² for 4 s, travels at constant velocity for 6 s, then decelerates at 2 m/s² until it stops. Calculate the total displacement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Graph Matching

Show four distance-time graphs and four descriptions of journeys. Students match each graph to its description.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can interpret both types of graph, calculate gradients and areas, and sketch graphs from descriptions
  • Getting there: Can calculate gradients but struggles with areas under velocity-time graphs
  • Not yet: Cannot interpret gradients from motion graphs or confuse the two graph types

Homework Suggestions

  • Draw a distance-time graph and velocity-time graph for a ball thrown upwards and caught at the same height
  • Calculate the acceleration of a car that goes from 0 to 60 mph (27 m/s) in 8 seconds
  • Plot a velocity-time graph from a set of data and calculate the total displacement
  • Research the difference between instantaneous speed and average speed, with examples
