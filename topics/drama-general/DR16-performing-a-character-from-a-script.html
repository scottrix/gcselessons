Lesson 2: Core Concepts: performing a character from a script

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on performing a character from a script. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Actioning: assigning a transitive verb to each line — 'I WARN', 'I BEG', 'I THREATEN' — gives every line a purpose and energy.
Key Fact: Vocal skills: pace, pitch, pause, volume, tone, emphasis, accent — each must be chosen deliberately for character and situation.
Key Fact: Physical skills: posture, gesture, facial expression, proxemics, eye contact, movement — the body communicates as much as the voice.
Key Fact: Line learning: learn lines EARLY so you can focus on delivery, not recall. Learn intention, not just words.
Key Fact: Truthfulness: the audience must believe the character — this comes from understanding motivation, not from 'acting' emotions.
Drama Exam Tips: For performance questions, always describe specific, concrete choices: 'I would deliver this line at a slower pace, with dropped volume, avoiding eye contact with the other character and moving USR to create physical distance — this shows the character's withdrawal and loss of confidence.'

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain what actioning is and how it helps an actor deliver lines more effectively.

Answer: Actioning assigns a transitive verb to each line (I CONVINCE, I INTIMIDATE, I PLEAD). It gives every line a purpose, prevents flat delivery, and ensures the actor is always DOING something to the other character, not just saying words.

Lesson 3: Application: performing a character from a script

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain what actioning is and how it helps an actor deliver lines more effectively.

Answer: Actioning assigns a transitive verb to each line (I CONVINCE, I INTIMIDATE, I PLEAD). It gives every line a purpose, prevents flat delivery, and ensures the actor is always DOING something to the other character, not just saying words.

Q2: Describe the vocal and physical skills you would use to perform a character experiencing a crisis of confidence.

Answer: Vocal: quieter volume, hesitant pace, rising pitch on questions, long pauses before responses. Physical: closed posture, avoiding eye contact, fidgeting hands, retreating spatially. Each choice justified by the character's internal state.

Q3: How can an actor maintain truthfulness in performance without simply 'acting' emotions?

Answer: Truthfulness comes from understanding what the character WANTS and pursuing it honestly, not from pretending to feel emotions. If you focus on the objective and the action, the emotion follows naturally. Stanislavski: play the action, not the feeling.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: performing a character from a script

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe how you would use vocal and physical skills to perform a key moment for a character from a play you have studied. Justify each choice with reference to the character's motivation. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify the character's objective in the moment; describe specific vocal choices (pace, pitch, pause, emphasis); describe specific physical choices (posture, gesture, proxemics); explain how each choice reveals character and motivation; show understanding of the scene's context; evaluate the intended effect on the audience. Mark scheme: Performing a character is assessed in Component 3: Texts in Practice (30% NEA). You perform two extracts from one play. ...

Exam Tips: Actioning verbs must be TRANSITIVE (done TO someone): I WARN, not 'I am angry' | Always link vocal/physical choices to CHARACTER MOTIVATION | Performing for an examiner: confidence, energy, projection and focus from the start | Never describe emotions — describe the ACTIONS that show the emotions
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Good acting is about showing strong emotions convincingly — you need to really feel the sadness or anger to perform well. Correct: Good acting is about pursuing objectives truthfully. If you focus on 'showing sadness', the performance looks fake. If you focus on what the character WANTS and the OBSTACLES they face, genuine emotion emerges naturally. Stanislavski's system is about action, not emotion. Act with purpose, not feeling.
