Lesson 2: Core Concepts: dramatic devices and conventions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on dramatic devices and conventions. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Dramatic irony: audience knows something characters don't — creates tension, humour or foreboding.
Key Fact: Soliloquy: character alone on stage speaking thoughts — reveals inner truth to audience.
Key Fact: Aside: character briefly shares thoughts with audience — conspiratorial, breaks fourth wall.
Key Fact: Cross-cutting: switching between two scenes — creates contrast, parallel or tension.
Key Fact: Juxtaposition: placing contrasting elements side by side — highlights differences, creates shock or comedy.
Drama Exam Tips: When writing about dramatic devices, use the formula: Device name + How it works + Why the playwright chose it + Effect on audience. E.g. 'The playwright uses dramatic irony when Sheila confesses but Mrs Birling doesn't realise, creating frustration and highlighting the theme of collective responsibility.'

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe three dramatic devices a playwright might use to reveal a character's true thoughts to the audience.

Answer: Three named devices (soliloquy, aside, direct address) with clear explanation of HOW they reveal truth and their different effects — soliloquy is extended, aside is brief and conspiratorial, direct address breaks the fiction entirely.

Lesson 3: Application: dramatic devices and conventions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe three dramatic devices a playwright might use to reveal a character's true thoughts to the audience.

Answer: Three named devices (soliloquy, aside, direct address) with clear explanation of HOW they reveal truth and their different effects — soliloquy is extended, aside is brief and conspiratorial, direct address breaks the fiction entirely.

Q2: How does dramatic irony create tension in a play you have studied?

Answer: Specific example from a studied play where the audience knows more than a character — explain how this knowledge creates anticipation, fear or humour; link to the playwright's purpose.

Q3: Explain how cross-cutting and juxtaposition can be used to highlight a play's themes.

Answer: Cross-cutting switches between scenes to show contrast; juxtaposition places opposing ideas together. Both highlight themes by forcing comparison. Give a specific example of each in practice.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: dramatic devices and conventions

Duration: 50 minutes

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How does the playwright use at least three dramatic devices to communicate the themes of the play? Refer to a text you have studied. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify three devices precisely by name; explain how each works in context; analyse how each connects to the play's themes; evaluate which is most effective and why; use precise terminology and detailed textual references. Exam Tips: Never confuse soliloquy (alone, extended) with aside (brief, others present) | Always explain the EFFECT of the device on the audience | Devices are CHOICES — always consider why the playwright used them | Link devices to the play's overall themes and message
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Dramatic devices are just tricks to make the play more interesting — they don't have deeper meaning. Correct: Dramatic devices are deliberate artistic choices that shape meaning. Dramatic irony in An Inspector Calls isn't just 'interesting' — it exposes the Birlings' hypocrisy. Soliloquy in Macbeth reveals psychological disintegration. Always link device to meaning.
