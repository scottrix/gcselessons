Lesson 2: Core Concepts: rehearsal techniques and strategies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on rehearsal techniques and strategies. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Hot-seating: one person sits in character and answers questions from others — reveals backstory, motivation and relationships.
Key Fact: Role on the wall: drawing an outline of a character and filling it with what they think, feel, experience — builds complex character understanding.
Key Fact: Thought tracking: tapping a performer in a freeze-frame to speak their character's inner thoughts — reveals subtext and hidden feelings.
Key Fact: Conscience alley: two lines of people voice opposing advice as a character walks between them — explores moral dilemmas.
Key Fact: Marking the moment: identifying the most significant moment in a scene and finding a way to highlight it (slow motion, freeze, repetition).
Drama Exam Tips: When writing about rehearsal techniques in your portfolio, use this structure: What we did (name the technique) → Why we did it (what we wanted to discover) → What we found (what it revealed) → How it improved the piece (the outcome). This shows AO4 evaluation skills.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain how hot-seating and role on the wall help develop character understanding during the devising process.

Answer: Hot-seating reveals what the character knows and feels through questioning; role on the wall shows what is hidden (inside) versus what is visible (outside). Both deepen character understanding — hot-seating is verbal, role on the wall is visual.

Lesson 3: Application: rehearsal techniques and strategies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain how hot-seating and role on the wall help develop character understanding during the devising process.

Answer: Hot-seating reveals what the character knows and feels through questioning; role on the wall shows what is hidden (inside) versus what is visible (outside). Both deepen character understanding — hot-seating is verbal, role on the wall is visual.

Q2: Describe how you would use thought tracking and conscience alley to explore a character's moral dilemma.

Answer: Thought tracking reveals the character's inner conflict in a specific moment; conscience alley externalises the two sides of the dilemma. Together they show the gap between what a character says and what they think.

Q3: What is 'marking the moment' and why is it an effective rehearsal technique?

Answer: Marking the moment identifies the most dramatically important point and highlights it — through slow motion, freeze-frame, repetition or sound. It's effective because it directs the audience's attention to the key turning point.

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe three rehearsal techniques you used during your devising process. Explain why you chose each one and how it improved your final piece. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: name three techniques precisely; explain the specific reason for choosing each (what you wanted to discover); describe what the technique revealed; show how it directly improved a specific moment in the final piece; evaluate which was most effective and why. Mark scheme: Rehearsal techniques are central to the devising portfolio (AO4: 30%). You must demonstrate that you used specific techn...

Exam Tips: Name each technique precisely — 'hot-seating', not 'asking questions in role' | Explain WHY you used each technique, not just WHAT it is | In your portfolio, show HOW each technique improved your piece | Rehearsal techniques are process tools — always link to the final performance outcome
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Rehearsal techniques are just warm-up games — they don't really affect the final performance. Correct: Rehearsal techniques are essential development tools. Hot-seating reveals character depths that transform performance choices; thought tracking finds subtext that shapes delivery; conscience alley explores dilemmas that drive the drama. They directly improve the quality and depth of the final piece.
