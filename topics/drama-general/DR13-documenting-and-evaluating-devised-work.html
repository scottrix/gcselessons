Lesson 2: Core Concepts: documenting and evaluating devised work

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on documenting and evaluating devised work. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The portfolio is worth as much as the performance — it must be detailed, reflective and analytical.
Key Fact: Document EVERY stage: stimulus response, research, exploration, rehearsal, refinement, performance.
Key Fact: Reflection: not just what you did, but WHY you did it and what you would change — show critical thinking.
Key Fact: Practitioner influence: explicitly show how Stanislavski, Brecht, Frantic Assembly etc. shaped your work.
Key Fact: Justify EVERY choice: 'We used cross-cutting because Brecht's V-effect encourages the audience to compare the two scenes and think critically about the social message.'
Drama Exam Tips: Structure each portfolio section using: What we did → Why we chose this approach → How we developed it → What effect we intended → How the audience responded → What we would improve. This systematic approach ensures full AO4 coverage.

Lesson 3: Application: documenting and evaluating devised work

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What should you include in a devising portfolio to achieve a high mark?

Answer: Stimulus analysis, research evidence, exploration techniques used, rehearsal process, key decisions and their justification, practitioner influence with examples, reflection on strengths and weaknesses, evaluation of the final piece's impact on the audience.

Q2: Explain the difference between describing what you did and evaluating why you did it. Why is evaluation more important?

Answer: Description: 'We used a freeze-frame.' Evaluation: 'We used a freeze-frame to mark the moment when the truth was revealed, creating a pause that allowed the audience to absorb the significance — this Brechtian technique forced critical reflection rather than emotional response.'

Q3: How would you document the influence of a practitioner on your devised piece?

Answer: Name the practitioner; explain the specific technique or idea used; describe how you applied it in rehearsal; evaluate its effect on the final piece; analyse how it shaped the audience's experience.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: documenting and evaluating devised work

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Write a portfolio entry evaluating how you used a practitioner's ideas to develop a key moment in your devised piece. <div class="

A grade 9 portfolio entry will: identify the practitioner and specific technique; describe the practical application in detail; explain the creative intention; analyse the effect on the audience; evaluate the success of the technique; suggest specific improvements; use precise theatrical terminology throughout. Mark scheme: The devising portfolio is assessed on AO4 (analyse and evaluate — 30% of the devising component). Marks are awarded for:...

Exam Tips: The portfolio is NOT a diary — it's an analytical document | Always justify: WHAT you did + WHY you did it + WHAT EFFECT it had | Use practitioner terminology throughout — 'alienation effect', 'given circumstances' | Include evidence: photos, diagrams, annotated scripts
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: The portfolio is just a record of what happened in rehearsals — you just need to describe the process. Correct: The portfolio is an EVALUATIVE document. Describing what happened earns basic marks; explaining WHY choices were made, HOW they developed the piece, and EVALUATING their effectiveness earns top marks. Every paragraph should contain analysis, not just description.
