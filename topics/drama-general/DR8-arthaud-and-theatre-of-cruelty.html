Lesson 2: Core Concepts: artaud and theatre of cruelty

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on artaud and theatre of cruelty. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Theatre of Cruelty: not physical pain but ASSAULT ON THE SENSES — theatre should overwhelm the audience, not just tell a story.
Key Fact: Total theatre: using every possible means — light, sound, movement, space, colour — to create a complete sensory experience.
Key Fact: No text dependence: words are not the primary medium — sound, movement and spectacle communicate beyond language.
Key Fact: Ritual and ceremony: Artaud wanted theatre to function like a sacred ritual — transformative, primal, cathartic.
Key Fact: Audience immersion: place the audience IN the action, not observing it — break down the barrier between performer and spectator.
Drama Exam Tips: Artaud questions require PRACTICAL IMAGINATION. Don't just quote theory — describe what the audience would actually experience: 'The audience would sit in the centre of the space with performers moving around them, loud drumming creating a pulse rhythm, harsh red light flooding the space, and actors using exaggerated gestural movements to communicate without words.'

Lesson 3: Application: artaud and theatre of cruelty

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain what Artaud meant by 'Theatre of Cruelty' — why is 'cruelty' misleading?

Answer: 'Cruelty' means intensity and rigour, not physical pain. Artaud wanted theatre to be a shock to the system that awakens the audience's subconscious — an assault on the senses that strips away rational civilised responses.

Q2: How would you use Artaud's ideas to create a sensory, immersive experience for an audience?

Answer: Practical examples: surround-sound audience, performers among spectators, harsh lighting changes, drumming rhythms, exaggerated physical gesture, symbolic objects, no conventional set. Each choice linked to Artaud's theory.

Q3: Compare Artaud's approach to theatre with Brecht's. How do they want the audience to respond differently?

Answer: Brecht wants the audience to THINK critically (V-effect, alienation); Artaud wants the audience to FEEL viscerally (immersion, sensory assault). Brecht distances; Artaud overwhelms. Both reject naturalism but for opposite reasons.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response You are directing a piece inspired by Artaud's Theatre of Cruelty. Describe how you would create a total theatre experience that immerses the audience. Justify each choice with reference to Artaud's theories. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: describe at least four sensory/immersive techniques; explain how each connects to Artaud's theory; show understanding of 'total theatre'; consider audience placement and experience; evaluate the intended emotional impact; contrast with conventional theatre. Mark scheme: Artaud questions primarily test AO2 (analyse how theatrical choices create meaning) and AO3 (apply practitioner ideas to...

Exam Tips: 'Cruelty' is NOT about physical pain — always clarify this | Artaud is about EXPERIENCE not INTELLECT — focus on senses and emotions | Show practical techniques: soundscapes, movement, audience positioning | Know that Artaud's ideas were largely unrealised — he wrote theory more than practice
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Artaud's Theatre of Cruelty means plays that are violent and disturbing to watch. Correct: 'Cruelty' refers to the strictness and intensity of the theatrical experience — it is about being rigorous and uncompromising in shaking the audience out of their complacency, not about depicting violence. It aims for a profound, primal emotional response, not shock for its own sake.
