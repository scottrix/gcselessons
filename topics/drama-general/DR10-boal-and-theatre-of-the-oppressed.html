Lesson 2: Core Concepts: boal and theatre of the oppressed

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on boal and theatre of the oppressed. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Forum theatre: a play is performed, then replayed with audience members ('spect-actors') stepping in to change the outcome — the audience becomes active.
Key Fact: Spect-actor: the audience member who stops the action and replaces a character — they are both spectator and actor.
Key Fact: Image theatre: participants use their bodies to create still images (sculptures) of oppression — reveals issues without words.
Key Fact: Invisible theatre: performed in public spaces without the audience knowing it's theatre — provokes real reactions and discussion.
Key Fact: Theatre for social change: Boal believed theatre should be a rehearsal for revolution — empowering people to change their world.
Drama Exam Tips: For Boal questions, always describe the PROCESS and its PURPOSE. Forum theatre isn't just audience participation — it's a structured method for exploring oppression and rehearsing solutions. Show you understand the political purpose behind each technique.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain how forum theatre works and why Boal believed it was important for social change.

Answer: Forum theatre: a short play showing oppression is performed, then replayed. Spect-actors shout 'Stop!', replace the oppressed character, and try different strategies. The Joker facilitates. It's important because it turns passive audiences into active problem-solvers, rehearsing real-world change.

Lesson 3: Application: boal and theatre of the oppressed

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain how forum theatre works and why Boal believed it was important for social change.

Answer: Forum theatre: a short play showing oppression is performed, then replayed. Spect-actors shout 'Stop!', replace the oppressed character, and try different strategies. The Joker facilitates. It's important because it turns passive audiences into active problem-solvers, rehearsing real-world change.

Q2: What is the role of the 'Joker' in forum theatre? How does this differ from a traditional director?

Answer: The Joker is a facilitator, not a director — they encourage intervention, ask questions and manage the forum. Unlike a director who controls the action, the Joker hands power to the spect-actors.

Q3: Compare Boal's Theatre of the Oppressed with Brecht's epic theatre — both want social change but use different methods. How?

Answer: Both want social change. Brecht: audience THINKS about issues from a distance (V-effect). Boal: audience ACTS on issues by intervening (forum theatre). Brecht is observation; Boal is participation. Both reject passive entertainment.

A grade 9 response will: describe the full forum theatre process from image theatre exploration to forum performance; explain the Joker's role; show how spect-actors intervene; analyse how the process empowers participants; link to Boal's political philosophy; evaluate the effectiveness of the techniques for social change. Mark scheme: Boal questions test AO1 (practitioner terminology), AO2 (analysing how techniques create meaning), and AO4 (evaluating e...

Exam Tips: The 'Joker' is NOT a clown — it's a facilitator role, always explain this | Boal's work is about EMPOWERMENT — always link to giving the audience agency | Forum theatre requires the 'stop' mechanism — describe the full process | Boal questions often ask you to compare with Brecht — know the similarities and differences
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Forum theatre is just interactive drama where the audience joins in for fun — it's basically improvisation games. Correct: Forum theatre is a structured political tool. The spect-actor intervenes to try to change the outcome of oppression — it's a rehearsal for real-world action. The Joker facilitates discussion about what worked and what didn't. It's purposeful, political and designed to empower, not just entertain.
