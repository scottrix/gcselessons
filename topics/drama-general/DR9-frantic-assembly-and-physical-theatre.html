Lesson 2: Core Concepts: frantic assembly and physical theatre

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on frantic assembly and physical theatre. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Physical theatre: the body is the primary means of expression — movement communicates meaning beyond words.
Key Fact: Frantic Assembly's chair work: using chairs as the central prop — creates dynamic, rhythmic, often aggressive sequences.
Key Fact: Push hands: two performers pushing against each other's hands — creates tension, balance and relational dynamics.
Key Fact: Lift and lean: one performer supports another's weight — requires trust, shows relationship and dependency.
Key Fact: Unison and counterpoint: moving together (unison) shows unity; moving in opposition (counterpoint) shows conflict or individuality.
Drama Exam Tips: For physical theatre questions, describe movement PRECISELY: who moves, how they move, what it represents, and the effect. 'Two performers stand close, hands pressed together, pushing rhythmically — the counter-tension represents their competing desires, the increasing force shows escalating conflict.'

Lesson 3: Application: frantic assembly and physical theatre

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe three Frantic Assembly techniques and explain the dramatic effect each can create.

Answer: Three named techniques with dramatic effect: chair work (rhythm, aggression, status); push hands (tension, balance of power); lift and lean (trust, dependency, vulnerability). Each linked to specific dramatic meaning.

Q2: How can physical theatre communicate emotions and relationships more effectively than dialogue alone?

Answer: Physical theatre bypasses language to communicate directly through the body — proximity, touch, weight and rhythm reveal relationship dynamics instantly. It can show conflict, intimacy or power shifts without a single word.

Q3: You are creating a devised piece about conflict. How would you use physical theatre techniques to explore this theme?

Answer: Practical response showing: push hands to show conflict escalation; unison breaking into counterpoint to show unity fracturing; chair work to create aggressive confrontations; lift/lean to show shifting power. Each linked to the theme of conflict.

Duration: 50 minutes

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How would you use Frantic Assembly techniques to devise a piece about a relationship breaking down? Describe and justify at least three physical theatre sequences. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: name three specific techniques; describe each sequence precisely (who, what, how); explain the dramatic meaning of each; show how the sequences develop the theme; consider the audience's experience; evaluate the effectiveness of physical theatre for this subject matter. Mark scheme: Physical theatre questions test AO3 (performance skills) and AO4 (evaluation). In devising, physical theatre techniques ...

Exam Tips: Name Frantic Assembly techniques precisely — 'chair duet', not 'chairs scene' | Physical theatre is about MEANING through MOVEMENT — not just 'it looks cool' | Always link physical choices to the emotional/relational content | Safety and trust are essential when discussing contact work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Physical theatre is just dancing — it doesn't have the same dramatic depth as scripted dialogue. Correct: Physical theatre uses movement as a primary language for dramatic meaning. Frantic Assembly's techniques communicate complex emotions and relationships through the body. Movement can be MORE powerful than words for showing conflict, intimacy and power because it bypasses intellectual processing and hits the audience viscerally.
