Lesson 2: Core Concepts: character creation and development

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on character creation and development. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Every character has a motivation — what they WANT drives every action and line.
Key Fact: Super-objective: the character's overarching goal across the whole play; scene objectives are smaller goals within each scene.
Key Fact: Status: the power dynamic between characters — it shifts moment to moment and is shown through body language, position and voice.
Key Fact: Character arc: how a character changes from beginning to end — static characters stay the same, dynamic characters transform.
Key Fact: Multi-roling: one actor playing multiple parts — requires clear physical and vocal differentiation between roles.
Drama Exam Tips: When analysing character, use the motivation-objective-action chain: What does the character want? What do they do to get it? What prevents them? How do they change? This systematic approach covers all assessment objectives.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the difference between a character's objective and their super-objective, using a character from a play you have studied.

Answer: Objective is the immediate want in a scene (e.g. to win an argument); super-objective is the overarching goal (e.g. to gain power). The character's scene objectives serve the super-objective.

Lesson 3: Application: character creation and development

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain the difference between a character's objective and their super-objective, using a character from a play you have studied.

Answer: Objective is the immediate want in a scene (e.g. to win an argument); super-objective is the overarching goal (e.g. to gain power). The character's scene objectives serve the super-objective.

Q2: How does an actor communicate a character's status change through performance? Give specific examples.

Answer: Status is shown through: body language (high status = open, still; low status = closed, fidgety); spatial positioning (higher = more powerful); vocal qualities (pace, pitch, volume). Give a specific example of a status shift.

Q3: What is the effect of multi-roling on the audience? How must the actor prepare for this challenge?

Answer: Multi-roling creates connections between characters, suggests social types, and challenges the audience. The actor must use clear physical and vocal switches — costume pieces, posture, accent, gesture.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: character creation and development

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe the character arc of a character from a play you have studied. How does the playwright use this character to communicate a key theme? <div class="

A grade 9 response will: track the character's journey from start to finish; identify key turning points; explain how the character's changes (or refusal to change) reflect the play's themes; use precise quotations or references; evaluate the playwright's purpose. Mark scheme: Character analysis tests AO1 (knowledge of character terminology), AO2 (analysing how character communicates meaning), a...

Exam Tips: Always identify what a character WANTS — their motivation drives everything | Track status shifts within scenes — they reveal power dynamics | For multi-roling questions, discuss both the ACTOR's technique and the DIRECTOR's intention | Character arcs matter — show how characters change across the whole play
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: A character's personality is fixed — they are either good or bad throughout the play. Correct: Characters are complex and can change. Their status shifts moment to moment; their understanding grows; their objectives clash with other characters'. The best character analysis tracks development and contradictions, not fixed labels.
