Lesson 2: Core Concepts: refining and polishing performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on refining and polishing performance. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Refining means making specific, deliberate improvements — not just running the piece again but fixing identified problems.
Key Fact: Timing and pacing: the speed at which moments unfold — too fast loses impact, too slow loses engagement. Every pause must be earned.
Key Fact: Transitions: how you move between scenes — clumsy transitions break the theatrical experience. Make them part of the drama.
Key Fact: Consistency: every performance must be at the same level — energy, focus and delivery must be reliable and repeatable.
Key Fact: Technical integration: lighting, sound and set must support the drama — rehearse with technical elements, not just add them at the end.
Drama Exam Tips: In the portfolio, show your refinement process: 'In early rehearsals, the confrontation scene felt rushed. We slowed the pacing, added a two-second pause before the key line, and used a lighting change to mark the shift. This created more tension and allowed the audience to anticipate the revelation.'

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: What is the difference between rehearsing and refining? Why is refinement essential?

Answer: Rehearsing is learning and practising the piece; refining is making deliberate, specific improvements based on observation and feedback. Refinement is essential because running a piece repeatedly embeds mistakes — refinement identifies and fixes them.

Lesson 3: Application: refining and polishing performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What is the difference between rehearsing and refining? Why is refinement essential?

Answer: Rehearsing is learning and practising the piece; refining is making deliberate, specific improvements based on observation and feedback. Refinement is essential because running a piece repeatedly embeds mistakes — refinement identifies and fixes them.

Q2: Describe how you would ensure smooth transitions between scenes in a devised piece.

Answer: Plan transitions as part of the drama: use movement, sound, lighting or narration to bridge scenes. Rehearse transitions until they are as precise as the scenes themselves. Never have blackouts with silence unless deliberately chosen.

Q3: How can pacing and timing affect the audience's experience of a key dramatic moment?

Answer: A slow build creates tension; a sudden change creates shock; a well-timed pause lets the audience absorb significance. Pacing controls emotional engagement — the same moment at different speeds creates completely different effects.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: refining and polishing performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe how you refined a key moment in your devised piece from first rehearsal to final performance. Explain the specific changes you made and their impact. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: describe the moment in its early form; identify specific problems; explain deliberate changes made (timing, positioning, delivery, technical); show the impact of each change; evaluate the overall improvement; demonstrate understanding that refinement is an ongoing analytical process. Mark scheme: Refinement questions test AO3 (performance skills) and AO4 (evaluation). In the portfolio, you must show the development...

Exam Tips: Refinement is about SPECIFIC improvements — name what you changed and why | Transitions should be DRAMATIC, not just functional | In your portfolio, document refinement — show the journey from first draft to polished piece | Technical elements must be integrated early, not bolted on
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Once you've learned the lines and blocking, the piece is ready — you just need to perform it confidently. Correct: Learning lines and blocking is the START of the process. Refinement involves: adjusting timing for maximum impact, polishing transitions, ensuring consistent energy, integrating technical elements, and responding to feedback. The difference between a grade 5 and grade 9 performance is in the refinement.
