Lesson 2: Core Concepts: performing for an examiner

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on performing for an examiner. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Component 3: perform two extracts from ONE play — assessed by a visiting examiner.
Key Fact: Group work: you are assessed INDIVIDUALLY even in a group performance — you must maintain character and focus throughout.
Key Fact: Preparation: full dress and technical rehearsals before the exam day — no surprises on the day.
Key Fact: Focus under pressure: if something goes wrong, STAY IN CHARACTER and adapt — the examiner assesses how you handle the moment.
Key Fact: Performance etiquette: silence backstage, full costume and make-up, clear entrances and exits, commitment from the first second.
Drama Exam Tips: For the practical exam, the examiner assesses: 1) Vocal skills (clarity, projection, pace, pitch), 2) Physical skills (movement, gesture, facial expression, spatial awareness), 3) Characterisation (understanding, truthfulness, development), 4) Interaction (listening, responding, ensemble awareness), 5) Overall effectiveness (impact, engagement, dramatic shape).

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: What should you do if something goes wrong during a performance for the examiner? Why is this important?

Answer: Stay in character and adapt — never break character or apologise. The examiner wants to see how you handle the unexpected. A covered mistake shows professionalism; a broken character shows immaturity. Adapt and carry on.

Lesson 3: Application: performing for an examiner

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What should you do if something goes wrong during a performance for the examiner? Why is this important?

Answer: Stay in character and adapt — never break character or apologise. The examiner wants to see how you handle the unexpected. A covered mistake shows professionalism; a broken character shows immaturity. Adapt and carry on.

Q2: Describe how you would prepare for a visiting examiner assessment from one week before to the performance day.

Answer: One week before: full run-throughs with notes. Three days before: technical and dress rehearsals. Day before: light rehearsal, rest, prepare costume. Day of: warm-up, focus, silence backstage, full commitment from the first moment.

Q3: How are individual performers assessed within a group performance? What does the examiner look for?

Answer: Each performer is marked individually on: vocal skills, physical skills, characterisation, interaction with others, and overall dramatic effectiveness. The group context matters but your personal choices, commitment and truthfulness earn your marks.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe how you would prepare for and perform in a practical exam with a visiting examiner. Explain what the examiner will be looking for and how you would demonstrate your best work. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: describe thorough preparation (rehearsal schedule, technical/dress rehearsals); explain performance etiquette (costume, focus, professionalism); detail how to demonstrate each assessed skill; show understanding of individual assessment within group context; explain how to handle unexpected situations; demonstrate awareness of what the examiner values. Exam Tips: The examiner assesses from the MOMENT you enter the performance space | Individual marks — your group's weakness should not drag you down if you perform well | Cover mistakes IN CHARACTER — never acknowledge them | Full costume, make-up and props show professionalism and commitment
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: If someone in your group forgets their lines or makes a mistake, your mark will be affected too. Correct: You are assessed INDIVIDUALLY. If another performer makes a mistake, the examiner watches how YOU respond — staying in character, adapting and supporting the scene shows professionalism and can actually enhance your mark. Breaking character or panicking hurts YOUR mark, not theirs.
