Lesson 2: Core Concepts: stanislavski and naturalism

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on stanislavski and naturalism. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Given circumstances: the facts of the play — time, place, situation, relationships. An actor must know these before they can act truthfully.
Key Fact: Magic if: 'What would I do if I were in this situation?' — helps the actor find truthful reactions, not fake 'acting'.
Key Fact: Emotion memory: recalling a personal emotional experience to fuel a scene's emotional truth — not reliving, but using the memory.
Key Fact: Units and objectives: breaking a scene into beats (units), each with a specific objective — gives structure and purpose to every moment.
Key Fact: Subtext: what a character really means beneath the words — naturalistic characters rarely say exactly what they think.
Drama Exam Tips: For Stanislavski questions, always demonstrate PRACTICAL APPLICATION. Don't just define terms — show how an actor would use them: 'To play Sheila, I would identify my given circumstances as a wealthy young woman in 1912, then use the magic if to imagine how I would feel discovering my involvement in Eva's death.'

Lesson 3: Application: stanislavski and naturalism

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain how an actor would use Stanislavski's 'magic if' and 'given circumstances' to prepare for a role.

Answer: Given circumstances provide the factual foundation; magic if personalises the situation. An actor first researches all the facts, then asks 'what would I do?' to find truthful responses. Give a specific character example.

Q2: What is subtext and why is it important in naturalistic performance? Give an example.

Answer: Subtext is the unspoken meaning beneath dialogue. In naturalism, characters don't say what they mean — the actor must play the subtext, not just the lines. E.g. 'I'm fine' said with tears = subtext of pain.

Q3: Compare Stanislavski's approach to Brecht's — how do they differ in their relationship with the audience?

Answer: Stanislavski wants the audience to empathise and believe (emotional identification); Brecht wants the audience to think critically (alienation effect). Stanislavski hides the art; Brecht makes the audience aware they are watching theatre.

Exam Tips: Always use Stanislavski's exact terms — 'given circumstances', not 'background info' | Show HOW you would apply each technique, not just what it means | Naturalism questions need you to link technique to truthful performance | Comparing practitioners is a common question — know the key differences
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Stanislavski's system means you just pretend to be the character and copy how they would behave. Correct: Stanislavski's system is about finding TRUTHFUL experience, not pretending. The magic if asks 'What would I do?' not 'What would the character do?' — the actor uses their own emotional reality within the given circumstances. It's internal truth, not external imitation.
