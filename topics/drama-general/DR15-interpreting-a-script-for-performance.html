Lesson 2: Core Concepts: interpreting a script for performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on interpreting a script for performance. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Read-through: first reading for overall story, second for character detail, third for design and staging ideas.
Key Fact: Extract the given circumstances: time, place, relationships, events before the play — these are the facts you cannot change.
Key Fact: Identify key moments: turning points, climaxes, revelations — these must be performed with the greatest precision.
Key Fact: Director's concept: the overall vision that shapes every choice — why THIS play NOW? What is the central idea?
Key Fact: Page to stage: translating the written text into live performance — every line needs a physical and vocal reality.
Drama Exam Tips: For script interpretation questions, show your analytical process: 'My first read-through identified the central conflict as... My second reading revealed the subtext in Scene 3 where... My director's concept focuses on... which means I would stage this moment by...'

Lesson 3: Application: interpreting a script for performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe the process you would use to analyse a script before beginning rehearsals.

Answer: Multiple read-throughs: first for story, second for character motivation and relationships, third for staging and design ideas. Extract given circumstances, identify key moments, consider the playwright's intentions and the play's themes.

Q2: What is a director's concept and why is it essential for a coherent production?

Answer: A director's concept is the overall creative vision that unifies all production choices. Without it, design, performance and staging choices may contradict each other. The concept answers: why this play now? What is the central idea we want to communicate?

Q3: How would you identify and prepare for the key moments in a scene from a play you have studied?

Answer: Identify moments where the power shifts, secrets are revealed or relationships change. Prepare by: understanding the given circumstances, determining character objectives, planning how to mark the moment physically/vocally, and rehearsing the build-up to create maximum impact.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: interpreting a script for performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Describe how you would interpret a scene from a play you have studied for performance. Explain your director's concept and how it would shape your staging choices. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: state a clear director's concept; describe the analytical process; explain how the concept shapes specific staging, performance and design choices; justify each choice with reference to the text; show how the interpretation reveals the play's themes; evaluate the effectiveness of the concept. Exam Tips: Script work begins with ANALYSIS, not rehearsal | The director's concept must be clear BEFORE practical choices are made | Key moments must be IDENTIFIED and then HIGHLIGHTED in performance | Always consider the PLAYWRIGHT'S intentions alongside your own interpretation
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: You just read the script, learn your lines and perform them — the playwright has already written everything you need. Correct: A script is a blueprint, not a building. The playwright provides the text, but the director and performers must make hundreds of interpretative choices: how to deliver each line, where to stand, when to pause, what the set looks like. Every production of the same play is different because interpretation transforms the written text into live meaning.
