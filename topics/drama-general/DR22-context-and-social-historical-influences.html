Lesson 2: Core Concepts: context and social/historical influences

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on context and social/historical influences. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Context: the social, cultural, historical and political circumstances in which a play was written AND in which it is performed.
Key Fact: Original context shapes the play's meaning — An Inspector Calls (1945, set 1912) uses dramatic irony about world wars the audience knew had happened.
Key Fact: Modern production context: a director may reset a play in a different period to make contemporary connections — this changes the meaning.
Key Fact: Social influences: class, gender, race, power structures — these are reflected in and critiqued by drama.
Key Fact: Historical influences: the period's events, beliefs and values — understanding them reveals the playwright's purpose.
Drama Exam Tips: When discussing context, always show how it SHAPES MEANING: 'The play was written in 1945 but set in 1912, creating dramatic irony — the audience knows that two world wars followed, which gives the Inspector's warning about collective responsibility greater urgency and makes the Birlings' complacency more shocking.'
TermMeaningExample
SocialClass, gender, attitudesReveals character motivation
HistoricalEvents, politics, technologyExplains dramatic irony
CulturalArt, philosophy, valuesExplains form and style

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Why is it important to understand the social and historical context of a play when analysing it?

Answer: Context reveals the playwright's intentions — they wrote for a specific audience with specific concerns. Without context, you miss the social commentary, the political critique and the historical references. E.g. An Inspector Calls is about post-war social responsibility, not just a family argument.

Lesson 3: Application: context and social/historical influences

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Drama Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Why is it important to understand the social and historical context of a play when analysing it?

Answer: Context reveals the playwright's intentions — they wrote for a specific audience with specific concerns. Without context, you miss the social commentary, the political critique and the historical references. E.g. An Inspector Calls is about post-war social responsibility, not just a family argument.

Q2: How might a modern production of a classic play change the meaning through its context? Give an example.

Answer: A modern production might reset the play in a contemporary context — e.g. setting The Crucible during modern 'cancel culture' rather than the McCarthy era. The story is the same but the context shifts the meaning, creating new relevance for a contemporary audience.

Q3: Explain how the original performance context of a play you have studied affects its meaning.

Answer: Specific example: e.g. Blood Brothers (1983) was written during Thatcher's Britain when class divisions were deepening — the musical's theme of class determining fate reflects this specific political moment. Understanding this context reveals the play's social purpose.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: context and social/historical influences

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain how the social and historical context of a play you have studied shapes its meaning. How might a modern production use context to make the play relevant to a contemporary audience? <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify the play's original context precisely; explain how context shapes specific dramatic choices; show how context influences character, theme and structure; consider how a modern production might reinterpret the context; evaluate the impact of context change on meaning; use specific examples throughout. Mark scheme: Context questions test AO1 (knowledge of the play's background) and AO2 (analysing how context shapes meaning). In both ...

Exam Tips: Always consider BOTH the original context AND the production's context | Context is not background — it SHAPES meaning | Use context to explain WHY the playwright made specific choices | Modern productions may deliberately change context — analyse WHY
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Context is just background information that you mention in the introduction — it doesn't really affect how you analyse the play. Correct: Context is ESSENTIAL to meaning. The playwright's choices are shaped by their social, historical and political circumstances. A play about class written in 1945 has different meaning than one written in 2025. Context explains WHY characters behave as they do, WHY the playwright includes certain events, and WHAT message they intended to communicate.
