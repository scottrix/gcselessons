LW3.3: Legal Profession and Access to Justice

Foundation Higher All Boards

Training, regulation, legal aid, pro bono, conditional fees, diversity, technology, reform

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA

Lesson 1: Legal Principles and Authority

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Case Name Recall

Rapid fire: teacher gives principle, students name the case (or vice versa) for Legal Profession and Access to Justice.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Cover the black-letter law for Legal Profession and Access to Justice:
  • Statutory provisions: key sections, definitions, elements
  • Leading cases: facts, ratio decidendi, obiter dicta, hierarchy
  • Legal tests and frameworks (e.g., Caparo, Caldwell, Woollin)
  • Defences, exceptions, and limitations
  • Relationship between statute and common law
Case Analysis

Detailed analysis of a leading case for Legal Profession and Access to Justice. Identify: material facts, legal issue, ratio, subsequent treatment.

Common Misconception

"All obiter dicta are binding"

Only ratio decidendi is binding. Obiter dicta may be persuasive but never binding. Distinguish carefully when reading cases.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide case summary cards with key facts/ratio pre-filled. Focus on 2-3 key cases. Use flowcharts for legal tests.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Analyse dissenting judgments. Trace doctrinal development through case law. Consider academic commentary and law reform proposals.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Ratio Extraction

Give students a short case extract. They identify the ratio in one sentence.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Accurate statement of law, correct case citations, identifies ratio
  • Getting there: Knows main principles but imprecise on case details or ratio
  • Not yet: Confuses cases, misstates law, cannot identify ratio

Lesson 2: Statutory Interpretation and Legal Reasoning

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Interpretation Exercise

Present a short statutory provision with ambiguous wording. Students suggest possible meanings.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Apply interpretation to Legal Profession and Access to Justice:
  • Literal, golden, mischief, purposive rules
  • Intrinsic and extrinsic aids to interpretation
  • Human Rights Act 1998 s.3: reading down compatibility
  • Presumptions: against retrospectivity, against ousting jurisdiction, etc.
  • Applying statutory provisions to problem facts
Statutory Application

Work through a problem scenario requiring interpretation of a key statute in Legal Profession and Access to Justice. Model the reasoning process.

Extended Problem Question

IRAC Practice: Multi-issue problem question on Legal Profession and Access to Justice. Requires: identifying issues, stating rules (statute + case law), applying to facts, concluding. 20 marks.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide IRAC template with prompts. Use simpler fact patterns. Focus on one interpretation rule at a time.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Compare domestic interpretation with EU/ECtHR approaches. Consider purposive vs textualist debates. Draft a legislative amendment to resolve ambiguity.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Rule Spotting

Quick fire: teacher reads fact pattern snippet, students identify which interpretation rule applies.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Correctly identifies issues, states rules accurately, applies logically, clear conclusion
  • Getting there: Identifies most issues, reasonable application, conclusion follows
  • Not yet: Misses key issues, misstates rules, application weak or missing

Lesson 3: Problem Questions and Exam Technique

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Issue Spotting Race

Give a problem question on Legal Profession and Access to Justice. Pairs list all legal issues in 2 minutes. Most comprehensive wins.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Master problem question technique for Legal Profession and Access to Justice:
  • IRAC/ILAC structure in depth
  • Fact identification: material vs immaterial facts
  • Authority selection: binding vs persuasive, primary vs secondary
  • Counter-arguments and distinguishing cases
  • Time management: planning, writing, reviewing
  • Common pitfalls: description over analysis, missing steps, fence-sitting
Model Answer Deconstruction

Analyse a high-scoring model answer for Legal Profession and Access to Justice. Highlight IRAC structure, authority use, evaluation.

Extended Timed Practice

Exam conditions: 25-minute problem question on Legal Profession and Access to Justice. Self/peer mark using examiner-style mark scheme.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide structured plan template. Extra time. Focus on one clear issue with full IRAC. Use open-book for first attempt.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Complex multi-party, multi-issue problem. Require policy analysis. Write a "perfect" answer then critique it. Peer teach the structure.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Mark Scheme Review

Students mark their own/peer's answer against simplified mark scheme. Identify one target for next time.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Complete IRAC for all issues, accurate law, strong application, decisive conclusions
  • Getting there: Good structure, mostly accurate, some application gaps
  • Not yet: Descriptive, misses issues, weak application, no clear conclusion

Homework Suggestions

  • Create summary notes or flashcards for key concepts and studies
  • Complete practice exam questions on this topic
  • Research one key study or theorist in more depth
  • Prepare a mind map linking this topic to other areas of the specification
