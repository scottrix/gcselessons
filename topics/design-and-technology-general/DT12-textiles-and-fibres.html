Lesson 2: Core Concepts: textiles and fibres

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on textiles and fibres. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Natural fibres: cotton (breathable, absorbent, soft), wool (warm, elastic, fire-resistant), silk (strong, lustrous, drapes well), linen (cool, crisp, creases easily).
Key Fact: Synthetic fibres: polyester (durable, quick-dry, wrinkle-resistant), nylon (strong, abrasion-resistant), elastane/Lycra (extreme stretch and recovery).
Key Fact: Woven fabrics: warp and weft interlaced at 90 degrees - stable, strong, limited stretch. Plain weave, twill weave (denim), satin weave.
Key Fact: Knitted fabrics: interlocking loops - stretchy, comfortable, can ladder. Weft knit (t-shirts) vs warp knit (more stable, swimwear).
Key Fact: Blended fabrics: combine natural and synthetic - polycotton (breathability + durability), cotton-lycra (comfort + stretch). Blends optimise properties and reduce cost.
D&T Exam Tips: For textile questions: 1) Identify fibre type, 2) State properties, 3) Explain fabric construction, 4) Link to garment requirements, 5) Consider care, cost and sustainability.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why polycotton is used for school shirts instead of 100% cotton or 100% polyester.

Answer: 100% cotton: comfortable but creases badly, shrinks. 100% polyester: durable and wrinkle-free but feels clammy, does not breathe. Polycotton (typically 65/35): retains cotton's breathability, gains polyester's durability and crease resistance, ideal for school uniforms.

Lesson 3: Application: textiles and fibres

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why polycotton is used for school shirts instead of 100% cotton or 100% polyester.

Answer: 100% cotton: comfortable but creases badly, shrinks. 100% polyester: durable and wrinkle-free but feels clammy, does not breathe. Polycotton (typically 65/35): retains cotton's breathability, gains polyester's durability and crease resistance, ideal for school uniforms.

Q2: Compare woven and knitted fabric constructions, explaining why each suits different garments.

Answer: Woven: warp + weft at 90 degrees, stable, holds shape - suits structured garments (shirts, trousers). Knitted: interlocking loops, stretchy, comfortable - suits body-hugging garments (t-shirts, socks, sportswear).

Q3: Discuss the environmental impact of synthetic fibres compared to natural fibres.

Answer: Synthetic: petrochemicals (non-renewable), microplastic shedding, non-biodegradable, but durable and recyclable. Natural: renewable, biodegradable, but cotton needs vast water and pesticides. Neither is perfectly sustainable - blended fabrics are hardest to recycle.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: textiles and fibres

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A sportswear company is designing a running top. Evaluate 100% cotton, 100% polyester, and a cotton-polyester blend, recommending the best choice. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify requirements (moisture-wicking, lightweight, quick-drying); 100% cotton - absorbs sweat, heavy when wet; 100% polyester - wicks moisture, quick-drying, retains shape; cotton-poly blend - compromise but not enough wicking. Conclude: 100% polyester (or polyester-elastane blend) for performance running wear.

Exam Tips: Know the difference between fibre (raw strand), yarn (spun fibres) and fabric (woven/knitted) | Specify the fibre type - 'polyester' not just 'synthetic' | Blend rationale: explain which property each fibre contributes to the blend
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Natural fibres are always more environmentally friendly than synthetic fibres. Correct: Cotton needs approx 10,000 litres of water per kg, heavy pesticide use. Polyester uses less water but consumes fossil fuels and sheds microplastics. A full LCA shows neither is clearly superior - organic cotton reduces pesticides; recycled polyester reduces fossil fuel dependence.
