Lesson 2: Core Concepts: investigating design contexts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on investigating design contexts. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Design brief: a concise statement of the problem to be solved - identifies the user, the need and the context. Not a solution - a starting point.
Key Fact: Design specification: a list of measurable criteria the product must meet - function, target user, dimensions, materials, cost, safety, aesthetics, environmental impact. Must be testable.
Key Fact: User research: interviews, questionnaires, observation, focus groups - reveals real needs, not assumptions. Ask open questions, observe users in context, identify pain points.
Key Fact: Market research: analyse competing products - features, price points, materials, strengths, weaknesses. Identify gaps in the market and opportunities for innovation.
Key Fact: Existing product analysis: dissect and evaluate similar products - how do they work? What materials? What manufacturing process? What could be improved?
D&T Exam Tips: For design context questions: 1) Identify the user and their needs, 2) Write a focused design brief (not a solution), 3) Create measurable specification points, 4) Choose appropriate research methods.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Write a design brief and three specification points for a product that helps elderly people open jar lids.

Answer: Brief: 'Design and make a device that enables elderly users with reduced grip strength to open vacuum-sealed jar lids safely and independently.' Specification: 1) Must accommodate jar diameters from 50-100 mm. 2) Must require no more than 20 N of grip force. 3) Must be manufacturable for under 8 GBP retail cost.

Lesson 3: Application: investigating design contexts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Write a design brief and three specification points for a product that helps elderly people open jar lids.

Answer: Brief: 'Design and make a device that enables elderly users with reduced grip strength to open vacuum-sealed jar lids safely and independently.' Specification: 1) Must accommodate jar diameters from 50-100 mm. 2) Must require no more than 20 N of grip force. 3) Must be manufacturable for under 8 GBP retail cost.

Q2: Explain the difference between a design brief and a design specification.

Answer: Design brief: a short statement describing the PROBLEM to solve - who, what, context. Does NOT specify the solution. Design specification: a detailed, measurable list of CRITERIA the final product must meet. The brief asks 'what is the problem?'; the specification answers 'what must the solution achieve?'

Q3: A designer wants to create a new type of school bag. Describe three research methods they should use and explain what each would reveal.

Answer: 1) User interviews with students - reveal what they carry, pain points, preferences. 2) Observation of students using bags - reveals actual behaviour vs what they say. 3) Competitor analysis - identifies feature gaps, price points, common failure points.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: investigating design contexts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A company has identified that cyclists struggle to carry groceries on their bicycles. Write a design brief, produce a specification with at least six measurable points, and describe two research methods. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: Brief: 'Design and make a device that enables urban cyclists to safely carry up to 10 kg of groceries on a standard commuter bicycle without affecting stability.' Specification: 1) Carry up to 10 kg. 2) Fit bicycles with 20-28 inch wheels without tools. 3) Maintain stability at 15 mph. 4) Weather-resistant (IPX4). 5) Retail cost under 40 GBP. 6) Device weight under 1.5 kg when empty. Research: 1) Observation at supermarkets. 2) Interviews with cyclists about grocery volumes and pain points.

Exam Tips: A design brief states the PROBLEM, not the solution - 'design a storage device' not 'design a shelf' | Specification points must be MEASURABLE - include numbers, sizes, weights, costs | Always link research methods to the specific information they will reveal
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: A design specification should describe exactly what the product will look like so the designer knows what to make. Correct: A specification defines what the product must ACHIEVE, not what it looks like. 'The product must hold at least 5 kg of books' is a specification point (testable, measurable). 'The product will be a blue wooden shelf with three compartments' is a design decision, not a specification. Specifying the solution too early constrains creativity.
