Lesson 2: Core Concepts: papers and boards

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on papers and boards. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Cartridge paper: 120-300 gsm, textured surface, good for sketching and marker work.
Key Fact: Corrugated board: two liner layers with fluted medium - excellent crush resistance, lightweight, used for packaging.
Key Fact: Foam-core board: polystyrene foam sandwiched between card layers - very lightweight, rigid, easy to cut; used for architectural models.
Key Fact: Solid white board: high-quality bleached board, 200+ gsm - smooth surface for printing and packaging.
Key Fact: Tracing paper: translucent, 40-90 gsm - used for overlay work, developing ideas, transferring designs.
D&T Exam Tips: When justifying paper/board choices, use SFP: Surface (smooth/textured), Function (rigidity, foldability, protection), Process (printing method, die-cutting compatibility). Always match the material to the specific product requirements.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why corrugated board is used for packaging rather than solid cardboard of the same weight.

Answer: Corrugated board's fluted structure creates air gaps between liners - this gives excellent compressive strength and cushioning while remaining lightweight. Solid cardboard of the same weight would be thinner and offer no cushioning.

Lesson 3: Application: papers and boards

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why corrugated board is used for packaging rather than solid cardboard of the same weight.

Answer: Corrugated board's fluted structure creates air gaps between liners - this gives excellent compressive strength and cushioning while remaining lightweight. Solid cardboard of the same weight would be thinner and offer no cushioning.

Q2: A designer needs to create a 3D architectural model. Justify the choice of foam-core board over corrugated board.

Answer: Foam-core: very smooth surface, extremely rigid for its weight, cuts cleanly with a craft knife, accepts pins. Corrugated board: visible fluting on edges, less refined. For architectural models, foam-core's clean finish and rigidity make it superior.

Q3: Compare the suitability of cartridge paper and layout paper for producing a final design presentation.

Answer: Cartridge paper (120-300 gsm): thick enough to stand alone, textured surface holds ink well, professional. Layout paper (50-60 gsm): thin, semi-transparent - for rough layout work, not final output.

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: papers and boards

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A company is designing packaging for a luxury perfume bottle. Justify the choice of board materials for both the inner protective insert and the outer presentation box. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify inner insert requirements (cushioning, precise cut-out for bottle, lightweight, no scratching); select foam-core or moulded pulp board. Outer box requirements (rigidity, premium surface, clean fold lines); select solid white board or rigid box board (500+ gsm) with wrap-around print.

Exam Tips: Always specify the GSM when discussing paper weight | Link board structure to function - explain WHY corrugated's fluting provides strength | Know the difference between paper (below 200 gsm) and board (above 200 gsm)
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Thicker paper is always better because it is stronger and more professional. Correct: Thickness must suit the function: tissue paper is perfect for delicate gift wrap; tracing paper needs translucency; cartridge paper needs weight and texture. Over-specifying GSM wastes material and increases cost.
