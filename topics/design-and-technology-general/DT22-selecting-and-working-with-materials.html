Lesson 2: Core Concepts: selecting and working with materials

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on selecting and working with materials. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Timber working: tenon saw (straight cuts), coping saw (curves), chisel (paring and mortising), plane (flat surfaces), file/sandpaper (smooth finish). Jointing: PVA glue, screws, dowels, dovetails, mortise-and-tenon.
Key Fact: Metal working: hacksaw (cutting bar/flat stock), pillar drill (accurate holes), file (shaping and deburring), sheet metal snips (cutting thin sheet), tap and die (internal/external threads). Joining: soldering, brazing, welding, rivets, nuts and bolts.
Key Fact: Polymer working: laser cutter (2D profiles from sheet acrylic/MDF), line bender (folds acrylic along a heated element), strip heater, drill (slow speed to avoid melting). Joining: acrylic cement (capillary action), solvent weld (PVC), ultrasonic welding, snap-fit joints.
Key Fact: Workshop safety: tie hair back, no loose clothing, safety goggles for all cutting/drilling, clamp work in a vice, use push sticks on machinery, know emergency stop positions.
Key Fact: Material preparation: measure twice cut once, mark out with scribe (metal) or pencil/marking gauge (timber), allow for material removed by the saw blade (kerf), file edges to remove burrs.
D&T Exam Tips: For practical process questions: 1) Name the tool/process, 2) Describe the steps in order, 3) State safety precautions, 4) Name the material it is suitable for, 5) Explain why this process is appropriate.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe how to bend a 3 mm acrylic sheet to create a 90-degree stand using a line bender.

Answer: 1) Mark the bend line on the protective film. 2) Place acrylic over the line bender's heating element with the bend line directly above it. 3) Heat for 2-3 minutes until flexible. 4) Bend to 90 degrees against a former or jig. 5) Hold until the plastic cools and hardens (do not quench in water - causes stress cracking). 6) Remove protective film.

Lesson 3: Application: selecting and working with materials

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe how to bend a 3 mm acrylic sheet to create a 90-degree stand using a line bender.

Answer: 1) Mark the bend line on the protective film. 2) Place acrylic over the line bender's heating element with the bend line directly above it. 3) Heat for 2-3 minutes until flexible. 4) Bend to 90 degrees against a former or jig. 5) Hold until the plastic cools and hardens (do not quench in water - causes stress cracking). 6) Remove protective film.

Q2: Explain the difference between soldering and brazing, and when each would be used.

Answer: Soldering: joins metals using a filler alloy melting below 450 degrees C. Used for electrical connections, circuit boards, plumbing - low strength, low temperature. Brazing: joins metals using a filler (brass alloy) melting above 450 degrees C (typically 700-900 degrees C). Used for structural joints in bicycle frames, furniture - much stronger than soldering.

Q3: State four workshop safety rules when using a pillar drill.

Answer: 1) Always clamp the workpiece in a vice - never hold by hand. 2) Wear safety goggles - swarf can fly from the drill bit. 3) Use the correct drill speed - slow for large bits in metal, faster for small bits in wood. 4) Ease pressure on the drill as the bit breaks through the underside.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: selecting and working with materials

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response You need to make a 3 mm acrylic display stand. Describe the complete making process from sheet material to finished product, including cutting, bending, joining and finishing, with appropriate safety precautions. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: 1) Marking out: draw the net on the acrylic's protective film. 2) Cutting: laser cut from 3 mm cast acrylic - ensure ventilation is on (fume extraction). 3) Bending: use a line bender - heat the bend line, bend to the required angle using a former, hold until cool. Safety: keep fingers away from the heating element. 4) Joining: apply acrylic cement (Tensol) using a fine brush - capillary action draws the solvent into the joint. Hold for 30 seconds. Safety: well-ventilated area, avoid skin contact. 5) Finishing: remove protective film, polish edges with wet-and-dry paper (240, 400, 600 grit), flame-polish edges with a blowtorch. Safety: keep blowtorch away from flammable materials.

Exam Tips: Describe processes step by step in chronological order - marks for sequence accuracy | Always include safety precautions when describing workshop processes | Know the difference between permanent joints (weld, solder, glue) and non-permanent (screws, bolts, nuts)
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: A laser cutter can cut through any material including thick metal. Correct: Laser cutters used in schools typically have 40-60 W CO2 tubes that can cut: acrylic (up to approx 10 mm), MDF/plywood (up to approx 6 mm), card, paper, fabric. They CANNOT cut: metals (CO2 laser wavelength reflects off metal surfaces), PVC (releases toxic chlorine gas), polycarbonate (discolours and produces fumes). Material choice for laser cutting is restricted - always check compatibility.
