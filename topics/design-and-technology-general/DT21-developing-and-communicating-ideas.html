Lesson 2: Core Concepts: developing and communicating ideas

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on developing and communicating ideas. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Freehand sketching: quick, expressive, captures ideas rapidly - the primary tool for initial concept generation. Annotations explain function, materials and dimensions.
Key Fact: Rendering: adding tone, texture and colour to sketches - creates realistic appearance, shows material properties, communicates design intent to clients.
Key Fact: CAD (Computer-Aided Design): 2D (vector for laser cutting), 3D (solid modelling for visualisation, FEA, 3D printing). Precision, easy modification, virtual testing, output for CAM.
Key Fact: Orthographic projection: 2D technical drawings showing front, side and plan views - precise, scaled, used for manufacture. Third-angle projection (UK standard).
Key Fact: Exploded views: show how components fit together - parts separated along assembly axes with lines showing connections. Essential for assembly instructions.
D&T Exam Tips: For communication questions: 1) Name the drawing/technique, 2) Describe its features, 3) Explain its purpose, 4) Compare with alternatives, 5) Justify its use for the specific context.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why designers begin with freehand sketches before moving to CAD, rather than going straight to computer modelling.

Answer: Freehand sketching is fast and fluid - designers can generate 10-20 concepts in the time it takes to model one in CAD. Sketching allows rapid exploration without software constraints. CAD is used later to refine the best concept with precision.

Lesson 3: Application: developing and communicating ideas

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why designers begin with freehand sketches before moving to CAD, rather than going straight to computer modelling.

Answer: Freehand sketching is fast and fluid - designers can generate 10-20 concepts in the time it takes to model one in CAD. Sketching allows rapid exploration without software constraints. CAD is used later to refine the best concept with precision.

Q2: Describe the key features of an orthographic projection drawing and explain why it is used for manufacturing.

Answer: Orthographic projection shows the object in 2D from multiple perpendicular views: front elevation, side elevation, plan. Drawn to scale, true dimensions, no perspective distortion, includes dimension lines, material specifications. Used for manufacture because it provides precise, unambiguous information.

Q3: A designer needs to show how a retractable pen assembles. Which drawing type is most appropriate and why?

Answer: An exploded view is most appropriate - it shows each component separated along the axis of assembly, with lines indicating how parts connect. This clearly communicates the assembly sequence, which neither orthographic (external form only) nor rendered perspective (does not reveal internal parts) can do effectively.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: developing and communicating ideas

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response You are designing a desk lamp. Produce an annotated freehand sketch of one concept, then explain how you would develop this idea using CAD, including two specific advantages CAD provides. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: produce a sketch showing the lamp's form (base, articulated arm, shade), annotate with materials (weighted steel base, aluminium arm, polymer shade), dimensions (base 200 mm, arm reach 400 mm), function (adjustable arm, touch dimmer). CAD development: 1) Parametric modelling - adjust proportions, test geometry. 2) FEA - simulate stress on arm joint, simulate thermal performance of LED housing.

Exam Tips: Always annotate sketches - an unannotated sketch scores minimal marks | Know the UK convention: third-angle projection | Explain the PURPOSE of each drawing type - when would you use it and why?
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: CAD has replaced sketching because it is more precise and professional, so sketching is no longer important. Correct: CAD and sketching are complementary, not competing. Sketching excels at rapid concept generation. CAD excels at precision, modification, virtual testing and manufacturing output. The design process moves from sketching (divergent - many ideas) to CAD (convergent - refining the best idea). A designer who skips sketching produces fewer, less creative solutions.
