Lesson 2: Core Concepts: new and emerging technologies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on new and emerging technologies. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Industry 4.0: the Fourth Industrial Revolution — cyber-physical systems, IoT, cloud computing and automation transforming manufacturing.
Key Fact: 3D printing (additive manufacturing): builds objects layer by layer from CAD models — rapid prototyping, custom medical implants, reduced waste.
Key Fact: Robotics: automated systems replacing repetitive tasks — increases consistency, reduces labour costs, raises ethical concerns about job losses.
Key Fact: Internet of Things (IoT): everyday objects connected to the internet — smart homes, wearable tech, data-driven design decisions.
Key Fact: Artificial Intelligence in design: generative design software explores thousands of solutions — optimises for weight, strength, material use.
D&T Exam Tips: When evaluating new technology, use the SEE framework: Social impacts (jobs, lifestyle), Economic impacts (cost, productivity), Environmental impacts (waste, energy). Always conclude with a justified recommendation.

Lesson 3: Application: new and emerging technologies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain how 3D printing has changed the prototype development process in product design.

Answer: 3D printing allows designers to create physical prototypes directly from CAD files in hours rather than weeks; it enables iterative testing, reduces material waste compared to subtractive methods, and allows complex geometries that CNC cannot produce.

Q2: Discuss the social and economic impacts of increasing automation in manufacturing.

Answer: Positive: increased productivity, consistent quality, reduced hazardous work for humans. Negative: job displacement, skills gap, initial investment costs, dependence on technology. A balanced answer considers both sides with specific examples.

Q3: Evaluate how the Internet of Things could influence the design of a domestic kettle.

Answer: An IoT kettle could: be controlled remotely via smartphone, learn user habits for energy efficiency, alert users to limescale build-up via sensors, integrate with smart-home energy monitoring. Evaluation should weigh convenience against cost, privacy and reliability concerns.

Error Review

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: new and emerging technologies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A company is considering replacing its traditional assembly line with robotic automation. Evaluate this decision, considering social, economic and environmental factors. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: define robotic automation and its capabilities; present social arguments (job losses vs new skilled roles, worker safety); economic arguments (high initial cost vs long-term savings, 24/7 operation, consistency); environmental arguments (energy use of robots vs human labour, reduced waste from errors); reach a justified conclusion considering the company's specific context — e.g. high-volume, precision manufacturing may justify automation whereas small-batch artisan production may not.

Exam Tips: Always link technology to a specific product or context — never write about technology in the abstract | Consider BOTH benefits AND drawbacks — exam questions often ask you to evaluate | Use precise terminology: 'additive manufacturing' not just '3D printing' for higher marks
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: New technology is always better than traditional methods and will completely replace manual manufacturing. Correct: New technology complements traditional methods — 3D printing suits complex low-volume parts but injection moulding remains cheaper for mass production. Designers choose the most appropriate technology for the context.
