Lesson 2: Core Concepts: ferrous and non-ferrous metals

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on ferrous and non-ferrous metals. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Ferrous metals contain iron - magnetic, prone to rust (except stainless). Mild steel: low carbon, ductile, weldable. Stainless steel: chromium content prevents corrosion - cutlery, medical instruments.
Key Fact: Cast iron: high carbon (2-4%), excellent compressive strength, self-lubricating - engine blocks, manhole covers.
Key Fact: Aluminium: lightweight (1/3 steel's density), corrosion-resistant (oxide layer), excellent conductor - aircraft, cans, window frames.
Key Fact: Copper: excellent electrical and thermal conductor, ductile, malleable, antimicrobial - wiring, plumbing, PCBs.
Key Fact: Brass: copper-zinc alloy - gold appearance, corrosion resistant, low friction, machinable - decorative hardware, valves, musical instruments.
D&T Exam Tips: For metal questions: 1) Classify as ferrous/non-ferrous, 2) State key properties with data, 3) Link to product requirements, 4) Consider surface treatments (galvanising, anodising).

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why stainless steel is used for surgical instruments whereas mild steel is not, despite both being ferrous metals.

Answer: Stainless steel has 10.5%+ chromium which forms a passive chromium oxide layer - self-healing, prevents rust even when scratched. Mild steel has no chromium and rusts rapidly.

Lesson 3: Application: ferrous and non-ferrous metals

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: D&amp;T Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why stainless steel is used for surgical instruments whereas mild steel is not, despite both being ferrous metals.

Answer: Stainless steel has 10.5%+ chromium which forms a passive chromium oxide layer - self-healing, prevents rust even when scratched. Mild steel has no chromium and rusts rapidly.

Q2: Justify the use of aluminium rather than steel for aircraft fuselage construction.

Answer: Aluminium: density 2.7 g/cm3 vs steel 7.8 - 1/3 the weight, crucial for fuel efficiency; corrosion-resistant; good strength-to-weight when alloyed. Trade-off: more expensive and less stiff.

Q3: A product designer needs a gold-coloured, corrosion-resistant material for a door handle. Compare brass and gold-anodised aluminium.

Answer: Brass: genuine gold appearance, naturally corrosion-resistant, heavy and tactile (premium feel), low friction, antimicrobial. Gold-anodised aluminium: lighter, cheaper, coating can scratch to reveal silver beneath. For a door handle: brass is superior.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: ferrous and non-ferrous metals

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A company is designing a lightweight camping stove. Compare aluminium and mild steel for the main body, justifying your recommendation with reference to at least four properties. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: compare Density (aluminium 2.7 vs steel 7.8 g/cm3), Thermal conductivity (205 vs 50 W/mK), Corrosion (aluminium oxide layer vs steel rusts), Strength (steel stronger but aluminium thicker walls can match strength while remaining lighter). Conclusion: aluminium for the main body.

Exam Tips: Ferrous = contains iron (think ferrum = Latin for iron) | Know that stainless steel is ferrous but does not rust - explain WHY (chromium oxide layer) | State alloy composition: brass = copper + zinc, bronze = copper + tin
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: All metals are magnetic because they are metals. Correct: Only ferrous metals (containing iron) are magnetic - mild steel, cast iron. Non-ferrous metals (aluminium, copper, brass, gold, silver) are NOT magnetic. Some stainless steel grades are weakly magnetic due to crystal structure.
