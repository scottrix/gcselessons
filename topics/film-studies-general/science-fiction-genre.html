Lesson 2: Core Concepts: science fiction genre

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on science fiction genre. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Science fiction is a genre that explores imagined futures, alternative realities and the consequences of scientific and technological advancement.
Key Fact: Core sci-fi themes include the relationship between humanity and technology, encounters with the alien or other, dystopian societies and the limits of knowledge.
Key Fact: Sci-fi iconography includes spaceships, futuristic cityscapes, advanced technology interfaces, alien creatures, robots and space suits.
Key Fact: Sci-fi settings range from deep space and alien planets to near-future Earth, dystopian cities and virtual reality environments.
Key Fact: Hard science fiction prioritises scientific accuracy and plausible extrapolation, while soft science fiction focuses on social and psychological themes.
Key Fact: Dystopian narratives are a major sci-fi sub-genre that imagine oppressive future societies as warnings about contemporary social and political trends.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How does your chosen science fiction film use genre conventions to explore ideas about humanity? <div class="

The film uses sci-fi conventions to interrogate what it means to be human with a depth that realism could not achieve. The dystopian cityscape — rain-drenched, polluted, with towering neon-lit buildings and perpetual darkness — establishes a world where humanity has degraded its environment and itself. The iconography of advanced technology alongside urban decay encodes the central tension: technological progress has not brought human progress. The artificial humans are the film's most powerful device. Conventionally, sci-fi uses them as threats, but here they display more emotion, more desire for life and more moral complexity than the cold, detached humans. The most powerful speech in the film comes from an artificial being expressing the most profoundly human moment. The test designed to distinguish human from artificial becomes an ironic motif: it measures empathy, yet the humans who administer it show none. Exam Tips: Always connect sci-fi's speculative elements to the real-world concerns they reflect — examiners want to see this link made explicit. | Use specific iconographic examples from your set films rather than discussing sci-fi iconography in general. | Identify which sub-genre your film belongs to and explain how it uses or subverts that sub-genre's conventions. | Consider the historical context of your film's production — what anxieties were current when it was made? | Discuss how sci-fi's visual design — special effects, production design, cinematography — contributes to its meaning.
Common Errors: ✗ Discussing sci-fi as purely about the future without connecting it to contemporary social concerns. ✓ Make the link: 'The dystopian surveillance state reflects post-9/11 anxieties about government monitoring and the erosion of civil liberties.' ✗ Listing sci-fi iconography without explaining its thematic significance. ✓ Analyse meaning: 'The towering corporate architecture visually encodes the dominance of corporate power over individual agency.' ✗ Confusing hard sci-fi and soft sci-fi, or treating all sci-fi as one undifferentiated category. ✓ Distinguish: 'The film belongs to the social sci-fi tradition, exploring questions of identity and empathy rather than scientific plausibility.' ✗
