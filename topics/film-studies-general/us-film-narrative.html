Lesson 2: Core Concepts: us film narrative

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on us film narrative. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Classical Hollywood narrative is the dominant storytelling model in US cinema, characterised by cause-and-effect logic, goal-oriented protagonists, narrative closure and invisible filmmaking style.
Key Fact: The three-act structure divides the narrative into setup (establishing characters and conflict), confrontation (complications and obstacles) and resolution (climax and closure).
Key Fact: Cause-and-effect logic means that events in the narrative are driven by character decisions and actions rather than coincidence, creating a sense of narrative necessity and coherence.
Key Fact: The protagonist in classical Hollywood is typically an active, goal-driven individual whose desires define the narrative trajectory and whose actions propel the plot forward.
Key Fact: Narrative closure resolves the central conflict and answers the story's key questions, providing the audience with a satisfying sense of completion and emotional resolution.
Key Fact: Binary oppositions — such as good versus evil, order versus chaos, individual versus society — provide the structural tension that drives the narrative forward.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How does your chosen US film use narrative structure to create meaning for the audience? <div class="

The film uses narrative structure deliberately to shape the audience's emotional and intellectual engagement, both following and departing from classical Hollywood conventions to create a layered viewing experience. At the macro level, the film adheres to a three-act structure: the setup establishes the protagonist within a specific social context and introduces the central conflict; the confrontation presents escalating obstacles that test the protagonist's resolve; the resolution delivers a climactic confrontation followed by a new equilibrium. This structure provides the satisfying arc that audiences expect, ensuring emotional investment. However, within this classical framework, the film introduces significant deviations. The cause-and-effect logic that typically drives Hollywood narrative is complicated by an unreliable protagonist whose motivations are gradually revealed to be more ambiguous than initially presented. Enigma codes are established in the opening sequence — questions about the protagonist's past — that are not fully resolved, resisting the classical imperative of closure.
