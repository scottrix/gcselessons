Lesson 2: Core Concepts: film scene analysis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on film scene analysis. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Film scene analysis requires systematic examination of how micro features including cinematography, mise-en-scene, editing and sound create meaning and generate response.
Key Fact: Begin by watching the extract at least twice: first for overall impression and narrative content, then for detailed identification of specific film language techniques.
Key Fact: Cinematography analysis covers shot type, camera angle, camera movement, composition, focus and lighting, each of which contributes to meaning and audience positioning.
Key Fact: Mise-en-scene analysis examines setting, props, costume, performance, hairstyle and makeup, considering how each element has been deliberately chosen to communicate meaning.
Key Fact: Sound analysis distinguishes diegetic from non-diegetic, on-screen from off-screen, and considers dialogue, music, sound effects and silence as meaning-making tools.
Key Fact: Editing analysis considers shot duration, transitions, continuity techniques, parallel editing and the rhythm created by cutting patterns within the sequence.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How should you analyse a film extract to demonstrate understanding of how micro features create meaning? <div class="

Effective extract analysis begins with careful viewing: watch at least twice, first absorbing the overall effect and narrative, then noting specific micro features with precision. Identify key moments where film language is most significant rather than attempting to discuss every shot. For each moment, examine how cinematography, mise-en-scene, sound and editing combine to create meaning. Use the PEAL structure for each paragraph: make a clear interpretive point, provide specific evidence from the extract, analyse how the technique creates meaning or positions the audience, and link back to the question. For example, when a character is isolated in a wide shot with empty space around them, bare setting, diegetic wind sound and slow editing pace, these features work together to construct loneliness and vulnerability. The wide shot reduces the character to a small figure, mise-en-scene removes comfort and warmth, sound emphasises emptiness, and slow cutting prolongs the discomfort. A strong analysis builds from these individual observations towards an overall reading, showing how the cumulative effect of micro features shapes audience understanding and emotional response.
