Lesson 2: Core Concepts: command words &amp; question types

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on command words &amp; question types. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Command words tell you exactly what the examiner expects: 'describe' means give an account, 'explain' means give reasons, 'analyse' means break down and examine, 'evaluate' means make a judgement.
Key Fact: Describe questions require you to identify and outline film language features or production contexts without needing to explain their effect.
Key Fact: Explain questions require you to give reasons why something produces a particular effect or meaning, connecting technique to impact.
Key Fact: Analyse questions require you to break down a sequence or concept into its component parts and examine how they work together to create meaning.
Key Fact: Evaluate questions require you to make a judgement about effectiveness, supported by evidence, weighing strengths against weaknesses or comparing different interpretations.
Key Fact: The Eduqas C680QS exam uses a range of question types from short one-mark identification questions to extended 15-mark analytical essays.

A 15-mark evaluate question requires a structured, sustained argument that reaches a supported judgement about effectiveness. Begin by reading the question carefully and identifying exactly what you are being asked to evaluate. Spend two to three minutes planning: note your key points, the specific examples you will use and your overall judgement. Open with a clear statement that establishes your position in response to the question. Develop your argument through focused paragraphs, each making a distinct point supported by specific evidence from the film. For each point, identify a technique or element, explain how it creates meaning and evaluate its effectiveness, considering both strengths and potential limitations. Use precise film language terminology throughout, as the mark scheme rewards technical accuracy. Address alternative interpretations where relevant, showing that you can weigh different readings before reaching your conclusion. Ensure your conclusion directly answers the question, summarising your judgement and the key reasons for it.
