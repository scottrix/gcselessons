Lesson 2: Core Concepts: global film industries

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Bollywood is the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai, producing more films annually than Hollywood and characterised by musical numbers, melodrama and family-centred narratives.
Key Fact: Nollywood is the Nigerian film industry, one of the largest in the world by output, known for low-budget direct-to-video productions that reflect local cultures and social issues.
Key Fact: Chinese cinema encompasses mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan, with a history spanning martial arts cinema, Fifth Generation art films and contemporary blockbuster productions.
Key Fact: World film industries differ from Hollywood in their production contexts, funding models, distribution networks and relationships with domestic audiences.
Key Fact: Bollywood films typically run three hours with intermissions, blending song, dance, romance and action into a distinct narrative format rooted in Indian cultural traditions.
Key Fact: Nollywood films are produced rapidly on low budgets, often shot on digital video in weeks, and distributed through informal markets rather than traditional cinema chains.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How do the production and distribution contexts of a global film industry differ from those of Hollywood, and what impact does this have on the films produced? <div class="

Bollywood differs fundamentally from Hollywood in production and distribution, which directly shapes the films it produces. Bollywood films are financed through a mix of private investment, corporate sponsorship and increasingly studio backing, rather than the Hollywood studio system. Production budgets are typically lower, with resources concentrated on elaborate musical sequences and star salaries rather than visual effects. Distribution relies heavily on domestic and diaspora audiences, with India's single-screen cinemas and overseas territories forming the primary market rather than global multiplex chains. This industrial context produces films that prioritise broad emotional appeal, family-friendly narratives and spectacular song-and-dance numbers that serve as both entertainment and marketing tools. The three-hour runtime accommodates multiple narrative threads and musical interludes, reflecting the cultural expectation that cinema provides a complete entertainment experience. Unlike Hollywood's genre specialisation, Bollywood films blend romance, action, comedy and drama within single productions to satisfy diverse audience members watching together. Exam Tips: Always name specific film industries and, where possible, specific films as examples rather than making general statements about world cinema. | Compare global film industries with Hollywood to demonstrate understanding of different production, distribution and exhibition contexts. | Use the correct terminology: Bollywood refers specifically to Hindi-language cinema from Mumbai, not all Indian cinema. | When discussing Nollywood, emphasise its unique distribution model and rapid production process rather than judging it against Hollywood budgets. | Consider both the cultural significance and the industrial realities of global film industries in your answers.
Common Errors: ✗ Using 'Bollywood' to refer to all Indian cinema. ✓ Bollywood refers specifically to the Hindi-language industry in Mumbai; other industries include Tollywood (Telugu), Kollywood (Tamil) and others. ✗ Describing Nollywood as low-quality because of its low budgets. ✓ Nollywood's low budgets reflect a different production model, not inferior quality; its cultural relevance and audience reach demonstrate its significance. ✗ Treating Chinese cinema as a single, uniform industry. ✓ Chinese cinema encompasses mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan, each with distinct production contexts, censorship regimes and aesthetic traditions. ✗ Assuming all world film industries aspire to the Hollywood model.
