Lesson 2: Core Concepts: asteroids, comets &amp; meteoroids

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on asteroids, comets &amp; meteoroids. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Asteroids are rocky/metallic bodies, mostly found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter
Key Fact: Comets are icy bodies that develop a coma and tail when they approach the Sun
Key Fact: A comet has a nucleus (solid core), coma (gas/dust cloud) and two tails: ion tail (pointing directly away from the Sun) and dust tail (curved)
Key Fact: Short-period comets originate from the Kuiper belt; long-period comets from the Oort cloud
Key Fact: Meteoroids are small rocky/metallic bodies in space; meteors ('shooting stars') are the visible streaks when they enter the atmosphere; meteorites are fragments that reach the ground
Key Fact: Notable asteroids include Ceres (also a dwarf planet) and Vesta

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on asteroids, comets &amp; meteoroids from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Know the difference: meteoroid (in space), meteor (in atmosphere), meteorite (on ground) | Comet tails are caused by solar wind and radiation pressure — NOT by the comet's motion | Both exam boards test comet structure in detail | The Oort cloud is hypothetical — we infer it from long-period comet orbits
Common Errors: ✗ Comet tails are caused by the comet moving through space ✓ Comet tails are pushed away from the Sun by the solar wind (ion tail) and radiation pressure (dust tail) ✗ Asteroids are leftover pieces of a planet that exploded ✓ Asteroids are primordial material that never accreted into a planet, mainly due to Jupiter's gravitational perturbation ✗ Meteors and meteorites are the same thing ✓ A meteor is the light phenomenon in the atmosphere; a meteorite is the physical object that reaches the ground
