Lesson 2: Core Concepts: telescopes &amp; instruments

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on telescopes &amp; instruments. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Refracting telescopes use a convex objective lens to gather and focus light
Key Fact: Reflecting telescopes use a concave primary mirror; Newtonian and Cassegrain are common designs
Key Fact: Magnification = focal length of objective / focal length of eyepiece
Key Fact: Resolving power is the ability to distinguish fine detail; larger apertures give better resolution
Key Fact: Radio telescopes use large dish antennas to detect radio wavelengths; they can observe day and night
Key Fact: Space telescopes (e.g. Hubble, JWST) avoid atmospheric distortion and observe wavelengths blocked by the atmosphere

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: telescopes &amp; instruments

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on telescopes &amp; instruments from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Magnification formula is often tested — know M = f_obj / f_eye | Reflectors solve chromatic aberration — this is the #1 advantage | Space telescopes: no atmosphere = no distortion + access to blocked wavelengths | Both boards test telescope types and their comparative advantages
Common Errors: ✗ Higher magnification always gives better images ✓ Magnification without adequate aperture produces dim, blurry images; resolving power and light-gathering depend on aperture, not magnification ✗ Radio telescopes need clear skies like optical telescopes ✓ Radio telescopes can observe through clouds and during the day — radio waves pass through the atmosphere and are not affected by weather ✗ Space telescopes are always better than ground-based ✓ Space telescopes are expensive, limited in size and difficult to maintain; large ground-based telescopes with adaptive optics can be equally effective for many observations
