SPG2: Punctuation for Effect

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Use a range of punctuation marks accurately and for deliberate effect.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Apply spelling rules and patterns accurately
  • Use punctuation marks correctly and for effect
  • Construct grammatically accurate sentences
  • Write clear, well-organised paragraphs
  • Edit and proofread writing for accuracy

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of sentence structure
  • Familiarity with common spelling patterns

Materials & Equipment

  • Spelling rules reference sheets
  • Punctuation practice worksheets
  • Grammar exercises
  • Editing and proofreading checklists

Lesson 1: Essential Punctuation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Punctuation Pyramid

Rank punctuation marks from most to least important.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
  • Apostrophes: contraction (don't) and possession (the dog's bowl). Never for plurals.
  • Commas: lists, after front adverbials, before conjunctions in compound sentences.
  • Semicolons: link two related independent clauses. 'She loved reading; he preferred maths.'
  • Colons: introduce explanation or list. 'She had one goal: to win.'
  • Dashes: create emphasis or interruption. 'He was — and always would be — a fighter.'
  • Speech punctuation: new speaker = new line. Comma/full stop inside speech marks.
Worked Example

Speech: 'I'm going,' she said. / 'I'm going!' she shouted. / 'Look at this.' He pointed.

Practice Task

Punctuate these sentences with apostrophes, commas, and speech marks.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a punctuation checklist.
Stretch:
Rewrite a passage using semicolons and colons instead of full stops.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Punctuation Sort

Display unpunctuated sentences. Students add correct punctuation.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses all punctuation marks accurately
  • Getting there: Uses basics correctly but semicolons inconsistent
  • Not yet: Frequently misuses punctuation

Lesson 2: Punctuation for Effect

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Rhythm Reader

Read two versions of the same passage aloud. Which sounds better?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
  • Full stops for emphasis: 'She smiled. She had won.'
  • Semicolons for sophistication
  • Colons for anticipation: 'There was one thing she needed to do: run.'
  • Dashes for drama
  • Ellipsis for trailing off
  • One-word sentences for impact: 'Silence.' 'Gone.'
Worked Example

Before: 'The rain fell heavily. The streets were empty.'

After: 'The rain fell — not gently, not gradually — but in great, angry sheets.'

Practice Task

Rewrite this passage using at least one semicolon, colon, and dash.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a 'mark upgrade' worksheet.
Stretch:
Write a dramatic passage where punctuation alone creates tension.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Punctuation Audit

Students highlight all punctuation in their own writing.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses punctuation deliberately for effects
  • Getting there: Shows some variety but not always purposefully
  • Not yet: Uses only basic punctuation

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete a spelling or grammar worksheet on the topic covered
  • Practise proofreading a paragraph for errors
  • Write a short passage using the skills learned, then self-assess
