SPG4: Paragraphing

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Write clear, well-organised paragraphs with effective cohesion and linking.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Apply spelling rules and patterns accurately
  • Use punctuation marks correctly and for effect
  • Construct grammatically accurate sentences
  • Write clear, well-organised paragraphs
  • Edit and proofread writing for accuracy

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of sentence structure
  • Familiarity with common spelling patterns

Materials & Equipment

  • Spelling rules reference sheets
  • Punctuation practice worksheets
  • Grammar exercises
  • Editing and proofreading checklists

Lesson 1: Paragraph Structure

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Paragraph Sort

Give 6 sentences from two paragraphs. Sort them and explain.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
  • Topic sentence: first sentence, states main idea.
  • Supporting sentences: develop, explain, or evidence.
  • New paragraph when: new idea, speaker, time, place, or for emphasis.
  • Topic test: can you summarise the paragraph in one sentence?
Worked Example

Poor: 'I like dogs. Cats are nice. My favourite colour is blue.'

Improved: 'Dogs make the best pets because of their loyalty. Unlike cats, a dog will greet you at the door every day. My dog Buster waits by the window from 3pm each afternoon. It is this unwavering devotion that makes dogs special.'

Practice Task

Write a paragraph about the benefits of reading with clear topic sentence and development.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide paragraph planning frame.
Stretch:
Write two linked paragraphs where the second references the first.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Topic Sentence Challenge

Display paragraph without topic sentence. Students write the opening.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Writes clear paragraphs with focused topic sentences
  • Getting there: Paragraphs have general topic but lack focus
  • Not yet: Writing lacks paragraph structure

Lesson 2: Cohesion and Linking

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Linking Challenge

Display two disconnected paragraphs. Add linking words.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
  • Additive: furthermore, moreover, in addition
  • Contrast: however, nevertheless, on the other hand
  • Causal: therefore, consequently, as a result
  • Sequence: first, then, next, finally
  • Referencing: using pronouns to avoid repetition. 'The government announced the policy. They claimed...'
Worked Example

Disconnected: 'The town centre has declined. Online shopping is cheaper. The council should act.'

Connected: 'The town centre has declined significantly. One major cause is online shopping, which offers lower prices. As a result, footfall has dropped by 30%. Therefore, the council must act.'

Practice Task

Add cohesive devices: 'Exercise improves mental health. Many people do not exercise. Stress levels are rising. The government should invest in sports facilities.'

Differentiation

Support:
Provide cohesive devices word bank.
Stretch:
Write a passage using referencing to avoid repeating any noun.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Linking Word Bingo

Students tick off each type of cohesive device they've used.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses range of cohesive devices naturally
  • Getting there: Uses some linking words but they feel forced
  • Not yet: Writing lacks cohesion

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete a spelling or grammar worksheet on the topic covered
  • Practise proofreading a paragraph for errors
  • Write a short passage using the skills learned, then self-assess
