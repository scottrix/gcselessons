P7: Series and Parallel Circuits

Foundation Higher All Boards

Applying knowledge of current, voltage, and resistance to solve problems in series, parallel, and combined circuits.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Apply rules for current, voltage, and resistance in series circuits
  • Apply rules for current, voltage, and resistance in parallel circuits
  • Solve combined series-parallel circuit problems
  • Use the relationship between power, current, and voltage
  • Analyze circuit diagrams to predict component behaviour

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of Ohm's Law V = IR (P6)
  • Knowledge of series and parallel circuit rules (P6)
  • Ability to rearrange equations

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Component circuit kit
  • Ammeter and voltmeter
  • Rheostat
  • Power supply (12 V)

Lesson 1: Series Circuit Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Students write three rules for series circuits: (1) Current is the same everywhere, (2) Voltages add up, (3) Resistances add up. Then solve: R₁ = 5 Ω, R₂ = 10 Ω in series with 15 V. What is the current? (Answer: 1 A)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
This lesson develops systematic problem-solving for series circuits.

Step-by-step method:
1. Draw the circuit and label all known values
2. Identify whether components are in series or parallel
3. Apply the appropriate rule for the unknown quantity
4. Use V = IR where needed
5. Check your answer makes sense (e.g., voltages should add up to supply)

Key relationships:
- V = IR (applies to individual components and whole circuit)
- In series: same I, V splits, R adds
- Total power: P = V × I (total power = sum of power in each component)

Common problem types:
- Given total V and resistances, find current and individual voltages
- Given current and resistance, find voltage across a component
- Given V and current through one component, find resistance of another
Worked Example

Three resistors in series: R₁ = 2 Ω, R₂ = 3 Ω, R₃ = 5 Ω. Supply = 20 V.
(a) R_total = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 Ω
(b) I = V ÷ R_total = 20 ÷ 10 = 2 A
(c) V₁ = IR₁ = 2 × 2 = 4 V
(d) V₂ = IR₂ = 2 × 3 = 6 V
(e) V₃ = IR₃ = 2 × 5 = 10 V
(f) Check: 4 + 6 + 10 = 20 V ✓
(g) Power in R₂: P₂ = V₂ × I = 6 × 2 = 12 W

Key Formulae:
V = IR
R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
P = V × I = I²R = V²/R

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a flowchart for solving series circuit problems. Students follow the steps with scaffolded questions.
Stretch:
Given that a bulb has resistance 12 Ω and a resistor has 8 Ω in series with a 10 V supply, calculate the power dissipated in each component. Which component gets hotter?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Spotting

Show a worked solution with a deliberate error. Students identify and correct the mistake.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can systematically solve series circuit problems and verify answers
  • Getting there: Can solve simple problems but makes errors in multi-step calculations
  • Not yet: Cannot apply series circuit rules or confuse current and voltage

Lesson 2: Parallel Circuit Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Parallel Rules

Students write three rules for parallel circuits: (1) Voltage is the same across each branch, (2) Current splits between branches, (3) Total resistance decreases. Then calculate: two 6 Ω resistors in parallel. (Answer: 3 Ω)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Parallel circuits require different rules from series circuits.

Step-by-step method for parallel circuits:
1. Identify that all branches have the same voltage
2. Calculate current in each branch using I = V ÷ R
3. Add branch currents to get total current
4. Calculate total resistance using R_total = V ÷ I_total

For two resistors in parallel:
R_total = (R₁ × R₂) ÷ (R₁ + R₂)

Key insight:
- Two identical resistors in parallel: total resistance is half of one resistor
- A very low resistance in parallel with a high resistance: total resistance is slightly less than the low resistance
- Adding more parallel branches always reduces total resistance
Worked Example

Two resistors in parallel: R₁ = 12 Ω, R₂ = 6 Ω. Supply = 12 V.
(a) V across each = 12 V
(b) I₁ = 12 ÷ 12 = 1 A
(c) I₂ = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A
(d) I_total = 1 + 2 = 3 A
(e) R_total = V ÷ I_total = 12 ÷ 3 = 4 Ω
(f) Check: R_total = (12 × 6) ÷ (12 + 6) = 72 ÷ 18 = 4 Ω ✓

Key Formulae for Two Resistors in Parallel:
R_total = (R₁ × R₂) ÷ (R₁ + R₂)
I_total = I₁ + I₂
V₁ = V₂ = V_supply

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a table with columns: Branch, Voltage, Resistance, Current. Students fill in each column for each branch, then add to get totals.
Stretch:
Three resistors (3 Ω, 6 Ω, 12 Ω) in parallel with a 24 V supply. Calculate the current through each branch and the total current. Verify using 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Calculation

Two bulbs (each 240 Ω) are connected in parallel to a 230 V supply. Calculate the current through each bulb and the total current drawn from the supply.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can solve parallel circuit problems systematically and calculate total resistance
  • Getting there: Can calculate branch currents but struggles with total resistance
  • Not yet: Cannot apply parallel circuit rules or confuse with series rules

Lesson 3: Combined Circuits and Power

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Circuit Analysis

Show a circuit with one resistor in series with two resistors in parallel. Ask students to identify which components are in series and which are in parallel.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Real circuits often combine series and parallel sections. Solving these requires breaking the circuit into parts.

Method for combined circuits:
1. Identify the parallel section(s) and calculate their total resistance
2. Replace the parallel section with its equivalent resistance
3. Now solve as a series circuit
4. Work backwards to find current and voltage in each branch

Electrical power:
Power is the rate at which energy is transferred. In circuits:
P = V × I (power = voltage × current)
P = I²R (useful for finding power dissipated in a resistor)
P = V²/R (useful when voltage and resistance are known)

Energy transferred:
E = P × t = V × I × t (energy = power × time)
Worked Example

A circuit has R₁ = 4 Ω in series with a parallel combination of R₂ = 6 Ω and R₃ = 12 Ω. Supply = 18 V.

Step 1: Parallel section: R₂₃ = (6 × 12) ÷ (6 + 12) = 72 ÷ 18 = 4 Ω
Step 2: Total resistance: R_total = 4 + 4 = 8 Ω
Step 3: Total current: I = 18 ÷ 8 = 2.25 A
Step 4: Voltage across R₁: V₁ = 2.25 × 4 = 9 V
Step 5: Voltage across parallel section: V₂₃ = 18 − 9 = 9 V
Step 6: I₂ = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 A, I₃ = 9 ÷ 12 = 0.75 A
Step 7: Check: 1.5 + 0.75 = 2.25 A ✓

Key Formulae:
P = V × I
P = I²R
P = V²/R
E = P × t = V × I × t

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a circuit diagram with step numbers marked. Students follow each step with guidance and fill in calculations.
Stretch:
A household circuit has 10 appliances (each 500 W) in parallel on a 230 V supply. Calculate the total current and the total resistance. Discuss the implications for fuse ratings.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

"Calculate the total resistance and the current through each resistor in the following circuit." Show a combined series-parallel circuit with values.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can solve combined series-parallel circuits and use power equations accurately
  • Getting there: Can solve simple combined circuits but struggles with complex multi-step problems
  • Not yet: Cannot identify series and parallel sections in combined circuits

Homework Suggestions

  • Solve 10 circuit problems mixing series, parallel, and combined circuits
  • Calculate the total power dissipated in a circuit with five resistors of different values
  • Design a circuit that uses both series and parallel connections, calculate all values, and draw it using correct symbols
  • Research how household wiring uses fuses and circuit breakers to protect against excessive current
