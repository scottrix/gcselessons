Lesson 2: Core Concepts: reducing the development gap

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on reducing the development gap. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Concept: The development gap is the difference in levels of wealth and standard of living between the richest and poorest countries in the world. Reducing this gap requires strategies that address economic, social, and political inequality.
Definition: Investment is when money is put into a country's economy to generate future returns. This can come from foreign direct investment (FDI), governments, or international organisations.
Key Point: Countries typically develop through the Clark-Fisher model: primary sector (farming, mining) → secondary sector (manufacturing) → tertiary sector (services) → quaternary sector (research, IT). Moving up this chain increases GNI per head.
Definition: Tourism is travel for pleasure or business. It is one of the world's largest industries and can bring significant income and employment to LICs and NEEs.
Definition: Aid is the transfer of resources from one country to another, or from an organisation to a country, to help with development or emergencies.
Advantages of Aid: Provides resources that LICs cannot afford; funds essential services like healthcare and education; responds to emergencies; builds infrastructure. Disadvantages: Can create dependency; may be misused by corrupt governments; tied aid benefits donor more than recipient; does not always reach the poorest people.

Lesson 3: Application: reducing the development gap

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Key Concept, Key Point, Advantages of Aid. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain how tourism can help reduce the development gap. Use a named example.

Answer: Tourism brings foreign exchange earnings and creates employment. In Jamaica, tourism contributes 30% of GDP and employs 25% of the workforce (350,000+ jobs). It also stimulates infrastructure development such as airports and roads. However, economic leakage means much of the revenue goes to foreign companies, and many jobs are low-paid and seasonal.

Q2: What is intermediate technology? Give two examples and explain how they help development.

Answer: Intermediate technology is simple, affordable technology suited to the skills and resources of local communities. Example 1: Treadle pumps in Bangladesh allow farmers to irrigate crops without expensive diesel pumps, increasing food production and income. Example 2: Solar cookers in Africa reduce the need for firewood (preventing deforestation) and reduce smoke inhalation, improving health.

Q3: Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using aid to reduce the development gap.

Answer: Advantages: Aid provides essential resources that LICs cannot afford, funding healthcare, education, and infrastructure; emergency aid saves lives after disasters; development aid can build long-term capacity. Disadvantages: Aid can create dependency where countries rely on external support rather than developing their own economies; it may be misused by corrupt governments; tied aid primarily benefits the donor country; and it does not always reach the poorest people who need it most.

Q4: Explain how microfinance can help reduce the development gap.

Answer: Microfinance provides small loans to people who cannot access traditional banking, especially women. The Grameen Bank in Bangladesh has lent to 9 million borrowers, enabling them to start small businesses (e.g. buying livestock or sewing machines). This creates sustainable income, empowers women, and has high repayment rates (97%). It helps reduce the development gap by building economic independence from the bottom up.

Q5: Using the case study of Jamaica, evaluate the effectiveness of tourism as a strategy to reduce the development gap.

Answer: Jamaica's tourism industry has been effective in some ways: it generates $3.7 billion in foreign exchange, employs 350,000 people, and has improved infrastructure. However, effectiveness is limited by high economic leakage (profits going to foreign hotel chains), low wages, seasonal employment, and vulnerability to external shocks (COVID-19 caused a 70% drop in visitors). Tourism alone is insufficient - Jamaica needs to diversify its economy and reduce leakage to make tourism more effective at reducing the development gap.

Q6: "Fairtrade is the most effective way to reduce the development gap." How far do you agree?

Answer: Fairtrade is effective because it guarantees a minimum price, provides a community premium for development projects, and improves working conditions. Ghana's Kuapa Kokoo cooperative has used Fairtrade premiums for schools and clinics. However, Fairtrade represents only a small share of global trade, products cost more, and it does not address structural inequality in the global trading system. Other strategies may be equally or more important: debt relief frees up government revenue, microfinance empowers entrepreneurs, and industrial development creates higher-value jobs. A combination of strategies is likely most effective.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: reducing the development gap

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on reducing the development gap from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always use a named case study with specific data when evaluating strategies | For "how far do you agree" questions, discuss multiple strategies and compare their effectiveness | Remember to consider both advantages AND disadvantages for every strategy | Use the Jamaica case study with specific statistics for tourism questions | Key terms to use: economic leakage, intermediate technology, Fairtrade premium, microcredit, HIPC | Higher mark questions require evaluation - don't just describe, assess how effective each strategy is
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often write vague answers without specific geographical evidence. Wrong: Writing generalised statements like 'it causes problems' Correct: Using specific data and named examples, e.g. 'the 2010 Haiti earthquake killed over 200,000 people due to poor building quality' Students often confuse causes and effects. Wrong: Mixing up what caused the event with what resulted from it Correct: Clearly separate causes (why it happened) from effects (what happened as a result) Students often describe rather than evaluate. Wrong: Listing strategies without assessing their effectiveness Correct: Weighing up strengths and weaknesses of each approach and reaching a supported judgement
