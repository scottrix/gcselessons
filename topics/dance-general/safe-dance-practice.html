Lesson 2: Core Concepts: safe dance practice

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on safe dance practice. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Safe dance practice protects dancers from injury and supports long-term health and performance
Key Fact: A warm-up gradually raises the heart rate, increases blood flow to muscles and prepares the body for dance
Key Fact: An effective warm-up includes pulse-raising activities, joint mobilisation, dynamic stretching and dance-specific movements
Key Fact: A cool-down gradually returns the body to a resting state and helps prevent stiffness and soreness
Key Fact: Cool-down should include gentle movement to lower the heart rate and static stretches to maintain flexibility
Key Fact: Injury prevention involves wearing appropriate footwear, using suitable flooring and following correct technique

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: safe dance practice

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on safe dance practice from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Remember the four stages of a warm-up in order: pulse raiser, joint mobilisation, dynamic stretching, dance-specific preparation | When discussing injury, always name the type (sprain or strain) and link it to a dance-specific cause | Nutrition answers should mention specific food groups and their function, not just 'eat healthily' | Safe practice questions often require you to connect environment, personal care and rehearsal management | Use the term 'progressive overload' when explaining how to build rehearsal intensity safely over time
Common Errors: ✗ Static stretching should be done at the start of a warm-up. ✓ Static stretching before activity can reduce muscle power; dynamic stretching is used in the warm-up and static stretching in the cool-down. ✗ A sprain and a strain are the same type of injury. ✓ A sprain affects a ligament at a joint, while a strain affects a muscle or tendon — they are different tissues and have different causes. ✗ Dancers should train through pain to build strength. ✓ Pain is a warning signal; training through pain can worsen an injury. Dancers should stop, seek advice and allow recovery. ✗ Drinking water only matters during the dance session. ✓ Hydration should be maintained before, during and after dancing
