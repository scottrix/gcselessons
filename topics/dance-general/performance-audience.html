Lesson 2: Core Concepts: performance &amp; the audience

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on performance &amp; the audience. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The relationship between performer and audience is central to the experience of dance
Key Fact: Different venues — studios, theatres, outdoor spaces — affect how a performance is presented and received
Key Fact: A proscenium arch theatre creates a clear separation between performer and audience, framing the dance like a picture
Key Fact: Thrust and in-the-round stages bring the audience closer, creating intimacy and changing the performer's focus
Key Fact: Site-specific performances use non-theatre spaces and respond directly to the environment, creating unique audience experiences
Key Fact: The audience-performer relationship involves energy exchange — the audience's attention and response affect the performer

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: performance &amp; the audience

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on performance &amp; the audience from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always name the type of venue or staging when discussing audience-performer relationships | When comparing venues, mention specific practical adjustments the dancer must make — facings, projection, spacing | The term 'fourth wall' is useful for proscenium arch answers — show you understand how it affects the relationship | Performance quality questions require you to link physical, technical and expressive skills together | When discussing audience interpretation, acknowledge that different viewers may respond differently based on their experience
Common Errors: ✗ All performances are the same regardless of venue. ✓ The venue significantly affects how a dance is performed and received — dancers must adapt projection, focus, spacing and energy to suit each space. ✗ The audience has no effect on the performer. ✓ The audience-performer relationship is a two-way exchange — the audience's attention, energy and response influence the performer's energy and commitment. ✗ Site-specific just means performing outside. ✓ Site-specific means the choreography is created in response to a particular location's features — it is not just any outdoor performance. ✗ Performance quality depends only on technique. ✓ Performance quality is the result of physical, technic
