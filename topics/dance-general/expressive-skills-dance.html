Lesson 2: Core Concepts: expressive skills in dance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on expressive skills in dance. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Expressive skills enable a dancer to communicate meaning, mood and intention to the audience
Key Fact: Projection is the ability to reach out to the audience with energy and presence, making the performance compelling to watch
Key Fact: Focus is the direction of the dancer's gaze and attention, guiding the audience's eye and expressing intention
Key Fact: Musicality is the dancer's response to the music, showing awareness of rhythm, tempo, accents and phrasing through movement
Key Fact: Communication is the overall ability to convey the choreographic intention and emotional content to the audience
Key Fact: Phrasing is the way a dancer shapes a sequence of movements, like sentences in speech, to create meaning and dynamics

Attempt a past-paper style question on expressive skills in dance from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always link expressive skills back to the choreographic intention — do not describe them in isolation | When writing about projection, mention both physical energy (extending through the body) and mental focus (engaging with the audience) | Musicality questions often require specific references to how the dancer responds to rhythm, accents or dynamics | Use the analogy of speech for phrasing — movements are grouped like sentences with pauses, emphasis and flow | In 6-mark answers, cover at least two expressive skills and explain how each helps communicate meaning
Common Errors: ✗ Projection just means dancing with lots of energy. ✓ Projection is about directing energy and presence outward to reach the audience — it is focused and intentional, not just loud or big movement. ✗ Focus means looking at the audience the whole time. ✓ Focus can be directed at the audience, at a partner, inward or at a point in space — the choice of focus communicates different meanings. ✗ Musicality is the same as counting the beats. ✓ Counting beats ensures timing, but musicality is a deeper response to the music including dynamics, accents, phrasing and emotional quality. ✗ Phrasing only refers to the choreography, not the dancer. ✓ While choreography creates movement phrases, the dance
