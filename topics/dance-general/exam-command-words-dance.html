Lesson 2: Core Concepts: command words &amp; question types

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on command words &amp; question types. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Command words tell you exactly what the examiner wants — misreading them loses marks
Key Fact: Describe means give a factual account of what you see, hear or know about the dance
Key Fact: Explain means give reasons — why something happens or why a choreographer made a choice
Key Fact: Analyse means break down and examine in detail, showing how parts create meaning or effect
Key Fact: Compare means identify similarities and differences between two or more things
Key Fact: Evaluate means make a judgement using evidence, weighing strengths and weaknesses

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: command words &amp; question types

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on command words &amp; question types from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Circle or underline the command word before writing anything — it sets your answer direction | For 'describe' questions, use specific dance terminology rather than vague descriptions | For 'explain' questions, always include the word 'because' to ensure you give reasons | In 'compare' questions, avoid writing about one thing then the other separately — link them | For 'evaluate' questions, end with a clear judgement statement supported by your evidence
Common Errors: ✗ Describing when the question asks you to explain. ✓ 'Explain' requires reasons — always include 'because' and show cause and effect, not just what happens. ✗ Writing about only one side in a compare question. ✓ 'Compare' requires both similarities and differences. Use linking words and address both items equally. ✗ Giving a personal opinion when asked to analyse. ✓ 'Analyse' requires objective examination of how parts create meaning — evidence-based, not opinion-based. ✗ Writing everything you know about a topic regardless of the command word. ✓ Match your answer to the command word. Each command word requires a specific type of response — irrelevant information gains no marks.
