Lesson 2: Core Concepts: stimulus &amp; starting points

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on stimulus &amp; starting points. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A stimulus is the starting point or inspiration for creating a dance
Key Fact: There are five main types of stimulus: visual, auditory, kinaesthetic, ideational and literary
Key Fact: A visual stimulus is something you see — a painting, photograph, sculpture or film
Key Fact: An auditory stimulus is something you hear — music, natural sounds, spoken word or silence
Key Fact: A kinaesthetic stimulus comes from physical sensation — a gesture, body action or quality of movement
Key Fact: An ideational stimulus is a concept or theme — an emotion, issue, memory or philosophical idea

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on stimulus &amp; starting points from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always name the specific type of stimulus in exam answers — visual, auditory, kinaesthetic, ideational or literary | When describing a stimulus, give a concrete example and link it to the movement it inspired | Remember that one type of stimulus can lead to many different choreographic outcomes | In NEA tasks, clearly state your stimulus type and explain why you chose it | For professional works questions, identify the choreographer's stimulus and how it shaped the piece
Common Errors: ✗ A stimulus is the music used for a dance. ✓ Music can be an auditory stimulus, but a stimulus can be any of five types: visual, auditory, kinaesthetic, ideational or literary. ✗ Ideational and literary stimuli are the same thing. ✓ An ideational stimulus is a concept or theme; a literary stimulus is specifically words such as a poem, story or quote. ✗ You can only use one stimulus when choreographing. ✓ Choreographers can combine multiple stimuli to create richer, more layered work. ✗ A kinaesthetic stimulus means watching someone else move. ✓ A kinaesthetic stimulus comes from your own physical sensation or body awareness, such as a gesture, breath or feeling of weight.
