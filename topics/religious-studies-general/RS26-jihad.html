Lesson 2: Core Concepts: jihad

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on jihad. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Jihad means "struggle" or "striving" — NOT "holy war." There are two types: the greater jihad (internal spiritual struggle against sin) and the lesser jihad (external struggle, which may include armed combat under strict conditions).
Exam Tip: The vast majority of Muslims interpret jihad primarily as the greater jihad — the inner struggle. Terrorist attacks that kill civilians violate every condition of lesser jihad and are condemned by mainstream Islamic scholars.
RS Exam Tips — Jihad: 1. For Jihad questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of Jihad to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: A British Muslim student who refuses to join friends in drinking alcohol, who fasts during Ramadan while sitting in the school canteen, and who maintains their five daily prayers despite exam stress, is practising greater jihad. These everyday acts of faithfulness require courage and self-discipline.
UK Context: The Manchester Arena bombing in 2017 was condemned by Muslim leaders across the UK. The Muslim Council of Britain stated: “This is a horrific and cowardly attack targeting innocent civilians. Our thoughts and prayers are with the victims and their families.” Over 500 British mosques held special prayers for the victims, and British Muslims raised over £20,000 for the victims’ families within days.
UK Context: British Muslim charities such as Islamic Relief, Muslim Aid and the National Zakat Foundation describe their humanitarian work as a form of jihad — striving to help those in need as a religious duty. In 2023, Islamic Relief UK provided emergency aid to over 1 million people worldwide, embodying the principle that jihad includes striving to relieve suffering and promote justice.
TermMeaningExample
Greater JihadThe personal, spiritual struggle to be a better Muslim and overcome sinResisting temptation, controlling anger, praying regularly, studying the Qur'an, being patient in hardship
Lesser JihadPhysical struggle to defend Islam or fight injustice; armed combat is a last resort with strict rulesDefending against attack, protecting the innocent, removing oppression

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the difference between greater and lesser jihad.

Answer: Greater jihad is the internal, spiritual struggle every Muslim faces daily — resisting temptation, controlling anger, being patient, praying regularly and trying to become a better Muslim. Muhammad said after a battle: "We have returned from the lesser jihad to the greater jihad." Lesser jihad is the external struggle, which may include armed combat to defend Islam or fight injustice. It has strict conditions: it must be declared by legitimate authority, be a last resort, and protect non-combatants. Most Muslims focus on greater jihad as the primary meaning.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: jihad

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on jihad from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think jihad means "holy war." Wrong: "Jihad means holy war." Correct: "Jihad means "struggle" or "striving." Greater jihad is the internal spiritual struggle to live as a good Muslim. Lesser jihad is armed struggle, which has strict conditions and is a last resort."
