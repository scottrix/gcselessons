Lesson 2: Core Concepts: islamic festivals

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on islamic festivals. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The two main Islamic festivals are Id-ul-Adha (Festival of Sacrifice) and Id-ul-Fitr (Festival of Fast-Breaking). Shi'a Muslims also observe Ashura , commemorating the martyrdom of Imam Husayn.
RS Exam Tips — Islamic Festivals: 1. For Islamic Festivals questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of Islamic Festivals to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: In 2023, the East London Mosque hosted over 10,000 worshippers for Id-ul-Fitr prayers, with additional prayer spaces set up in local parks. The mosque also distributed 2,000 free meals to the local community, regardless of faith, as part of the Id celebration.
UK Context: Islamic Relief UK’s qurbani programme distributes meat to over 2 million people in 30 countries each year. British Muslims can pay for qurbani online, specifying where the sacrifice should take place, with many choosing to have it performed in conflict zones or areas affected by famine where the need is greatest.
UK Context: The London Ashura procession, which passes through central London each year, is one of the largest Shi’a religious observances in Europe. In 2023, approximately 5,000 people participated, with the Metropolitan Police providing a safe escort and road closures.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two Islamic festivals.

Answer: 1) Id-ul-Adha — the Festival of Sacrifice, celebrated during Hajj. Muslims sacrifice an animal to remember Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son at Allah's command. The meat is shared between family, friends and the poor. It teaches obedience to Allah and generosity. 2) Id-ul-Fitr — the Festival of Fast-Breaking, marking the end of Ramadan. Muslims attend special prayers, celebrate with food and gifts, and pay Zakah al-Fitr so the poor can also celebrate. It is a joyful thanksgiving for the strength to complete the fast.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: islamic festivals

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on islamic festivals from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think jihad means "holy war." Wrong: "Jihad means holy war." Correct: "Jihad means "struggle" or "striving." Greater jihad is the internal spiritual struggle to live as a good Muslim. Lesser jihad is armed struggle, which has strict conditions and is a last resort."
