Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the five pillars

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the five pillars. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Shahadah is the first and most important Pillar of Islam: "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is his messenger." This declaration is said daily in prayer and is the only thing required to become a Muslim.
RS Exam Tips — The Five Pillars: 1. For The Five Pillars questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of The Five Pillars to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: When Malcolm X converted to Islam in 1964, he recited the Shahadah to formally become a Muslim. This simple declaration transformed his identity and worldview. In the UK today, new Muslims recite the Shahadah at mosques across the country, with the London Central Mosque reporting approximately 200 conversions per year.
UK Context: In the UK, both Sunni and Shi’a mosques are found in major cities. The London Central Mosque in Regent’s Park is predominantly Sunni, while the Islamic Centre of England in London serves the Shi’a community. Both communities observe Ramadan and perform Hajj, but their additional practices differ.
UK Context: During Ramadan in the UK, where summer fasting days can last over 18 hours, British Muslims demonstrate remarkable commitment. Many continue to work full shifts while fasting, and mosques across the country organise community iftars. In 2023, the East London Mosque served over 1,000 free meals every evening during Ramadan, demonstrating how the Pillars of fasting and charity combine to strengthen community life.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why the Shahadah is the most important Pillar of Islam.

Answer: The Shahadah is the most important Pillar because it sums up the entire Islamic faith: tawhid (one God) and risalah (Muhammad as prophet). Without the Shahadah, none of the other Pillars would make sense — prayer, fasting, charity and pilgrimage all depend on the belief that Allah is the one God and Muhammad is his messenger. It is the entry point to Islam and is recited constantly throughout a Muslim's life, from birth to death.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: the five pillars

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on the five pillars from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think jihad means "holy war." Wrong: "Jihad means holy war." Correct: "Jihad means "struggle" or "striving." Greater jihad is the internal spiritual struggle to live as a good Muslim. Lesser jihad is armed struggle, which has strict conditions and is a last resort."
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):
