Lesson 2: Core Concepts: risalah: prophethood

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Muslims believe Allah sent prophets (rasul and nabi) to every nation to guide people to the truth. All prophets brought the same message: worship one God and live righteously. Muhammad is the final and greatest prophet — the "Seal of the Prophets" (Surah 33:40).
RS Exam Tips — Risalah: Prophethood: 1. For Risalah: Prophethood questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of Risalah: Prophethood to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: The stories of the prophets are central to Muslim religious education in Britain. Madrasahs (Islamic schools) teach children about the lives and teachings of all the major prophets, emphasising their patience, courage and devotion to Allah.
UK Context: The Prophet’s migration from Makkah to Madinah (the Hijrah, 622 CE) marks the beginning of the Islamic calendar. This event, commemorated every year by Muslims worldwide, symbolises the move from persecution to the establishment of a just community — a theme that resonates with British Muslims who value religious freedom and social justice.
UK Context: In the UK, the Muslim and Christian communities often engage in interfaith dialogue about their respective views of Jesus. Organisations such as the Christian-Muslim Forum promote understanding by emphasising the shared reverence for Jesus while honestly acknowledging theological differences.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two Muslim beliefs about prophethood.

Answer: 1) Muhammad is the final and greatest prophet — the "Seal of the Prophets" (Surah 33:40). He received the Qur'an from Jibril and his life (Sunnah) is the perfect model for Muslims to follow. No prophet will come after him. 2) All prophets brought the same message of tawhid — worship one God and live righteously. This means Islam is not a new religion but the final, complete version of the message given to all prophets from Adam to Muhammad.

Lesson 3: Application: risalah: prophethood

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: RS Exam Tips — Risalah: Prophethood, UK Context, UK Context. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two Muslim beliefs about prophethood.

Answer: 1) Muhammad is the final and greatest prophet — the "Seal of the Prophets" (Surah 33:40). He received the Qur'an from Jibril and his life (Sunnah) is the perfect model for Muslims to follow. No prophet will come after him. 2) All prophets brought the same message of tawhid — worship one God and live righteously. This means Islam is not a new religion but the final, complete version of the message given to all prophets from Adam to Muhammad.

Q2: "Muhammad is the most important prophet." Evaluate this statement.

Answer: Muslims consider Muhammad the most important prophet because he delivered the final, complete revelation (the Qur'an), his Sunnah provides a perfect guide for all aspects of life, and he is the "Seal of the Prophets." However, Muslims also revere other prophets — Ibrahim is called the "Friend of Allah" and Musa spoke directly to God. All prophets are equally chosen by Allah, but Muhammad has a unique role as the final messenger whose message is preserved perfectly in the Qur'an for all humanity.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse Sunni and Shi'a beliefs about predestination. Wrong: "All Muslims believe everything is predestined and humans have no free will." Correct: "Most Muslims believe God has foreknowledge but humans have free will to choose; the balance between predestination and free will is understood differently across traditions."
