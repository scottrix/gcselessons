Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the role of the church

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the role of the church. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Jesus taught "Love your neighbour as yourself" (Mark 12:31) and the Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37) shows that Christians must help anyone in need, regardless of who they are. The local church puts this teaching into practice.
RS Exam Tips — The Role of the Church: 1. For The Role of the Church questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of The Role of the Church to its real-world impact on believers and society

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two ways the local church serves the community.

Answer: 1) Food banks — many churches run food banks that provide emergency food supplies to people in poverty. The Trussell Trust, a Christian charity, runs over 1,200 food banks. This puts Jesus' teaching to "love your neighbour" into practical action. 2) Street pastors — trained church volunteers go out at night to help vulnerable people on the streets, offering practical help, listening and a safe presence. They demonstrate Christian care for the marginalised.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: the role of the church

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: RS Exam Tips — The Role of the Church. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two ways the local church serves the community.

Answer: 1) Food banks — many churches run food banks that provide emergency food supplies to people in poverty. The Trussell Trust, a Christian charity, runs over 1,200 food banks. This puts Jesus' teaching to "love your neighbour" into practical action. 2) Street pastors — trained church volunteers go out at night to help vulnerable people on the streets, offering practical help, listening and a safe presence. They demonstrate Christian care for the marginalised.

Q2: "The Church should focus on worship, not social action." Evaluate this statement.

Answer: Some Christians argue the Church's primary role is worship and spiritual guidance — social action is the government's job. However, Jesus himself healed the sick, fed the hungry and cared for the outcast. The Parable of the Good Samaritan teaches that loving your neighbour requires practical action. Worship and social action are not alternatives — worship inspires service, and service demonstrates worship. Churches that serve their communities often find this is the most effective form of evangelism.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: the role of the church

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on the role of the church from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think all Christians celebrate the Eucharist in the same way. Wrong: "All Christians believe the bread and wine literally become Jesus' body and blood." Correct: "Catholics believe in transubstantiation; Protestants have different views including consubstantiation and memorial symbolism."
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):

  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of the role of the church
  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in the role of the church?"

Plenary (5 minutes)
