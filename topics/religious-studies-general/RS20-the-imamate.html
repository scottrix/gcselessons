Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the imamate

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the imamate. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Imamate is a Shi'a Muslim belief that Allah appointed twelve Imams to lead the Muslim community after Muhammad's death. These Imams are divinely chosen, infallible and have special spiritual authority. This is one of the Five Roots of Usul ad-Din in Shi'a Islam.
Exam Tip: The Imamate is the key difference between Sunni and Shi'a Islam. Sunni Muslims believe caliphs should be chosen by the community; Shi'a Muslims believe Allah appointed the Imams. This is why the two traditions have different approaches to authority, law and leadership.
RS Exam Tips — The Imamate: 1. For The Imamate questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of The Imamate to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: In the UK, Shi’a communities follow the guidance of marja (religious authorities) who are considered the most learned scholars qualified to interpret the teachings of the Imams. The Al-Khoei Foundation in London follows the marja’iyya of Ayatollah Sistani, one of the most senior Shi’a clerics in the world.
UK Context: The concept of the Mahdi motivates Shi’a Muslims to work for justice and social change in the present, preparing the world for the Mahdi’s return. British Shi’a organisations are active in charity, interfaith work and social justice campaigns, reflecting this belief.
UK Context: In the UK, the Muslim Council of Britain includes both Sunni and Shi’a member organisations, demonstrating that cooperation on shared concerns is possible despite theological differences. The Christian-Muslim Forum also includes both Sunni and Shi’a representatives.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two Shi'a beliefs about the Imamate.

Answer: 1) Shi'a Muslims believe Allah appointed twelve Imams to guide the Muslim community after Muhammad. They are divinely chosen, not elected by the people, and are descendants of Muhammad through Ali and Fatimah. 2) The Imams are infallible — they cannot make errors in interpreting the Qur'an or Islamic law. This guarantees that the true meaning of Islam is preserved without distortion. The twelfth Imam (the Mahdi) is in occultation and will return to restore justice.

Lesson 3: Application: the imamate

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Exam Tip, RS Exam Tips — The Imamate, UK Context. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two Shi'a beliefs about the Imamate.

Answer: 1) Shi'a Muslims believe Allah appointed twelve Imams to guide the Muslim community after Muhammad. They are divinely chosen, not elected by the people, and are descendants of Muhammad through Ali and Fatimah. 2) The Imams are infallible — they cannot make errors in interpreting the Qur'an or Islamic law. This guarantees that the true meaning of Islam is preserved without distortion. The twelfth Imam (the Mahdi) is in occultation and will return to restore justice.

Q2: "The Imamate is the most important Shi'a belief." Evaluate this statement.

Answer: The Imamate is certainly distinctive to Shi'a Islam and shapes their entire approach to authority, law and community. Without the Imamate, Shi'a Islam would lose its unique identity. However, tawhid (the oneness of God) is arguably more fundamental — it is the first root of Usul ad-Din and the foundation of all Islamic belief. The Imamate depends on tawhid but tawhid does not depend on the Imamate. Both are essential to Shi'a Islam, but tawhid is the root and the Imamate is the branch that gives Shi'a Islam its distinctive character.

Error Review

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: the imamate

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on the imamate from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse Sunni and Shi'a beliefs about predestination. Wrong: "All Muslims believe everything is predestined and humans have no free will." Correct: "Most Muslims believe God has foreknowledge but humans have free will to choose; the balance between predestination and free will is understood differently across traditions."
