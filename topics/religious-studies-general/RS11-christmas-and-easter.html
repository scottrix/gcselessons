Lesson 2: Core Concepts: christmas and easter

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on christmas and easter. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam Tip: Some Christians are concerned that Christmas has become too commercialised — the focus on presents and Santa Claus can overshadow the birth of Jesus. Churches work to keep the religious meaning central through nativity services, carol concerts and Advent reflections.
RS Exam Tips — Christmas and Easter: 1. For Christmas and Easter questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why Easter is the most important Christian festival.

Answer: Easter is the most important festival because it celebrates the resurrection — the event on which the entire Christian faith depends. Without the resurrection, Jesus would be just a dead teacher, and there would be no salvation. As Paul wrote: "If Christ has not been raised, our preaching is useless and so is your faith" (1 Corinthians 15:14). The resurrection proves Jesus' divinity, demonstrates God's power over death, and gives Christians the hope of eternal life.

Lesson 3: Application: christmas and easter

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Exam Tip, RS Exam Tips — Christmas and Easter. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why Easter is the most important Christian festival.

Answer: Easter is the most important festival because it celebrates the resurrection — the event on which the entire Christian faith depends. Without the resurrection, Jesus would be just a dead teacher, and there would be no salvation. As Paul wrote: "If Christ has not been raised, our preaching is useless and so is your faith" (1 Corinthians 15:14). The resurrection proves Jesus' divinity, demonstrates God's power over death, and gives Christians the hope of eternal life.

Q2: Explain two ways Christians celebrate Christmas.

Answer: 1) Many Christians attend a Midnight Mass or Christmas Day service, which includes nativity readings from Luke's Gospel, carols, and a celebration of Jesus' birth. 2) Christians set up nativity scenes in churches and homes, depicting the birth of Jesus in a manger with Mary, Joseph and the shepherds, to remind themselves and their families of the true meaning of Christmas amid commercialisation.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: christmas and easter

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on christmas and easter from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think all Christians celebrate the Eucharist in the same way. Wrong: "All Christians believe the bread and wine literally become Jesus' body and blood." Correct: "Catholics believe in transubstantiation; Protestants have different views including consubstantiation and memorial symbolism."
