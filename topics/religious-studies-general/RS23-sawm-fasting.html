Lesson 2: Core Concepts: sawm: fasting

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Sawm (fasting) during Ramadan is the third Pillar of Islam. Muslims do not eat, drink, smoke or engage in sexual activity from dawn (Fajr) to sunset (Maghrib) for the entire month of Ramadan — the month when the Qur'an was first revealed to Muhammad.
RS Exam Tips — Sawm: Fasting: 1. For Sawm: Fasting questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of Sawm: Fasting to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: In the UK, summer Ramadan fasting days can last over 18 hours, from approximately 2:30am to 9:30pm. Despite this extreme challenge, surveys show that over 90% of British Muslims fast during Ramadan, with many reporting that the spiritual benefits far outweigh the physical difficulty.
UK Context: During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, British Muslims faced the unusual challenge of fasting during Ramadan while mosques were closed. Communities adapted by organising virtual iftars, online tarawih prayers and contactless food distribution to those in need, demonstrating how the spirit of Ramadan could be maintained even under extreme restrictions.
UK Context: In 2024, the Muslim Council of Britain issued guidance to schools on supporting fasting students during exam season, recommending that schools provide quiet rest areas, allow students to avoid strenuous PE activities, and schedule important exams for the morning when fasting students have more energy. This reflects the growing recognition of Ramadan as an important part of British life.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two reasons why Muslims fast during Ramadan.

Answer: 1) Self-discipline and taqwa — fasting teaches Muslims to control their physical desires and focus on spiritual matters. By resisting the urge to eat and drink, Muslims strengthen their willpower and their devotion to Allah. 2) Empathy with the poor — experiencing hunger during daylight hours helps Muslims understand the daily suffering of those who do not have enough food. This motivates greater charity (zakah) and compassion.
