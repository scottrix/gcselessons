Lesson 2: Core Concepts: aims of punishment

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on aims of punishment. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: There are three main aims of punishment: retribution (punishment because the offender deserves it), deterrence (putting people off committing crimes) and reformation/rehabilitation (helping the offender change their behaviour).
RS Exam Tips — Aims of Punishment: 1. For Aims of Punishment questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of Aims of Punishment to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: In the UK, debates about often involve both religious and secular voices. Parliament considers evidence from faith communities alongside scientific, legal and ethical perspectives, reflecting Britain’s commitment to both religious freedom and democratic decision-making.
UK Context: The British system of establishing laws through parliamentary debate, judicial interpretation and public consultation allows religious perspectives to be heard alongside other viewpoints. Faith communities regularly submit evidence to parliamentary committees and participate in public consultations on issues related to .
UK Context: British examiners specifically look for the use of specific scriptural references (e.g. Surah numbers, Bible chapter and verse) and named scholars or traditions. Vague references to ‘what Christians believe’ or ‘what Muslims think’ without specific evidence will not achieve the highest marks.
TermMeaningExample
RetributionPunishment proportionate to the crime; the offender deserves itA longer sentence for a more serious crime
DeterrencePunishment that discourages the offender and others from reoffendingSevere penalties to warn others
ReformationPunishment that helps the offender change and rejoin societyEducation, training, therapy in prison

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two aims of punishment and a religious view on each.

Answer: 1) Retribution — punishment that is proportionate to the crime because the offender deserves it. Christianity accepts retribution as justice but warns against revenge; Jesus replaced "an eye for an eye" with "turn the other cheek." 2) Reformation — punishment that helps the offender change and rejoin society. Islam values reformation because the Qur'an teaches repentance and forgiveness — "Whoever forgives and makes reconciliation, his reward is with Allah" (Surah 42:40). Both traditions believe people can change through God's grace.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: aims of punishment

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on aims of punishment from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse retribution with revenge. Wrong: "Retribution means getting revenge on criminals." Correct: "Retribution is punishment that is proportionate to the crime, based on justice and the principle that wrongdoers deserve punishment. Revenge is personal and emotional, often disproportionate. Retribution is a principle of justice; revenge is not."
