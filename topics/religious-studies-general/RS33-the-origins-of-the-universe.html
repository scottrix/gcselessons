Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the origins of the universe

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the origins of the universe. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: GCSE RS requires you to understand both scientific and religious explanations for the origins of the universe, and how they relate to each other.
RS Exam Tips — The Origins of the Universe: 1. For The Origins of the Universe questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of The Origins of the Universe to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: In the UK, debates about often involve both religious and secular voices. Parliament considers evidence from faith communities alongside scientific, legal and ethical perspectives, reflecting Britain’s commitment to both religious freedom and democratic decision-making.
UK Context: The British system of establishing laws through parliamentary debate, judicial interpretation and public consultation allows religious perspectives to be heard alongside other viewpoints. Faith communities regularly submit evidence to parliamentary committees and participate in public consultations on issues related to .
UK Context: British examiners specifically look for the use of specific scriptural references (e.g. Surah numbers, Bible chapter and verse) and named scholars or traditions. Vague references to ‘what Christians believe’ or ‘what Muslims think’ without specific evidence will not achieve the highest marks.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain how the Big Bang theory can be compatible with religious belief.

Answer: Many Christians and Muslims accept the Big Bang as the scientific explanation for HOW the universe began, while believing that God/Allah was the cause behind it. The Catholic Church officially accepts the Big Bang, and the Qur'an's description of the universe being "split apart" (Surah 21:30) can be read as referencing the Big Bang. Science explains the mechanism; religion explains the meaning and purpose. The two address different questions and are not necessarily in conflict.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: the origins of the universe

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on the origins of the universe from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse sanctity of life with quality of life. Wrong: "Sanctity of life means life should have good quality." Correct: "Sanctity of life means all life is sacred because it is God-given and has intrinsic value. Quality of life refers to the standard of health, comfort and happiness. They are different principles that can lead to different ethical conclusions."
