Lesson 2: Core Concepts: akhirah: life after death

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on akhirah: life after death. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Muslims believe that physical death is not the end — there is an afterlife (akhirah). After death, the soul is questioned in the grave by Munkar and Nakir, and on the Day of Judgement (Yawm al-Din), all people will be resurrected and judged by Allah.
Exam Tip: Belief in akhirah is one of the Six Articles of Faith. Without it, the whole Islamic moral framework would collapse — there would be no ultimate justice and no reason to follow Allah's commands if this life is all there is.
RS Exam Tips — Akhirah: Life After Death: 1. For Akhirah: Life After Death questions, always include specific scriptural or source references where relevant 2. Explain both religious and non-religious viewpoints 3. Use specialist vocabulary precisely (e.g. terms from the specification) 4. Evaluate by analysing strengths and weaknesses of different views before reaching your conclusion 5. Link your discussion of Akhirah: Life After Death to its real-world impact on believers and society
UK Context: The Islamic funeral ritual reflects belief in akhirah: the body is washed, wrapped in a simple white shroud and buried facing Makkah as quickly as possible. Mourners recite the Shahadah and pray for the deceased’s forgiveness. In the UK, Muslim burials must comply with both Islamic law (which requires burial, not cremation) and UK cemetery regulations.
UK Context: The Prophet said: “The one who has the least punishment in Hell will have two shoes of fire, and his brain will boil because of them” (Sahih Bukhari). Even the least punishment is severe, which motivates Muslims to avoid sin and seek Allah’s forgiveness through prayer, charity and good deeds.
UK Context: In 2023, Birmingham City Council approved a new Muslim burial section at Brandwood End Cemetery, reflecting the growing need for Islamic burial facilities in the city, which has one of the largest Muslim populations in the UK outside London.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two Muslim beliefs about akhirah.

Answer: 1) The Day of Judgement — Muslims believe Allah will resurrect all people and judge them based on their deeds. The books of deeds (recorded by Raqib and Atid) will be presented, and people will be sent to Jannah (Paradise) or Jahannam (Hell). 2) Barzakh — after death, the soul enters a state of waiting in the grave. The angels Munkar and Nakir question the deceased about their faith. This state continues until the Day of Judgement.

Lesson 3: Application: akhirah: life after death

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Exam Tip, UK Context, UK Context. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two Muslim beliefs about akhirah.

Answer: 1) The Day of Judgement — Muslims believe Allah will resurrect all people and judge them based on their deeds. The books of deeds (recorded by Raqib and Atid) will be presented, and people will be sent to Jannah (Paradise) or Jahannam (Hell). 2) Barzakh — after death, the soul enters a state of waiting in the grave. The angels Munkar and Nakir question the deceased about their faith. This state continues until the Day of Judgement.

Q2: "Belief in life after death gives life meaning." Evaluate this statement with reference to Islamic teaching.

Answer: Muslims would strongly agree — without akhirah, life would lack ultimate purpose and justice. The wicked could escape consequences and the righteous could suffer without reward. Akhirah motivates moral behaviour and gives comfort. However, some non-religious people find meaning in this life through relationships, contribution to society and personal growth. For Muslims, though, earthly life (dunya) is a test for the eternal life (akhirah) — meaning comes from knowing this life is a preparation for something greater.

Exam-Style Question

Exam Tips: Always refer to specific sources of authority (scripture, sacred texts, religious leaders) | Explain contrasting beliefs — different views within and between traditions | Use key terminology precisely (e.g. tawhid, atonement, sanctity of life) | For 12-mark evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a justified conclusion | Link beliefs to their impact on individuals, communities and society | Acknowledge diversity — no religious tradition has a single, uniform view
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse Sunni and Shi'a beliefs about predestination. Wrong: "All Muslims believe everything is predestined and humans have no free will." Correct: "Most Muslims believe God has foreknowledge but humans have free will to choose; the balance between predestination and free will is understood differently across traditions."
