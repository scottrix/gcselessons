B23: Principles of Homeostasis

Foundation Higher All Boards

Learn how the body maintains stable internal conditions through negative feedback, involving receptors, coordination centres, and effectors.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define homeostasis and explain why it is important
  • Describe the components of a homeostatic control system: receptors, coordination centres, effectors
  • Explain how negative feedback works to maintain stable conditions
  • Give examples of conditions controlled by homeostasis

Prerequisites

  • Basic knowledge of body systems (B6)
  • Understanding of nervous and hormonal communication

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Thermostat analogy diagrams
  • Negative feedback loop templates

Lesson 1: What is Homeostasis?

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Thermostat Analogy

Ask: "How does a thermostat work?" Discuss that it monitors temperature, compares it to a set point, and turns heating on/off to maintain the set temperature. The body works the same way — introduce homeostasis.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Homeostasis: the regulation of the internal conditions of a cell or organism to maintain optimum conditions for function, in response to internal and external changes. Conditions controlled by homeostasis:
  • Blood glucose concentration
  • Body temperature (~37°C)
  • Water levels (and ion concentrations)
  • Blood pressure
Components of a homeostatic system:
  • Receptors: detect changes in the environment (stimuli). They are the "sensors." Examples: thermoreceptors (detect temperature), osmoreceptors (detect water potential), chemoreceptors (detect chemical changes like blood glucose).
  • Coordination centre: receives and processes information from receptors. Examples: brain (especially the hypothalamus), pancreas, kidneys.
  • Effectors: organs or tissues that bring about responses to correct the change. Examples: muscles (contract), glands (secrete hormones or enzymes).
Negative feedback: the main mechanism of homeostasis.
  1. A change occurs in the internal environment (e.g. body temperature rises)
  2. Receptors detect the change
  3. Coordination centre processes the information
  4. Effectors bring about a response that reverses the change (e.g. sweat glands produce sweat → cooling)
  5. The condition returns to its set point (e.g. temperature drops back to 37°C)
  6. The effector activity reduces (negative feedback — the response reduces the original stimulus)
Negative feedback keeps conditions within a narrow range around the set point, not exactly at one value.
Worked Example

"Describe the negative feedback loop that maintains body temperature when it rises above 37°C." Answer: (1) Thermoreceptors in the skin and brain detect the rise in temperature. (2) The hypothalamus (coordination centre) processes this information. (3) Effectors are activated: sweat glands produce sweat (evaporation cools the skin), blood vessels near the skin surface dilate (vasodilation — more heat lost from blood to surroundings). (4) Body temperature falls back towards 37°C. (5) As temperature returns to normal, sweat production reduces and blood vessels constrict again.

Common Misconception

"Homeostasis keeps conditions exactly at one value"

Homeostasis maintains conditions within a narrow range around a set point. For example, body temperature fluctuates between about 36.1°C and 37.2°C throughout the day. The set point is approximately 37°C, but conditions oscillate around it.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use the thermostat analogy throughout. Simplify: "Receptors detect change → Brain processes → Effectors act → Condition returns to normal." Provide a flow diagram template.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Compare negative feedback with positive feedback (e.g. oxytocin during childbirth, blood clotting). When is positive feedback useful? Why is negative feedback more common?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Three Components

For body temperature: name the receptor (thermoreceptors), coordination centre (hypothalamus), and two effectors (sweat glands, blood vessels). Write on mini-whiteboards.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can define homeostasis, describe the three components, and explain negative feedback with an example
  • Getting there: Can define homeostasis but cannot describe the components or negative feedback
  • Not yet: Cannot define homeostasis or describe the control system

Lesson 2: Negative Feedback in Detail

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Real-World Example

Ask: "What happens to your body when you get too hot? What about when you're too cold?" Brainstorm all the responses students can think of.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Detailed negative feedback examples: 1. Thermoregulation (temperature):
  • Too hot: thermoreceptors → hypothalamus → sweat glands (sweating), vasodilation → temperature drops
  • Too cold: thermoreceptors → hypothalamus → shivering (muscles contract, generating heat), vasoconstriction, hairs stand up (trap air for insulation) → temperature rises
2. Blood glucose:
  • Too high: receptors → pancreas → insulin secreted → glucose taken up by cells → blood glucose falls
  • Too low: receptors → pancreas → glucagon secreted → glycogen converted to glucose in liver → blood glucose rises
3. Water balance:
  • Too little water: osmoreceptors → hypothalamus → pituitary gland releases ADH → kidneys reabsorb more water → urine becomes more concentrated → water level rises
  • Too much water: osmoreceptors → hypothalamus → less ADH → kidneys reabsorb less water → urine becomes dilute → water level falls
Importance of homeostasis:
  • Enzymes work best at specific temperatures and pH — deviations reduce enzyme efficiency
  • Cells need the right water concentration to function — too much water causes cells to swell and burst; too little causes them to shrivel
  • Blood glucose must be maintained for respiration — too high or too low is dangerous
Worked Example

"Explain why it is important to maintain blood glucose concentration within a narrow range." Answer: (1) Glucose is the main substrate for aerobic respiration, which releases energy for all cells. (2) If blood glucose is too low, cells (especially brain cells) cannot respire adequately, leading to dizziness, confusion, and potentially death. (3) If blood glucose is too high, it can damage blood vessels and kidneys over time (diabetes). (4) Enzymes involved in metabolism work best at specific conditions, and glucose levels affect many metabolic pathways.

Common Misconception

"Negative feedback stops when conditions return to normal"

Negative feedback is a continuous process — the body is always monitoring and adjusting. Even when conditions appear stable, small fluctuations are constantly being corrected. Homeostasis is dynamic, not static.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on one example (body temperature). Write out the full feedback loop: stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → return to set point. Practice describing this for temperature going up and down.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research what happens when homeostasis fails: diabetes (blood glucose), hypothermia (temperature), dehydration (water balance). Create a table showing each condition, what goes wrong, and the consequences.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Loop Builder

Students construct a negative feedback loop for water balance, filling in: stimulus, receptor, coordination centre, effector, response. Share and check.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe negative feedback loops for at least two conditions with clear reasoning
  • Getting there: Can describe one feedback loop but struggles with others
  • Not yet: Cannot describe a negative feedback loop

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a poster showing a negative feedback loop for body temperature with labelled diagrams
  • Research what happens to the body in extreme heat (heatstroke) and extreme cold (hypothermia)
  • Design a flowchart showing the negative feedback loop for blood glucose regulation
  • Explain why athletes need to maintain hydration during sport, using homeostasis concepts
