P22: Wave Properties

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding transverse and longitudinal waves, wave terminology, and the wave equation v = fλ.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves
  • Define wavelength, frequency, amplitude, and period
  • Use the wave equation v = fλ
  • Describe how waves transfer energy without transferring matter
  • Interpret wave diagrams and graphs

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of energy transfers (P1)
  • Ability to rearrange equations

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Rope or slinky spring
  • Ripple tank or simulation

Lesson 1: Transverse and Longitudinal Waves

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Wave Demo

Shake a rope up and down to create a transverse wave. Push a slinky forwards and backwards for a longitudinal wave. "What is different about particle motion?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Waves transfer energy without transferring matter. Particles oscillate but do not travel with the wave.

Transverse waves: particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of travel. Examples: light, all EM waves, water waves, seismic S-waves.

Longitudinal waves: particles oscillate parallel to the direction of travel. Characterised by compressions and rarefactions. Examples: sound, seismic P-waves.

Wave terminology:
- Wavelength (λ): distance between adjacent points in phase (m)
- Amplitude (A): maximum displacement from equilibrium (m)
- Frequency (f): number of complete waves per second (Hz)
- Period (T): time for one complete wave (s). T = 1/f
Worked Example

A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 0.4 m. v = fλ = 5 × 0.4 = 2 m/s

Key Equation:
v = fλ
T = 1/f
Common Misconception: Students think waves transfer matter. Waves transfer energy — particles oscillate but do not travel with the wave.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide labelled diagrams of transverse and longitudinal waves. Students identify wavelength and amplitude.
Stretch:
A wave has period 0.02 s and wavelength 1.5 m. Calculate frequency and wave speed.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Wave Type Sort

Classify: light, sound, water, seismic P-wave, seismic S-wave as transverse or longitudinal.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can distinguish wave types, define all terms, and use v = fλ
  • Getting there: Can use v = fλ but struggles with terminology
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish transverse from longitudinal

Lesson 2: Describing Waves

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Graph Reading

Show displacement-distance and displacement-time graphs. "What does each show? How do you find wavelength from one and period from the other?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Displacement-distance graph: shows wave shape at one instant. Wavelength = distance between two adjacent crests.

Displacement-time graph: shows oscillation of one particle over time. Period = time for one complete oscillation.

Wave speed: measure wavelength and period from respective graphs, then use v = fλ.
Key Relationships:
f = 1/T
v = fλ

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can interpret both graph types and calculate all wave quantities
  • Getting there: Can read wavelength but struggles with time graphs
  • Not yet: Cannot interpret wave graphs

Lesson 3: Wave Behaviour

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Reflection: waves bounce off a surface. Angle of incidence = angle of reflection.

Refraction: waves change direction when entering a different medium. Speed and wavelength change; frequency stays constant.

Diffraction: waves spread out when passing through a gap or around an obstacle. More noticeable when gap ≈ wavelength.
Worked Example

Water wave: λ = 0.2 m, v = 2 m/s in deep water. Enters shallow water where v = 1.5 m/s. f = 2/0.2 = 10 Hz (unchanged). New λ = 1.5/10 = 0.15 m.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe reflection, refraction, diffraction and apply v = fλ during refraction
  • Getting there: Can describe behaviours but cannot apply wave equation during refraction
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish the three wave behaviours

Homework Suggestions

  • Calculate the frequency of a radio wave with λ = 1.5 m (v = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
  • Draw transverse and longitudinal waves with all labels
  • Research how a ripple tank demonstrates diffraction
