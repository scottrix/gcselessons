P23: Electromagnetic Spectrum

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding the electromagnetic spectrum, the properties of each type of EM radiation, and their uses and dangers.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the EM spectrum in order of wavelength/frequency
  • Give uses and dangers for each type of EM radiation
  • Describe shared properties of all EM waves
  • Use c = fλ for electromagnetic waves

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of wave properties (P22)
  • Knowledge of the wave equation v = fλ

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Prisms and torches (for dispersion demo)
  • UV beads

Lesson 1: The EM Spectrum

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Rainbow in a Box

Shine a torch through a prism. "What happens? Are there waves we cannot see?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
All EM waves travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum. All are transverse waves. All can be reflected, refracted, and diffracted.

The EM spectrum (longest λ to shortest):
- Radio waves: communication (radio/TV)
- Microwaves: cooking, mobile phones, satellite
- Infrared: heating, remote controls, thermal imaging
- Visible light: sight, photosynthesis, fibre optics
- Ultraviolet: sterilisation, fluorescent lamps, sunbeds
- X-rays: medical imaging, security scanning
- Gamma rays: radiotherapy, sterilising equipment

Key relationships: shorter λ → higher f → higher energy. All travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum.

Dangers: UV causes skin cancer and cataracts. X-rays and gamma rays are ionising and can cause cancer.
Worked Example

Frequency of a microwave with λ = 0.03 m: f = c ÷ λ = (3 × 10⁸) ÷ 0.03 = 1 × 10¹⁰ Hz = 10 GHz

Key Equation:
c = fλ (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can order the EM spectrum, give uses for each, and calculate f/λ
  • Getting there: Can order but struggles with calculations
  • Not yet: Cannot order the EM spectrum

Lesson 2: EM Wave Applications

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Detailed uses: radio waves diffract around buildings. Microwaves heat food (water absorbs them). Infrared used in remote controls and thermal cameras. Visible light carries data through fibre optics. UV kills bacteria. X-rays pass through soft tissue but not bone. Gamma rays destroy cancer cells.
Worked Example

Radio station at 98.5 MHz: λ = c/f = (3 × 10⁸) ÷ (98.5 × 10⁶) = 3.05 m

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe uses and dangers of all EM wave types
  • Getting there: Can list uses but cannot explain why specific types are chosen
  • Not yet: Cannot link EM properties to applications

Homework Suggestions

  • Create an EM spectrum poster with wavelengths, frequencies, uses, and dangers
  • Calculate wavelength for three different frequencies of EM radiation
  • Research health risks of UV exposure
