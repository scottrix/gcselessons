P26: Black Body Radiation

Higher OnlyAll Boards

Understanding how all objects emit and absorb electromagnetic radiation, the ideal black body model, and cosmic microwave background radiation.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe how all objects emit and absorb EM radiation
  • Explain what a perfect black body is
  • Describe how radiation depends on temperature
  • Explain how a pyrometer measures temperature
  • Describe the discovery of cosmic microwave background radiation

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of the EM spectrum (P23)
  • Knowledge of thermal energy and temperature

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Infrared thermometer or thermal imaging camera
  • Black and shiny surfaces (for emission comparison)

Lesson 1: Black Bodies and Thermal Radiation

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
All objects above 0 K emit EM radiation. At room temperature, mainly infrared. As temperature increases, more radiation emitted and peak wavelength shifts to shorter wavelengths. At ~500°C, objects glow red. At very high T, emit all visible colours (appear white).

Black body: idealised object that absorbs all radiation incident on it and emits radiation based only on its temperature. A small hole in a hollow object approximates a black body.

Temperature effects:
- Higher T → more radiation per second
- Higher T → peak wavelength shifts shorter
- Total energy ∝ T⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann law)

Cosmic Microwave Background: discovered 1965 by Penzias and Wilson. Uniform microwave radiation from all directions. Temperature 2.7 K. Evidence for the Big Bang — leftover radiation from when the universe was extremely hot, now cooled by expansion.
Worked Example

Object A at 300 K, Object B at 600 K. B emits (600/300)⁴ = 2⁴ = 16 times more radiation per second than A. B's peak wavelength is half of A's.

Key Relationships:
Total energy radiated ∝ T⁴
Peak wavelength ∝ 1/T
All objects above 0 K emit thermal radiation

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe black body radiation, explain temperature effects, and link to CMB
  • Getting there: Can describe thermal radiation but cannot explain black body model
  • Not yet: Cannot explain how temperature affects radiation

Lesson 2: Applications and the Big Bang

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Applications:
- Astronomy: star colour indicates temperature (blue hotter than red)
- Thermal imaging: detects infrared for building heat loss, medical diagnosis
- Pyrometers: measure furnace temperature without contact
- Greenhouse effect: Earth absorbs visible light, re-emits infrared; CO₂ traps it

Big Bang and CMB: universe began as hot dense point ~13.8 billion years ago. As it expanded, it cooled. CMB is leftover radiation from when universe was ~380 000 years old. Now at 2.7 K in microwave region.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain applications and link CMB to Big Bang
  • Getting there: Can describe applications but cannot explain Big Bang connection
  • Not yet: Cannot link temperature to radiation

Homework Suggestions

  • Research thermal imaging camera applications
  • Explain why a black t-shirt feels warmer in the sun
  • Research Penzias and Wilson's discovery of the CMB
