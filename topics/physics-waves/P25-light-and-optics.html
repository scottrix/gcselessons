P25: Light and Optics

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding reflection, refraction, lenses, and total internal reflection through ray diagrams and optical principles.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Draw ray diagrams for plane mirrors, convex and concave lenses
  • Apply the law of reflection
  • Describe refraction and draw ray diagrams at boundaries
  • Explain total internal reflection and its applications
  • Describe how lenses form images

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of wave properties (P22)
  • Knowledge of the EM spectrum (P23)
  • Ability to use a protractor

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator, protractor, ruler
  • Plane mirror, glass block, convex lens
  • Ray box or laser pointer

Lesson 1: Reflection

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection (measured from the normal).

Properties of images in plane mirrors: same size, same distance behind mirror, virtual, laterally inverted.

Ray diagrams: draw mirror, incident ray, normal, reflected ray at equal angle. Extend reflected ray behind mirror with dashed line to locate image.
Law of Reflection:
Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection
Common Misconception: Students measure angles from the mirror surface instead of the normal.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can draw accurate ray diagrams and state the law of reflection
  • Getting there: Can state the law but makes errors in diagrams
  • Not yet: Cannot draw reflection ray diagrams

Lesson 2: Refraction

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Refraction: change in direction when entering a different medium. Denser → bends towards normal. Less dense → bends away. Frequency stays constant. Speed and wavelength change.

Refractive index: n = c/v. Water: 1.33. Glass: 1.5. Diamond: 2.42.

Snell's Law (Higher): n₁sin(θ₁) = n₂sin(θ₂)

Total internal reflection (Higher): when light goes from denser to less dense medium at angle > critical angle, all light reflects back. Applications: fibre optics, prisms in binoculars, sparkling diamonds.
Worked Example

Light from air (n=1) into glass (n=1.5) at 30°. 1×sin(30°) = 1.5×sin(θ₂). sin(θ₂) = 0.333. θ₂ = 19.5° (bends towards normal).

Key Equations:
n = c/v
n₁sin(θ₁) = n₂sin(θ₂)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can draw refraction diagrams, apply Snell's Law, explain total internal reflection
  • Getting there: Can draw diagrams but cannot apply Snell's Law
  • Not yet: Cannot draw refraction diagrams

Lesson 3: Lenses

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Convex (converging) lens: thicker in middle, converges parallel rays to principal focus F. Focal length f = distance from lens to F.

Concave (diverging) lens: thinner in middle, diverges rays (appear to come from virtual focus).

Three principal rays for convex lenses:
1. Parallel to axis → through focus on other side
2. Through centre → straight line
3. Through focus → emerges parallel

Image formation (convex): beyond 2F → real, inverted, diminished. At 2F → real, inverted, same size. Between F and 2F → real, inverted, magnified. Within F → virtual, upright, magnified.

Thin lens equation (Higher): 1/f = 1/u + 1/v. Magnification = v/u.
Worked Example

Object 15 cm from lens, f = 10 cm. 1/10 = 1/15 + 1/v. 1/v = 1/30. v = 30 cm. Magnification = 30/15 = 2×.

Key Equations:
1/f = 1/u + 1/v
Magnification = v ÷ u

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can draw lens ray diagrams, use 1/f = 1/u + 1/v, describe images
  • Getting there: Can draw diagrams but cannot use thin lens equation
  • Not yet: Cannot draw lens ray diagrams

Homework Suggestions

  • Draw ray diagrams for all five object positions with a convex lens (f = 10 cm)
  • Calculate image distance and magnification for three object distances
  • Research how a camera lens and human eye use convex lenses
