P24: Sound and Ultrasound

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding sound as a longitudinal wave, pitch and frequency, loudness and amplitude, and the uses of ultrasound.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe sound as a longitudinal wave
  • Explain frequency/pitch and amplitude/loudness relationships
  • State human hearing range (20 Hz – 20 kHz)
  • Describe how ultrasound is used in medicine and industry
  • Calculate distances from echo times

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Tuning forks
  • Signal generator and speaker
  • Oscilloscope (software or virtual)

Lesson 1: Properties of Sound

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Sound in Action

Strike a tuning fork near polystyrene pieces on a speaker cone. They bounce. "What does this tell us about sound?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Sound is produced by vibrating objects creating compressions and rarefactions (longitudinal wave). Needs a medium (cannot travel through vacuum). Speed of sound in air: ~340 m/s. In water: ~1500 m/s. In steel: ~6000 m/s.

Pitch and frequency: pitch depends on frequency. Higher frequency = higher pitch. Human range: 20 Hz – 20 kHz. Below 20 Hz: infrasound. Above 20 kHz: ultrasound.

Loudness and amplitude: loudness depends on amplitude. Greater amplitude = louder. Louder sounds transfer more energy.

On an oscilloscope: frequency from time period, amplitude from wave height.
Worked Example

Sound at 440 Hz, v = 340 m/s. λ = 340 ÷ 440 = 0.773 m. T = 1 ÷ 440 = 0.00227 s = 2.27 ms.

Key Equations:
v = fλ
T = 1/f

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe sound as longitudinal wave, explain pitch/loudness, use v = fλ
  • Getting there: Can describe sound but struggles with calculations
  • Not yet: Cannot describe sound waves or distinguish pitch from loudness

Lesson 2: Ultrasound

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Ultrasound: sound with frequency > 20 kHz. Works by sending pulses that reflect off objects. Distance = (speed × time) ÷ 2.

Uses: medical imaging (sonograms), kidney stone detection, industrial NDT (cracks in metal), sonar (sea depth), ultrasonic cleaning.
Worked Example

Ship sends ultrasound, echo returns in 0.4 s. v = 1500 m/s. Distance = 1500 × 0.4 ÷ 2 = 300 m

Key Equation:
Distance = (speed × time) ÷ 2

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain ultrasound, give uses, calculate distances from echoes
  • Getting there: Can describe uses but cannot perform calculations
  • Not yet: Cannot explain ultrasound reflection or calculate distances

Homework Suggestions

  • Calculate wavelength of 40 kHz ultrasound in air
  • Research fetal heartbeat monitoring with Doppler ultrasound
  • Compare speed of sound in air, water, and steel
