P28: Electromagnets and the Motor Effect

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding how current-carrying conductors experience a force in a magnetic field, and how this is used in motors and loudspeakers.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe how electromagnets work
  • Explain what happens when a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field
  • Use Fleming's left-hand rule to determine force direction
  • Describe the structure and operation of a DC motor
  • Describe the structure and operation of a loudspeaker
  • Explain how to increase the force on the conductor

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of magnetic fields (P27)
  • Basic knowledge of electric circuits (P6, P7)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Bar magnets, wire, battery, switch
  • Motor effect demonstration kit (or simulation)
  • DC motor kit

Lesson 1: Forces on Current-Carrying Conductors

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Motor Effect Demo

Place a wire between two magnets, connect to a battery. The wire jumps. "What force is acting? How can we predict the direction?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
The motor effect:
- When a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force
- This is the motor effect
- The force is due to the interaction between the magnetic field of the current and the external magnetic field
- No force if the current is parallel to the field lines

Fleming's left-hand rule:
- Thumb = direction of force (motion)
- First finger = direction of magnetic field (N to S)
- Second finger = direction of current
- All three are perpendicular to each other

Increasing the force:
- Increase current
- Increase strength of magnetic field
- Use a coil of wire (more turns) instead of a single wire
- Use a soft iron core in the coil

Force equation (Higher):
F = BIL
F = force (N), B = magnetic flux density (T), I = current (A), L = length of conductor in field (m)
Worked Example

A wire of length 0.2 m carries 3 A in a field of 0.5 T. F = 0.5 × 3 × 0.2 = 0.3 N

Key Equation:
F = BIL
(B in tesla, I in amps, L in metres)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain the motor effect, use Fleming's left-hand rule, apply F = BIL
  • Getting there: Can apply Fleming's left-hand rule but cannot use F = BIL
  • Not yet: Cannot explain why a force acts on a current-carrying conductor

Lesson 2: The DC Motor

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Structure of a DC motor:
- Coil of wire (armature) mounted between magnets
- Split-ring commutator reverses current every half-turn
- Brushes maintain electrical contact while coil rotates
- Power supply provides current

How it works:
1. Current flows through coil in magnetic field
2. Fleming's left-hand rule: force acts on each side of coil
3. Forces are in opposite directions → coil rotates
4. At vertical position, commutator reverses current
5. Force direction reverses → coil continues rotating in same direction

Increasing speed:
- Increase current or voltage
- Increase number of turns on coil
- Increase magnetic field strength

Uses: fans, pumps, mixers, drills, washing machines, electric vehicles

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe DC motor structure, explain how it works, identify the role of the commutator
  • Getting there: Can describe structure but cannot explain how continuous rotation is achieved
  • Not yet: Cannot describe the basic components of a DC motor

Lesson 3: Loudspeakers and Applications

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Loudspeaker:
- Coil of wire attached to a cone, placed in a magnetic field
- Audio signal (alternating current) flows through the coil
- Current changes direction rapidly → force changes direction rapidly
- Coil and cone vibrate → produce sound waves
- Frequency of vibration = frequency of sound. Amplitude of vibration = loudness

Galvanometer (analogue meter):
- Coil pivots in magnetic field
- When current flows, force causes rotation
- Spring provides restoring force
- Angle of deflection proportional to current

Commutator in motors: without it, coil would oscillate back and forth. Split-ring reverses current at the right moment to maintain rotation.

Use of soft iron core: increases the strength of the magnetic field inside the coil, increasing the force.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain how loudspeakers and galvanometers work using the motor effect
  • Getting there: Can describe loudspeaker but cannot link current changes to vibration
  • Not yet: Cannot apply the motor effect to practical devices

Homework Suggestions

  • Use Fleming's left-hand rule to determine force direction for 5 different setups
  • Research how a brushed DC motor differs from a brushless motor
  • Calculate force using F = BIL for three different values
