P27: Magnets and Magnetic Fields

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding magnetic poles, magnetic fields, the forces between magnets, and how to draw magnetic field patterns.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the properties of permanent magnets
  • Explain the forces between magnetic poles
  • Draw magnetic field patterns for bar magnets and Earth
  • Describe how induced magnetism works
  • Identify magnetic materials (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt)

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of forces from KS3
  • Knowledge of the particle model (P10)

Materials & Equipment

  • Bar magnets, compasses
  • Iron filings and paper
  • Various materials to test for magnetism (iron, steel, copper, wood, plastic)

Lesson 1: Magnetic Poles and Fields

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Compass Challenge

Give each student a compass. Ask them to bring a bar magnet near it. "What happens? Why does the compass needle move?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Properties of magnets:
- Have two poles: north (N) and south (S)
- Like poles repel, unlike poles attract
- Can attract magnetic materials (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) without being permanent magnets
- Can be permanent (hard magnetic materials) or temporary (soft magnetic materials)

Magnetic fields:
- A magnetic field exists around every magnet
- The direction is defined as the direction a compass north pole points
- Field lines go from N to S outside the magnet, S to N inside
- Closer field lines = stronger field
- Strongest at the poles

Field patterns:
- Bar magnet: loops from N to S outside, S to N inside
- Two unlike poles: lines connect between them (attract)
- Two like poles: lines repel each other (no lines between them)

Induced magnetism:
- A magnetic material becomes temporarily magnetic when placed near a magnet
- Iron is easily magnetised and demagnetised (soft magnetic material)
- Steel retains magnetism (hard magnetic material, used for permanent magnets)

Earth's magnetic field:
- Earth acts like a giant bar magnet
- Magnetic north pole is near the geographic south pole (compasses point north)
- The magnetic field protects us from solar wind and cosmic radiation
Common Misconception: Students think the north pole of a compass points to the geographic North Pole because there is a magnetic north there. Actually, the magnetic pole near geographic north is a magnetic south pole (which attracts the compass north).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe magnetic poles, draw field patterns, explain attraction/repulsion
  • Getting there: Can describe poles but cannot draw field patterns accurately
  • Not yet: Cannot describe basic properties of magnets

Lesson 2: Magnetic Materials and Earth's Field

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Magnetic materials:
- Ferromagnetic: iron, steel, nickel, cobalt (strongly attracted to magnets)
- Paramagnetic: aluminium, platinum (weakly attracted)
- Diamagnetic: copper, gold, water (very weakly repelled)

Induced magnetism and temporary magnets:
- Iron becomes magnetic near a magnet (induced magnetism)
- Iron loses magnetism when removed from the magnetic field (soft magnetic material)
- Steel retains magnetism (hard magnetic material)

Uses of temporary magnets:
- Scrapyard cranes: electromagnet picks up cars, turned off to release
- Magnetic door catches, fridge magnets (using soft iron backing)

Earth's magnetic field:
- The geomagnetic field is generated by convection currents of molten iron in the outer core
- The field is approximately dipolar (like a bar magnet)
- Magnetic declination: angle between true north and magnetic north (varies by location and time)
- The field strength varies across Earth's surface

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can identify magnetic materials, explain induced magnetism, and describe Earth's magnetic field
  • Getting there: Can identify magnetic materials but cannot explain induced magnetism
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish magnetic from non-magnetic materials

Homework Suggestions

  • Test household objects for magnetism and classify them as magnetic or non-magnetic
  • Draw the magnetic field pattern between two bar magnets in four configurations
  • Research how a compass works and explain why it points north
