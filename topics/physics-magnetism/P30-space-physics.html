P30: Space Physics

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding the Solar System, the life cycles of stars, nuclear fusion, and evidence for the Big Bang theory.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the structure of the Solar System
  • Explain how gravity affects orbital motion
  • Describe the life cycle of a star
  • Explain nuclear fusion in stars
  • Describe evidence for the Big Bang (red shift, CMB)

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of gravity (P19)
  • Basic knowledge of nuclear reactions (P14)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Solar System scale model materials (if doing practical)
  • Red shift simulation

Lesson 1: The Solar System and Orbits

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Planet Order

Give students planet cards. Ask them to put them in order from the Sun. "What holds them in their orbits?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
The Solar System:
- Star: Sun
- 8 planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune
- Inner (terrestrial) planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (rocky)
- Outer (gas giant) planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune (mainly gas/ice)
- Dwarf planets: Pluto, Ceres, Eris (do not clear their orbit)
- Other objects: asteroids, comets, meteors, moons

Gravity and orbits:
- Gravity provides the centripetal force that keeps planets in orbit
- Greater mass → stronger gravitational pull
- Greater distance → weaker gravitational pull
- Orbital speed decreases with distance from the Sun
- Moon orbits Earth; Earth orbits Sun; Sun orbits centre of Milky Way galaxy

The seasons (not due to distance from Sun):
- Earth's axis tilted at 23.5° to the orbital plane
- As Earth orbits the Sun, different hemispheres receive more direct sunlight
- Summer: hemisphere tilted towards Sun → more direct sunlight, longer days
- Winter: hemisphere tilted away from Sun → less direct sunlight, shorter days
Common Misconception: Students think seasons are caused by Earth being closer to or further from the Sun. Seasons are caused by Earth's axial tilt.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe the Solar System, explain orbits, and describe the cause of seasons
  • Getting there: Can list planets but cannot explain orbits or seasons
  • Not yet: Cannot describe the structure of the Solar System

Lesson 2: Stellar Evolution and the Big Bang

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Life cycle of a star (low/medium mass — like the Sun):
1. Nebula: cloud of gas and dust (mainly hydrogen)
2. Gravity pulls gas together → protostar
3. Hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium (nuclear fusion) → star (main sequence)
4. Hydrogen runs out → red giant (swells, outer layers cool and redden)
5. Outer layers expelled → planetary nebula
6. Remaining core → white dwarf (cools over billions of years)

Life cycle of a star (high mass):
1. Same as above up to step 3
4. Hydrogen runs out → supergiant (much larger)
5. Core fuses heavier elements up to iron
6. Iron fusion absorbs energy → core collapses → supernova explosion
7. Remaining core → neutron star (or black hole if very massive)

Nuclear fusion:
- Light nuclei (hydrogen) forced together under extreme temperature and pressure
- They fuse to form a heavier nucleus (helium)
- Mass of products < mass of reactants → mass difference released as energy
- E = mc² (Einstein's mass-energy equivalence)
- Happens at temperatures >10 million °C
- Powers all main sequence stars

Evidence for the Big Bang:
1. Red shift: light from distant galaxies is shifted towards longer (redder) wavelengths. This shows galaxies are moving away from us → universe is expanding
2. Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation (CMB): uniform microwave radiation from all directions, temperature 2.7 K. Leftover radiation from when the universe was ~380 000 years old
3. Hubble's law: more distant galaxies move away faster

The Big Bang theory:
- Universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense point
- It has been expanding and cooling ever since
- Matter clumped together under gravity to form stars and galaxies
- Cannot explain what caused the Big Bang or what came before
Key Concept:
E = mc²
(Mass-energy equivalence: mass can be converted to energy during nuclear fusion)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe both stellar life cycles, explain fusion, describe Big Bang evidence
  • Getting there: Can describe the Sun-like life cycle but struggles with high-mass stars and fusion
  • Not yet: Cannot describe stellar life cycles or explain the Big Bang

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a diagram showing the life cycle of a star with labels
  • Research what happens inside a black hole
  • Explain why red shift is evidence for the expanding universe
  • Calculate the energy released if 1 kg of mass is converted to energy using E = mc²
