P29: Electromagnetic Induction

FoundationHigherAll Boards

Understanding how a voltage is induced when a conductor moves through a magnetic field, and how this is used in generators and transformers.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how a voltage is induced in a conductor moving through a magnetic field
  • State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction
  • State Lenz's law
  • Describe the structure and operation of an AC generator
  • Explain how a transformer works

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of magnetic fields (P27, P28)
  • Knowledge of AC/DC electricity (P6)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Coil of wire, galvanometer, bar magnets
  • Generator demonstration kit
  • Transformer kit (or simulation)

Lesson 1: Inducing a Voltage

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Induction Demo

Move a magnet into a coil connected to a galvanometer. The needle deflects. "What causes the voltage? Does the magnet need to be moving?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Electromagnetic induction:
- A voltage is induced when a conductor cuts through magnetic field lines
- The conductor must be part of a complete circuit for a current to flow
- The induced voltage depends on:
- Speed of movement (faster → bigger voltage)
- Strength of magnetic field (stronger → bigger voltage)
- Number of turns in the coil (more turns → bigger voltage)

Faraday's law (Higher):
- The induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux
- In symbols: ε = -N × ΔΦ/Δt
- EMF is induced when magnetic flux through a coil changes

Lenz's law (Higher):
- The direction of the induced current is such that it opposes the change that caused it
- This is a consequence of the conservation of energy

AC generator (dynamo):
- Coil rotates in a magnetic field
- Flux through the coil changes → voltage induced
- Voltage alternates as coil rotates (AC output)
- Slip rings and brushes maintain electrical contact
- Increasing speed of rotation → increases frequency and voltage
- Increasing magnetic field strength → increases voltage
- Increasing number of turns → increases voltage
Worked Example

A coil has 100 turns and the magnetic flux through it changes from 0.02 Wb to 0 Wb in 0.1 s. ε = N × ΔΦ/Δt = 100 × 0.02/0.1 = 20 V

Key Equations:
ε = -N × ΔΦ/Δt
(Faraday's law: induced EMF = number of turns × rate of change of flux)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain how voltage is induced, state Faraday's law, describe AC generator
  • Getting there: Can describe induction but cannot state Faraday's law
  • Not yet: Cannot explain why moving a conductor induces a voltage

Lesson 2: Transformers

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Transformer:
- Two coils of wire (primary and secondary) wrapped around an iron core
- Alternating current in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic field in the core
- This changing field passes through the secondary coil
- A voltage is induced in the secondary coil (electromagnetic induction)

Step-up transformer: more turns on secondary → higher voltage, lower current
Step-down transformer: fewer turns on secondary → lower voltage, higher current

Transformer equation:
Vp/Vs = Np/Ns

Ideal transformer:
- Power in = Power out
- Vp × Ip = Vs × Is

Uses in the National Grid:
- Step-up transformers increase voltage for transmission (reduces energy loss in cables)
- Step-down transformers decrease voltage for safe domestic use (230 V)
Worked Example

Primary: 100 turns, 230 V. Secondary: 2000 turns. Vs = (2000/100) × 230 = 4600 V. Step-up transformer.

Key Equations:
Vp/Vs = Np/Ns
Vp × Ip = Vs × Is (power conservation)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain transformer operation, calculate using Vp/Vs = Np/Ns, explain National Grid role
  • Getting there: Can describe transformer but cannot calculate output voltage
  • Not yet: Cannot explain how a transformer works

Homework Suggestions

  • Design a generator with 200 turns and predict the output voltage if it rotates at 50 Hz in a 0.5 T field
  • Calculate the secondary voltage and current for a step-up transformer with 10:1 ratio
  • Research why high voltage is used for national grid transmission
