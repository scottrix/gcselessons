N11: Fractions and Ratio

Foundation Higher All Boards

Work with fractions in ratio contexts. Find fractions of amounts, share quantities in given ratios, solve problems combining fractions and ratios.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Calculate fractions of amounts (including non-unit fractions)
  • Express one quantity as a fraction of another
  • Share quantities in a given ratio (including 3-part ratios)
  • Convert between fractions and ratios
  • Solve multi-step problems combining fractions and ratios

Prerequisites

  • Fraction arithmetic (add, subtract, multiply, divide)
  • Simplifying fractions
  • Equivalent fractions
  • Basic ratio notation (a:b)

Materials & Equipment

  • Counters or cubes for sharing
  • Bar model templates
  • Ratio tables
  • Scientific calculator

Lesson 1: Fractions of Amounts and Ratio Basics

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Fraction of Amount

Find: 1/4 of 20, 2/5 of 30, 3/8 of 40, 5/6 of 42

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Fraction of Amount: Divide by denominator, multiply by numerator.
3/5 of 40 = (40 ÷ 5) × 3 = 8 × 3 = 24

Fraction as Operator: "of" means multiply. 2/3 of 15 = 2/3 × 15 = 10

Ratio Notation: a:b means for every a of first, there are b of second.
Total parts = a + b. Fraction of total = a/(a+b) and b/(a+b).

Sharing in Ratio: Total ÷ total parts = value of 1 part. Multiply by each share.
Share £60 in ratio 2:3 → Total parts = 5. 1 part = £12. Shares: £24 and £36.
Worked Example 1: Fraction of Amount

Find 4/7 of 56 kg.
Method 1: 56 ÷ 7 = 8, 8 × 4 = 32 kg
Method 2: 4/7 × 56 = (4×56)/7 = 224/7 = 32 kg
Answer: 32 kg

Worked Example 2: Sharing in Ratio

Share 84 sweets in ratio 4:3.
Total parts = 4 + 3 = 7
1 part = 84 ÷ 7 = 12 sweets
First share: 4 × 12 = 48 sweets
Second share: 3 × 12 = 36 sweets
Check: 48 + 36 = 84 ✓
Answer: 48 and 36

Worked Example 3: Ratio to Fraction

Ratio of boys to girls is 3:5. What fraction are boys?
Total parts = 3 + 5 = 8
Boys = 3/8 of total
Girls = 5/8 of total
If 240 students: boys = 3/8 × 240 = 90, girls = 150

Fraction of Amount:
(a/b) of Q = (Q ÷ b) × a = aQ/b

Ratio a:b → Total parts = a+b
Value of 1 part = Total ÷ (a+b)
Share 1 = a × Value, Share 2 = b × Value

Ratio → Fraction:
a:b → a/(a+b) and b/(a+b)

Fraction → Ratio:
Fraction p/q of total → ratio p : (q-p)

Common Misconception

Ratio 2:3 means 2/3 of the total

Ratio 2:3 means 2 parts to 3 parts. Total = 5 parts. First is 2/5, second is 3/5.

Differentiation

Support:
Bar models for visual sharing. "Divide by bottom, times top" chant. Ratio tables with columns.
Stretch:
3-part ratios (2:3:5). Given one share, find total (inverse). Algebraic: share x in ratio a:b, express shares in terms of x.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Mixed Practice

1) 5/8 of £72 = £45
2) Share 100 in ratio 3:2 → 60, 40
3) Ratio 3:7, total 500 → 150, 350
4) 2/5 are red, rest blue. Ratio red:blue = 2:3

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates fractions of amounts; shares in 2-3 part ratios; converts between fractions/ratios
  • Getting there: Unit fractions OK; non-unit fraction errors; forgets to add ratio parts for total
  • Not yet: No method for fraction of amount; confuses ratio parts with fractions

Lesson 2: Combined Fraction and Ratio Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Inverse Problems

3/5 of a number is 24. Find the number. (24 ÷ 3 × 5 = 40)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Finding Original Amount: If fraction of amount known, divide by fraction (multiply by reciprocal).
3/4 of x = 30 → x = 30 ÷ 3/4 = 30 × 4/3 = 40

Ratio with Difference: "A gets 12 more than B, ratio 5:3"
Difference in parts = 5 - 3 = 2 parts = 12 → 1 part = 6
A = 5×6=30, B=3×6=18

Changing Ratios: "Ratio 3:2. After each gets 5 more, ratio 4:3"
Use algebra: 3x+5 / 2x+5 = 4/3 → cross-multiply.
Worked Example 1: Finding Original from Fraction

2/3 of a tank holds 40 litres. Capacity?
Let capacity = C
2/3 × C = 40
C = 40 × 3/2 = 60 litres
Check: 2/3 of 60 = 40 ✓

Worked Example 2: Ratio with Difference

Money shared in ratio 7:4. A gets £18 more than B. Total amount?
Difference in parts = 7 - 4 = 3 parts = £18
1 part = £6
Total parts = 7 + 4 = 11
Total = 11 × £6 = £66
Check: A = £42, B = £24, diff = £18 ✓

Worked Example 3: Changing Ratio (Algebraic)

Ratio of red to blue counters is 3:5. Add 6 red, remove 2 blue. New ratio 2:3. Original total?
Let red = 3x, blue = 5x
New: (3x+6) : (5x-2) = 2 : 3
3(3x+6) = 2(5x-2)
9x + 18 = 10x - 4
x = 22
Original: red = 66, blue = 110, total = 176

Inverse Fraction Problems:
If (a/b) of x = y, then x = y × (b/a)

Ratio with Difference:
Ratio a:b, difference = d
1 part = d/(a-b) (assuming a>b)
Total = (a+b) × d/(a-b)

Changing Ratio:
Set up algebra with variable x for original parts. Form equation from new ratio.

Common Misconception

3/4 of x = 12, so x = 12 × 3/4 = 9

3/4 of x = 12 → x = 12 ÷ 3/4 = 12 × 4/3 = 16. Divide by the fraction (multiply by reciprocal).

Differentiation

Support:
Bar models for inverse problems. "If 3 parts = 12, 1 part = 4, 4 parts = 16". Scaffolded ratio difference problems.
Stretch:
Ratio problems with 3 quantities changing. "Ratio a:b:c, after changes..." Fraction/ratio problems with percentages combined.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Challenge

In a class, ratio boys:girls = 4:5. 4 boys leave, 3 girls join. New ratio 3:5. Original class size?
4x-4 / 5x+3 = 3/5 → 5(4x-4) = 3(5x+3) → 20x-20 = 15x+9 → 5x=29 → x=5.8? Not integer.
Check: 4:5, 4 leave, 3 join → 3:5? 4x-4/5x+3=3/5 → 20x-20=15x+9 → 5x=29. Problem has no integer solution.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves inverse fraction problems; handles ratio difference and changing ratio problems
  • Getting there: Inverse fraction method; ratio difference OK; struggles with algebraic changing ratio
  • Not yet: Multiplies instead of divides for inverse; no strategy for ratio problems

Homework Suggestions

  • Fraction of amount and sharing in ratio worksheet
  • "Find the original amount" fraction problems
  • Ratio difference problems (e.g., "A gets £15 more...")
  • CorbettMaths "Ratio" and "Fraction of Amount" exercises
