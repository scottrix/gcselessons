N2: Four Operations

Foundation Higher All Boards

Apply the four operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) to integers, decimals, and negative numbers. Includes formal written methods and calculator use.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Add and subtract integers and decimals using column methods
  • Multiply and divide integers and decimals (including by powers of 10)
  • Apply operations with negative numbers using sign rules
  • Solve multi-step problems choosing appropriate operations
  • Use calculators efficiently for complex calculations

Prerequisites

  • Place value up to millions and thousandths
  • Times tables up to 12 × 12
  • Basic column addition/subtraction
  • Understanding of negative numbers on a number line

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator
  • Squared paper (for column alignment)
  • Place value grid
  • Mini whiteboards for practice

Lesson 1: Addition and Subtraction (Integers & Decimals)

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Mental Maths Challenge

Calculate mentally: 47 + 38, 92 - 57, 12.5 + 3.7, 10 - 4.6

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Column Addition: Align decimal points. Add from right to left. Carry to next column when sum ≥ 10.
Example: 45.67 + 23.8
45.67
+ 23.80
-------
69.47

Column Subtraction: Align decimal points. Subtract top from bottom. Borrow from left when needed.
Example: 82.4 - 37.65
82.40
- 37.65
-------
44.75

Negative Numbers: Use number line. Adding negative = subtracting. Subtracting negative = adding.
5 + (-3) = 2, 5 - (-3) = 8, -4 + (-6) = -10, -4 - (-6) = 2
Worked Example 1: Decimal Subtraction with Borrowing

Calculate: 204.5 - 87.68

Step 1: Align decimals, add placeholder zero
204.50
- 87.68
--------
Step 2: Borrow from tenths (5 becomes 4, 0 becomes 10)
204.50
- 87.68
--------
2 (10-8)
Step 3: Borrow from units (4 becomes 3, 4 becomes 14)
14-6=8
Step 4: Borrow from tens (0 becomes 9, 2 becomes 1, but tens is 0 so borrow from hundreds)
1 9 14 4 10
1 1 6 8 2
Answer: 116.82

Worked Example 2: Negative Number Operations

Calculate: -12 - (-7) + (-15) - 8

Step 1: Simplify signs: -12 + 7 - 15 - 8
Step 2: Group positives and negatives: -(12 + 15 + 8) + 7 = -35 + 7
Step 3: -35 + 7 = -28
Answer: -28

Sign Rules for Addition/Subtraction:
+ + = + (e.g., 5 + 3 = 8)
+ - = - (e.g., 5 + (-3) = 2)
- + = - (e.g., -5 + 3 = -2)
- - = + (e.g., -5 - (-3) = -2)

"Two negatives make a positive" only applies to subtracting a negative or multiplying two negatives.

Common Misconception

-5 - 3 = -2 (thinking two negatives make positive)

-5 - 3 = -8. You are subtracting 3 from -5, going further negative. -5 + (-3) = -8.

Differentiation

Support:
Use number lines for negatives. Provide squared paper for column alignment. Limit to 2 decimal places.
Stretch:
Multi-step word problems. Missing number problems (e.g., 4_._ + _._7 = 82.34). Add/subtract in standard form.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Spot the Error

12.6 - 4.8 = 8.2 ✓
-7 + (-4) = -3 ✗ (should be -11)
15 - (-6) = 9 ✗ (should be 21)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Accurate column methods with decimals; correct negative number arithmetic; solves multi-step problems
  • Getting there: Good with positives; sign errors with negatives; decimal alignment issues
  • Not yet: Cannot align decimals; confuses borrowing; no strategy for negatives

Lesson 2: Multiplication and Division

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Times Table Speed

Complete: 7×8, 9×6, 12×11, 56÷8, 84÷7, 108÷9

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Short Multiplication: Multiply each digit, carry tens. For decimals: ignore decimals, multiply, then count total decimal places.
Example: 3.4 × 2.5 → 34 × 25 = 850 → 2 decimal places total → 8.50 = 8.5

Long Multiplication: Multiply by each digit of bottom number, shift left, add results.
Example: 124 × 23 = 124×3 + 124×20 = 372 + 2480 = 2852

Division: Short division (bus stop) for single-digit divisors. Long division for two-digit divisors.
Decimal division: Move decimal in divisor and dividend same amount.
Example: 4.56 ÷ 1.2 → 45.6 ÷ 12 = 3.8

Multiplying/Dividing by Powers of 10: Digits move, decimal point stays. ×10 → left 1, ÷10 → right 1.
Worked Example: Decimal Multiplication

Calculate: 2.34 × 1.6

Step 1: Remove decimals: 234 × 16
Step 2: Long multiplication:

Step 4: Apply: 3.744

Multiplication/Division by Powers of 10:
× 10 → digits move 1 place LEFT
× 100 → digits move 2 places LEFT
× 1000 → digits move 3 places LEFT
÷ 10 → digits move 1 place RIGHT
÷ 100 → digits move 2 places RIGHT

Negative Rules:
× or ÷ same signs → positive
× or ÷ different signs → negative
(-) × (-) = (+) (-) ÷ (-) = (+)
(+) × (-) = (-) (+) ÷ (-) = (-)

Common Misconception

0.5 × 0.2 = 1.0 (because 5×2=10)

0.5 × 0.2 = 0.10 = 0.1. Count decimal places: 1+1=2, so answer has 2 decimal places.

Differentiation

Support:
Grid method for multiplication. Chunking for division. Stick to ×/÷ by 2,3,4,5,10.
Stretch:
Multiply/divide in standard form. Recurring decimals as fractions. Compound measures problems.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Calculator Check

Estimate first, then calculate: 23.4 × 1.9 ≈ 23 × 2 = 46. Actual: 44.46
144 ÷ 0.12 = 14400 ÷ 12 = 1200

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Efficient written methods; correct decimal placement; handles negatives; estimates to check
  • Getting there: Can multiply integers; decimal placement errors; long division gaps
  • Not yet: Relies on calculator; no written method; place value confusion

Lesson 3: Mixed Problems and Calculator Efficiency

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Estimation Practice

Estimate: 28.7 × 4.9, 198.6 ÷ 3.1, -12.5 + 8.7 × 2

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Order of Operations (BIDMAS/BODMAS): Brackets, Indices, Division/Multiplication (left to right), Addition/Subtraction (left to right).
Calculator Skills: Use fraction button, ANS key, memory, brackets.
Example: (3.4 + 2.6)² ÷ (5.1 - 1.9)
Calculator: (3.4+2.6)x²÷(5.1-1.9) = 36 ÷ 3.2 = 11.25

Multi-step Problems: Read carefully, identify operations, work systematically, check answer makes sense.
Worked Example: Multi-step Problem

A plumber charges £45 call-out + £32 per hour. Job takes 3.5 hours. VAT at 20%. Total cost?

Labour: 32 × 3.5 = 112
Subtotal: 45 + 112 = 157
VAT: 157 × 0.20 = 31.40
Total: 157 + 31.40 = 188.40
Answer: £188.40

Worked Example: Calculator Efficiency

Calculate: √(4.5² + 6.2²) to 2 decimal places

Calculator: √(4.5x²+6.2x²) = √(20.25+38.44) = √58.69 = 7.661...
Answer: 7.66

BIDMAS Order:
B - Brackets ( ) [ ] { }
I - Indices x² √x x³
D/M - Division and Multiplication (left to right)
A/S - Addition and Subtraction (left to right)

Calculator Tips:
• Use brackets for numerator/denominator: (4.5+2.3)÷(6.1-1.8)
• ANS key uses previous answer
• SD button toggles fraction/decimal/surd
• x², x³, √, ³√ for powers and roots

Common Misconception

8 + 4 × 2 = 24 (working left to right)

8 + 4 × 2 = 8 + 8 = 16. Multiplication before addition.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide BIDMAS prompt card. Break multi-step problems into numbered steps. Use calculator for arithmetic, focus on structure.
Stretch:
Reverse operations (find input given output). Problems with multiple solutions. Algebraic multi-step.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Diagnostic Questions

1) 15 - 3 × 4 = ? (3)
2) (15 - 3) × 4 = ? (48)
3) -6 × -3 + 10 = ? (28)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies BIDMAS correctly; uses calculator efficiently; solves contextual problems; estimates to verify
  • Getting there: Knows BIDMAS but applies inconsistently; calculator syntax errors
  • Not yet: Works strictly left to right; cannot interpret worded problems

Homework Suggestions

  • Mixed four operations worksheet (integers, decimals, negatives)
  • Create 3 word problems requiring different operations; swap with peer
  • Calculator practice: Use ANS, fraction button, brackets for: (7.2+3.8)² ÷ √(6.25)
  • CorbettMaths "Four Operations" practice questions
