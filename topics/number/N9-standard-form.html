N9: Standard Form

Foundation Higher All Boards

Write numbers in standard form (A × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ A < 10). Convert between standard form and ordinary numbers. Calculate with standard form using index laws.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Convert large numbers to standard form (positive powers of 10)
  • Convert small numbers to standard form (negative powers of 10)
  • Convert from standard form to ordinary numbers
  • Compare and order numbers in standard form
  • Calculate with standard form (multiply, divide, add, subtract)

Prerequisites

  • Place value and powers of 10 (10, 100, 1000, 0.1, 0.01...)
  • Index laws (N7): 10ᵐ × 10ⁿ = 10^(m+n), 10ᵐ ÷ 10ⁿ = 10^(m-n)
  • Multiplying/dividing decimals by powers of 10
  • Calculator standard form mode (EXP or ×10ˣ button)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator (EXP / ×10ˣ button)
  • Place value chart showing powers of 10
  • Standard form matching cards
  • Real-world data (distances, masses, sizes)

Lesson 1: Converting To and From Standard Form

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Powers of 10

Write as powers of 10: 1000, 0.01, 1000000, 0.0001, 1, 10⁻⁴

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Standard Form: A × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer.

Large Numbers (n > 0): Move decimal point LEFT n places.
45000 = 4.5 × 10⁴ (decimal moves 4 places left)
7,200,000 = 7.2 × 10⁶

Small Numbers (n < 0): Move decimal point RIGHT |n| places.
0.0032 = 3.2 × 10⁻³ (decimal moves 3 places right)
0.0000056 = 5.6 × 10⁻⁶

From Standard Form: Reverse the process. Positive n → move right, negative n → move left.
Worked Example 1: To Standard Form

Write in standard form:
(a) 67000 = 6.7 × 10⁴
(b) 0.0048 = 4.8 × 10⁻³
(c) 3,200,000,000 = 3.2 × 10⁹
(d) 0.000000091 = 9.1 × 10⁻⁸
(e) 45 × 10³ = 4.5 × 10⁴ (adjust A to 1≤A<10)
(f) 0.7 × 10⁵ = 7 × 10⁴

Worked Example 2: From Standard Form

Write as ordinary numbers:
(a) 4.2 × 10³ = 4200
(b) 6.75 × 10⁻² = 0.0675
(c) 9.1 × 10⁶ = 9,100,000
(d) 2.05 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.0000205
(e) 10⁴ = 10000
(f) 10⁻³ = 0.001

Worked Example 3: Ordering and Comparing

Order from smallest: 3.2×10⁴, 4.1×10³, 2.8×10⁴, 5.6×10⁵

Compare powers first: 10³ < 10⁴ < 10⁵
4.1×10³ is smallest (only 10³)
Then 10⁴ terms: 2.8×10⁴ < 3.2×10⁴
Then 5.6×10⁵
Order: 4.1×10³ < 2.8×10⁴ < 3.2×10⁴ < 5.6×10⁵

Standard Form: A × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ A < 10, n ∈ ℤ

Converting:
• Large: Count digits from first non-zero to decimal point → n
• Small: Count zeros after decimal before first non-zero → |n|

Calculator: Use EXP or ×10ˣ button
Enter 4.5 EXP 6 for 4.5×10⁶
Enter 3.2 EXP (-4) for 3.2×10⁻⁴

Common Misconception

45 × 10³ is in standard form

A must be 1 ≤ A < 10. 45 × 10³ = 4.5 × 10⁴.

Differentiation

Support:
Place value grid with powers of 10 labelled. "Decimal moves left → positive power" mnemonic. Practice with 1-digit A first.
Stretch:
Standard form with negative A? (Not standard). Calculations without calculator. Real data: mass of electron, distance to stars.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Fix These

12 × 10⁵ → 1.2 × 10⁶
0.8 × 10⁻³ → 8 × 10⁻⁴
230 × 10² → 2.3 × 10⁴

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Converts fluently both ways; orders correctly; spots non-standard form
  • Getting there: Converts large numbers well; struggles with small numbers (negative powers); A not in range
  • Not yet: Confuses direction of decimal move; thinks 10⁻³ = -1000; no concept of magnitude

Lesson 2: Calculating with Standard Form

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Mental Powers

10³ × 10⁴ = ? 10⁷ ÷ 10² = ? (10⁴)² = ? 10⁰ = ?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Multiplication: (A×10ⁿ) × (B×10ᵐ) = (A×B) × 10^(n+m)
Then adjust if A×B ≥ 10 or < 1.

Division: (A×10ⁿ) ÷ (B×10ᵐ) = (A÷B) × 10^(n-m)
Adjust if needed.

Addition/Subtraction: Must have SAME power of 10. Convert one, then add/subtract coefficients.
(3×10⁴) + (2×10³) = (3×10⁴) + (0.2×10⁴) = 3.2×10⁴

Calculator: Use EXP button throughout. But know how to do manually for non-calculator paper.
Worked Example 1: Multiplication

Calculate: (3×10⁴) × (2×10³)

Step 1: Multiply coefficients: 3 × 2 = 6
Step 2: Add powers: 10⁴ × 10³ = 10⁷
Step 3: 6 × 10⁷ (already in standard form)
Answer: 6 × 10⁷

Worked Example 2: Multiplication with Adjustment

Calculate: (4×10⁵) × (3×10⁴)

4 × 3 = 12
10⁵ × 10⁴ = 10⁹
12 × 10⁹ → adjust: 1.2 × 10¹⁰
Answer: 1.2 × 10¹⁰

Worked Example 3: Division and Addition

(a) (8×10⁷) ÷ (2×10³) = 4×10⁴
(b) (6×10⁵) ÷ (2×10⁷) = 3×10⁻²
(c) (5×10⁴) + (3×10³) = 5×10⁴ + 0.3×10⁴ = 5.3×10⁴
(d) (7×10⁶) - (2×10⁴) = 7×10⁶ - 0.02×10⁶ = 6.98×10⁶

Calculations in Standard Form:
Multiply: (A×10ⁿ)(B×10ᵐ) = (AB)×10^(n+m)
Divide: (A×10ⁿ)/(B×10ᵐ) = (A/B)×10^(n-m)
Add/Subtract: Match powers first!
A×10ⁿ + B×10ᵐ → convert to same power

Always check: 1 ≤ coefficient < 10

Common Misconception

(4×10⁵) + (3×10⁵) = 7×10¹⁰

Powers stay the same when adding: (4+3)×10⁵ = 7×10⁵. Only multiply powers when multiplying.

Differentiation

Support:
Template: "Coefficients: ___ Powers: ___ Adjust: ___". Calculator for checking. Start with same-power addition.
Stretch:
Mixed operations: (3×10⁴)² ÷ (2×10³). Solve: (x×10⁵) × (2×10³) = 6×10⁹. Real-world: speed of light × time = distance.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Calculator Check

(2.5×10⁶) × (4×10⁻³) = 1×10⁴ = 10000
(9×10⁸) ÷ (3×10²) = 3×10⁶
(6×10⁴) + (4×10⁵) = 4.6×10⁵

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Multiplies/divides fluently; adjusts coefficient; adds/subtracts by matching powers
  • Getting there: Multiplies/divides; forgets to adjust; adds without matching powers
  • Not yet: Adds powers when adding; cannot use calculator EXP; no magnitude sense

Homework Suggestions

  • Conversion worksheet: ordinary ↔ standard form (mixed large/small)
  • Calculations worksheet: multiply, divide, add, subtract
  • Real-world: Research 5 quantities in standard form (e.g., mass of Earth, radius of atom)
