N8: Surds

Higher All Boards

Higher tier only. Simplify surds, perform arithmetic with surds, rationalise denominators. Express answers in exact surd form.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Identify surds and distinguish from rational numbers
  • Simplify surds using square factors (√(ab) = √a × √b)
  • Add, subtract, multiply and divide surds
  • Rationalise denominators (simple and compound)
  • Solve problems giving exact answers in surd form

Prerequisites

  • Square numbers and square roots (N6)
  • Index laws including fractional indices (N7)
  • Fraction arithmetic
  • Expanding brackets (including (a+b)(a-b) = a²-b²)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator (surds mode / √ button)
  • Square number reference (1² to 20²)
  • Surd simplification flowchart
  • Mini whiteboards

Lesson 1: Simplifying Surds

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Rational or Surd?

Classify: √4, √7, √16, √12, ³√8, ³√9, √0.25, √2. Which are exact decimals?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Surd Definition: An irrational root (usually square root) that cannot be simplified to a rational number. √2, √3, √5, √6, √7, √8, √10...

Simplifying Surds: Factor out perfect squares.
√(ab) = √a × √b (for a,b ≥ 0)
√12 = √(4×3) = √4 × √3 = 2√3
√75 = √(25×3) = 5√3
√98 = √(49×2) = 7√2

Strategy: Find largest square factor. Divide by 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64...
Worked Example 1: Simplifying Surds

Simplify: (a) √45 (b) √128 (c) √200 (d) √(9/16) (e) ³√54

(a) √45 = √(9×5) = 3√5
(b) √128 = √(64×2) = 8√2
(c) √200 = √(100×2) = 10√2 (also √(25×8) = 5√8 = 5×2√2 = 10√2)
(d) √(9/16) = √9/√16 = 3/4
(e) ³√54 = ³√(27×2) = 3³√2

Worked Example 2: Surd Arithmetic — Addition/Subtraction

Simplify: (a) 3√2 + 5√2 (b) 7√3 - 2√3 (c) √8 + √18 (d) 4√5 - √20

(a) Like surds: 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2
(b) 7√3 - 2√3 = 5√3
(c) √8 = 2√2, √18 = 3√2 → 2√2 + 3√2 = 5√2
(d) √20 = 2√5 → 4√5 - 2√5 = 2√5

Worked Example 3: Surd Arithmetic — Multiplication/Division

Simplify: (a) √3 × √12 (b) 2√5 × 3√2 (c) √50 ÷ √2 (d) (3√2)²

(a) √3 × √12 = √36 = 6 (or 2√3 × √3 = 2×3 = 6)
(b) 2×3 × √5×√2 = 6√10
(c) √50 ÷ √2 = √(50/2) = √25 = 5
(d) (3√2)² = 9 × 2 = 18

Surd Rules:
√a × √b = √(ab) (a,b ≥ 0)
√a ÷ √b = √(a/b) (b > 0)
√a × √a = a
(√a)² = a

Simplifying: √(k²×n) = k√n (k > 0)

Like Surds: a√n + b√n = (a+b)√n
Unlike Surds: √2 + √3 cannot be combined

Common Misconception

√(a+b) = √a + √b

√(9+16) = √25 = 5, but √9 + √16 = 3+4 = 7. NOT equal.

Differentiation

Support:
Square factor table. Step-by-step: "Find square factor → split root → simplify". Start with √8, √12, √18, √20, √27, √28, √32, √45, √48, √50.
Stretch:
Simplify: √(50+√400), √(72/8), √(x⁴y³) for x,y>0. Nested surds: √(2+√3).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Simplify Fully

√27 + √75 - √12 = 3√3 + 5√3 - 2√3 = 6√3
(2+√3)(2-√3) = 4 - 3 = 1 (difference of squares)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Simplifies surds efficiently; combines like surds; multiplies/divides correctly
  • Getting there: Finds square factors but misses largest; forgets to simplify before adding
  • Not yet: Treats √a+√b as √(a+b); cannot identify square factors; no surd concept

Lesson 2: Expanding Brackets and Rationalising — Simple Denominators

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Expand

(√3 + 2)(√3 - 2) = ? Notice the result.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Expanding Brackets with Surds: Use FOIL/grid. Remember √a × √a = a.
(√2 + 3)(√2 - 1) = 2 - √2 + 3√2 - 3 = -1 + 2√2

Rationalising Simple Denominators: Multiply numerator and denominator by the surd.
1/√2 = √2/2
5/√3 = 5√3/3
√5/√2 = √10/2

Why Rationalise? Exact form convention. Easier to compare/estimate. Historical: division by irrational was hard before calculators.
Worked Example 1: Expanding Brackets

Expand and simplify: (a) (√5 + 2)(√5 - 3) (b) (2√3 - 1)² (c) (√7 + √2)(√7 - √2)

(a) 5 - 3√5 + 2√5 - 6 = -1 - √5
(b) (2√3)² - 2(2√3)(1) + 1 = 12 - 4√3 + 1 = 13 - 4√3
(c) (√7)² - (√2)² = 7 - 2 = 5 (difference of squares)

Worked Example 2: Rationalising Simple Denominators

Rationalise: (a) 3/√5 (b) √7/√2 (c) (3+√2)/√3 (d) 1/(2√5)

(a) 3/√5 × √5/√5 = 3√5/5
(b) √7/√2 × √2/√2 = √14/2
(c) (3+√2)/√3 × √3/√3 = (3√3+√6)/3
(d) 1/(2√5) × √5/√5 = √5/10

Rationalising Denominator (Simple):
For a/√b: multiply by √b/√b → a√b/b

For (c+√d)/√b: multiply by √b/√b → (c√b+√(bd))/b

Difference of Squares:
(√a + √b)(√a - √b) = a - b
(√a + b)(√a - b) = a - b²

Common Misconception

1/(√2+1) = 1/√2 + 1 = √2/2 + 1

Cannot split fraction over addition. Must multiply by conjugate: (√2-1)/(√2-1).

Differentiation

Support:
Grid method for expanding. "Multiply top and bottom by the surd" mantra. Practice √a × √a = a repeatedly.
Stretch:
Simplify: (√2+√3)², 1/(√5-√2), (√x+√y)/(√x-√y). Show 1/(√(n+1)-√n) = √(n+1)+√n.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Rationalise

4/√7 = 4√7/7
(√3-1)/√2 = (√6-√2)/2
1/(3√5) = √5/15

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Expands brackets with surds; rationalises simple denominators; simplifies final answers
  • Getting there: Multiplies surds correctly; forgets to simplify after rationalising; sign errors in expansion
  • Not yet: √a × √a = √(a²); splits fractions incorrectly; no conjugate concept

Lesson 3: Rationalising Compound Denominators and Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Conjugate Pairs

Find the conjugate: √3+2, 5-√7, √2+√3, 1-2√5. What happens when multiplied?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Compound Denominators: Denominator is sum/difference involving surds.
Use CONJUGATE: (√a + √b) has conjugate (√a - √b). Product = a - b (rational).

Method: Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

Exact Answers in Geometry: Pythagoras, area, volume often give surd answers. Leave as exact surds unless asked for decimal.
Worked Example 1: Rationalising Compound Denominators

Rationalise and simplify: (a) 1/(√3+1) (b) (2√2)/(3-√2) (c) (√5+√2)/(√5-√2)

(a) Multiply by (√3-1)/(√3-1):
(√3-1)/(3-1) = (√3-1)/2

(b) Multiply by (3+√2)/(3+√2):
2√2(3+√2)/(9-2) = (6√2+4)/7

(c) Multiply by (√5+√2)/(√5+√2):
(5+2√10+2)/(5-2) = (7+2√10)/3

Worked Example 2: Geometry with Exact Surds

Right triangle: hypotenuse 10cm, one leg 6cm. Find other leg exactly.

x² + 6² = 10²
x² = 100 - 36 = 64
x = √64 = 8 cm (rational — not a surd example)

Better: Hypotenuse 10cm, leg 7cm. Find other leg.
x² = 100 - 49 = 51
x = √51 cm (exact surd form)

Worked Example 3: Simplifying Complex Expressions

Simplify: (√8 + √2)² ÷ √18

√8 = 2√2, so √8 + √2 = 3√2
(3√2)² = 9 × 2 = 18
√18 = 3√2
18 ÷ 3√2 = 6/√2 = 6√2/2 = 3√2
Answer: 3√2

Rationalising Compound Denominator:
For a/(√b + √c): multiply by (√b - √c)/(√b - √c)
Denominator: (√b)² - (√c)² = b - c (rational)

General: 1/(√a ± √b) = (√a ∓ √b)/(a - b)

Exact Form Convention: Leave as √n, a√b, (a√b+c√d)/e — no decimals unless requested.

Common Misconception

Multiply by (√b + √c) for denominator (√b + √c)

Must use CONJUGATE (√b - √c) to get difference of squares: (√b)² - (√c)² = b - c.

Differentiation

Support:
Conjugate identification practice first. Scaffolded steps: 1) Identify conjugate 2) Multiply 3) Simplify denominator 4) Simplify numerator.
Stretch:
Rationalise: 1/(√2+√3+√5). Solve: (x+√2)/(x-√2) = 3. Prove √n rational ⇔ n is perfect square.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Show that...

(√2+1)/(√2-1) = 3+2√2
Multiply by (√2+1)/(√2+1):
(√2+1)²/(2-1) = (2+2√2+1)/1 = 3+2√2 ✓

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Rationalises any denominator; simplifies complex surd expressions; gives exact geometric answers
  • Getting there: Rationalises simple and compound; arithmetic errors in expansion; doesn't fully simplify
  • Not yet: Only rationalises simple denominators; expands conjugates incorrectly

Homework Suggestions

  • Surd simplification and arithmetic mixed exercise
  • Rationalising denominators worksheet (simple → compound)
  • "Show that" problems: (√7+2)/(√7-2) = 11+4√7
  • Geometry problems requiring exact surd answers
  • CorbettMaths "Surds" Higher practice questions
