N5: Systematic Listing

Foundation Higher All Boards

Use systematic listing strategies and the product rule for counting to find the number of outcomes for combined events. Includes sample space diagrams and probability applications.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • List all outcomes systematically for combined events (tables, trees, lists)
  • Apply the product rule for counting: n₁ × n₂ × n₃ × ...
  • Distinguish between permutations (order matters) and combinations (order doesn't matter)
  • Use systematic listing to calculate probabilities
  • Solve problems involving arrangements and selections

Prerequisites

  • Basic probability vocabulary (outcome, event, sample space)
  • Multiplication facts
  • Understanding of "and" vs "or" in probability
  • Fraction arithmetic for probability answers

Materials & Equipment

  • Dice, coins, spinners for practical listing
  • Sample space diagram templates (grids, tables)
  • Coloured counters for visual arrangements
  • Scientific calculator

Lesson 1: Systematic Listing and Product Rule

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

How Many Outfits?

3 shirts (red, blue, green) and 2 trousers (black, jeans). List all outfits.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Systematic Listing: Organised method to ensure no outcomes missed and no duplicates.
Strategies: Tables (2 events), Tree diagrams (sequential events), Organised lists.

Product Rule: If event A has n₁ outcomes and event B has n₂ outcomes, total combined outcomes = n₁ × n₂.
Extends to 3+ events: n₁ × n₂ × n₃ × ...

With/Without Replacement: With replacement: choices stay same. Without: choices reduce.
Worked Example 1: Sample Space Table

Two fair 4-sided dice (numbered 1-4). List all outcomes and find P(total = 5).

Table (Die A × Die B):
1 2 3 4
1 (1,1)(1,2)(1,3)(1,4)
2 (2,1)(2,2)(2,3)(2,4)
3 (3,1)(3,2)(3,3)(3,4)
4 (4,1)(4,2)(4,3)(4,4)

Total outcomes = 4 × 4 = 16
Totals of 5: (1,4), (2,3), (3,2), (4,1) = 4 outcomes
P(total = 5) = 4/16 = 1/4

Worked Example 2: Product Rule

A restaurant offers: 4 starters, 6 mains, 3 desserts. How many 3-course meals?

Starters: 4 choices
Mains: 6 choices
Desserts: 3 choices
Total = 4 × 6 × 3 = 72 different meals

Worked Example 3: Without Replacement

A bag has 5 red, 3 blue counters. Pick 2 without replacement. How many ordered pairs?

1st pick: 8 choices
2nd pick: 7 choices (one removed)
Total = 8 × 7 = 56 ordered outcomes

If order doesn't matter (just colours): RR, RB, BR, BB but RB=BR → 4 combinations
List: RR (5×4=20), RB (5×3=15), BR (3×5=15), BB (3×2=6). Total 56 ✓

Product Rule for Counting:
n(A and B) = n(A) × n(B)
n(A and B and C) = n(A) × n(B) × n(C)

With Replacement: choices unchanged each stage
Without Replacement: choices reduce by 1 each stage

Permutations (order matters): nPr = n!/(n-r)!
Combinations (order irrelevant): nCr = n!/[r!(n-r)!]

Common Misconception

For two dice, (3,4) and (4,3) are the same outcome

They are DIFFERENT ordered outcomes. Die A=3, Die B=4 is different from Die A=4, Die B=3. Sample space has 36 outcomes, not 21.

Differentiation

Support:
Use physical objects (coloured cubes). Provide table templates. Start with 2 events, 2-3 outcomes each.
Stretch:
Product rule with restrictions (e.g., "no two adjacent same colour"). Factorial notation intro. Birthday problem (23 people, P(shared birthday) > 0.5).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Count

1) 3-digit codes from digits 1-5, repetition allowed: 5×5×5 = 125
2) 3-digit codes, no repetition: 5×4×3 = 60
3) Choose 2 from 5 colours, order irrelevant: 5×4÷2 = 10

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Creates systematic lists/tables; applies product rule; distinguishes with/without replacement
  • Getting there: Lists outcomes but may miss/duplicate; product rule for independent events only
  • Not yet: Random listing; confuses product rule with addition; no systematic approach

Lesson 2: Permutations, Combinations and Probability

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Arrange the Letters

How many ways to arrange letters in CAT? List them.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Permutations (Order Matters): Arranging r items from n distinct items.
nPr = n × (n-1) × ... × (n-r+1) = n!/(n-r)!
Example: Arrange 3 books from 5: 5P3 = 5×4×3 = 60

Combinations (Order Doesn't Matter): Choosing r items from n.
nCr = nPr / r! = n!/[r!(n-r)!]
Example: Choose 3 books from 5: 5C3 = 5×4×3/3×2×1 = 10

Identical Items: Divide by factorial of repeats.
ARRANGE: 7!/(2!2!) = 5040/4 = 1260 (A=1, R=2, N=1, G=1, E=1)
Worked Example: Permutations

How many 4-digit PIN codes using digits 0-9 if no digit repeated?

1st digit: 10 choices (0-9)
2nd digit: 9 choices
3rd digit: 8 choices
4th digit: 7 choices
Total = 10 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 5040
Or 10P4 = 10!/6! = 5040

Worked Example: Combinations

Choose a committee of 3 from 8 people. How many committees?

Order doesn't matter (committee is a set).
8C3 = 8!/(3!5!) = (8×7×6)/(3×2×1) = 56
Check: 8P3 = 336, divide by 3! = 6 → 56 ✓

Worked Example: Probability with Listing

3 red, 2 blue balls. Pick 2 without replacement. P(both same colour)?

Total outcomes: 5P2 = 5×4 = 20 (ordered)
Both red: 3×2 = 6
Both blue: 2×1 = 2
P(same) = (6+2)/20 = 8/20 = 2/5

Alternative (combinations): 5C2 = 10 total pairs. 3C2=3 red pairs, 2C2=1 blue pair. P=4/10=2/5 ✓

Factorials:
n! = n × (n-1) × ... × 2 × 1
0! = 1

Permutations: nPr = n!/(n-r)!
Combinations: nCr = n!/[r!(n-r)!]

Relationship: nCr = nPr / r!

Identical items: n!/(a!b!c!...) where a,b,c are frequencies

Common Misconception

nCr = nPr × r!

nCr = nPr ÷ r! because each combination corresponds to r! permutations.

Differentiation

Support:
Use physical arrangements. "Does ABC = CBA?" discussion. Calculator nCr/nPr buttons.
Stretch:
Arrangements in a circle (divide by n). Arrangements with restrictions (A and B not together). Binomial expansion link.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Which Formula?

1) 10 runners, gold/silver/bronze medals: 10P3 = 720 (order matters)
2) Choose 5 cards from 52: 52C5 (order irrelevant)
3) Arrange letters of BANANA: 6!/(3!2!) = 60

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Selects correct formula; calculates factorials; solves probability with combinations
  • Getting there: Understands difference; calculates simple permutations; combination formula errors
  • Not yet: Confuses permutations/combinations; cannot compute factorials

Homework Suggestions

  • Systematic listing worksheet: tables, trees, product rule
  • Permutation/combination mixed exercise
  • Real-world: How many different pizzas with 3 toppings from 10? (10C3 = 120)
  • CorbettMaths "Product Rule for Counting" and "Combinations" exercises
