N7: Integer Indices

Foundation Higher All Boards

Apply the laws of indices for integer powers (positive, negative, zero). Simplify expressions involving multiplication, division, and brackets. Evaluate numerical expressions with integer indices.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • State and apply the three index laws for multiplication, division, and powers
  • Understand and use zero index (a⁰ = 1) and negative indices (a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ)
  • Simplify algebraic expressions with integer indices
  • Evaluate numerical expressions with positive, negative, and zero indices
  • Convert between negative indices and fractions

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of index notation (aⁿ = a × a × ... × a)
  • Basic algebra: simplifying like terms, expanding brackets
  • Fraction arithmetic (reciprocals)
  • Negative number arithmetic

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator (x^y button)
  • Index law summary cards
  • Mini whiteboards for practice
  • Algebra tiles (optional, for visualisation)

Lesson 1: Index Laws — Multiplication, Division, Powers

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Expand and Simplify

Write out: a³ × a², b⁵ ÷ b², (c²)³ as repeated multiplication. What do you notice?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Law 1 — Multiplication: aᵐ × aⁿ = a^(m+n) (add indices)
Example: x³ × x⁴ = x⁷ (xxx × xxxx = xxxxxxx)

Law 2 — Division: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = a^(m-n) (subtract indices)
Example: y⁷ ÷ y³ = y⁴ (yyyyyyy ÷ yyy = yyyy)

Law 3 — Power of a Power: (aᵐ)ⁿ = a^(m×n) (multiply indices)
Example: (z²)⁵ = z¹⁰ (z² × z² × z² × z² × z²)

Coefficients: Handle numbers separately from variables.
3x² × 4x³ = (3×4) × (x²×x³) = 12x⁵
Worked Example 1: Multiplication and Division

Simplify: (a) 5x³ × 2x⁴ (b) 18y⁷ ÷ 3y² (c) 2a²b³ × 5ab²

(a) 5×2 = 10, x³×x⁴ = x⁷ → 10x⁷
(b) 18÷3 = 6, y⁷÷y² = y⁵ → 6y⁵
(c) 2×5 = 10, a²×a = a³, b³×b² = b⁵ → 10a³b⁵

Worked Example 2: Power of a Power

Simplify: (a) (x³)² (b) (2y²)³ (c) (3a²b)⁴

(a) x^(3×2) = x⁶
(b) 2³ × (y²)³ = 8 × y⁶ = 8y⁶
(c) 3⁴ × (a²)⁴ × b⁴ = 81 × a⁸ × b⁴ = 81a⁸b⁴

Worked Example 3: Mixed Laws

Simplify: (4x³y²)² × 2x²y ÷ 8x⁴y³

Step 1: (4x³y²)² = 16x⁶y⁴
Step 2: 16x⁶y⁴ × 2x²y = 32x⁸y⁵
Step 3: 32x⁸y⁵ ÷ 8x⁴y³ = 4x⁴y²
Answer: 4x⁴y²

Index Laws (Integer Indices):
1. aᵐ × aⁿ = a^(m+n)
2. aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = a^(m-n)
3. (aᵐ)ⁿ = a^(mn)

Also: (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ, (a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ

Coefficients: Multiply/divide numbers normally, apply laws to variables separately.

Common Misconception

x³ + x³ = x⁶

x³ + x³ = 2x³ (add coefficients). Index laws only apply to MULTIPLICATION and DIVISION.

Differentiation

Support:
Expand fully first (write out xxxx...). Colour-code base and index. Provide law reference sheet.
Stretch:
Simplify: (2x⁻²y³)⁻² ÷ (4x³y⁻¹)². Prove (aᵐ)ⁿ = a^(mn) by expanding. Solve 2^x × 2³ = 2⁷.

Plenary (5 minutes)

True or False?

a⁵ × a² = a¹⁰ (F, a⁷)
a⁵ ÷ a² = a³ (T)
(a⁵)² = a⁷ (F, a¹⁰)
2x³ × 3x² = 5x⁵ (F, 6x⁵)
(x²y)³ = x⁶y³ (T)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies all three laws fluently; handles coefficients; simplifies multi-step expressions
  • Getting there: Knows laws but mixes up add/subtract/multiply; coefficient errors
  • Not yet: Adds indices for addition; no law recognition; expands incorrectly

Lesson 2: Zero and Negative Indices

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Pattern Spotting

2⁴=16, 2³=8, 2²=4, 2¹=2, 2⁰=?, 2⁻¹=?, 2⁻²=? Continue the pattern.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Zero Index: a⁰ = 1 (for any a ≠ 0)
From law 2: aⁿ ÷ aⁿ = a^(n-n) = a⁰. But aⁿ ÷ aⁿ = 1. So a⁰ = 1.

Negative Indices: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ (reciprocal)
From law 2: a⁰ ÷ aⁿ = a^(0-n) = a⁻ⁿ. But a⁰ ÷ aⁿ = 1/aⁿ. So a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.

Fraction with Negative Index: (a/b)⁻ⁿ = (b/a)ⁿ = bⁿ/aⁿ
Moving Between Num/Denom: a⁻ⁿ in numerator → aⁿ in denominator, and vice versa.
Worked Example 1: Zero and Negative Indices

Evaluate: (a) 7⁰ (b) 5⁻² (c) 10⁻³ (d) (2/3)⁻² (e) 4⁻¹ × 4³

(a) 7⁰ = 1
(b) 5⁻² = 1/5² = 1/25 = 0.04
(c) 10⁻³ = 1/1000 = 0.001
(d) (2/3)⁻² = (3/2)² = 9/4 = 2.25
(e) 4⁻¹ × 4³ = 4² = 16 (or 1/4 × 64 = 16)

Worked Example 2: Algebraic Negative Indices

Simplify, writing with positive indices: (a) x⁻³ (b) 2a⁻²b³ (c) (3x⁻¹y²)⁻² (d) x⁻³/x⁻⁵

(a) x⁻³ = 1/x³
(b) 2b³/a²
(c) 3⁻² × (x⁻¹)⁻² × (y²)⁻² = 1/9 × x² × y⁻⁴ = x²/(9y⁴)
(d) x⁻³ ÷ x⁻⁵ = x^(-3-(-5)) = x² OR (1/x³) × x⁵ = x²

Worked Example 3: Complex Simplification

Simplify: (2x³y⁻²)² ÷ (4x⁻¹y³)⁻¹

Numerator: (2x³y⁻²)² = 4x⁶y⁻⁴
Denominator: (4x⁻¹y³)⁻¹ = 4⁻¹x¹y⁻³ = x/(4y³)
Division: 4x⁶y⁻⁴ ÷ (x/(4y³)) = 4x⁶y⁻⁴ × (4y³/x) = 16x⁵y⁻¹ = 16x⁵/y
Answer: 16x⁵/y

Zero and Negative Indices:
a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0)
a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ (a ≠ 0)
1/a⁻ⁿ = aⁿ

Fraction Base:
(a/b)⁻ⁿ = (b/a)ⁿ = bⁿ/aⁿ

Examples:
5⁰ = 1, 5⁻¹ = 1/5, 5⁻² = 1/25
x⁻³ = 1/x³, 1/x⁻³ = x³
(2/3)⁻² = (3/2)² = 9/4

Common Misconception

0⁰ = 0 or 0⁰ = 1 (undefined actually)

0⁰ is undefined. a⁰ = 1 only for a ≠ 0.

Differentiation

Support:
Pattern continuation from positive indices. "Flip and change sign" mnemonic for negatives. Fraction reciprocal practice.
Stretch:
Solve: 2^(x-1) = 8, 5^(2x) = 125. Simplify: (x⁻²y³)⁻¹ × (x³y⁻¹)². Express 0.0001 as power of 10.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Write with Positive Indices

a⁻³b² = b²/a³
2x⁻¹y⁻² = 2/(xy²)
(3a⁻²)⁻¹ = a²/3
x⁻⁴/x⁻² = 1/x²

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Evaluates zero/negative indices; converts between forms; simplifies algebraic expressions fully
  • Getting there: Understands reciprocal concept; struggles with negative index on fractions; coefficient errors
  • Not yet: Thinks a⁻ⁿ = -aⁿ; cannot handle zero index; confuses 1/x⁻ⁿ

Homework Suggestions

  • Index laws mixed worksheet (positive, zero, negative)
  • "Simplify and write with positive indices" exercise
  • Evaluate: 3⁰, 4⁻², (1/2)⁻³, 10⁻⁴, (-2)⁻³
  • CorbettMaths "Index Laws" and "Negative Indices" practice
