N14: Estimation and Approximation

Foundation Higher All Boards

Estimate calculations by rounding to 1 significant figure. Use approximation to check calculator answers. Understand the difference between estimation and exact calculation.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Round numbers to 1 significant figure for estimation
  • Estimate calculations (including with roots, powers, fractions)
  • Use estimation to check calculator answers
  • Understand overestimation vs underestimation
  • Apply estimation to real-world contexts (shopping, DIY, travel)

Prerequisites

  • Rounding to decimal places and significant figures
  • Place value understanding
  • Four operations with decimals
  • Square/cube roots and powers (N6)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator (for checking)
  • Rounding/estimation reference cards
  • Real-world price lists, receipts, measuring tasks
  • Mini whiteboards for quick estimation practice

Lesson 1: Rounding to 1 Significant Figure and Estimating

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Rounding

Round to 1 significant figure: 47, 382, 0.056, 4.07, 0.00083, 999

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Significant Figures: First non-zero digit is 1st significant figure.
Round to 1 sig fig: Look at 2nd sig fig. 5 or more → round up.
47 → 50, 382 → 400, 0.056 → 0.06, 4.07 → 4, 0.00083 → 0.0008, 999 → 1000

Estimation Rule: Round ALL numbers to 1 sig fig, then calculate.
Exception: Roots/powers - round inside first, then apply operation.

Estimation with Division: Make numbers "friendly" if possible.
49.2 ÷ 10.1 ≈ 50 ÷ 10 = 5 (actual 4.87)

Estimation with Roots: Estimate root separately.
√52.3 ≈ √49 = 7 (actual 7.23)
Worked Example 1: Rounding to 1 Sig Fig

Round to 1 significant figure:
(a) 347 → 300
(b) 0.0048 → 0.005
(c) 6.072 → 6
(d) 99.6 → 100
(e) 0.000402 → 0.0004
(f) 50.5 → 50

Worked Example 2: Estimating Calculations

Estimate: (a) 4.7 × 21.3 (b) 189 ÷ 4.2 (c) (3.9)² (d) √82.5 (e) (0.48 × 21.3) / 0.19

(a) 5 × 20 = 100 (actual: 100.11)
(b) 200 ÷ 4 = 50 (actual: 45)
(c) 4² = 16 (actual: 15.21)
(d) √81 = 9 (actual: 9.08)
(e) (0.5 × 20) / 0.2 = 10 / 0.2 = 50 (actual: 53.8)

Worked Example 3: Real-World Estimation

Shopping: £2.99, £4.49, £0.89, £3.25, £1.19. Estimate total.
Round: £3, £4, £1, £3, £1 = £12
Actual: £12.81 (estimate slightly low)

DIY: 12.7 m² tiles at £18.99/m². Estimate cost.
13 × 20 = £260 (actual: £241.17, overestimate)

Estimation Procedure:
1. Round each number to 1 significant figure
2. Perform calculation with rounded numbers
3. For roots/powers: round inside first, then apply
4. For division: adjust to make "friendly" if helpful

Over/Under Estimation:
• Rounding up → overestimate (for +, ×)
• Rounding down → underestimate (for +, ×)
• Division: numerator up/denominator down → overestimate

Common Misconception

Estimate 0.03 × 0.04 as 0 × 0 = 0

Round to 1 sig fig: 0.03 → 0.03 (already 1 sf), 0.04 → 0.04. Estimate: 0.03 × 0.04 = 0.0012. Don't round to zero!

Differentiation

Support:
Rounding to 1 sig fig practice first. Number lines for visual rounding. "1st non-zero digit" highlighter.
Stretch:
Estimate with standard form: (3.2×10⁴) × (4.7×10⁻²). Estimate percentage of amount. Fermi problems (piano tuners in London).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Estimate or Exact?

Estimate: 47.8 × 19.6 ≈ 50 × 20 = 1000
Calculator: 47.8 × 19.6 = 936.88
Is estimate reasonable? Yes, within 7%.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Rounds to 1 sf correctly; estimates all operations; identifies over/under; checks calculator answers
  • Getting there: Rounds integers; decimals to 1 sf tricky; estimates + and ×; division/roots need support
  • Not yet: Rounds to decimal places not sig figs; rounds small decimals to 0; no estimation concept

Lesson 2: Using Estimation to Check and Solve Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Calculator Check

Calculator shows: 45.6 × 12.3 = 568.8. Estimate to check: 50 × 10 = 500 or 50 × 12 = 600. Reasonable?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Checking Calculator Answers: Always estimate first! If estimate and calculator differ greatly, re-enter.
Example: 12.4 × 3.8. Estimate: 10 × 4 = 40. Calculator: 47.12. Close? Yes.
If calculator gave 4.712, decimal place error detected.

Estimation in Context: "About how many..." "Roughly what..."
Bus seats 52, 37 buses. Estimate passengers: 50 × 40 = 2000.

Approximation vs Exact: Estimation gives rough answer. Exact uses precise values. Both have place.

Error Intervals (intro): Rounding to 1 sf gives range. 50 (1 sf) means 45 ≤ x < 55.
Worked Example 1: Checking with Estimation

Student calculates: 24.6 × 18.3 = 45.018. Use estimation to check.
Estimate: 20 × 20 = 400. Actual should be around 450. 45 is far too small.
Error: Forgot decimal. 24.6 × 18.3 = 450.18 ✓

Worked Example 2: Estimation with Fractions/Decimals

Estimate: (19.7 × 3.2) / (0.48 + 1.52)
Numerator: 20 × 3 = 60
Denominator: 0.5 + 1.5 = 2
Estimate: 60 / 2 = 30
Actual: (63.04) / 2 = 31.52. Good estimate.

Worked Example 3: Over/Under Estimate

Estimate 4.3 × 7.8 by rounding to 1 sf.
4.3 → 4 (down), 7.8 → 8 (up)
Estimate: 4 × 8 = 32
Actual: 4.3 × 7.8 = 33.54
One rounded down, one up → hard to tell. Both rounded up (5×8=40) would overestimate.

Using Estimation to Check:
1. Estimate before calculating (mental/rough)
2. Calculate exactly (calculator/written)
3. Compare: same order of magnitude? Correct decimal place?
4. If not close → re-check entry/working

Estimation Language:
"Approximately" "Roughly" "About" "Estimate" → round to 1 sf
"Calculate" "Work out" "Find" → exact answer

Common Misconception

Estimation means "guess"

Estimation is a structured calculation using rounded numbers. It follows mathematical rules, not random guessing.

Differentiation

Support:
"Estimate first" habit. Side-by-side: estimate | calculate | compare. Highlight decimal place errors caught by estimation.
Stretch:
Given estimate and exact, find possible original numbers. Estimate compound calculations. Relative error: |estimate - exact|/exact.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Find the Error

1) 0.4 × 0.6 = 2.4 (estimate: 0.4×0.6=0.24, decimal error)
2) 150 ÷ 0.3 = 50 (estimate: 150÷0.3≈150÷0.3=500, missed zero)
3) √250 = 5 (estimate: √250≈15.8, forgot root)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Routinely estimates before calculating; spots calculator errors; explains over/under
  • Getting there: Estimates when prompted; checks simple calculations; decimal place awareness
  • Not yet: No estimation habit; cannot round to 1 sf; accepts any calculator output

Homework Suggestions

  • Estimation worksheet: round to 1 sf, then calculate (+, -, ×, ÷, powers, roots)
  • "Check these calculator answers" exercise (some right, some wrong)
  • Real-world: Estimate weekly shop, then compare with receipt
  • Fermi estimation: How many golf balls fit in a bus?
  • CorbettMaths "Estimation" practice questions
