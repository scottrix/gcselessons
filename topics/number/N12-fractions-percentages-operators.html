N12: Fractions, Percentages and Operators

Foundation Higher All Boards

Convert between fractions, decimals and percentages. Use percentage multipliers for increase/decrease. Solve reverse percentage problems. Interpret fractions and percentages as operators.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Convert fluently between fractions, decimals and percentages
  • Calculate percentages of amounts (with/without calculator)
  • Use percentage multipliers for increase/decrease
  • Solve reverse percentage problems (find original amount)
  • Calculate percentage change and percentage profit/loss
  • Interpret fractions/percentages as operators (e.g., "of" means multiply)

Prerequisites

  • Fraction, decimal, percentage equivalence (1/2=0.5=50%, 1/4=0.25=25%, etc.)
  • Finding fractions of amounts
  • Multiplying/dividing decimals
  • Basic algebra for reverse percentages

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculator (percentage button %)
  • Fraction/decimal/percentage equivalence fans
  • Multiplier reference cards
  • Bar models for visualising percentages

Lesson 1: FDP Conversions and Percentages of Amounts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

FDP Equivalence

Fill gaps: 1/2 = ___ = 50%, 0.2 = ___ = 20%, ___ = 0.75 = 75%, 3/8 = ___ = ___

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Fraction → Percentage: × 100. 3/4 = 75%, 2/5 = 40%
Percentage → Fraction: ÷ 100, simplify. 60% = 60/100 = 3/5
Decimal → Percentage: × 100 (digits move left 2). 0.35 = 35%
Percentage → Decimal: ÷ 100 (digits move right 2). 8% = 0.08

Percentage of Amount: Convert % to decimal/fraction, multiply.
Non-calculator: Build from 10%, 5%, 1%.
30% of 140 = 10% (14) × 3 = 42
17.5% of 80 = 10% (8) + 5% (4) + 2.5% (2) = 14

Calculator: Use % button or decimal multiplier.
Worked Example 1: FDP Conversions

Convert: (a) 3/8 to % and decimal (b) 62.5% to fraction and decimal (c) 0.125 to % and fraction

(a) 3/8 = 3÷8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
(b) 62.5% = 62.5/100 = 625/1000 = 5/8 = 0.625
(c) 0.125 = 12.5% = 125/1000 = 1/8

Worked Example 2: Percentage of Amount

Find 23% of 450 (calculator) and 35% of 240 (non-calculator)

Calculator: 450 × 0.23 = 103.5 or 450 × 23% = 103.5

Non-calculator: 35% of 240
10% = 24, 30% = 72, 5% = 12
35% = 72 + 12 = 84

Worked Example 3: One Amount as % of Another

Express 42 as a percentage of 60.
42/60 = 7/10 = 0.7 = 70%
Or: (42÷60)×100 = 70%

Key Equivalences:
1/2 = 0.5 = 50% | 1/4 = 0.25 = 25% | 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
1/5 = 0.2 = 20% | 2/5 = 0.4 = 40% | 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%
1/3 = 0.3̇ = 33.3̇% | 2/3 = 0.6̇ = 66.6̇%
1/10 = 0.1 = 10% | 1/100 = 0.01 = 1%

Percentage of Amount: (P/100) × Q or P% × Q
A as % of B: (A/B) × 100

Common Misconception

7% = 0.7

7% = 0.07. Move digits TWO places right (÷100).

Differentiation

Support:
FDP conversion triangle. 10%/1% building blocks. Percentage spider diagrams (100% in centre).
Stretch:
Percentages > 100%. Compound percentages. "A is 20% more than B, B is 25% less than C" chain.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Fire

0.3 = 30% | 45% = 0.45 | 2/5 = 40% | 12.5% = 1/8 | 0.06 = 6%

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Converts fluently all three ways; calculates % of amounts efficiently; expresses one as % of another
  • Getting there: Common equivalences known; calculator % OK; non-calculator 10%/5%/1% method
  • Not yet: Place value errors (7% = 0.7); cannot convert fraction to %; no mental strategies

Lesson 2: Percentage Multipliers and Increase/Decrease

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Multiplier Match

Increase by 20% → × 1.2 | Decrease by 15% → × 0.85 | Increase by 5% → × 1.05

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Percentage Multiplier: Single multiplication for increase/decrease.
Increase by p%: multiply by (1 + p/100)
Decrease by p%: multiply by (1 - p/100)

Examples:
Increase £80 by 15%: 80 × 1.15 = £92
Decrease 250kg by 12%: 250 × 0.88 = 220kg

Repeated Percentage Change: Multiply multipliers.
Increase by 10% then 20%: × 1.1 × 1.2 = × 1.32 (32% total, not 30%)

Compound Interest/Depreciation: Same multipliers repeatedly.
£1000 at 4% for 3 years: 1000 × 1.04³ = £1124.86
Worked Example 1: Using Multipliers

(a) Increase 300 by 12%: 300 × 1.12 = 336
(b) Decrease 85 by 30%: 85 × 0.7 = 59.5
(c) Increase by 5% then decrease by 5%: × 1.05 × 0.95 = × 0.9975 (slight decrease)
(d) VAT at 20%: price × 1.2. Price incl VAT = £120, ex-VAT = 120 ÷ 1.2 = £100

Worked Example 2: Compound Change

Car value £15000. Depreciates 15% per year. Value after 3 years?
Multiplier = 1 - 0.15 = 0.85
Value = 15000 × 0.85³ = 15000 × 0.614125 = £9211.88

Worked Example 3: Finding Multiplier from Change

Price increases from £40 to £46. Percentage increase?
Multiplier = 46/40 = 1.15 → 15% increase

Population decreases from 5000 to 4250. Percentage decrease?
Multiplier = 4250/5000 = 0.85 → 15% decrease

Percentage Multipliers:
Increase p%: × (1 + p/100)
Decrease p%: × (1 - p/100)

Common Multipliers:
+10% → ×1.1 | +20% → ×1.2 | +25% → ×1.25 | +50% → ×1.5
-10% → ×0.9 | -20% → ×0.8 | -25% → ×0.75 | -50% → ×0.5

Repeated: Multiply multipliers together
Compound: Initial × (multiplier)^n

Common Misconception

Increase by 10% then decrease by 10% = back to original

×1.1 then ×0.9 = ×0.99 (1% decrease). 100 → 110 → 99.

Differentiation

Support:
Multiplier table to complete. "Add for increase, subtract for decrease" from 1. Bar models showing 100% → 115%.
Stretch:
Find p given: increase by p% then decrease by p% gives 4% decrease. Compound interest with different rates each year.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Multiplier or %?

× 1.35 → 35% increase
× 0.65 → 35% decrease
Price £80 → £104 → ×1.3 → 30% increase
£500 → £425 → ×0.85 → 15% decrease

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses multipliers fluently; calculates compound change; finds multiplier from before/after
  • Getting there: Finds correct multiplier; applies once; forgets to multiply for repeated change
  • Not yet: Adds percentages for repeated change; calculates increase then adds (two steps); decimal errors

Lesson 3: Reverse Percentages and Percentage Change

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Reverse Thinking

After 20% increase, price is £72. Original? (72 ÷ 1.2 = £60)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Reverse Percentage: Given final amount after % change, find original.
Divide by multiplier (NOT subtract percentage).
Sale price £45 after 25% off: 45 ÷ 0.75 = £60
Price incl 20% VAT = £144: 144 ÷ 1.2 = £120

Percentage Change: (Change ÷ Original) × 100
Change = New - Original (increase positive, decrease negative)

Profit/Loss: Same formula. Profit % = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100. Loss % = (Loss ÷ Cost) × 100.
Worked Example 1: Reverse Percentage

(a) After 15% increase, value is 460. Original?
460 ÷ 1.15 = 400

(b) Sale: 30% off, pay £56. Original price?
56 ÷ 0.7 = 80

(c) Car depreciates 12% p.a. Worth £12000 after 2 years. Original?
12000 ÷ 0.88² = 12000 ÷ 0.7744 = £15495.87

Worked Example 2: Percentage Change

Population 2020: 25000. 2023: 28750. Percentage increase?
Change = 28750 - 25000 = 3750
% increase = (3750/25000) × 100 = 15%

Worked Example 3: Profit/Loss

Buy for £80, sell for £104. Profit %?
Profit = 24
Profit % = (24/80) × 100 = 30%

Buy for £120, sell for £96. Loss %?
Loss = 24
Loss % = (24/120) × 100 = 20%

Reverse Percentage:
Original = Final ÷ Multiplier
Multiplier = 1 ± p/100

Percentage Change:
% change = (New - Original) / Original × 100

Profit % = (Selling - Cost) / Cost × 100
Loss % = (Cost - Selling) / Cost × 100

ALWAYS divide by ORIGINAL value (denominator = original)

Common Misconception

Reverse: 20% off, sale £80. Original = 80 + 20% = 96

20% off means 80% of original. 80 ÷ 0.8 = 100. The 20% is of the ORIGINAL, not the sale price.

Differentiation

Support:
"Divide by multiplier" card. Bar model: 100% → 80% = £80, so 10% = £10, 100% = £100.
Stretch:
Two successive changes: "Increase by x%, then decrease by y%, final = z. Find x." Percentage change over multiple periods.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Mixed Reverse

1) After 25% increase: £125 → Original £100
2) After 10% decrease: £180 → Original £200
3) VAT 20%, total £240 → Ex-VAT £200
4) Profit 40%, selling £98 → Cost £70

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves reverse % by dividing; calculates % change correctly; identifies original as denominator
  • Getting there: Reverse % with simple multipliers; % change formula known but wrong denominator
  • Not yet: Adds/subtracts % for reverse; uses new value as denominator for % change

Homework Suggestions

  • Percentage multipliers worksheet (increase, decrease, compound)
  • Reverse percentage problems (sale prices, VAT, depreciation)
  • Percentage change and profit/loss mixed exercise
  • Real-world: Track a share price or savings account for compound interest
  • CorbettMaths "Percentage Change" and "Reverse Percentages"
