Lesson 2: Core Concepts: practical assessment technique

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on practical assessment technique. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The NEA (Non-Exam Assessment) is a practical project that typically contributes 25-40% of the GCSE grade.
Key Fact: AQA Engineering NEA (40%): students produce engineering drawings, a working prototype and a portfolio.
Key Fact: WJEC Eduqas has two NEA units: Manufacturing Engineering Products (Unit 1) and Designing Engineering Products (Unit 2).
Key Fact: The portfolio must show clear progression through research, design, planning, manufacture and evaluation stages.
Key Fact: Research should be relevant and specific: investigate existing products, materials, processes and user needs.
Key Fact: Design development shows how ideas evolve from initial sketches through to the final chosen solution with justification.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: practical assessment technique

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on practical assessment technique from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: In NEA questions, emphasise the LINK between stages: research informs specification, which drives design, which guides manufacture, which is evaluated against the specification. | Annotation is the most commonly under-used NEA skill - annotate everything. | Evaluation must reference specific specification criteria - generic comments gain no marks. | Show design DEVELOPMENT: initial ideas should differ from the final design, with clear reasons for changes. | Manufacturing plans must be realistic and specific - name actual tools, machines and materials.
Common Errors: ✗ More pages in the portfolio always means a higher mark. ✓ Quality of evidence and depth of thinking matter far more than quantity; a concise, well-annotated portfolio scores higher. ✗ The NEA is only about making the product. ✓ The NEA assesses the full process: research, design, planning, manufacture AND evaluation. Portfolio evidence is weighted equally with or more than the physical product. ✗ You should choose the easiest product to manufacture for the NEA. ✓ The NEA rewards challenge and skill: a simple product demonstrating limited competence scores lower than an ambitious project showing a range of skills. ✗ Evaluation is just saying whether you liked the product. ✓ Evaluation syste
