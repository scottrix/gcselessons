B41: Biodiversity and the Effect of Human Interaction on Ecosystems

Foundation Higher All Boards

Explore what biodiversity means, why it matters, and how human activities are threatening ecosystems and species around the world.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define biodiversity and explain why it is important
  • Identify the main human activities that damage ecosystems
  • Explain the consequences of deforestation, pollution, and global warming on biodiversity
  • Understand the need for conservation and sustainability

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of ecosystems and food webs (B40)
  • Knowledge of adaptation (B33)

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Images/videos of deforestation, pollution, and habitat destruction
  • Worksheets on human impact

Lesson 1: Biodiversity and Human Impact

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Stats

Show: "Current extinction rates are 100-1000 times higher than natural background rates." Ask: "Why does it matter if species go extinct? How does this affect ecosystems?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Biodiversity: the variety of all living organisms in an area.
  • Includes variety of species, genes, and ecosystems
  • Higher biodiversity = more stable ecosystems
  • Currently declining worldwide due to human activities
Why biodiversity matters:
  • Ecosystems with high biodiversity are more resilient to change
  • Provides resources: food, medicine, materials, clean water, pollination
  • Each species plays a role in the ecosystem (even if we don't know what it is)
  • Loss of one species can trigger a chain reaction affecting many others
Human activities damaging ecosystems:
  • Deforestation:
    • Rainforests: cleared for timber, cattle farming, palm oil plantations, soy growing
    • Destruction of habitat → loss of biodiversity
    • Release of stored CO₂ when trees are burned → contributes to global warming
    • Reduces number of trees that absorb CO₂ → more greenhouse effect
    • Soil erosion → land becomes infertile
  • Pollution:
    • Water pollution: sewage, fertilisers (eutrophication), toxic chemicals → kills aquatic organisms
    • Air pollution: burning fossil fuels → acid rain → damages plants and acidifies lakes
    • Land pollution: pesticides, herbicides, industrial waste → harms soil organisms
    • Light pollution: affects nocturnal animals and navigation (e.g. sea turtle hatchlings)
    • Noise pollution: disturbs animal communication and behaviour
  • Global warming:
    • Increased CO₂ and methane → enhanced greenhouse effect → rising temperatures
    • Melting ice caps → loss of polar bear and penguin habitat
    • Rising sea levels → flooding of coastal habitats
    • Changes in migration and breeding patterns
  • Overexploitation:
    • Overfishing → depletion of fish stocks
    • Hunting for traditional medicine (e.g. rhino horn)
    • Pet trade → removal of wild animals
  • Introduction of non-native species:
    • Compete with or prey on native species
    • Can devastate native ecosystems (e.g. grey squirrels outcompeting red squirrels in UK)
Worked Example

"Explain two ways deforestation affects biodiversity." Answer: (1) Habitat loss: when trees are cut down, organisms that lived in the forest lose their habitat and food source, leading to population decline and possible local extinction. (2) Climate change: burning trees releases stored CO₂, contributing to global warming, which affects ecosystems worldwide through changing temperatures and weather patterns.

Common Misconception

"Only tropical rainforests are being deforested"

Deforestation happens worldwide. The UK has lost most of its ancient woodland — only about 2% of the original cover remains. Boreal forests in Russia and Canada are also being cleared. All forest types are under threat.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on: "Biodiversity = variety of life. Humans damage ecosystems through deforestation, pollution, and global warming." Use images and videos to show the impact. Provide a simple matching activity.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research the biodiversity crisis — how many species are estimated to be at risk? Investigate the concept of "ecosystem services" and their economic value.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Three Threats

Students name three human activities that damage ecosystems and one effect of each. Quick share.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain biodiversity and multiple human impacts on ecosystems
  • Getting there: Knows biodiversity matters but cannot identify human impacts
  • Not yet: Cannot define biodiversity or identify human impacts

Lesson 2: Eutrophication and Conservation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Dead Zone

Ask: "Why do fertilisers running off farmland cause dead zones in rivers and lakes?" Introduce eutrophication.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Eutrophication: the enrichment of water with nutrients (especially nitrates from fertilisers), leading to excessive growth of algae.
  1. Fertilisers containing nitrates wash off farmland into rivers/lakes
  2. Excess nitrates cause rapid growth of algae (algal bloom) on the water surface
  3. Algal bloom blocks light from reaching underwater plants → plants die
  4. Dead plants are decomposed by bacteria
  5. Decomposing bacteria use up the dissolved oxygen in the water (aerobic respiration)
  6. Oxygen levels drop → fish and other aquatic organisms suffocate and die
  7. Creates a "dead zone" with very little life
Conservation and sustainability:
  • Conservation: the protection and management of ecosystems and biodiversity
  • Sustainability: meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their needs
Methods of conservation:
  • Nature reserves and national parks: protected areas where habitats are preserved
  • Breeding programmes: for endangered species (e.g. captive breeding of pandas)
  • Reforestation: replanting trees in deforested areas
  • Reducing pollution: laws to limit sewage discharge, use of clean energy
  • Sustainable farming: crop rotation, reduced pesticide use, organic farming
  • Recycling: reduces need for raw materials and reduces landfill
  • International agreements: CITES (trade in endangered species), Paris Agreement (climate change)
Peat bogs:
  • UK peat bogs store huge amounts of carbon and are important habitats
  • Being destroyed for garden compost and agriculture
  • Conservation: stop using peat compost, restore peat bogs
Worked Example

"Describe the process of eutrophication." Answer: (1) Fertilisers run off farmland into water. (2) Excess nitrates cause algal blooms. (3) Algae block light from underwater plants. (4) Underwater plants die. (5) Decomposing bacteria use up oxygen. (6) Fish and other organisms die from lack of oxygen.

Common Misconception

"Fertilisers directly poison fish"

Fertilisers do not directly poison fish. The process of eutrophication causes algal blooms, which block light and lead to oxygen depletion. It is the lack of oxygen (not the fertiliser itself) that kills the fish.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Simplify eutrophication: "Fertiliser → algae grow → plants die → bacteria use oxygen → fish die." Provide a flow diagram to label. For conservation: "We can help by recycling, using less plastic, and protecting nature reserves."
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Investigate peat bogs in the UK — why are they important? How much carbon do they store? Research the "Extinction Rebellion" movement and its aims.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Eutrophication Steps

Students number the steps of eutrophication in the correct order. Quick self-check.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain eutrophication and evaluate conservation methods
  • Getting there: Can describe eutrophication but cannot evaluate conservation
  • Not yet: Cannot describe eutrophication or conservation methods

Homework Suggestions

  • Research one example of habitat destruction (e.g. Amazon rainforest, Great Barrier Reef) and explain its effects on biodiversity
  • Create a poster about conservation methods with at least 5 strategies
  • Investigate the carbon footprint of everyday activities and suggest ways to reduce it
  • Research the concept of "rewilding" and give examples of rewilding projects in the UK
