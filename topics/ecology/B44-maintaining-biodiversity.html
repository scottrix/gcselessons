B44: Maintaining Biodiversity

Foundation Higher All Boards

Learn about the methods used to maintain and restore biodiversity, including breeding programmes, habitat management, and reforestation.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2 | Tier: Foundation and Higher | Duration: 50 min per lesson | Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why maintaining biodiversity is important
  • Describe methods used to maintain biodiversity: breeding programmes, protection of rare habitats, reforestation, reduction of deforestation, controlled burning
  • Understand the role of seed banks and gene banks
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of different conservation strategies

Materials

  • Whiteboard and pens, case study worksheets, images of conservation projects

Lesson 1: Methods of Maintaining Biodiversity

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Question

Ask: "What would happen if all bees went extinct? How would it affect humans and ecosystems?" Discuss pollination and food chains.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Why maintain biodiversity:
  • Ecosystems need a variety of species to function properly
  • Species provide ecosystem services: pollination, decomposition, water purification, soil formation
  • Each species has a role — removing one can cause a cascade of effects
  • Biodiversity provides resources: food, medicine, materials
Methods of maintaining biodiversity:
  • Breeding programmes: captive breeding of endangered species in zoos and special centres, then releasing into protected wild habitats (e.g. Giant Panda, Arabian Oryx)
  • Protection of rare habitats: establishing nature reserves, national parks, and Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) to preserve ecosystems
  • Reforestation: planting trees in areas that have been deforested. Can be: natural regeneration (letting nature regrow) or planting saplings. Restores habitats and absorbs CO₂
  • Reduction of deforestation: sustainable logging, using less paper, buying sustainable products (FSC-certified wood), reducing demand for palm oil
  • Controlled burning: in some ecosystems (e.g. heathland, grassland), controlled burning prevents overgrowth and maintains habitat diversity. Burns old vegetation to allow new growth.
  • Seed banks: store seeds from many plant species at low temperatures. Acts as insurance against extinction. Example: Svalbard Global Seed Vault stores seeds from crops worldwide.
  • Gene banks: store genetic material (sperm, eggs, embryos, tissue samples) from endangered species. Can be used for future breeding programmes.
Worked Example

"Give two methods used to maintain biodiversity and explain how each one helps." Answer: (1) Breeding programmes — captive breeding of endangered species increases their numbers before releasing them into the wild, boosting wild populations. (2) Seed banks — storing seeds preserves genetic diversity and provides insurance against species extinction in the wild.

Common Misconception

"Conservation means leaving nature completely alone"

Some ecosystems need active management to maintain biodiversity. For example, heathlands need controlled burning to prevent them becoming woodland, and some nature reserves need invasive species removed to protect native wildlife.

Differentiation

Support: Focus on three methods: breeding programmes, nature reserves, seed banks. Provide images of each.
Stretch: Research the Svalbard Global Seed Vault — how does it work and why is it important?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Students name three methods to maintain biodiversity and write one advantage of each.

Assessment

  • Got it: Can describe and explain multiple methods of maintaining biodiversity
  • Getting there: Can name methods but cannot explain how they help
  • Not yet: Cannot describe methods of maintaining biodiversity

Lesson 2: Evaluating Conservation Strategies

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Case Study

Discuss: "Should we spend money saving the Giant Panda when that money could help hungry people? How do we decide which species to prioritise?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Evaluating conservation strategies:
  • Breeding programmes:
    • Advantages: increases numbers of endangered species, maintains genetic diversity
    • Disadvantages: expensive, animals may not survive in the wild, small population = inbreeding risk
  • Nature reserves:
    • Advantages: protects habitats, allows natural processes to continue, relatively cheap long-term
    • Disadvantages: limited area, habitats can still be damaged by pollution/climate change, may conflict with human land use
  • Reforestation:
    • Advantages: restores habitat, absorbs CO₂, prevents soil erosion
    • Disadvantages: takes decades to mature, may not restore full biodiversity, monoculture plantations are less valuable than natural woodland
  • Seed banks:
    • Advantages: preserves genetic diversity, relatively cheap, acts as insurance
    • Disadvantages: does not protect organisms in the wild, seeds may not germinate after long storage, cannot store all species
Balancing conservation with human needs:
  • Conservation must consider the needs of local communities
  • Sustainable development: meeting current needs without harming future generations
  • Example: ecotourism provides income while protecting wildlife
  • International cooperation needed: many species cross national borders
Worked Example

"Evaluate the use of captive breeding programmes for conservation." Advantages: (1) Increases numbers of critically endangered species. (2) Maintains genetic diversity in a controlled environment. Disadvantages: (1) Very expensive to maintain. (2) Animals raised in captivity may lack survival skills. (3) Small populations are vulnerable to inbreeding and disease.

Common Misconception

"All conservation is successful"

Conservation is expensive and not always successful. Some species have been saved (e.g. Giant Panda, White Rhinoceros) while others have gone extinct despite conservation efforts. Success depends on funding, political will, and the severity of the threat.

Differentiation

Support: Provide a simple pros/cons table for three conservation methods.
Stretch: Debate: "Should conservation funding be based on the ecological importance of a species or its appeal to humans?"

Plenary (5 minutes)

Students write one advantage and one disadvantage of each of two conservation methods.

Assessment

  • Got it: Can evaluate conservation methods with balanced arguments
  • Getting there: Can describe methods but cannot evaluate
  • Not yet: Cannot describe or evaluate conservation methods

Homework Suggestions

  • Research one conservation success story and one failure. Explain why one succeeded and the other did not.
  • Design a conservation plan for a local area (e.g. a park or woodland)
  • Investigate the Svalbard Global Seed Vault — how does it work and why is it needed?
  • Write a balanced argument: "Should more money be spent on conservation?"
