B42: Trophic Levels in Ecosystems

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understand how energy is transferred between trophic levels and why only a small percentage of energy is available to each level.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the trophic levels in an ecosystem
  • Understand how energy is transferred between trophic levels
  • Explain why pyramids of biomass are always pyramid-shaped
  • Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer between trophic levels

Materials and Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Pyramid of biomass diagram templates
  • Worksheets on energy transfer calculations

Lesson 1: Trophic Levels and Energy Transfer

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Energy Question

Ask: "If 1000J of energy is stored in grass, how much of that energy ends up in a rabbit that eats the grass?" (Answer: much less.)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Trophic levels: each step in a food chain.
  • Level 1: producers (plants) — make food by photosynthesis
  • Level 2: primary consumers (herbivores) — eat producers
  • Level 3: secondary consumers (carnivores) — eat primary consumers
  • Level 4: tertiary consumers (top carnivores)
Energy transfer: only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level. The other 90% is lost through:
  • Respiration (energy lost as heat for life processes)
  • Excretion (energy lost in faeces and urine)
  • Not all parts eaten (bones, bark, roots)
  • Some organisms not eaten before they die
Example: Grass (10,000J) → Rabbit (1,000J) → Fox (100J). Only 10% passes each time. This is why food chains rarely have more than 4-5 trophic levels.
Worked Example

"Calculate efficiency: grass stores 50,000J, rabbit stores 5,000J." Answer: Efficiency = (5,000 / 50,000) x 100 = 10%.

Common Misconception

"Energy is destroyed"

Energy is not destroyed — it is lost as heat through respiration. But heat cannot be used by organisms, so it is lost from the food chain.

Differentiation

Support: Focus on "10% passes to next level, rest lost as heat." Provide simple diagram.
Stretch: Research why carnivores need to eat more food per day than herbivores.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Students calculate energy at each level: Grass = 100,000J. What is at levels 2, 3, 4?

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe trophic levels and calculate efficiency
  • Getting there: Understands energy is lost but cannot calculate
  • Not yet: Cannot describe trophic levels

Lesson 2: Pyramids of Biomass

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Shape Question

Ask: "Why do pyramids of biomass always have a wide base and narrow top?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Pyramids of biomass: diagrams showing mass of living material at each trophic level.
  • Always pyramid-shaped (wide base, narrow top)
  • Base = producers (largest biomass — they make their own food)
  • Each level above is smaller because less energy is available
  • Biomass is measured as dry mass (g/m²)
Why always pyramid-shaped:
  • Producers capture energy from sunlight
  • Only ~10% of energy passes to each higher level
  • Less energy = less biomass can be supported
  • Therefore biomass always decreases at higher levels
Biomass can be calculated from data and plotted as horizontal bars. The shape should be wider at the bottom.
Worked Example

"Grass = 500g/m², Rabbits = 50g/m², Foxes = 5g/m². Explain the shape." Answer: Biomass decreases by about 10% at each level because energy is lost through respiration and incomplete feeding.

Common Misconception

"Pyramids of number always match pyramids of biomass"

Pyramids of NUMBER can be inverted (one tree supports many insects). But pyramids of BIOMASS are always pyramid-shaped.

Differentiation

Support: Label a pre-drawn pyramid. Focus on "producers have most biomass."
Stretch: Design a method to measure biomass in a local habitat using quadrats.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Students draw a pyramid from given data. Quick shape check.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain pyramid shape and draw from data
  • Getting there: Knows shape but cannot draw from data
  • Not yet: Cannot explain or draw pyramids of biomass

Homework Suggestions

  • Calculate energy transfer efficiency for 5 different food chains
  • Draw pyramids of biomass from provided datasets
  • Research why apex predators are rare compared to producers
  • Investigate how deforestation affects energy transfer in an ecosystem
