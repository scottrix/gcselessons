G15: Measuring & Bearings

Foundation Higher All Boards

Master three-figure bearings: measure from North, clockwise, three digits. Solve navigation problems using scale drawings, angle facts, and trigonometry. Construct and interpret scale diagrams.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Understand and use three-figure bearing notation (000° to 359°)
  • Measure and draw bearings accurately using protractor
  • Calculate bearings using angle facts (parallel lines, triangle sum)
  • Solve bearing problems with scale drawings
  • Apply trigonometry (Pythagoras, SOHCAHTOA) to bearing problems (Higher)

Prerequisites

  • Angle facts (G3)
  • Parallel line angles (G3)
  • Measuring angles with protractor

Materials & Equipment

  • Protractors, rulers, sharp pencils
  • Squared paper for scale drawings
  • Compasses (for constructing North lines)

Lesson 1: Understanding and Measuring Bearings

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Compass Directions

N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW. Convert to three-figure bearings: N=000°, NE=045°, E=090°, SE=135°, S=180°, SW=225°, W=270°, NW=315°.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Bearing definition: Angle measured clockwise from North (000°). Always three digits (000°–359°).
Key rules:
1. Start at North (vertical up on page)
2. Measure CLOCKWISE
3. Write as THREE FIGURES (045°, not 45°)
Measuring bearing of B from A:
1. Draw North line at A (vertical up)
2. Draw line AB
3. Measure clockwise angle from North to AB
4. Record as three figures
Drawing bearing of B from A:
1. Draw North line at A
2. Place protractor centre at A, 0° at North
3. Mark angle clockwise
4. Draw line through mark
Back bearing (reciprocal): Bearing of A from B = (bearing of B from A ± 180°) mod 360°. Add 180° if < 180°, subtract 180° if ≥ 180°.
North lines are PARALLEL: Use corresponding/alternate/co-interior angles (G3) to find bearings between multiple points.
Worked Example

"Bearing of B from A is 070°. Find bearing of A from B." Answer: 070° + 180° = 250°. (Or: North lines parallel → co-interior angles sum to 180°).

Worked Example

"Bearing of B from A is 230°. Find bearing of A from B." Answer: 230° − 180° = 050°.

Common Misconception

"Bearing is measured from the horizontal (East) or anticlockwise"

ALWAYS from North (vertical up), ALWAYS clockwise. Three figures: 090° not 90°, 005° not 5°.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Protractor with 0° at top (or rotate paper). Practise: "North line up, measure clockwise". Use bearing template: circle with North marked, 10° increments. Colour-code: North (red), bearing angle (blue).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Bearings on sphere (great circle navigation — not GCSE). Vector approach: bearing θ → direction vector (sin θ, cos θ). Three-point resection (find position from three bearings).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Bearing Bingo

Teacher calls: "North-East" → students write 045°. "Back bearing of 120°" → 300°. "Bearing of A from B if B from A is 315°" → 135°.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Measures and draws bearings accurately, calculates back bearings, uses three figures
  • Getting there: Understands concept but forgets three figures or measures anticlockwise
  • Not yet: Confuses clockwise/anticlockwise, cannot measure from North

Lesson 2: Bearing Problems with Scale Drawings

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Scale Drawing

1 cm = 1 km. Draw line 5 cm at bearing 060°. End point is 5 km away at 060°.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Scale drawings for bearings:
- Choose suitable scale (1 cm = 1 km, 1 cm = 10 km, etc.)
- Draw North line at starting point
- Measure bearing, draw line to scale
- Repeat for each leg of journey
- Final position measured from start
Solving problems:
1. Draw scale diagram accurately
2. Measure distance and bearing of final position from start
3. OR: Use geometry/trigonometry to calculate (more accurate)
Angle facts in bearings:
- North lines parallel → alternate angles equal, corresponding equal, co-interior sum 180°
- Bearing of B from A = θ. Bearing of A from B = θ ± 180°
- Interior angles of triangle formed by three points sum to 180°
Worked Example

"Ship sails 6 km on bearing 050°, then 8 km on bearing 140°. Find distance and bearing from start." Answer: Scale 1 cm = 1 km. Draw North at O. 6 cm at 050° → A. North at A (parallel). 8 cm at 140° → B. Measure OB ≈ 10.8 cm = 10.8 km. Bearing of B from O ≈ 100°. (Can also use cosine rule: OB² = 6²+8²−2×6×8×cos(90°) = 36+64 = 100 → OB = 10 km. Angle at A = 90° (140°−50°). Sine rule for bearing.)

Common Misconception

"The angle between two bearings is just the difference"

Angle between bearing 050° and 140° is 90° (difference). But angle between 350° and 010° is 20° (not 340°). Always take smaller angle: min(|θ₁−θ₂|, 360−|θ₁−θ₂|).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide pre-drawn North lines. Use larger scales (1 cm = 1 km). Step-by-step: 1) Draw start, 2) Draw North, 3) Measure bearing, 4) Draw line to scale. Check with ruler/protractor.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Solve using cosine/sine rules instead of scale drawing. Multi-leg journeys (3+ legs). "Ship at A, bearing to lighthouse 030°. Ship sails 5 km on 090°, new bearing to lighthouse 340°. Find distance to lighthouse."

Plenary (5 minutes)

Scale Drawing Check

Students swap scale drawings. Check: North lines parallel? Bearings measured clockwise from North? Scale consistent? Measurements accurate?

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Constructs accurate scale diagrams, solves multi-leg bearing problems
  • Getting there: Draws single bearing correctly, struggles with multiple legs or North line alignment
  • Not yet: Cannot draw bearing to scale

Lesson 3: Trigonometry and Advanced Bearing Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

SOHCAHTOA Recap

Right triangle: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse relative to angle. sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Using trigonometry for bearings: Create right triangles by drawing North lines and East/West lines.
Method: 1) Draw diagram with North lines. 2) Identify right triangles. 3) Use known angles (bearings, alternate angles) and sides. 4) Apply trig ratios. 5) Find missing distances/bearings.
Components: A bearing of θ means direction vector has East component = distance × sin θ, North component = distance × cos θ. (Note: sin for East, cos for North because angle from North).
Multiple legs: Add East components, add North components. Resultant displacement: Pythagoras for distance, tan⁻¹(East/North) for bearing (adjust quadrant).
Example: Bearing 050°, distance 6 km. East = 6 sin 50° = 4.60. North = 6 cos 50° = 3.86.
Careful with quadrants: Bearing 000°–090°: East +, North +. 090°–180°: East +, North −. 180°–270°: East −, North −. 270°–360°: East −, North +.
Worked Example

"Walk 4 km on bearing 030°, then 5 km on bearing 120°. Find final displacement using components." Answer: Leg 1: E = 4 sin 30° = 2, N = 4 cos 30° = 3.46. Leg 2: E = 5 sin 120° = 5 sin 60° = 4.33, N = 5 cos 120° = −2.5. Total: E = 6.33, N = 0.96. Distance = √(6.33²+0.96²) = 6.40 km. Bearing = tan⁻¹(6.33/0.96) = tan⁻¹(6.59) = 81.3° → 081° (since E+, N+).

Common Misconception

"sin for North component, cos for East component"

Bearing measured from North. So angle from vertical. North = adjacent = cos. East = opposite = sin. Mnemonic: "North is adjacent to North line".

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Stick to scale drawings for Foundation. Provide component table template. Use bearing ≤ 90° first, then extend.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Bearing problems with sine/cosine rules (non-right triangles). "Ship A at (0,0), Ship B at (10,0). A sails 045° at 20 km/h. B sails 315° at 15 km/h. When are they closest?" Relative velocity vectors.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Component Race

Rapid fire: "5 km at 060° → E = ?, N = ?" "3 km at 210° → E = ?, N = ?" "E=4, N=3 → distance, bearing?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses trigonometry/components to solve bearing problems accurately
  • Getting there: Sets up components but sign errors (N/S, E/W) or quadrant errors for bearing
  • Not yet: Cannot relate bearing angle to trig ratios

Homework Suggestions

  • Bearing worksheet: measure, draw, calculate back bearings, scale drawings
  • Trigonometry bearing problems (Higher): component method
  • Real-world: Plan a 3-leg walk using OS map. Give bearings and distances for each leg.
  • Navigation puzzle: "Start at school. 045° 2 km. 135° 3 km. 225° 2 km. Where are you?" (Back near start)
