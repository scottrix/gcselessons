G17: Circles: Area & Circumference

Foundation Higher All Boards

Calculate circumference and area of circles using π. Find radius/diameter from circumference or area. Solve problems involving semicircles, quarter circles, and compound shapes with circles.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Know and use C = πd = 2πr and A = πr²
  • Calculate circumference/area given radius or diameter
  • Find radius/diameter given circumference or area (reverse)
  • Calculate perimeter and area of semicircles and quarter circles
  • Solve compound shape problems involving circles

Prerequisites

  • Circle parts (G9)
  • Substitution and rearranging formulae (A5)

Materials & Equipment

  • Calculators (π button)
  • String for circumference investigation
  • Circle templates, compasses

Lesson 1: Circumference and Area Formulas

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Discover Pi

Measure circumference and diameter of 5 circular objects (tins, lids, wheels). Calculate C/d. All ≈ 3.14. This is π.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Pi (π): Ratio of circumference to diameter. Irrational ≈ 3.14159... Use π button on calculator, or 3.14/22/7 for non-calculator.
Circumference: C = πd = 2πr. d = diameter, r = radius (d = 2r).
Area: A = πr². Derive by cutting circle into sectors, rearranging to parallelogram: base = ½C = πr, height = r → A = πr × r = πr².
Using formulas: Identify r or d. Substitute. Use π button. Round appropriately (usually 1 dp or 3 sf).
Reverse problems: Given C, find r: r = C/(2π). Given A, find r: r = √(A/π).
Non-calculator: Leave in terms of π (e.g., 36π cm²) or use π ≈ 3.14 or 22/7.
Worked Example

"Circle radius 7 cm. Circumference and area (in terms of π)?" Answer: C = 2π×7 = 14π cm. A = π×7² = 49π cm².

Worked Example

"Circle circumference 44 cm. Radius? (Use π = 22/7)" Answer: 44 = 2 × (22/7) × r → 44 = (44/7)r → r = 7 cm.

Worked Example

"Circle area 50π cm². Diameter?" Answer: 50π = πr² → r² = 50 → r = √50 = 5√2 cm. d = 10√2 cm.

Common Misconception

"Diameter = 2 × radius, so area = πd²"

Area = πr² = π(d/2)² = πd²/4. NOT πd². Remember: radius is half diameter, so square gives ¼ factor.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Formula triangles: C = πd (cover d → C/π). A = πr² (cover r → √(A/π)). Use 3.14 for π. Colour-code radius (red) and diameter (blue) in diagrams.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Derive area formula using limits (sectors → parallelogram). Explore π history. Calculate circumference of Earth (radius 6371 km). "Squaring the circle" impossibility proof.

Plenary (5 minutes)

In Terms of Pi

Students give answers in terms of π. "r=5 → C=?, A=?" "d=10 → C=?, A=?" "C=20π → r=?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses both formulas fluently, reverse problems, leaves in terms of π
  • Getting there: Forward problems OK, reverse problems confuse r and d
  • Not yet: Confuses C = πd and A = πr², forgets to square r

Lesson 2: Semicircles, Quarter Circles, and Compound Shapes

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Half and Quarter

Circle area 64π. Semicircle area? Quarter circle area? Circle circumference 20π. Semicircle perimeter (including diameter)?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Semicircle: Half circle. Area = ½πr². Perimeter = ½(2πr) + d = πr + 2r (curved + diameter).
Quarter circle: Area = ¼πr². Perimeter = ¼(2πr) + 2r = ½πr + 2r (curved + two radii).
Compound shapes with circles: Split into rectangles, triangles, circles/semicircles/quarters. Add/subtract areas.
Shaded regions: Often "area of rectangle minus semicircle" or "square minus quarter circles at corners".
Perimeter of compound shapes: Sum of outer edges only. Internal edges not counted.
Worked Example

"Semicircle radius 6 cm. Area and perimeter?" Answer: Area = ½π×6² = 18π cm². Perimeter = π×6 + 12 = 6π + 12 cm.

Worked Example

"Square side 10 cm. Four quarter circles radius 5 cm cut from corners. Shaded area?" Answer: Square = 100 cm². Four quarters = one circle radius 5 → π×25 = 25π. Shaded = 100 − 25π cm².

Common Misconception

"Perimeter of semicircle = πr" (forgetting diameter)"

Perimeter includes the straight edge (diameter). Semicircle perimeter = πr + 2r. Quarter circle perimeter = ½πr + 2r.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide diagrams with split lines. Colour-code: add (green), subtract (red). Perimeter: trace outer boundary with highlighter.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Area of lens (intersection of two circles). Area of circle segment (sector − triangle). Circle packing problems (max circles in rectangle).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Compound Shape Challenge

Rectangle 12×8 with semicircle on one 12 side. Find total area and perimeter.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates semicircle/quarter circle area and perimeter, solves compound shapes
  • Getting there: Area OK, forgets straight edges in perimeter
  • Not yet: Cannot split compound shapes

Lesson 3: Problem Solving and Applications

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Real Circles

Bicycle wheel diameter 70 cm. Distance for 100 revolutions? (C = 70π cm, 100 revs = 7000π cm = 70π m ≈ 219.9 m).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Problem types:
- Revolutions: distance = revolutions × circumference
- Wheels/gears: ratio of circumferences = ratio of revolutions
- Running track: two straights + two semicircles
- Garden/pool: circular or part-circular shapes
- Algebraic: r = x, A = πx² = 100 → find x
Units: Convert: cm to m, m² to cm². 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m² = 10,000 cm².
Bounds (Higher): Radius measured as 5 cm (nearest cm). Max area = π×5.5², min area = π×4.5².
Worked Example

"Running track: two straights 80 m, two semicircles radius 30 m. Inside lane distance?" Answer: Two semicircles = one circle C = 2π×30 = 60π m. Straights = 160 m. Total = 160 + 60π ≈ 348.5 m.

Worked Example

"Circular pond diameter 4 m. Path 1 m wide around it. Area of path?" Answer: Outer radius = 2+1 = 3 m. Inner radius = 2 m. Path area = π(3²−2²) = π(9−4) = 5π m².

Common Misconception

"Revolutions × diameter = distance"

Distance = revolutions × CIRCUMFERENCE = revolutions × πd. Not revolutions × d.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Step-by-step problem templates. Use 3.14 for π. Focus on one problem type at a time. Visual diagrams for each scenario.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Rolling without slipping: cycloids. Belt/chain drives: crossed belts. Circle inscribed in triangle (incircle). Circumscribed circle (circumcircle). Area of annulus (ring) = π(R²−r²).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Fire

"Wheel diameter 60 cm. 50 revolutions = ? m" "Circle area 36π → radius?" "Semicircle perimeter 10π+20 → radius?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves multi-step circle problems, including revolutions, tracks, paths
  • Getting there: Solves basic area/circumference, struggles with compound or reverse problems
  • Not yet: Confuses formulas, forgets π

Homework Suggestions

  • Circle worksheet: circumference, area, reverse, semicircles, compound shapes
  • Measure 3 circular objects at home. Calculate circumference and area.
  • Problem: "A goat is tied to a corner of a 10×10 m barn with a 15 m rope. Grazing area?" (3/4 circle + quarters)
  • Past paper questions on circles
