G5: Congruence Criteria

Foundation Higher All Boards

Learn the four triangle congruence criteria (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS). Identify congruent triangles, write congruence statements, and use congruence to prove geometric facts.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Understand congruence: same shape and size (all corresponding sides and angles equal)
  • Know and apply the four congruence criteria: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS
  • Identify why ASS/SSA and AAA are NOT congruence criteria
  • Write formal congruence statements (△ABC ≅ △DEF) with correct vertex order
  • Use congruence to prove other geometric properties

Prerequisites

  • Geometric notation (G1)
  • Triangle properties (G4)
  • Constructions (G2) — for understanding why criteria work

Materials & Equipment

  • Card triangles for matching activities
  • Congruence criteria reference cards
  • Dynamic geometry software (optional)

Lesson 1: The Four Criteria

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Same or Different?

Show pairs of triangles. Some identical, some same angles different sizes (similar), some same two sides but different included angle. Students decide: congruent, similar, or neither?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Congruence definition: Two shapes are congruent if one can be transformed (translated, rotated, reflected) to exactly match the other. All corresponding sides equal, all corresponding angles equal.
Notation: △ABC ≅ △DEF means A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Order matters! Corresponding vertices in same position.
Four criteria (minimum conditions):
1. SSS (Side-Side-Side): Three sides equal → triangles congruent.
2. SAS (Side-Angle-Side): Two sides and INCLUDED angle equal → congruent. Angle must be BETWEEN the two sides.
3. ASA (Angle-Side-Angle): Two angles and INCLUDED side equal → congruent. Side must be BETWEEN the two angles. (AAS also works — third angle determined by 180° sum).
4. RHS (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side): Right angle, hypotenuse, and one other side equal → congruent. ONLY for right triangles.
Why they work: Link to constructions (G2). SSS = construct triangle from 3 sides. SAS = construct from 2 sides and included angle. ASA = construct from 2 angles and included side. RHS = construct right triangle from hypotenuse and one side.
Worked Example

"△ABC and △DEF: AB = DE = 5 cm, BC = EF = 7 cm, ∠B = ∠E = 40°. Are they congruent?" Answer: Yes, SAS. Two sides (AB, BC and DE, EF) and included angle (∠B, ∠E) equal. Write: △ABC ≅ △DEF (SAS).

Worked Example

"△PQR and △STU: ∠P = ∠S = 30°, ∠Q = ∠T = 80°, PQ = ST = 6 cm. Congruent?" Answer: Yes, ASA (or AAS). Two angles and included side. Third angles: ∠R = 70°, ∠U = 70°. Write: △PQR ≅ △STU (ASA).

Common Misconception

"ASS (Angle-Side-Side) is a congruence criterion"

ASS/SSA is NOT a valid criterion (ambiguous case). Two sides and a non-included angle can give 0, 1, or 2 triangles. RHS is the special case where the angle is 90° — this works because the right angle fixes the orientation.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use physical triangle cards to match. Colour-code corresponding parts. Provide criteria checklist: "Do I have SSS? SAS? ASA? RHS?" Practise writing congruence statements with correct vertex order.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Explore why AAS works (angle sum) but ASS doesn't. Prove criteria using transformations or constructions. Investigate the ambiguous case of SSA with the sine rule (Higher preview). Use congruence to prove angle bisector theorem.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Valid or Not?

Show 5 triangle pairs with given equal parts. Students identify criterion or explain why not congruent (e.g., AAA = similar only, ASS = ambiguous).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies correct criterion, writes congruence statements with correct vertex order, explains why ASS/AAA fail
  • Getting there: Knows criteria names but confuses included/non-included, vertex order wrong
  • Not yet: Cannot match criteria to given information

Lesson 2: Congruence Proofs and Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Spot the Criterion

Diagram with two triangles sharing a side or angle. Students identify which congruence criterion applies and what extra info is needed.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:

1. State triangles to prove congruent (△ABC and △DEF)
2. List three pairs of equal sides/angles with reasons
3. State congruence criterion (SSS/SAS/ASA/RHS)
4. Conclude △ABC ≅ △DEF
5. Use CPCTC (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent) to prove other equalities
Common proof setups:
- Diagonals of parallelogram bisect (ASA: alternate angles + common side)
- Base angles of isosceles triangle (RHS: altitude from apex)
- Diagonals of kite/rhombus properties
- Midpoint theorem constructions
Worked Example

"Prove diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other." Answer: In parallelogram ABCD, diagonals AC, BD intersect at O. Prove △AOB ≅ △COD. AB ∥ CD → ∠ABO = ∠CDO (alternate). AB = CD (opposite sides). ∠BAO = ∠DCO (alternate). ASA → △AOB ≅ △COD. AO = OC, BO = OD (CPCTC).

Common Misconception

"AAA proves congruence"

AAA proves SIMILARITY (same shape, not necessarily same size). For congruence, need at least one side length (ASA, AAS, SAS, SSS, RHS).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide proof templates with blanks. Focus on identifying equal parts in diagrams. Use "reason bank" for justifications (given, alternate angles, common side, etc.).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove: "If two triangles have two sides and the altitude to the third side equal, they are congruent." Explore congruence in 3D (tetrahedra). Formal proof of RHS criterion using Pythagoras (Hypotenuse² = Side1² + Side2²).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Complete the Proof

Scaffolded proof with missing reasons. Students fill in: "∠A = ∠D because ____", "AB = DE because ____", "Therefore △ABC ≅ △DEF by ____".

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Constructs complete congruence proofs, uses CPCTC, identifies shared sides/angles in complex diagrams
  • Getting there: Can identify criteria but struggles to structure formal proof
  • Not yet: Cannot identify three matching pairs in a diagram

Homework Suggestions

  • Congruence worksheet: identify criterion, write statements, complete proofs
  • Prove: "The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at 90°" using congruent triangles
  • Prove: "The angle bisector of the apex of an isosceles triangle is also the perpendicular bisector of the base"
  • Create a "Congruence Criteria" revision card with diagrams and examples for each
