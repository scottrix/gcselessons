G20: Pythagoras & Trigonometry

Foundation Higher All Boards

Master Pythagoras' theorem for right triangles and trigonometric ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) for finding missing sides and angles. Solve 2D and 3D problems.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 5
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Use Pythagoras' theorem: a² + b² = c² for right triangles
  • Identify when to use Pythagoras vs trigonometry
  • Use SOHCAHTOA: sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj
  • Find missing sides and angles in right triangles
  • Solve 3D problems (diagonal of cuboid, angle between line and plane)

Prerequisites

  • Squares and square roots (N6)
  • Rearranging formulae (A5)
  • Angle facts (G3)

Materials & Equipment

  • Scientific calculators (sin, cos, tan, inverse)
  • Right triangle templates
  • 3D models (cuboids, pyramids)

Lesson 1: Pythagoras' Theorem

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Square Numbers

3²+4²=5². 5²+12²=13². 8²+15²=17². Students find more Pythagorean triples.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Pythagoras' theorem: In a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of squares on the other two sides. a² + b² = c² (c = hypotenuse, longest side, opposite right angle).
Using the theorem:
- Find hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²)
- Find shorter side: a = √(c² − b²)
Identifying hypotenuse: Always opposite right angle. Always longest side.
Checking right angle: If a² + b² = c², triangle is right-angled (converse).
Common triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, 9-40-41. Multiples also work (6-8-10, 9-12-15).
Units: All lengths in same units before calculating.
Worked Example

"Right triangle: sides 6 cm and 8 cm. Hypotenuse?" Answer: c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. c = 10 cm. (3-4-5 triple ×2).

Worked Example

"Right triangle: hypotenuse 13 cm, one side 5 cm. Other side?" Answer: a² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144. a = 12 cm. (5-12-13 triple).

"a² + b² = c² works for ALL triangles"

ONLY for right triangles. For non-right triangles, use cosine rule (Higher G22) or split into right triangles.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Pythagoras triangle diagram: squares on sides. "Add squares of two shorter sides, square root for hypotenuse." "Square hypotenuse, subtract square of known side, square root for missing side." Use triples for practice.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Proof of Pythagoras (multiple proofs: area, similar triangles, Garfield). Pythagoras in 3D: diagonal of cuboid = √(l²+w²+h²). Distance between two points in 3D. Fermat's Last Theorem (no integer solutions for n>2).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Right or Not?

Sides given. Students check if right triangle: 6,8,10 (yes). 7,8,9 (no). 9,12,15 (yes). 10,10,10 (no).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies Pythagoras correctly, identifies hypotenuse, solves reverse problems
  • Getting there: Finds hypotenuse OK, forgets to subtract when finding shorter side
  • Not yet: Confuses hypotenuse, adds instead of subtracts

Lesson 2: Introduction to Trigonometry (SOHCAHTOA)

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Label the Triangle

Right triangle with angle θ marked. Students label: hypotenuse (opposite right angle), opposite (opposite θ), adjacent (next to θ, not hypotenuse).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Labelling: Hypotenuse (H) = longest, opposite right angle. Opposite (O) = opposite angle θ. Adjacent (A) = next to θ (and right angle).
SOHCAHTOA:
- SOH: sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
- CAH: cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
- TOA: tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent
Finding sides: Known angle, known side → find unknown side. Choose ratio with known and unknown. Rearrange: unknown = known × ratio (if unknown is numerator) or unknown = known / ratio (if unknown is denominator).
Finding angles: Known two sides → find angle. Choose ratio with two known sides. Use inverse: θ = sin⁻¹(O/H), cos⁻¹(A/H), tan⁻¹(O/A). Calculator in DEGREE mode.
Worked Example

"Right triangle: angle 30°, hypotenuse 10 cm. Find opposite." Answer: sin 30° = opp/10 → opp = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm.

Worked Example

"Right triangle: opposite 6 cm, adjacent 8 cm. Find angle." Answer: tan θ = 6/8 = 0.75. θ = tan⁻¹(0.75) = 36.9° (to 1 dp).

"sin⁻¹ means 1/sin (reciprocal)"

sin⁻¹ is INVERSE function (finds angle from ratio). 1/sin is cosecant (csc). Calculator sin⁻¹ button = shift sin.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): SOHCAHTOA triangle mnemonic. Formula triangles: cover unknown. Step-by-step: 1) Label O, A, H, 2) Choose ratio, 3) Write equation, 4) Rearrange, 5) Calculate. Degrees mode check.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Derive sin/cos/tan from unit circle. Exact values for 30°, 45°, 60° (G21). Trig identities: sin²+cos²=1, tan=sin/cos. Reciprocal functions: csc, sec, cot.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Which Ratio?

Given: angle and hypotenuse, find opposite → sin. Angle and adjacent, find hypotenuse → cos. Opposite and adjacent, find angle → tan. Rapid fire.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Labels correctly, chooses ratio, finds sides and angles
  • Getting there: Labels OK, chooses wrong ratio, or calculator in radian mode
  • Not yet: Cannot identify opposite/adjacent/hypotenuse

Lesson 3: Mixed Pythagoras and Trigonometry Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Pythagoras or Trig?

"Two sides known, find third" → Pythagoras. "One side and one angle known" → Trig. "Two sides known, find angle" → Trig.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Decision tree:
- Right triangle? Yes → continue. No → split or cosine rule (Higher).
- Two sides known? Yes → Pythagoras (if finding third side) or Trig (if finding angle).
- One side + one angle known? → Trig.
Multi-step problems: Often need Pythagoras THEN trig, or vice versa. E.g., find missing side with Pythagoras, then find angle with trig.
Angles of elevation/depression: Horizontal line. Angle up = elevation. Angle down = depression. Equal (alternate angles). Draw horizontal line at observer's eye level.
Bearings with trig (G15): Components: East = distance × sin(bearing), North = distance × cos(bearing).
Worked Example

"Ladder 5 m long leans against wall. Foot 2 m from wall. Angle with ground?" Answer: cos θ = 2/5 = 0.4. θ = cos⁻¹(0.4) = 66.4°. Height up wall = √(5²−2²) = √21 = 4.58 m.

Worked Example

"Cliff 80 m high. Angle of depression to boat 15°. Distance from cliff base to boat?" Answer: tan 15° = 80/d → d = 80/tan15° = 80/0.268 = 298.5 m.

"Angle of depression measured from vertical"

Angle of depression measured from HORIZONTAL down. Equal to angle of elevation from boat to observer (alternate angles, horizontal lines parallel).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Flowchart: "What do you know? What do you want? Which tool?" Colour-code: Pythagoras (blue), Trig (red). Scaffolded multi-step: "Step 1: Find AC. Step 2: Find angle B."
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): 3D trigonometry: angle between line and plane, angle between two planes. Diagonal of cuboid. Pyramid height from slant edge. Problems requiring sine/cosine rules (preview G22).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Method Match

Problems on cards. Students sort: Pythagoras, Trig (find side), Trig (find angle), Multi-step.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Selects correct method, solves multi-step 2D problems, elevation/depression
  • Getting there: Solves single-step, struggles to combine Pythagoras and trig
  • Not yet: Cannot decide which method to use

Lesson 4: 3D Trigonometry

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Cuboid Diagonal

Box 3×4×12. Diagonal of base = 5 (3-4-5). Space diagonal = √(5²+12²) = 13. Or directly: √(3²+4²+12²) = 13.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
3D Pythagoras: Space diagonal of cuboid d = √(l² + w² + h²). Derive: base diagonal = √(l²+w²). Then d² = (l²+w²) + h².
Angle between line and plane: Angle between line and its projection on plane. Drop perpendicular from line to plane. Angle in right triangle formed by line, projection, perpendicular.
Angle between two planes: Angle between their normals, or angle between lines perpendicular to intersection in each plane.
Pyramid problems: Height = perpendicular from apex to base centre. Slant height = along face. Use right triangle: height, half-base, slant height.
Strategy: 1) Identify right triangles in 3D. 2) Draw 2D cross-sections. 3) Label known lengths/angles. 4) Apply Pythagoras/trig in 2D.
Worked Example

"Cuboid 6×8×10. Angle between space diagonal and base?" Answer: Base diagonal = √(6²+8²) = 10. Space diagonal = √(10²+10²) = 10√2. sin θ = 10/(10√2) = 1/√2. θ = 45°. Or tan θ = 10/10 = 1 → θ = 45°.

Worked Example

"Square-based pyramid: base 10 cm, slant edge 13 cm. Height?" Answer: Half diagonal of base = ½×10√2 = 5√2. Height h: h² + (5√2)² = 13² → h² + 50 = 169 → h² = 119 → h = √119 ≈ 10.9 cm.

"Angle between diagonal and base is angle between diagonal and base diagonal"

Yes! The projection of space diagonal onto base is the base diagonal. So angle between space diagonal and base = angle between space diagonal and base diagonal.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Physical cuboid models. Draw 2D cross-sections on paper. Colour-code: base (blue), vertical (red), diagonal (green). Step-by-step: find base diagonal first.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Angle between two planes (e.g., two faces of pyramid). Line of intersection method. 3D coordinates: vectors for lines, dot product for angles. Tetrahedron angles.

Plenary (5 minutes)

3D Visualization

Describe 3D shape. Students sketch cross-section containing given line and point. Identify right triangle.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Finds 3D diagonals, angles line-plane, solves pyramid problems
  • Getting there: Finds space diagonal, struggles to identify correct 2D cross-section
  • Not yet: Cannot see right triangles in 3D

Lesson 5: Problem Solving and Applications

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Real World

"Tree height: measure shadow 5 m, sun elevation 40°. Height = 5 × tan 40° = 4.2 m."

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Problem-solving strategy:
1. Draw diagram (2D or 3D cross-section)
2. Label all knowns
3. Mark right angles
4. Identify right triangles
5. Choose Pythagoras or trig
6. Calculate step by step
7. Check: answer sensible? units?
Common contexts:
- Heights/distances (trees, buildings, cliffs)
- Ladders, guy ropes, ramps
- Bearings and navigation (G15)
- Roof pitches, stair angles
- 3D packaging, boxes, pyramids
Worked Example

"Flagpole on building. From point 30 m away, angle to top of flagpole 50°, to bottom 35°. Flagpole height?" Answer: Building height = 30 tan 35° = 21.0 m. Total height = 30 tan 50° = 35.8 m. Flagpole = 35.8 − 21.0 = 14.8 m.

"In 3D, just use the 3D formula for everything"

3D problems are solved by finding 2D RIGHT TRIANGLES within the 3D shape. There is no "3D trig formula" — just Pythagoras and SOHCAHTOA applied to 2D slices.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide diagram templates for common problems (ladder, cliff, building). Step-by-step with diagrams pre-drawn.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Optimisation: "Shortest ladder to reach over fence to wall." "Maximum area of rectangle in semicircle." Trigonometric equations from geometry problems.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Create a Problem

Students write a Pythagoras/trig problem with diagram and solution. Swap and solve.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves complex 2D and 3D problems, creates correct diagrams, checks answers
  • Getting there: Solves standard 2D problems, 3D requires scaffolding
  • Not yet: Cannot translate word problem to diagram

Homework Suggestions

  • Pythagoras and trigonometry mixed worksheet
  • 3D trigonometry problems: cuboids, pyramids
  • Real-world: Measure height of tree/building using clinometer (or phone app) and trigonometry
  • Past paper questions (including 3D and elevation/depression)
