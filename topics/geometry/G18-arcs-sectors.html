G18: Arcs & Sectors

Foundation Higher All Boards

Calculate arc length and sector area using proportional reasoning. Find angle or radius given arc/sector. Solve problems involving segments and compound shapes with sectors.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Calculate arc length: L = (θ/360) × 2πr = (θ/360) × πd
  • Calculate sector area: A = (θ/360) × πr²
  • Find angle θ given arc length or sector area
  • Find radius given arc length or sector area
  • Calculate perimeter of sector (arc + 2 radii) and area of segment (sector − triangle)

Prerequisites

  • Circle circumference and area (G17)
  • Fractions and proportion (N11, R7)

Materials & Equipment

  • Calculators with π button
  • Protractors, compasses
  • Circle templates with angle markings

Lesson 1: Arc Length and Sector Area

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Proportion

Full circle = 360°. Angle θ = fraction θ/360 of circle. Arc = same fraction of circumference. Sector = same fraction of area.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Proportional reasoning: Sector angle θ out of 360°. Fraction = θ/360.
Arc length: L = (θ/360) × circumference = (θ/360) × 2πr = (θ/360) × πd.
Sector area: A = (θ/360) × area = (θ/360) × πr².
Simplify fractions: θ/360 often simplifies. 90° = ¼, 60° = ⅙, 45° = ⅛, 30° = 1/12, 120° = ⅓, etc.
Perimeter of sector: P = arc length + 2r = (θ/360)×2πr + 2r.
Reverse problems: Given L and r, find θ: θ = (L × 360) / (2πr). Given A and r, find θ: θ = (A × 360) / (πr²). Given L and θ, find r: r = (L × 360) / (θ × 2π).
Worked Example

"Sector radius 8 cm, angle 60°. Arc length and sector area?" Answer: Fraction = 60/360 = 1/6. Arc = 1/6 × 2π×8 = 16π/6 = 8π/3 cm. Area = 1/6 × π×64 = 64π/6 = 32π/3 cm².

Worked Example

"Arc length 10π cm, radius 12 cm. Find angle." Answer: 10π = (θ/360) × 2π×12 = (θ/360) × 24π. 10π = 24πθ/360. 10 = 24θ/360 = θ/15. θ = 150°.

Common Misconception

"Arc length formula uses diameter: L = (θ/360) × πd, so sector area also uses diameter"

Sector area ALWAYS uses radius: A = (θ/360) × πr². Only arc length can use d (since πd = 2πr). Area uses r².

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Fraction table: 90°=¼, 60°=⅙, etc. Formula cards. Step-by-step: 1) Write fraction θ/360, 2) Simplify, 3) Multiply by full circle formula, 4) Calculate.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Radian measure: θ radians → arc = rθ, sector = ½r²θ. 180° = π rad. Convert: rad = deg × π/180. Area of segment = sector − triangle = ½r²(θ − sin θ) (radians). Perimeter of segment.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Fraction Match

Angles: 30°, 45°, 60°, 72°, 120°, 135°. Students write fraction of circle and arc/area formulas for each.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates arc/area fluently, solves reverse problems, finds perimeter of sector
  • Getting there: Forward problems OK, reverse problems confuse formulas
  • Not yet: Cannot set up proportion θ/360

Lesson 2: Segments and Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Segment vs Sector

Sector = pizza slice (two radii + arc). Segment = sector minus triangle (chord + arc). Shade each on diagram.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Segment area: Area of sector − area of triangle. Triangle: ½r²sinθ (if θ in degrees, use ½r²sinθ) or ½ab sin C (two radii = r, included angle θ). So segment = (θ/360)πr² − ½r²sinθ.
Chord length: c = 2r sin(θ/2). (From isosceles triangle, bisect angle → right triangle with angle θ/2, opposite = c/2, hypotenuse = r).
Perimeter of segment: Arc length + chord length = (θ/360)×2πr + 2r sin(θ/2).
Problem types:
- Given sector, find segment area
- Given segment area and radius, find angle (requires solving equation — may need trial/solver)
- Compound shapes: sector + triangle, sector − triangle, overlapping sectors
- Real world: pizza slices, pie charts, clock faces, circular gardens with paths
Worked Example

"Sector radius 10 cm, angle 120°. Find segment area." Answer: Sector = (120/360)π×100 = 100π/3. Triangle = ½×10²×sin120° = 50×√3/2 = 25√3. Segment = 100π/3 − 25√3 cm².

Worked Example

"Clock face radius 20 cm. Area swept by minute hand in 20 minutes?" Answer: 20 min = ⅓ hour = 120°. Sector area = (120/360)π×400 = 400π/3 cm².

Common Misconception

"Triangle in sector is always ½r² (forgetting sin θ)"

Triangle area = ½ × r × r × sin θ = ½r²sinθ. Only ½r² when θ = 90° (sin 90° = 1).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Focus on sector area/arc length. Segment as extension. Provide triangle area formula ½r²sinθ on card. Use calculator for sin θ.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Segment area in radians: ½r²(θ − sin θ). Overlapping circles (lens area). Circular segment as function of chord height (sagitta). Derive chord length formula.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Sector Puzzle

"Sector perimeter = 30 cm, radius = 6 cm. Find angle." 30 = (θ/360)×12π + 12. 18 = θπ/30. θ = 540/π ≈ 171.9°.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Finds segment area, solves reverse problems, handles compound shapes with sectors
  • Getting there: Sector area/arc length OK, segment area forgets triangle or sin θ
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish sector from segment

Homework Suggestions

  • Arcs and sectors worksheet: forward, reverse, perimeter, segments
  • Pie chart problems: "Sector angle 72°, radius 10 cm. Arc length and area?"
  • Clock problems: "Area swept by hour hand in 3 hours?" "Minute hand in 45 minutes?"
  • Past paper questions on arcs and sectors
