G10: Circle Theorems

Higher Only All Boards

Higher tier: Master all 8 circle theorems. Apply them to find missing angles, prove geometric results, and solve multi-step problems. Understand proofs of each theorem.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 4
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • State and apply all 8 circle theorems
  • Identify which theorem applies in a given diagram
  • Find missing angles using circle theorems with clear reasoning
  • Prove circle theorems using isosceles triangles and angle facts
  • Solve multi-step problems combining circle theorems with other angle facts

Prerequisites

  • Circle parts (G9)
  • Angle facts (G3)
  • Triangle properties (G4)

Materials & Equipment

  • Circle theorem reference cards (8 theorems with diagrams)
  • Compasses, rulers, protractors for investigation
  • Dynamic geometry software (GeoGebra) for exploration

Lesson 1: The First Four Theorems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Investigate

Draw circle, chord AB, point C on major arc. Measure ∠ACB. Move C along arc. Angle stays constant! Repeat for different chords.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Theorem 1: Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference. ∠AOB = 2∠ACB (same arc AB). Proof: OA = OB = OC (radii) → isosceles triangles. Exterior angle = sum of opposite interior angles.
Theorem 2: Angles in the same segment are equal. ∠ACB = ∠ADB (same arc AB). Follows from Theorem 1 (both half of ∠AOB).
Theorem 3: Angle in a semicircle = 90°. Diameter AB, point C on circumference → ∠ACB = 90°. Special case of Theorem 1: angle at centre = 180° (straight line), so angle at circumference = 90°.
Theorem 4: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°. A, B, C, D on circle. ∠A + ∠C = 180°, ∠B + ∠D = 180°. Proof: ∠A = ½∠BOC (reflex), ∠C = ½∠BOC (obtuse). Sum = ½(360°) = 180°.
Key skill: Identify the arc/chord each angle "stands on". Angle at circumference stands on arc opposite to it.
Worked Example

"∠AOB = 110° (centre). Find ∠ACB (circumference, same arc AB)." Answer: ∠ACB = ½ × 110° = 55°.

Worked Example

"Cyclic quadrilateral ABCD. ∠A = 70°, ∠B = 85°. Find ∠C and ∠D." Answer: ∠C = 180° − 70° = 110°. ∠D = 180° − 85° = 95°.

Common Misconception

"Any quadrilateral in a circle is cyclic"

A cyclic quadrilateral has ALL FOUR vertices ON the circumference. If one vertex is inside or outside, it's not cyclic and opposite angles don't sum to 180°.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use colour-coded arcs: each angle stands on one arc. Provide theorem cards with diagrams. Practice identifying "angle at centre" vs "angle at circumference" for same arc.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove all four theorems formally. Explore: angle between chord and tangent = angle in alternate segment (Theorem 7). Converse theorems: if opposite angles sum to 180°, quadrilateral is cyclic. If angle in semicircle = 90°, chord is diameter.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Theorem Match

Show diagrams. Students hold up card: Theorem 1, 2, 3, or 4. Or: "Which theorem?" "Angle at centre is 100°, angle at circumference is?" → Theorem 1.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies and applies Theorems 1-4 correctly, states reasoning clearly
  • Getting there: Knows theorems but struggles to identify which arc an angle stands on
  • Not yet: Confuses angle at centre with angle at circumference

Lesson 2: Theorems 5-8 (Tangents and Alternate Segment)

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Tangent-Radius

Draw circle, radius OT, tangent at T. Measure angle. Always 90°. Prove: if not 90°, perpendicular from O would be shorter than radius → line would cut circle twice.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Theorem 5: Tangent ⟂ radius at point of contact. OT ⟂ tangent at T. Used with Pythagoras for lengths.
Theorem 6: Tangents from an external point are equal. PA = PB (tangents from P). Proof: △OAP ≅ △OBP (RHS: OA=OB radii, OP common, ∠OAP=∠OBP=90°).
Theorem 7: Alternate segment theorem. Angle between tangent and chord = angle in alternate segment. ∠(tangent, AB) = ∠ACB (C on opposite arc).
Theorem 8: Perpendicular from centre to chord bisects the chord. (Covered in G9, but part of circle theorem set). If OM ⟂ AB, then AM = MB. Converse: line from centre to midpoint of chord is perpendicular.
Applying Theorem 7: Identify: tangent, chord, angle in alternate segment (on opposite side of chord). "Angle between tangent and chord through point of contact equals angle in alternate segment."
Worked Example

"Tangent at A, chord AB. ∠BAT = 40°. Find ∠ACB (C on major arc)." Answer: ∠ACB = ∠BAT = 40° (alternate segment theorem).

Worked Example

"Two tangents from P to circle centre O. PA = 10 cm, radius = 6 cm. Find OP." Answer: OA ⟂ PA. Right triangle OAP: OA = 6, PA = 10. OP² = 6² + 10² = 136. OP = √136 = 2√34 cm.

Common Misconception

"Alternate segment theorem: angle between tangent and chord equals angle in SAME segment"

It's the ALTERNATE (opposite) segment. The chord divides circle into two segments. Angle between tangent and chord equals angle in the segment AWAY from the tangent.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Colour-code: tangent (red), chord (blue), alternate segment angle (green). Physical model: card circle with tangent line. Theorem 7 is hardest — extra practice identifying the three elements.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove Theorem 7 using Theorems 1 and 5. (∠BAT + ∠OAB = 90°, ∠OAB = ∠OBA, ∠ACB = 90° - ∠OBA → ∠BAT = ∠ACB). Explore: intersecting chords theorem (not in GCSE but related).

Plenary (5 minutes)

All 8 Theorems

Quick fire: "Angle at centre?" "Angles in same segment?" "Angle in semicircle?" "Cyclic quadrilateral?" "Tangent-radius?" "Equal tangents?" "Alternate segment?" "Perpendicular bisects chord?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies all 8 theorems, including alternate segment, solves problems with tangents
  • Getting there: Knows Theorems 1-4, 5, 6, 8 but struggles with alternate segment (Theorem 7)
  • Not yet: Cannot apply tangent theorems or alternate segment

Lesson 3: Multi-Step Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Angle Chase

Complex diagram with cyclic quadrilateral, tangents, chords. Find all angles. Students work in pairs.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Problem-solving strategy:
1. Mark ALL given angles on diagram
2. Identify cyclic quadrilaterals (opposite angles sum to 180°)
3. Look for angles on same arc (equal)
4. Look for angle at centre (double circumference)
5. Look for tangent-radius (90°)
6. Look for alternate segment (tangent-chord = opposite angle)
7. Use triangle angle sum (180°) and straight line (180°)
8. Write reason for EVERY angle found
Algebra in circle theorems: Angles as expressions (e.g., cyclic quadrilateral: x, 2x, 3x, 4x → sum opposite = 180°).
Proving cyclic: Show opposite angles sum to 180°, or exterior angle = interior opposite angle.
Worked Example

"A, B, C, D on circle. AB is diameter. Tangent at A. ∠CAB = 30°. Find ∠BCA, ∠BDA, angle between tangent and AC."
∠BCA = 90° (angle in semicircle, Theorem 3).
∠ABC = 60° (triangle sum: 30+90+60=180).
∠BDA = ∠BCA = 60° (same segment, Theorem 2).
Tangent-AC angle = ∠ABC = 60° (alternate segment, Theorem 7).

Common Misconception

"If I see a triangle in a circle, it's automatically a right triangle"

Only if one side is a DIAMETER (angle in semicircle). Any other chord gives a non-right angle (unless proven otherwise).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Scaffolded angle chase: "Step 1: Find angle X using Theorem ___." Provide theorem menu. Use highlighters for different arcs.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Problems with two circles intersecting. Prove: "If two circles intersect, the common chord is bisected perpendicularly by the line of centres." Explore Miquel's theorem, Ptolemy's theorem (cyclic quadrilateral diagonals).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Create a Problem

Students design a circle theorem problem with at least 3 theorems needed. Must have unique solution. Swap and solve.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves complex multi-theorem problems, writes clear proofs, uses algebra
  • Getting there: Solves 2-step problems, misses alternate segment or cyclic quadrilateral opportunities
  • Not yet: Applies only one theorem at a time, cannot chain reasoning

Lesson 4: Proofs and Exam Technique

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Prove It

"Prove angle in semicircle = 90°." Use Theorem 1: angle at centre = 180° (diameter is straight line through centre). Angle at circumference = half = 90°.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Proof structure for circle theorems:
1. Draw radii to create isosceles triangles
2. Label base angles (equal)
3. Use angle sum of triangle (180°)
4. Use exterior angle theorem or angle at centre = 360° − reflex
5. Conclude required relationship
Standard proofs to know:
- Theorem 1: Angle at centre = 2×angle at circumference (using isosceles triangles and exterior angles)
- Theorem 3: Angle in semicircle (special case of Theorem 1)
- Theorem 4: Cyclic quadrilateral (using Theorem 1 with reflex/obtuse angles at centre)
- Theorem 7: Alternate segment (using Theorems 1 and 5)
Exam technique:
- "Give a reason for each step" → quote theorem name/number
- "Prove that..." → formal proof with diagram, given, to prove, numbered steps
- Don't assume points are on circle unless stated
- Check: did I use the fact that it's a circle? (radii equal, etc.)
Worked Example

"Prove: The angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact is equal to the angle in the alternate segment."
Given: Circle centre O. Tangent at A. Chord AB. C on major arc AB.
Prove: ∠BAT = ∠ACB.
Proof:
1. Draw diameter AD. Draw chord BD.
2. ∠ABD = 90° (angle in semicircle, Theorem 3).
3. ∠BAD + ∠ADB = 90° (triangle sum).
4. OA ⟂ tangent (Theorem 5) → ∠OAT = 90°.
5. ∠OAT = ∠OAB + ∠BAT = 90°.
6. OA = OB (radii) → ∠OAB = ∠OBA.
7. ∠OBA = ∠ADB (angles in same segment, Theorem 2).
8. So ∠OAB = ∠ADB.
9. From 3: ∠BAD + ∠OAB = 90°. From 5: ∠OAB + ∠BAT = 90°.
10. Therefore ∠BAD = ∠BAT.
11. ∠BAD = ∠BCD? No, ∠BAD = ∠BCD (same segment). But we need ∠ACB.
Better: ∠ACB = ∠ADB (same segment AB). ∠OAB = ∠ADB (step 7). ∠BAT = ∠BAD - ∠OAB? Wait.
Standard proof: Let ∠ACB = θ. ∠AOB = 2θ (Theorem 1). OA = OB → ∠OAB = 90°−θ. OA ⟂ tangent → ∠BAT = 90°−∠OAB = 90°−(90°−θ) = θ. QED.

Common Misconception

"In exams, I can just write 'circle theorem' as a reason"

Must specify WHICH theorem: "Angle at centre is twice angle at circumference" or "Theorem 1". "Alternate segment theorem" or "Theorem 7". Vague reasons lose marks.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide proof templates with numbered blanks. Focus on Theorem 1 and 3 proofs. Use "reason bank" for reference.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove converse theorems. "Prove that if a line is drawn from the centre perpendicular to a chord, it bisects the chord" (RHS congruence). Past paper proof questions (AQA, Edexcel Higher).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Proof Speed Round

3 minutes: Write proof of Theorem 1 (angle at centre). Key steps: radii → isosceles → base angles → exterior angle → centre angle = 2×circumference.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Writes formal proofs for all theorems, solves exam-style multi-step problems
  • Getting there: Can prove Theorems 1, 3, 4 but struggles with alternate segment proof
  • Not yet: Cannot structure a geometric proof using circle theorems

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete circle theorem worksheet (all 8 theorems, mixed problems)
  • Prove Theorem 4 (cyclic quadrilateral) using Theorem 1 and reflex angles
  • Past paper circle theorem questions (Higher tier)
  • Create a circle theorem revision poster with all 8 theorems, diagrams, and proofs
