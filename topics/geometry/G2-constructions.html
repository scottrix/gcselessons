G2: Constructions

Foundation Higher All Boards

Master standard ruler and compass constructions. Learn to construct perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors, perpendicular lines, and specific angles using only a compass and straightedge.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Construct a perpendicular bisector of a line segment
  • Construct an angle bisector
  • Construct a perpendicular to a line from/at a given point
  • Construct angles of 60°, 30°, 90°, 45° without a protractor
  • Construct triangles given SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS
  • Solve locus problems using constructions

Prerequisites

  • Geometric notation (G1)
  • Angle types and properties
  • Basic compass and ruler skills

Materials & Equipment

  • Compass, ruler, sharp pencil, eraser
  • Plain paper (not squared) for constructions
  • Tracing paper (for checking)
  • Visualiser or document camera for demonstration

Lesson 1: Perpendicular and Angle Bisectors

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Why Constructions?

Ask: "Why do we use compass and straightedge instead of measuring with a ruler and protractor?" Discuss: exactness, proof, historical significance, loci foundations.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Compass technique: Hold at top, rotate paper not compass. Sharp pencil. Light arcs.
Perpendicular bisector of AB: 1) Compass at A, radius > ½AB, draw arcs above/below. 2) Same radius at B, draw arcs crossing first arcs. 3) Line through intersections = perpendicular bisector.
Why it works: Intersections are equidistant from A and B. Locus of points equidistant from A and B is perpendicular bisector.
Angle bisector of ∠ABC: 1) Compass at B, any radius, draw arc cutting BA and BC. 2) Compass at each intersection, same radius, draw arcs crossing inside angle. 3) Line from B through intersection = angle bisector.
Why it works: Intersection is equidistant from BA and BC. Locus of points equidistant from two lines is angle bisector.
Perpendicular from point to line: Point P, line l. 1) Arc from P crosses l at two points. 2) From those points, arcs below l. 3) Line through P and intersection.
Perpendicular at point on line: Point P on l. 1) Equal arcs either side of P on l. 2) From those points, larger arcs above l. 3) Line through P and intersection.
Worked Example

"Construct the perpendicular bisector of a 6 cm line segment. Measure the distance from the midpoint to each end." Answer: Midpoint divides segment into two 3 cm halves. Any point on bisector is equidistant from endpoints (check with compass).

Common Misconception

"Construction arcs can be erased after drawing the line"

Construction arcs MUST be left visible. They are the evidence of the construction method. Examiners check for arcs. Erasing them loses marks.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use card templates with pre-drawn arcs. Practise compass grip on scrap paper first. Break each construction into numbered steps with diagrams. Use coloured pencils for different arcs.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove why each construction works using congruent triangles (SSS, SAS). Construct a regular hexagon inside a circle. Explore constructions impossible with straightedge and compass alone (trisecting an angle, doubling the cube).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Construction Check

Students swap constructions with a partner. Check: arcs visible? line straight? accurate? Label correctly?

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Accurate constructions with clear arcs, correct labelling
  • Getting there: Correct method but arcs faint or line slightly off
  • Not yet: Incorrect radius, missing arcs, inaccurate lines

Lesson 2: Constructing Angles and Triangles

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

60° Angle

Construct a 60° angle using equilateral triangle method. All sides equal → all angles 60°.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
60° angle: Construct equilateral triangle. Draw base AB. Compass radius AB at A, arc. Same radius at B, arc crossing. Join intersection to A and B. ∠CAB = 60°.
30° angle: Bisect 60° angle.
90° angle: Perpendicular bisector or perpendicular at point on line.
45° angle: Bisect 90° angle.
Triangle SSS: Draw base. Arcs from ends with other two lengths. Intersection = third vertex.
Triangle SAS: Draw base. Construct angle at one end. Mark second side length on angle arm. Join to other end of base.
Triangle ASA: Draw base. Construct both base angles. Intersection of arms = third vertex.
Triangle RHS: Draw side. Construct perpendicular at one end. Mark hypotenuse length from other end. Intersection = third vertex.
Worked Example

"Construct triangle ABC with AB = 5 cm, BC = 4 cm, CA = 6 cm." Answer: Draw AB = 5 cm. Centre A, radius 6 cm, arc. Centre B, radius 4 cm, arc. Intersection = C. Join AC and BC. Check: AB = 5, BC = 4, CA = 6.

Common Misconception

"Any three lengths make a triangle"

Triangle inequality: sum of two shorter sides > longest side. E.g., 2, 3, 6 cannot form a triangle (2+3 < 6). Arcs won't intersect.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide step-by-step construction cards for each triangle type. Pre-draw base lines. Focus on one construction type per lesson.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Construct triangle given base, opposite angle, and sum/difference of other sides. Explore ambiguous case (SSA) — when does it give 0, 1, or 2 triangles? Construct the circumcircle and incircle of a triangle.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Which Construction?

Show 5 triangle descriptions. Students identify which construction method (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS) and why.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Constructs all angle types and triangle types accurately
  • Getting there: Can do 60°, 90°, SSS but struggles with ASA/RHS
  • Not yet: Cannot construct 60° angle or SSS triangle

Lesson 3: Loci and Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

What is a Locus?

"A goat is tied to a post with a 5 m rope. What shape is the region it can graze?" Circle — locus of points 5 m from post.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Locus definition: Set of all points satisfying a condition.
Standard loci:
- Points at fixed distance from a point → circle
- Points at fixed distance from a line → parallel lines (two sides)
- Points equidistant from two points → perpendicular bisector
- Points equidistant from two lines → angle bisector
Combined loci: Intersection of two loci = points satisfying both conditions.
Regions: "Closer to A than B" → side of perpendicular bisector. "Within 3 cm of line" → region between parallels.
Scale drawings: 1 cm = 1 m, etc. Constructions on scale drawings solve real problems.
Worked Example

"A treasure is buried: closer to tree A than tree B, and within 10 m of the fence line. Shade the region." Answer: 1) Draw perpendicular bisector of AB. Shade side closer to A. 2) Draw parallel lines 10 m from fence. Shade between them. 3) Overlap = possible treasure locations.

Common Misconception

"Locus of points equidistant from two parallel lines is a line parallel to them"

It IS a line parallel to them AND halfway between them. Must specify the position, not just the direction.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use physical models: string for circles, rulers for parallels. Colour-code regions. Start with single locus, build to combined.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Locus of points where angle APB is constant (circle arc). Locus of points with constant ratio of distances to two fixed points (Apollonius circle). Solve 3-loci problems.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Design a Locus Problem

Students create their own locus problem with a story context. Swap and solve.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Constructs and combines loci to shade regions correctly
  • Getting there: Constructs single loci but struggles with combined regions
  • Not yet: Cannot construct basic loci accurately

Homework Suggestions

  • Practice all 4 standard constructions until fluent (perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, perpendicular from point, perpendicular at point)
  • Construct a 15° angle (bisect 30°). Construct a 75° angle (60° + 15°).
  • Draw a scale plan of a room. Use loci to find where to place a table: "equidistant from two walls" and "at least 1 m from door".
  • Research: Why did ancient Greeks restrict constructions to straightedge and compass?
