G23: Area of Triangle Formula

Higher Only All Boards

Higher tier: Derive and use area = ½ab sin C. Apply with sine/cosine rules to find areas of triangles without known height. Solve problems involving segments, sectors, and composite shapes.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Derive and use area = ½ab sin C (two sides and included angle)
  • Apply formula when height is unknown
  • Combine with sine/cosine rules to find area from various given information
  • Solve problems: area given → find angle/side; area of polygons by splitting into triangles

Prerequisites

  • Sine and cosine rules (G22)
  • Exact trig values (G21)
  • Right triangle trigonometry (G20)

Materials & Equipment

  • Calculators
  • Formula cards: ½ab sin C, sine rule, cosine rule
  • Triangle templates

Lesson 1: Deriving and Using ½ab sin C

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Height from Trig

Triangle sides a, b, included angle C. Drop perpendicular from A to BC. Height h = b sin C (or a sin B). Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × a × b sin C.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Derivation: Area = ½ × base × perpendicular height. In triangle ABC, use base a, height = b sin C. Area = ½ a b sin C. Also = ½ b c sin A = ½ c a sin B.
When to use: Given two sides and included angle (SAS). Don't need height!
Calculator: Ensure DEGREE mode. sin of angle > 90° is positive (sin 120° = sin 60° = √3/2). Obtuse angles give same area as supplementary acute angle.
Exact values: For 30°, 45°, 60°, 120°, 135°, 150° use surds (G21).
Reverse problems: Given area and two sides, find included angle: sin C = 2A/(ab). C = sin⁻¹(2A/ab). Check ambiguous case (C or 180°−C). Given area, one side, one angle → find other side.
Worked Example

"Triangle sides 7 cm, 9 cm, included angle 40°. Area?" Answer: A = ½ × 7 × 9 × sin 40° = 31.5 × 0.6428 = 20.2 cm² (3 sf).

Worked Example

"Triangle area 30 cm², sides 8 cm, 10 cm. Find included angle." Answer: 30 = ½ × 8 × 10 × sin C = 40 sin C. sin C = 30/40 = 0.75. C = 48.6° or 131.4°. Both valid (8+10+131.4 < 180? No, 131.4+... check). Actually A+B+C=180, if C=131.4, A+B=48.6 possible. Two triangles.

"Area = ½ab sin C works with ANY two sides and ANY angle"

MUST be the INCLUDED angle (angle BETWEEN the two sides). If given sides a, b and angle A (not between them), cannot use directly. Need sine rule first to find angle C or side c.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Emphasise "INCLUDED angle". Diagram: sides a and b with angle C between them. Formula card: Area = ½ × (side) × (side) × sin(included angle).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Prove: Area = ½ab sin C = ½bc sin A = ½ca sin B → sine rule! (Divide by ½abc: sin C/c = sin A/a = sin B/b). Area of quadrilateral = ½ × diagonal × (sum of perpendicular heights) or split into two triangles.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Which Formula?

Given: base and height → ½bh. Two sides + included angle → ½ab sin C. Three sides → Heron's (not GCSE) or cosine rule + ½ab sin C. Two sides + non-included angle → sine rule first.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses ½ab sin C fluently, solves reverse problems, recognises included angle
  • Getting there: Uses formula but sometimes uses non-included angle
  • Not yet: Cannot identify included angle, reverts to ½bh

Lesson 2: Combined Problems and Applications

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Polygon Areas

Regular hexagon side 5 cm. Split into 6 equilateral triangles. Area = 6 × ½×5×5×sin 60° = 75 × √3/2 = 37.5√3 cm².

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Combined with sine/cosine rules:
- Given SSS: cosine rule → angle → ½ab sin C
- Given SAS: direct ½ab sin C
- Given SSA: sine rule → third side/angle → area
Area of polygons: Split into triangles from centre or vertex.
- Regular n-gon: n × ½r² sin(360°/n) (r = circumradius) or n × ½a² tan(180°/n)? No, better: n isosceles triangles from centre. Central angle = 360°/n. Area = n × ½r² sin(360°/n).
Segment area (G18): Sector − triangle = ½r²(θ − sin θ) (θ in radians) or (θ/360)πr² − ½r² sin θ.
Problems with algebra: Sides as expressions. Area given → equation → solve for variable.
Worked Example

"Triangle sides 8, 10, 12. Area?" Answer: Cosine rule for angle between 8 and 10 (opposite 12). cos C = (64+100−144)/(160) = 20/160 = 0.125. C = 82.8°. Area = ½×8×10×sin 82.8° = 40 × 0.992 = 39.7.

Worked Example

"Regular octagon side 6 cm. Area?" Answer: Split into 8 isosceles triangles from centre. Central angle = 45°. Need radius r. Half-triangle: 3/r = tan(22.5°)? Or: ½r² sin 45° × 8 = 4r²√2/2 = 2√2 r². Find r from side: side = 2r sin(22.5°). r = 3/sin22.5° ≈ 7.84. Area ≈ 2√2 × 61.5 ≈ 174 cm². (Exact: r = 3/sin(π/8)).

"Area of parallelogram = ab sin θ (same as triangle)"

Parallelogram = 2 triangles → Area = ab sin θ (no ½). Rectangle: θ=90°, sin 90°=1 → ab. Rhombus: diagonals d1, d2 → Area = ½d1d2.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Flowchart: Given what? → Steps. 1) Identify known. 2) Find included angle if needed (cosine/sine). 3) Use ½ab sin C. 4) Calculator check.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Area of cyclic quadrilateral (Brahmagupta: √((s−a)(s−b)(s−c)(s−d))). Area of triangle from medians (¾ area of triangle formed by medians). Pick's theorem for lattice polygons. Heron's formula proof.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Area Challenge

"Triangle sides x, x+1, x+2. Area = 6√6. Find x." (x=5 → 5,6,7? Area 6√6? No. x=6 → 6,7,8? s=10.5. √(10.5×4.5×3.5×2.5) = 6√6? Check: 10.5×4.5×3.5×2.5 = 413.4375. 6√6 ≈ 14.7. √413.4 ≠ 14.7. Try x=5: sides 5,6,7. s=9. √(9×4×3×2)=√216=6√6. Yes! x=5.)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Combines area formula with sine/cosine rules, solves polygon and segment problems
  • Getting there: Uses ½ab sin C directly, but struggles when angle must be found first
  • Not yet: Cannot link sine/cosine rules to area formula

Homework Suggestions

  • Area formula worksheet: direct, reverse, combined with sine/cosine rules
  • Polygon area problems: regular pentagon, hexagon, octagon with given side
  • Segment area problems (combine G18 and G23)
  • Past paper Higher questions on triangle area
