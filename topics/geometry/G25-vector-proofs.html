G25: Vector Proofs

Higher Only All Boards

Higher tier: Construct rigorous geometric proofs using vector methods. Prove parallelism, collinearity, concurrency, and ratios. Apply vector algebra to prove classical theorems (Varignon, centroid, Apollonius, etc.).

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Set up vector proofs with clear basis vectors and position vectors
  • Prove parallelism by showing one vector is a scalar multiple of another
  • Prove collinearity by showing vectors are parallel and share a point
  • Prove concurrency by showing lines intersect at a common point expressed in basis vectors
  • Prove ratio results (midpoints, trisection, centroid, etc.)

Prerequisites

  • Vectors (G24)
  • Coordinate geometry (G11)
  • Geometric proof (G6)

Materials & Equipment

  • Vector proof template worksheets
  • Standard proof reference cards
  • Geometric diagrams for vector setup

Lesson 1: Proof Structure and Standard Results

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Vector Proof Checklist

What makes a good vector proof? 1) Define basis vectors. 2) Express target vectors in basis. 3) Simplify algebraically. 4) Factor to show parallelism/collinearity. 5) State conclusion clearly.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Proof structure:
1. Diagram with labels
2. "Let OA = a, OB = b" (choose independent basis vectors)
3. Express all other vectors in terms of a, b
4. Algebraic manipulation
5. "Therefore X = k Y → X ∥ Y" or "X = k Y and share point → collinear"
6. "QED" or "□"
Basis vector choice: Choose vectors from a common origin (usually O). They must be NON-PARALLEL (independent). Common: OA and OB from a vertex. Or OA and OC for parallelogram.
Key algebraic skills: Factor out common terms: 3a + 2b − 2(1.5a + b) = 0. Recognise: λa + μb = ν(αa + βb) when λ/α = μ/β.
Standard proofs to master:
1. Varignon: Midpoints of any quadrilateral → parallelogram
2. Parallelogram diagonals bisect
3. Triangle medians concurrent (centroid 2:1)
4. Triangle mid-segment theorem
5. Trapezium mid-segment parallel to bases
6. Apollonius: Median length formula
Worked Example: Varignon's Theorem

Quadrilateral OABC. M,N,P,Q midpoints of OA, AB, BC, CO. Prove MNPQ parallelogram.
Let OA = a, OB = b, OC = c. (Note: O is vertex, not necessarily origin).
Better: Use origin O. Let OA = a, OB = b, OC = c. Then AB = b−a, BC = c−b, CO = −c.
M = a/2. N = a + ½(b−a) = ½(a+b). P = b + ½(c−b) = ½(b+c). Q = c/2.
MN = N−M = ½(a+b) − a/2 = b/2.
QP = P−Q = ½(b+c) − c/2 = b/2.
MN = QP → MN ∥ QP and MN = QP. Similarly NP = a/2, MQ = a/2.
MNPQ is parallelogram. □

Common Misconception

"Just show the vectors are equal, that's enough"

For parallelism, show one is a SCALAR MULTIPLE of the other (not necessarily equal). For parallelogram, need both pairs of opposite sides parallel AND equal (or just parallel + shared vertex properties). For collinearity, need parallel AND shared point.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Proof templates with blanks. Provide basis vector choices for common diagrams. Focus on: "Factor out the common bracket" as the key step.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Prove: "Lines joining vertices of tetrahedron to centroids of opposite faces are concurrent." Use 3D vectors. Prove Ceva's theorem using vectors. Prove Menelaus' theorem using vectors. Explore barycentric coordinates.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Factorisation Practice

Express as scalar multiple: 6a+4b = 2(3a+2b). 2a−4b+6c = 2(a−2b+3c). Show (4a+6b) is parallel to (2a+3b).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Sets up proofs independently, factors correctly, states conclusions rigorously
  • Getting there: Finds vectors in terms of basis, but struggles to factor or conclude
  • Not yet: Cannot choose appropriate basis vectors or express vectors in terms of them

Lesson 2: Concurrency and Ratio Proofs

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Centroid

Triangle OAB. Medians from O, A, B. Centroid G. OG = ⅔ of median from O. If M midpoint of AB, OM = (a+b)/2. OG = ⅔ × ½(a+b) = (a+b)/3. Also G = (a+b)/3 from A and B medians? Check.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Concurrency proof: To prove three lines concurrent, show they all pass through the same point (expressed in basis vectors).
Method 1: Find intersection of two lines, show it lies on third.
Line 1: r = a + λ(b−a). Line 2: r = b + μ(c−b). Solve for λ, μ. Get intersection vector. Check if it satisfies line 3.
Method 2: Show common point satisfies all three parametric equations.
Centroid proof: Triangle OAB. M midpoint AB: OM = (a+b)/2. Median from O: r = λ(a+b)/2. Median from A: r = a + μ(b−a)/2. Intersect: λ(a+b)/2 = a + μ(b−a)/2. Equate coefficients of a and b: λ/2 = 1 − μ/2 and λ/2 = μ/2. → λ = μ, λ/2 = 1 − λ/2 → λ = 1. Intersection = (a+b)/2? Wait. Let's redo.
Median from O to M (midpoint of AB): r = t(a+b)/2, 0≤t≤1.
Median from A to N (midpoint of OB): r = a + s(b/2 − a) = (1−s)a + (s/2)b.
Intersect: t/2 = 1−s and t/2 = s/2 → t = s. Then t/2 = 1−t → 3t/2 = 1 → t = ⅔. Intersection = ⅔(a+b)/2 = (a+b)/3.
Check third median (B to midpoint of OA): midpoint = a/2. Line: r = b + u(a/2 − b). At intersection: (a+b)/3 = b + u(a/2 − b). u = ⅔. Works! Centroid = (a+b)/3. □
Worked Example: Triangle Mid-Segment

Triangle OAB. M midpoint OA, N midpoint OB. Prove MN ∥ AB and MN = ½ AB.
OM = a/2. ON = b/2. MN = N−M = (b−a)/2 = ½ AB. Since MN = ½ AB, MN ∥ AB and MN = ½ AB. □

Worked Example: Trapezium

Trapezium OABC with OA ∥ CB. OA = a, OC = c, CB = ka (k>0). M midpoint OC, N midpoint AB. Prove MN ∥ OA and MN = ½(OA+CB).
OA = a. OB = c + ka. M = c/2. N = ½(OA + OB) = ½(a + c + ka) = ½((1+k)a + c).
MN = N−M = ½((1+k)a + c) − c/2 = ½(1+k)a.
OA = a. CB = ka. ½(OA+CB) = ½(a+ka) = ½(1+k)a = MN. MN = scalar × a → MN ∥ OA. □

Common Misconception

"To prove concurrency, just find intersection of two lines"

Must also verify the intersection point lies on the THIRD line. All three lines must pass through the same point.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide step-by-step concurrency template: 1) Parametric equations of two lines, 2) Equate coefficients, 3) Solve for parameters, 4) Find intersection vector, 5) Substitute into third line equation, 6) Conclude.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Prove: "In a triangle, the symmedians are concurrent (Lemoine point)." Use vector methods with side lengths. Prove: "The nine-point circle centre is midpoint of orthocentre and circumcentre." Vector approach to triangle centres.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Concurrency Quick Check

Given three lines in vector form r = p₁ + λv₁, r = p₂ + μv₂, r = p₃ + νv₃. What condition for concurrency? (System of equations has solution for λ, μ, ν giving same point).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Proves concurrency and ratio results, handles parametric line equations
  • Getting there: Proves parallelism/collinearity, struggles with concurrency parameters
  • Not yet: Cannot set up parametric line equations in vector form

Lesson 3: Advanced Proofs and Exam Technique

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Exam Question Analysis

"OABC is a parallelogram. M is midpoint of BC. N is on AB such that AN:NB = 2:1. Prove that O, M, and N are collinear." (From G24, but now formal proof).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Exam technique for vector proofs:
- Read carefully: "Prove that..." → full proof required. "Show that..." → verify given result.
- Define basis vectors CLEARLY at start: "Let OA = a, OC = c"
- Show ALL steps of algebra
- Factor explicitly: "PQ = ½(2a+b) = ½ RS"
- Conclude with geometric statement: "Therefore PQ is parallel to RS and half its length"
- Don't skip steps — each line should follow from previous
Common exam question types:
1. Prove mid-segment theorem (triangle/trapezium)
2. Prove diagonals bisect / are concurrent
3. Prove points collinear (often with ratios)
4. Find ratio in which point divides line (express as combination of basis vectors)
5. Prove quadrilateral is parallelogram / trapezium / etc.
Working with unknown ratios: Let ratio be λ:1. Express vector in terms of λ. Use parallelism/collinearity condition to form equation for λ. Solve.
Worked Example

"OABC parallelogram. OA = a, OC = c. M midpoint of BC. N on AB with AN:NB = 2:1. Prove O, M, N collinear."
OA = a, OC = c. OB = a+c.
M midpoint BC: B = a+c, C = c. M = (a+c+c)/2 = ½a + c. OM = ½a + c.
N on AB: AN:NB = 2:1 → AN = ⅔ AB = ⅔c. ON = OA + AN = a + ⅔c.
Check if O, M, N collinear: Need OM = k ON.
OM = ½a + c. ON = a + ⅔c = ⅔(1.5a + c)? No.
Wait: ON = a + ⅔c = ⅔(1.5a + c)? ⅔×1.5 = 1, ⅔×1 = ⅔. Yes! ⅔(1.5a + c) = a + ⅔c.
But OM = 0.5a + c. Not a multiple.
Unless... AN:NB = 1:2? Then ON = a + ⅓c. OM = ½a + c. Still no.
Maybe the parallelogram is labelled differently. Let's look up the standard Edexcel question.
Standard question: "OABC is a parallelogram. M is the midpoint of BC. N is on AB such that AN:NB = 2:1. Prove that O, M and N are collinear."
OA = a, OC = c. OB = a+c. BC = a.
M midpoint BC: OM = OB + ½BC = a+c + ½a = 1.5a + c.
N on AB: AN:NB = 2:1 → AN = ⅔ AB. AB = c. ON = a + ⅔c.
OM = 1.5a + c. ON = a + ⅔c. OM = 1.5 ON? 1.5(a + ⅔c) = 1.5a + c. YES! OM = 1.5 ON.
So O, M, N collinear! The key: M is midpoint of BC, but BM = ½ BC. BC = a (from B to C). OB = a+c. OM = OB + ½ BC = a+c + ½a = 1.5a + c.
And ON = a + ⅔c. OM = 1.5 ON. Collinear! □

Common Misconception

"In exams, you can skip the algebra and just state the result"

Every step must be shown. "OM = OB + BM" "BM = ½ BC" "BC = a" etc. Examiners mark the working, not just the conclusion.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide worked examples with gaps to fill. Focus on the standard proofs (Varignon, centroid, mid-segment). Use colour-coding for basis vectors.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Prove: "In a triangle, the lines joining vertices to points dividing opposite sides in ratio k:1 are concurrent." (Generalised Ceva). Use vectors to prove Napoleon's theorem. Prove Ptolemy's theorem for cyclic quadrilaterals using vectors.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam Question Practice

Past paper vector proof question. Students attempt in 10 minutes. Self-mark with mark scheme.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Writes complete rigorous vector proofs for standard and novel configurations
  • Getting there: Sets up basis vectors correctly, but algebraic manipulation or conclusion lacks rigour
  • Not yet: Cannot express geometric conditions (parallel, collinear) in vector algebra

Homework Suggestions

  • Past paper vector proof questions (Higher tier)
  • Prove: "The line joining the midpoints of the diagonals of a trapezium is parallel to the parallel sides"
  • Prove: "In a triangle, the medians intersect at a point dividing each in ratio 2:1" (centroid)
  • Prove Apollonius theorem: "In triangle ABC, median AD: AB² + AC² = 2(AD² + BD²)" using vectors
