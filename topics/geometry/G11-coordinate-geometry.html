G11: Coordinate Geometry

Foundation Higher All Boards

Apply coordinate geometry: midpoint, distance, gradient, equations of lines. Find intersections, prove geometric properties using coordinates, solve problems involving parallel and perpendicular lines.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Find midpoint of a line segment given endpoints
  • Calculate distance between two points
  • Find gradient of a line from two points or equation
  • Write equation of line given gradient and point, or two points
  • Determine if lines are parallel (equal gradients) or perpendicular (product = -1)
  • Find intersection of two lines

Prerequisites

  • Coordinates (A8)
  • Linear equations (A17, A18)
  • Straight line graphs (A9, A10)

Materials & Equipment

  • Graph paper, rulers
  • Formula cards: midpoint, distance, gradient
  • Graphing software (Desmos, GeoGebra)

Lesson 1: Midpoint, Distance, and Gradient

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Pythagoras on Grid

Points (1,2) and (4,6). Horizontal distance = 3, vertical = 4. Distance = 5 (3-4-5 triangle). Gradient = 4/3.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Midpoint of A(x₁,y₁) and B(x₂,y₂): M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2). Average of x's, average of y's.
Distance AB: √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]. Derive from Pythagoras: horizontal leg = |x₂−x₁|, vertical leg = |y₂−y₁|.
Gradient (slope) of AB: m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁) = rise/run. Positive = uphill, negative = downhill, zero = horizontal, undefined = vertical.
Gradient of line from equation: y = mx + c → m is gradient. ax + by + c = 0 → m = −a/b.
Special cases: Horizontal line: gradient 0, equation y = k. Vertical line: gradient undefined, equation x = k.
Worked Example

"A(2,3), B(8,7). Find midpoint, distance, gradient." Answer: Midpoint = ((2+8)/2, (3+7)/2) = (5,5). Distance = √[(8−2)² + (7−3)²] = √[36+16] = √52 = 2√13. Gradient = (7−3)/(8−2) = 4/6 = 2/3.

Common Misconception

"Gradient = (x₂−x₁)/(y₂−y₁)" (swapped)

Gradient = rise/run = (change in y)/(change in x) = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁). "y over x" — vertical change over horizontal change.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use grid squares to count rise and run. Formula triangles: distance² = Δx² + Δy². Provide worked examples with positive coordinates first, then negative.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Locus of points equidistant from two points (perpendicular bisector equation). Distance from point to line formula (Higher). Divide line segment in ratio m:n (section formula).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Calculate

Students find midpoint, distance, gradient for 3 pairs of points. Include negative coordinates and fractional answers.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies all three formulas correctly, handles negative coordinates, simplifies surds
  • Getting there: Knows formulas but makes sign errors with negative coordinates
  • Not yet: Confuses midpoint/distance/gradient formulas

Lesson 2: Equations of Lines and Parallel/Perpendicular

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Gradient Detective

Lines: y = 2x+1, y = 2x−3, y = -½x+4, 2y = 4x+6. Which are parallel? Which perpendicular? (Parallel: 1,2,4 have m=2. Perpendicular: 3 has m=-½, product with 2 = -1).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Equation forms: y = mx + c (gradient-intercept). ax + by + c = 0 (general). y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) (point-gradient).
Finding equation: Given m and (x₁,y₁): y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). Given two points: find m, then use point-gradient.
Parallel lines: Equal gradients (m₁ = m₂). Never intersect.
Perpendicular lines: m₁ × m₂ = −1 (negative reciprocal). m₂ = −1/m₁. Exception: horizontal (m=0) ⟂ vertical (undefined).
Intersection of two lines: Solve simultaneous equations (A19). Substitute or eliminate.
Perpendicular bisector: Midpoint of segment, gradient = negative reciprocal of segment gradient.
Worked Example

"Find equation of line through (3,−2) perpendicular to y = 2x + 1." Answer: m₁ = 2 → m₂ = −1/2. y + 2 = −½(x − 3) → y = −½x + 3/2 − 2 → y = −½x − ½. Or 2y = −x − 1 → x + 2y + 1 = 0.

Worked Example

"Find intersection of y = 2x − 3 and y = −x + 6." Answer: 2x − 3 = −x + 6 → 3x = 9 → x = 3. y = 2(3) − 3 = 3. Intersection = (3,3).

Common Misconception

"Perpendicular gradient is just the negative (e.g., 2 → -2)"

Perpendicular gradient is NEGATIVE RECIPROCAL: 2 → -1/2, -3 → 1/3, 1/4 → -4. Product must be -1.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide perpendicular gradient table: 1↔-1, 2↔-1/2, 3↔-1/3, etc. Use "flip and change sign" mnemonic. Step-by-step: 1) Find gradient, 2) Flip, 3) Change sign, 4) Use point-gradient.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Equation of line through intersection of two lines (family of lines). Foot of perpendicular from point to line. Distance from point to line. Reflection of point in line.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Parallel or Perpendicular?

Pairs of equations. Students identify and justify. E.g., 2x+3y=6 and 4x+6y=10 (parallel, same gradient). 3x-2y=5 and 2x+3y=7 (perpendicular, gradients 3/2 and -2/3).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Finds equations, identifies parallel/perpendicular, solves intersections
  • Getting there: Finds equations given m and point, but struggles with perpendicular gradient or simultaneous equations
  • Not yet: Cannot find gradient from equation or points

Lesson 3: Geometric Proofs with Coordinates

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Prove Rectangle

Vertices (0,0), (4,0), (4,3), (0,3). Check: opposite sides parallel (gradients 0 and undefined), adjacent sides perpendicular (0 × undefined conceptually). Distances: 4, 3, 4, 3.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Proving quadrilateral types using coordinates:
- Parallelogram: both pairs opposite sides parallel (equal gradients), OR diagonals bisect (same midpoint)
- Rectangle: parallelogram + one right angle (adjacent gradients product = -1), OR diagonals equal
- Rhombus: parallelogram + all sides equal (distance formula), OR diagonals perpendicular
- Square: rectangle + rhombus properties
- Trapezium: one pair opposite sides parallel
- Kite: two pairs adjacent sides equal (distance formula)
Proving collinearity: Three points A, B, C collinear if gradient AB = gradient BC (or AC).
Proving ratio division: Section formula: point dividing AB in ratio m:n is ((nx₁+mx₂)/(m+n), (ny₁+my₂)/(m+n)).
Circle equation (centre origin): x² + y² = r². (Centre (a,b): (x−a)² + (y−b)² = r² — Higher).
Intersection line and circle: Substitute line equation into circle equation → quadratic → 0, 1, or 2 solutions (miss, tangent, intersect).
Worked Example

"Prove A(1,2), B(5,4), C(3,8), D(-1,6) form a parallelogram." Answer: Gradient AB = (4-2)/(5-1) = 1/2. Gradient CD = (6-8)/(-1-3) = -2/-4 = 1/2. AB ∥ CD. Gradient BC = (8-4)/(3-5) = 4/-2 = -2. Gradient DA = (2-6)/(1-(-1)) = -4/2 = -2. BC ∥ DA. Both pairs parallel → parallelogram.

Common Misconception

"Equal side lengths prove a square"

Rhombus also has 4 equal sides. Need right angle (perpendicular adjacent sides) or equal diagonals to prove square.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide checklist: Parallelogram → check gradients. Rectangle → check gradients + one perpendicular. Rhombus → check distances. Square → both. Use colour-coding for sides.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Prove: "Midpoints of any quadrilateral form a parallelogram" (Varignon). Use coordinates (0,0), (a,0), (b,c), (0,c) for general quadrilateral. Prove diagonals of parallelogram bisect using midpoint formula.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Name the Quadrilateral

Coordinates given. Students use gradient/distance to classify. E.g., (0,0), (4,0), (5,3), (1,3) → parallelogram (not rectangle, not rhombus).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses coordinates to prove quadrilateral types, collinearity, ratio division
  • Getting there: Finds gradients/distances but struggles to structure proof
  • Not yet: Cannot apply coordinate methods to geometric problems

Homework Suggestions

  • Coordinate geometry worksheet: midpoint, distance, gradient, equations, intersections
  • Prove: "The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular" using coordinates (place rhombus at (a,0), (0,b), (-a,0), (0,-b))
  • Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of segment joining (2,5) and (8,1)
  • Find the intersection of line y = 2x - 1 with circle x² + y² = 25
