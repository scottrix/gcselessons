G16: Area & Volume

Foundation Higher All Boards

Calculate areas of 2D shapes (rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, trapeziums, compound shapes) and volumes/surface areas of 3D solids (cuboids, prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres).

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 4
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Recall and use area formulas for rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, trapeziums
  • Calculate area of compound shapes by splitting
  • Calculate volume of cuboids, prisms (including cylinders), pyramids, cones, spheres
  • Calculate surface area of 3D solids
  • Solve problems involving area/volume (including algebraic sides)

Prerequisites

  • 2D shape properties (G4)
  • 3D shapes (G12)
  • Units of measure (G14)

Materials & Equipment

  • Formula reference cards
  • Squared paper, isometric paper
  • 3D models for surface area visualization

Lesson 1: Area of 2D Shapes

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Formula Recall

Quick fire: "Rectangle area?" "Triangle?" "Parallelogram?" "Trapezium?" "Circle?" (Circle in G17).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Area formulas:
- Rectangle: A = length × width = lw
- Triangle: A = ½ × base × height = ½ bh (height perpendicular to base)
- Parallelogram: A = base × height = bh (height perpendicular to base)
- Trapezium: A = ½(a + b)h (a, b = parallel sides, h = perpendicular distance)
- Compound shapes: Split into known shapes, add/subtract areas
Units: Area in square units (cm², m², km²). Convert: 1 m² = 10,000 cm².
Finding missing lengths: Rearrange formula. Triangle: h = 2A/b. Trapezium: h = 2A/(a+b).
Algebraic sides: e.g., rectangle sides x and x+3, area = 40 → x(x+3)=40 → x²+3x−40=0 → solve.
Worked Example

"Trapezium: parallel sides 5 cm and 9 cm, height 4 cm. Area?" Answer: A = ½(5+9)×4 = ½×14×4 = 28 cm².

Worked Example

"Compound shape: rectangle 10×6 with triangle (base 6, height 4) on top. Total area?" Answer: Rectangle = 60. Triangle = ½×6×4 = 12. Total = 72 cm².

Common Misconception

"Triangle area = base × height" (forgetting ½)

Triangle is HALF of rectangle with same base and height. Always ½ × base × perpendicular height.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Formula cards with diagrams. Colour-code base and height. For compound shapes, provide template with splitting lines. Use grid paper: count squares for verification.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Area of triangle using ½ab sin C (preview G23). Area of regular polygons (split into triangles). Heron's formula for triangle area from 3 sides. Area between curves (calculus preview).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Missing Length

"Triangle area 24 cm², base 8 cm. Height?" "Trapezium area 35 cm², parallel sides 5 and 9. Height?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies all area formulas, solves compound shapes, rearranges for missing lengths
  • Getting there: Knows rectangle/triangle, forgets trapezium formula or ½ for triangle
  • Not yet: Confuses perimeter and area

Lesson 2: Volume of Prisms and Cylinders

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Volume = Area × Length

Prism: uniform cross-section. Volume = cross-sectional area × length/height. Demonstrate with multilink cubes.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Volume formulas:
- Cuboid: V = l × w × h
- Prism (any uniform cross-section): V = cross-sectional area × length
- Cylinder: V = πr²h (cross-section = circle area πr²)
- Triangular prism: V = ½ × base × height of triangle × length
Units: Volume in cubic units (cm³, m³). 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³. 1 litre = 1000 cm³ = 1000 ml.
Surface area of prisms: Sum of areas of all faces. Net approach: draw net, find area of each face, sum.
- Cuboid: 2(lw + lh + wh)
- Cylinder: 2πr² + 2πrh (two circles + curved rectangle 2πr × h)
Missing dimensions: Rearrange formulas. Cylinder: h = V/(πr²), r = √(V/πh).
Worked Example

"Cylinder radius 3 cm, height 10 cm. Volume and surface area?" Answer: V = π×3²×10 = 90π ≈ 282.7 cm³. SA = 2π×3² + 2π×3×10 = 18π + 60π = 78π ≈ 245.0 cm².

Worked Example

"Triangular prism: right triangle base 3 cm, 4 cm, length 12 cm. Volume?" Answer: Triangle area = ½×3×4 = 6. V = 6 × 12 = 72 cm³.

Common Misconception

"Volume of cylinder = 2πrh" (confusing with curved surface area)

Volume = πr²h (area of circle × height). Curved surface area = 2πrh. Total surface area = 2πr² + 2πrh.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Use physical prisms. Fill with cubes to verify volume formula. Net templates for surface area: cut out, find area of each piece, sum.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Volume of frustum (truncated prism/pyramid). Optimisation: "Cylinder volume fixed at 500 cm³. Find dimensions for minimum surface area." Volume of solids with holes (subtract).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Volume or Surface Area?

Scenarios: "Paint a room" (SA), "Fill a pool" (Volume), "Wrap a present" (SA), "Sand in sandbox" (Volume). Students identify.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates volume and SA of all prisms/cylinders, rearranges formulas
  • Getting there: Volume OK, surface area misses faces or uses wrong formula for cylinder
  • Not yet: Cannot identify cross-section of prism

Lesson 3: Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Pyramid vs Prism

Same base, same height. Pyramid volume = ⅓ prism volume. Demonstrate with rice/sand: 3 pyramids fill 1 prism.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Pyramid: V = ⅓ × base area × height. SA = base area + sum of triangular face areas.
Cone: V = ⅓πr²h. SA = πr² + πrl (base + curved), where l = slant height = √(r²+h²).
Sphere: V = ⁴⁄₃πr³. SA = 4πr².
Hemisphere: V = ⅔πr³. SA = 3πr² (curved 2πr² + base πr²).
Composite solids: Add/subtract volumes. E.g., cylinder with cone on top, sphere in cylinder.
Similar shapes (link to G19): Length scale factor k → Area scale factor k² → Volume scale factor k³.
Worked Example

"Cone: radius 4 cm, slant height 5 cm. Height, volume, surface area?" Answer: h = √(5²−4²) = 3 cm. V = ⅓π×4²×3 = 16π ≈ 50.3 cm³. SA = π×4² + π×4×5 = 16π + 20π = 36π ≈ 113.1 cm².

Worked Example

"Sphere radius 6 cm. Volume and surface area?" Answer: V = ⁴⁄₃π×6³ = 288π ≈ 904.8 cm³. SA = 4π×6² = 144π ≈ 452.4 cm².

Common Misconception

"Slant height = vertical height for cones/pyramids"

Slant height (l) is distance from apex to edge of base along face. Vertical height (h) is perpendicular to base. l > h always. l² = r² + h² for cones.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Provide formula sheet with diagrams. Emphasise: pyramid/cone = ⅓ prism/cylinder. For SA, break into base + triangles. Use Pythagoras for slant height step-by-step.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Derive sphere volume using calculus (integration of discs). Prove SA of sphere = 4πr² (Archimedes). Composite solids: cylinder with hemispherical ends, cone on cylinder, frustum of cone.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Shape Match

Formulas on cards. Shapes on cards. Match formula to shape. Include similar shapes: "If length ×2, volume ×?" (8).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses all volume/SA formulas, handles composite solids, finds slant height
  • Getting there: Knows sphere/cone/pyramid formulas, forgets ⅓ or slant height
  • Not yet: Confuses volume and surface area formulas

Lesson 4: Problem Solving and Applications

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Real World

"Tin can: radius 3.5 cm, height 12 cm. How much metal needed? How much soup fits?" (SA and Volume).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Problem-solving strategy:
1. Identify shape(s)
2. Draw diagram with dimensions
3. Choose correct formula(s)
4. Check units (convert if needed)
5. Calculate
6. Answer with correct units
Common problem types:
- Capacity: volume of container (litres = cm³/1000)
- Material needed: surface area (minus open faces)
- Packing: how many small boxes fit in large box (volume ÷ volume, but consider arrangement)
- Cost: area × cost per m², or volume × cost per m³
- Rate: flow rate × time = volume
Algebraic problems: Sides as expressions. E.g., "Cube volume = (x+2)³ = 64. Find x."
Worked Example

"Water tank: cylinder radius 50 cm, height 2 m. Capacity in litres?" Answer: r = 0.5 m, h = 2 m. V = π×0.5²×2 = 0.5π ≈ 1.57 m³ = 1570 litres.

Worked Example

"Open-top box from 20×20 card: cut squares x by x from corners, fold up. Volume V = x(20−2x)². Max volume?" Answer: This is optimisation (calculus). At GCSE: trial values or symmetry. Vertex of cubic. Max at x = 20/6 ≈ 3.33 cm.

Common Misconception

"Surface area of open box = 5 faces only"

Yes, but must identify WHICH face is missing. Open top = no top face. Open cylinder (pipe) = curved surface only (no circles). Read carefully!

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Structured problem sheet: 1) Shape? 2) Formula? 3) Substitute? 4) Calculate? 5) Units? Real-life contexts (painting rooms, filling tanks).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Optimisation problems (max volume for given surface area, min surface area for given volume). Rates of change: filling cone, water level rising. Density problems: mass = density × volume.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam Question

Past paper question on area/volume. Students attempt in 10 minutes. Peer mark with mark scheme.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves multi-step real-world problems, handles composite solids, uses algebra
  • Getting there: Solves standard problems, struggles with composite shapes or unit conversions
  • Not yet: Cannot select correct formula or substitute correctly

Homework Suggestions

  • Area/volume worksheet: all shapes, compound, composite solids
  • Find volume and surface area of 3 household objects (measure, calculate)
  • Past paper questions on area/volume
  • Design a package (net) for a product with given volume. Minimise surface area (material).
