G13: Plans & Elevations

Foundation Higher All Boards

Draw and interpret plans, front elevations, and side elevations of 3D solids. Construct 3D shapes from given views. Use isometric paper for 3D representation.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Draw plan, front elevation, side elevation of a 3D solid
  • Reconstruct 3D solid from given plan and elevations
  • Draw 3D solids on isometric paper
  • Understand scale in architectural drawings

Prerequisites

  • 3D shapes (G12)
  • 2D shapes and properties (G4)

Materials & Equipment

  • Isometric paper, squared paper
  • Multilink cubes or similar blocks
  • Rulers, pencils

Lesson 1: Drawing Plans and Elevations

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

What Do You See?

Place a model (e.g., L-shape from cubes) on table. Students draw what they see from: directly above, front, left side. Compare drawings.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Definitions:
- Plan: View from directly above (bird's eye view). Shows footprint.
- Front elevation: View from front (looking horizontally). Shows height and width.
- Side elevation: View from side (usually left side). Shows height and depth.
Drawing conventions:
- All views to same scale (usually 1 cm = 1 m or 1:100, etc.)
- Hidden edges shown as dashed lines (optional at GCSE)
- Line up: plan above front elevation, side elevation to right of front
- Dimensions: plan shows length × width, front shows width × height, side shows depth × height
Using cubes (multilink): Build shape. Count cubes in each row/column for plan. Count max height in each column for elevations.
Non-cube shapes: For prisms, cylinders, etc., draw outline of face visible from that direction. Cylinder: plan = circle, elevations = rectangles.
Worked Example

"Cuboid 4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm (L×W×H). Draw plan, front, side." Answer: Plan: 4×3 rectangle. Front: 4×2 rectangle. Side: 3×2 rectangle. All aligned.

Worked Example

"Shape made of 6 cubes: base layer 3 cubes in row, second layer 2 cubes on left two, third layer 1 cube on left. Draw plan and elevations." Answer: Plan: 3 squares in row, with 2 squares above left two, 1 above leftmost (show as 3×3 grid with shading for heights). Front: heights 3, 2, 1 from left. Side: heights 3, 1 (from front? depends on orientation).

Common Misconception

"Plan and front elevation are the same shape"

Plan shows horizontal footprint (length × width). Front shows vertical face (width × height). They show different dimensions. Only same if depth = height and viewing square face.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Use physical cubes. Build, then place paper on top for plan trace. Place paper in front for front elevation trace. Grid paper with 1 cm squares = 1 cube. Colour-code heights on plan.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Draw plans/elevations of shapes with holes, overhangs, or curved surfaces. Include dashed hidden lines. Scale drawings: 1:50, 1:100. Convert between scales.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Spot the Difference

Show two similar shapes with different elevations. Students identify difference from elevations only.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Draws accurate plans/elevations for cube solids and prisms, aligns views correctly
  • Getting there: Draws plan correctly, but elevations misaligned or wrong heights
  • Not yet: Cannot translate 3D to 2D views

Lesson 2: Isometric Drawing and Reconstruction

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Isometric Cube

On isometric paper, draw a cube. All edges equal length along 3 axes (120° apart). Vertical lines vertical, sloping lines at 30° to horizontal.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Isometric drawing: 3D representation on 2D paper. Three axes: vertical, 30° right, 30° left. All measurements along axes are true length (no perspective distortion).
Drawing on isometric paper: Dots form equilateral triangles. Follow dot lines. Cubes: draw rhombus for top face, drop verticals for height, complete.
Reconstructing from elevations: Given plan, front, side → build 3D shape.
Method: 1) Draw plan grid. 2) For each cell, height = max from front/side elevations. 3) Build column of that height. 4) Check all elevations match.
Ambiguity: Sometimes multiple 3D shapes give same elevations (e.g., hidden holes). Need all three views to reduce ambiguity.
Scale drawings: Architectural plans use scales (1:50, 1:100, 1:200). 1 cm on drawing = 50 cm real. Convert: real length = drawing length × scale factor.
Worked Example

"Plan shows 2×2 square with heights: front-left 3, front-right 1, back-left 2, back-right 2. Front elevation shows heights 3, 1. Side elevation shows heights 3, 2. Draw isometric." Answer: Build columns: (FL=3, FR=1, BL=2, BR=2). Draw on isometric: bottom layer all 4 cubes. Second layer: FL, BL, BR. Third layer: FL only.

Common Misconception

"On isometric paper, horizontal lines are drawn horizontal"

NO horizontal lines in isometric (except for curves). All "horizontal" edges go at 30° to horizontal (along the dot diagonals). Vertical lines stay vertical.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Use isometric paper with highlighted axes. Build with cubes first, then draw what you built. Tracing over teacher's model.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Draw isometric of cylinder, cone, pyramid (not just cubes). Reconstruct shape from plan + 2 elevations where solution not unique (find all possibilities). Explore 3D coordinate geometry: (x,y,z) for each cube.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Elevation Puzzle

Given plan and front elevation only. How many different 3D shapes possible? Discuss ambiguity.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Draws accurate isometric drawings, reconstructs 3D from 3 elevations, uses scale
  • Getting there: Draws isometric cubes, reconstructs simple shapes, struggles with complex heights
  • Not yet: Cannot draw on isometric paper or interpret elevations

Homework Suggestions

  • Draw plan, front, side elevations of 5 household objects (book, mug, box, etc.)
  • Build a shape from 10 cubes. Draw its plan and elevations. Give to partner to rebuild.
  • Draw on isometric paper: a house (cuboid + triangular prism roof), a staircase (cubes)
  • Scale practice: Room 4 m × 3 m. Draw plan at 1:50 and 1:100.
