G12: 3D Shapes

Foundation Higher All Boards

Identify and describe properties of 3D shapes: cubes, cuboids, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, spheres. Understand faces, edges, vertices, nets, and cross-sections.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Name and identify common 3D shapes
  • Count faces, edges, vertices (Euler's formula: V - E + F = 2)
  • Draw and interpret nets of 3D shapes
  • Identify cross-sections of 3D shapes
  • Understand plans and elevations (link to G13)

Prerequisites

  • 2D shape properties (G4)
  • Geometric notation (G1)

Materials & Equipment

  • 3D shape models (polydron, plastic shapes, or card nets)
  • Squared/isometric paper
  • Scissors, tape for net construction

Lesson 1: Properties of 3D Shapes

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Shape Sort

Students sort 3D shapes into groups: prisms (uniform cross-section), pyramids (apex), curved surfaces (cylinder, cone, sphere).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Polyhedra (flat faces):
- Cube: 6 square faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices. All faces congruent squares.
- Cuboid: 6 rectangular faces (3 pairs congruent), 12 edges, 8 vertices.
- Prisms: Uniform cross-section. Triangular prism: 5 faces (2 triangles, 3 rectangles), 9 edges, 6 vertices. Pentagonal prism: 7 faces, 15 edges, 10 vertices. General: n-gonal prism has n+2 faces, 3n edges, 2n vertices.
- Pyramids: Polygon base, triangular faces meeting at apex. Square-based pyramid: 5 faces (1 square, 4 triangles), 8 edges, 5 vertices. Tetrahedron (triangular pyramid): 4 triangles, 6 edges, 4 vertices. General: n-gonal pyramid has n+1 faces, 2n edges, n+1 vertices.
Curved surfaces:
- Cylinder: 2 circular faces, 1 curved surface, 2 edges (circles), 0 vertices.
- Cone: 1 circular face, 1 curved surface, 1 edge (circle), 1 vertex (apex).
- Sphere: 1 curved surface, 0 edges, 0 vertices.
Euler's formula: For convex polyhedra: V − E + F = 2. Check: Cube: 8−12+6=2. Tetrahedron: 4−6+4=2.
Worked Example

"A hexagonal prism has how many faces, edges, vertices?" Answer: n=6. Faces = 6+2 = 8 (2 hexagons, 6 rectangles). Edges = 3×6 = 18. Vertices = 2×6 = 12. Check Euler: 12−18+8 = 2. ✓

Common Misconception

"A cylinder has 3 faces (2 circles + 1 rectangle)"

A cylinder has 2 circular faces and 1 CURVED surface. The curved surface is not a face (faces are flat). When unrolled, it becomes a rectangle, but on the 3D shape it's curved. Similarly, a cone has 1 circular face and 1 curved surface.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Use physical models. Count faces/edges/vertices by touching and marking. Colour-code: faces (red stickers), edges (blue tape), vertices (green dots). Nets: pre-drawn with tabs.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Explore Euler's formula for non-convex polyhedra (Euler characteristic). Platonic solids: why only 5? (angle sum at vertex < 360°). Archimedean solids. Dual polyhedra (cube-octahedron, dodecahedron-icosahedron).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Euler Check

Students verify V−E+F=2 for 5 different polyhedra. Discuss why it works (topological invariant).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies all common 3D shapes, states F/E/V correctly, uses Euler's formula
  • Getting there: Knows cube/cuboid/prism/pyramid, confuses cylinder/cone faces/surfaces
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish prism from pyramid, counts incorrectly

Lesson 2: Nets of 3D Shapes

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Which Net?

Show 4 nets, only 1 makes a cube. Students predict which. Then cut and fold to check.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Net definition: 2D pattern that folds to make 3D shape. All faces connected along edges.
Cube nets: 11 distinct nets. Common features: 6 squares, connected edge-to-edge, no overlap when folded.
Cuboid nets: 3 pairs of rectangles. Many arrangements.
Prism nets: Two congruent n-gon ends, n rectangles in a row connecting them.
Pyramid nets: One n-gon base, n triangles around it meeting at apex.
Cylinder net: Two circles + rectangle (height = cylinder height, width = circumference = 2πr).
Cone net: Circle (base) + sector of circle (curved surface). Sector radius = slant height, arc length = base circumference.
Checking nets: 1) Correct number and shape of faces. 2) Faces connected correctly. 3) No overlaps when folded. 4) All edges match up.
Worked Example

"Draw net of square-based pyramid with base 4 cm, slant height 5 cm." Answer: Square 4×4. Four isosceles triangles base 4 cm, height 5 cm (slant height). Arrange triangles around square.

Worked Example

"Cylinder radius 3 cm, height 5 cm. Draw net." Answer: Two circles radius 3 cm. Rectangle height 5 cm, width = circumference = 2π×3 = 6π ≈ 18.85 cm.

Common Misconception

"Any arrangement of 6 squares makes a cube net"

Only 11 arrangements work. Common fails: 4 squares in a row (can't close), squares disconnected, squares overlapping when folded. Must be able to fold without overlap.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Pre-drawn nets to cut and fold. Start with cube (11 nets cards — sort into works/doesn't work). Use polydron frames to build then unfold.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Find all 11 cube nets systematically. Design net for truncated pyramid (frustum). Explore: shortest path on surface between two points (unfold net, straight line). Net of sphere? Impossible (no flat net without distortion).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Net Detective

Show net with one face missing. Students draw missing face in correct position and shape.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Draws accurate nets for prisms, pyramids, cylinder, cone
  • Getting there: Draws cube/cuboid nets, struggles with pyramid triangle dimensions or cylinder rectangle width
  • Not yet: Cannot visualise 3D from 2D net

Lesson 3: Cross-Sections and Plan/Elevation Intro

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Slice It

Playdough or clay shapes. Slice with string/wire. What 2D shape is the cross-section? Horizontal, vertical, angled cuts.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Cross-section: 2D shape from cutting 3D shape with a plane.
Prisms: Horizontal cut → same as cross-section (uniform). Vertical/angled cut → various shapes.
Pyramids: Horizontal cut → similar polygon to base (smaller). Vertical through apex → triangle. Vertical not through apex → trapezium.
Cylinder: Horizontal → circle. Vertical through axis → rectangle. Angled → ellipse.
Cone: Horizontal → circle. Vertical through axis → triangle. Angled → ellipse/parabola/hyperbola (conic sections — A-level).
Sphere: Any cut → circle (different sizes).
Plans and elevations (link to G13):
- Plan: view from above (horizontal projection)
- Front elevation: view from front (vertical projection)
- Side elevation: view from side
- All drawn to scale. Hidden lines often dashed.
Worked Example

"A cone is cut horizontally ⅓ of the way up from base. Describe cross-section." Answer: Circle. Radius = ⅔ of base radius (similar triangles: height ratio 2:3, radius ratio 2:3).

Common Misconception

"Cross-section of a pyramid is always a triangle"

Only if cut vertically through apex. Horizontal cut gives similar polygon to base. Angled cut gives trapezium or irregular polygon.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Physical slicing with playdough. Match cross-section cards to cut descriptions. Focus on horizontal cuts first (easiest).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Conic sections: derive ellipse/parabola/hyperbola from cone cuts. Cross-sections of torus (doughnut). Cavalieri's principle: equal cross-sectional areas → equal volumes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Cross-Section Match

Match 3D shape + cut description to 2D cross-section shape. E.g., "Cylinder, angled cut" → Ellipse.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies cross-sections for all standard cuts of common 3D shapes
  • Getting there: Knows horizontal cuts, struggles with angled cuts
  • Not yet: Cannot predict cross-section shape

Homework Suggestions

  • Build 3D shapes from nets (cube, cuboid, triangular prism, square pyramid, cylinder, cone)
  • Draw nets for: cuboid 3×4×5, hexagonal prism, pentagonal pyramid
  • Sketch cross-sections: cube cut diagonally, cylinder cut at 45°, cone cut horizontally at half height
  • Investigate: Why are there exactly 11 nets of a cube?
