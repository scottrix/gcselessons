G9: Circle Parts

Foundation Higher All Boards

Identify and name all parts of a circle: radius, diameter, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment. Understand relationships between parts and use correct notation.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Identify and name: centre, radius, diameter, circumference, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment
  • Understand relationships: diameter = 2×radius, tangent ⟂ radius at point of contact
  • Distinguish between major/minor arcs, sectors, segments
  • Use correct notation: O for centre, r for radius, d for diameter, AB for chord

Prerequisites

  • Geometric notation (G1)
  • Perpendicular lines (G1, G3)

Materials & Equipment

  • Circle diagrams for labelling
  • Compasses, rulers
  • String for circumference investigation

Lesson 1: Naming Circle Parts

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Circle Hunt

Students find circles in classroom (clock, bins, wheels). Identify centre, radius, diameter in real objects.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Centre (O): Fixed point equidistant from all points on circumference.
Radius (r): Distance from centre to circumference. All radii equal. Plural: radii.
Diameter (d): Chord passing through centre. Longest chord. d = 2r. All diameters equal.
Circumference: Perimeter of circle. Distance around. C = πd = 2πr.
Chord: Line segment joining two points on circumference. Diameter is special chord through centre.
Tangent: Line touching circle at exactly ONE point (point of tangency). Tangent ⟂ radius at point of contact.
Arc: Part of circumference. Minor arc (< 180°), major arc (> 180°), semicircle (= 180°). Notation: arc AB (minor), arc ACB (major).
Sector: Region bounded by two radii and an arc. Minor sector (< 180°), major sector (> 180°), semicircle.
Segment: Region bounded by a chord and an arc. Minor segment, major segment.
Notation: Circle with centre O: circle O or ⊙O. Radius OA. Chord AB. Tangent at T. Arc AB. Sector AOB. Segment (chord AB).
Worked Example

"Label all parts on diagram: centre O, radius 5 cm, chord AB = 6 cm, tangent at C." Answer: Draw circle centre O. Mark radius OA = 5 cm. Draw chord AB = 6 cm (not through O). At point C on circumference, draw line perpendicular to OC — this is tangent.

Common Misconception

"A chord is always shorter than the diameter"

A diameter IS a chord (the longest one). All diameters are chords, but not all chords are diameters. Chord length ranges from 0 to d.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use physical circle cut-outs. Fold to find centre, diameter, radius. Colour-code: radius (red), diameter (blue), chord (green), tangent (purple), arc (orange). Matching game: name ↔ diagram.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove: tangent ⟂ radius (by contradiction: if not perpendicular, line would intersect circle twice). Explore: angle in semicircle = 90° (preview G10). Construct tangents from external point (equal tangents).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Parts Bingo

Teacher describes: "Line touching circle at one point" → Tangent. "Line from centre to edge" → Radius. "Region between chord and arc" → Segment.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Labels all parts correctly, explains relationships, uses correct notation
  • Getting there: Knows radius/diameter/chord, confuses sector/segment, forgets tangent ⟂ radius
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish basic parts

Lesson 2: Circle Properties and Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Radius-Diameter

Quick: "Radius 7 cm → diameter?" "Diameter 12 m → radius?" "Radius r → circumference formula?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Key relationships:
- d = 2r, r = d/2
- Tangent ⟂ radius at point of contact
- Equal chords are equidistant from centre
- Perpendicular from centre to chord bisects the chord
- Angle in semicircle = 90° (preview G10)
- Tangents from external point are equal in length
Using properties to solve problems:
- Right triangles formed by radius, tangent, and line from centre to external point
- Right triangles formed by radius, half-chord, and perpendicular from centre
- Pythagoras theorem (G20) frequently used with circle problems
Cyclic quadrilaterals: Four vertices on circle (preview G10). Opposite angles sum to 180°.
Worked Example

"Chord AB = 8 cm in circle radius 5 cm. Find distance from centre to chord." Answer: Perpendicular from O to AB bisects AB → half-chord = 4 cm. Right triangle: radius 5, half-chord 4, distance x. x² + 4² = 5² → x² = 9 → x = 3 cm.

Worked Example

"Tangent from point P touches circle at T. PT = 12 cm, radius = 5 cm. Find OP." Answer: OT ⟂ PT (tangent-radius). Right triangle OTP: OT = 5, PT = 12. OP² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. OP = 13 cm. (5-12-13 triangle).

Common Misconception

"The line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is always perpendicular to the chord"

This is TRUE (perpendicular from centre bisects chord, and converse: line from centre to midpoint of chord is perpendicular). But students often confuse with "line from centre to ANY point on chord" — only the midpoint works.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide right triangle templates. Focus on Pythagoras with circles: radius as hypotenuse, half-chord as one side. Step-by-step: 1) Draw radius to tangent/chord, 2) Identify right angle, 3) Apply Pythagoras.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Intersecting chords theorem: AP × PB = CP × PD (chords intersecting inside). Secant-tangent theorem: PT² = PA × PB (tangent and secant from external point). Construct tangents to two circles (common tangents).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Which Property?

Show diagrams. Students state which circle property applies and what they can deduce. E.g., "Line from centre to chord midpoint" → perpendicular to chord.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Applies circle properties with Pythagoras to find missing lengths
  • Getting there: Identifies right triangles but forgets tangent ⟂ radius or chord bisector property
  • Not yet: Cannot identify right triangles in circle diagrams

Homework Suggestions

  • Label all parts on 5 circle diagrams (include major/minor arcs, sectors, segments)
  • Practice problems: chord lengths, tangent lengths, distances from centre
  • Prove: "The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord" (using congruent triangles)
  • Investigate: What happens to chord length as it moves closer to the centre?
