G14: Units of Measure

Foundation Higher All Boards

Master metric and imperial units for length, mass, capacity, and time. Convert between units, solve problems involving compound measures, and choose appropriate units for estimation.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Know metric units: mm, cm, m, km; g, kg; ml, l
  • Convert between metric units (×/÷ 10, 100, 1000)
  • Know approximate imperial equivalents: 1 inch ≈ 2.5 cm, 1 foot ≈ 30 cm, 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km, 1 lb ≈ 450 g, 1 pint ≈ 570 ml
  • Convert between metric and imperial using given conversions
  • Solve problems involving time, speed, distance, density, pressure

Prerequisites

  • Place value and decimals (N1, N10)
  • Multiplication/division by powers of 10

Materials & Equipment

  • Measuring equipment: rulers, tape measures, scales, measuring jugs
  • Conversion reference cards
  • Real-world objects for estimation

Lesson 1: Metric Units and Conversions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Estimate and Measure

Students estimate: height of door, width of book, mass of apple, capacity of mug. Then measure. Discuss appropriate units.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Length: mm, cm, m, km. 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm, 1 km = 1000 m.
Mass: g, kg, tonne (t). 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 t = 1000 kg.
Capacity/Volume: ml, l. 1 l = 1000 ml. Also: 1 cm³ = 1 ml, 1 m³ = 1000 l.
Converting metric: Larger to smaller → multiply. Smaller to larger → divide.
- km → m: ×1000. m → km: ÷1000.
- m → cm: ×100. cm → m: ÷100.
- cm → mm: ×10. mm → cm: ÷10.
- kg → g: ×1000. g → kg: ÷1000.
- l → ml: ×1000. ml → l: ÷1000.
Area conversions: 1 m² = 10,000 cm² (100×100). 1 cm² = 100 mm². 1 km² = 1,000,000 m².
Volume conversions: 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ (100×100×100). 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³.
Worked Example

"Convert 3.5 km to m." Answer: 3.5 × 1000 = 3500 m.

Worked Example

"Convert 2500 mm to m." Answer: 2500 ÷ 10 = 250 cm. 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5 m. Or: 2500 ÷ 1000 = 2.5 m.

Worked Example

"Area of rectangle 2 m × 50 cm. Find area in m² and cm²." Answer: 50 cm = 0.5 m. Area = 2 × 0.5 = 1 m². In cm²: 2 m = 200 cm. Area = 200 × 50 = 10,000 cm². Check: 1 m² = 10,000 cm². ✓

Common Misconception

"1 m² = 100 cm²"

1 m = 100 cm, but 1 m² = 100 cm × 100 cm = 10,000 cm². Area scale factor = (length scale factor)². Volume scale factor = (length scale factor)³.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Conversion ladder visual: km → m → cm → mm (×1000, ×100, ×10 down; ÷10, ÷100, ÷1000 up). Use place value chart: move digits left/right. Practice with whole numbers first.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Compound units: speed (m/s, km/h), density (g/cm³, kg/m³), pressure (N/m², Pa). Convert between them: 1 km/h = 1000/3600 m/s = 5/18 m/s. 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Dimensional analysis.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Conversion Chain

5 km = ? mm. 5 × 1000 × 100 × 10 = 5,000,000 mm. Students create own chains.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Converts fluently between all metric units, including area/volume
  • Getting there: Converts length/mass/capacity, forgets area/volume scale factors
  • Not yet: Confuses multiply/divide direction

Lesson 2: Imperial Units and Metric-Imperial Conversions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Imperial in Real Life

Where do we still use imperial? Road signs (miles), milk/beer (pints), height (feet/inches), weight (stones/pounds), fuel (mpg).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Key approximate equivalences (memorise):
- Length: 1 inch ≈ 2.5 cm, 1 foot = 12 inches ≈ 30 cm, 1 yard = 3 feet ≈ 90 cm, 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km
- Mass: 1 ounce ≈ 28 g, 1 pound (lb) = 16 oz ≈ 450 g (0.45 kg), 1 stone = 14 lb ≈ 6.35 kg
- Capacity: 1 pint ≈ 570 ml (0.57 l), 1 gallon = 8 pints ≈ 4.5 l
Converting using given equivalences: Use unitary method or conversion factor.
Example: 5 miles to km. 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km → 5 × 1.6 = 8 km.
Example: 10 kg to lb. 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb → 10 × 2.2 = 22 lb.
Compound measures with imperial: Speed in mph, fuel in mpg. Convert: 60 mph ≈ 60 × 1.6 = 96 km/h.
Estimation: Use approximations for mental maths. 5 miles ≈ 8 km (Fibonacci: 5, 8, 13, 21... miles ↔ km). 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb → halve kg for rough lb? No, double kg ≈ lb (actually 2.2).
Worked Example

"A recipe uses 2 lb of flour. How many grams?" Answer: 1 lb ≈ 450 g → 2 × 450 = 900 g. (More precise: 1 lb = 453.592 g → 907 g).

Worked Example

"Car does 40 mpg. How many km per litre?" Answer: 40 miles ≈ 40 × 1.6 = 64 km. 1 gallon ≈ 4.5 l. 64 km / 4.5 l ≈ 14.2 km/l.

"1 foot = 12 inches = 12 × 2.5 = 30 cm exactly"

1 inch = 2.54 cm exactly. 1 foot = 12 × 2.54 = 30.48 cm. GCSE uses approximations (2.5 cm, 30 cm) but students should know they are approximate.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Conversion cards with key facts. Practice: "5 miles ≈ 8 km" as anchor. Use ratio tables: miles:km = 5:8 = 10:16 = 15:24.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Exact conversions: 1 inch = 2.54 cm, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 gallon = 4.54609 l. Temperature: °F to °C (F = 9/5 C + 32). Historical context: origin of imperial units.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Convert

Rapid fire: "6 inches to cm?" "3 feet to cm?" "2 miles to km?" "10 lb to kg?" "3 pints to litres?"

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses approximations fluently, solves multi-step conversion problems
  • Getting there: Knows main equivalences, struggles with compound measures (mpg, etc.)
  • Not yet: Cannot recall key approximations

Lesson 3: Compound Measures and Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Speed, Distance, Time

Formula triangle: Distance = Speed × Time. Speed = Distance/Time. Time = Distance/Speed. Units must match (km, km/h, h).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Speed: distance/time. Units: m/s, km/h, mph. Convert: km/h → m/s: ÷3.6. m/s → km/h: ×3.6.
Density: mass/volume. Units: g/cm³, kg/m³. 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water = 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.
Pressure: force/area. Units: N/m² (Pa), N/cm². 1 N/cm² = 10,000 N/m².
Other compound measures: Flow rate (l/min, m³/s), population density (people/km²), rates of pay (£/hour).
Problem solving: Identify known and unknown. Choose formula. Convert units to match. Calculate. Check units of answer.
Bounds (Higher): Measurements are rounded. Speed = distance/time. Max speed = max distance / min time. Min speed = min distance / max time.
Worked Example

"Car travels 150 km in 2 hours. Average speed in m/s?" Answer: Speed = 150/2 = 75 km/h. 75 km/h = 75 ÷ 3.6 = 20.83 m/s. Or: 150 km = 150,000 m. 2 hours = 7200 s. 150,000/7200 = 20.83 m/s.

Worked Example

"Block of wood: mass 400 g, volume 500 cm³. Density in kg/m³?" Answer: Density = 400/500 = 0.8 g/cm³. 0.8 g/cm³ = 0.8 × 1000 = 800 kg/m³.

"To convert km/h to m/s, divide by 3600"

km/h → m/s: km to m (×1000), h to s (÷3600). Net: ×1000/3600 = ×1/3.6 = ÷3.6. Divide by 3.6, not 3600.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Formula triangles for speed, density, pressure. Unit conversion tables. Step-by-step: 1) Write formula, 2) Convert units, 3) Substitute, 4) Calculate, 5) Answer with units.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Kinematics: SUVAT equations (constant acceleration). Density of mixtures (alloy problems). Pressure in fluids (P = ρgh). Bounds with compound measures: max density = max mass / min volume.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Unit Check

Given formula, students state units of answer. E.g., "Force (N) ÷ Area (m²) = ?" → N/m² (Pa). "Mass (kg) ÷ Volume (cm³) = ?" → kg/cm³ (convert to kg/m³: ×1,000,000).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves compound measure problems with unit conversions, uses bounds (Higher)
  • Getting there: Solves speed/density/pressure with consistent units, struggles with mixed units
  • Not yet: Cannot recall formulas or convert units

Homework Suggestions

  • Conversion worksheet: metric, imperial, compound measures
  • Measure 5 objects at home in metric and imperial (height, mass, capacity)
  • Speed problems: "Train travels 240 km at 80 km/h. How long? Convert to m/s."
  • Density problems: "Gold density 19.3 g/cm³. Volume of 1 kg gold bar?"
  • Research: Why does UK still use miles on road signs?
