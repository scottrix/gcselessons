G19: Similar Shapes

Higher Only All Boards

Higher tier: Understand similarity in 2D and 3D. Use length scale factor to find area scale factor (k²) and volume scale factor (k³). Solve problems with similar triangles, similar solids, and scale models.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Identify similar shapes (corresponding angles equal, sides in proportion)
  • Find and use length scale factor (k)
  • Apply area scale factor (k²) and volume scale factor (k³)
  • Solve problems with similar triangles (parallel lines, shared angles)
  • Work with scale models and maps

Prerequisites

  • Congruence criteria (G5)
  • Enlargement (G7)
  • Area and volume formulas (G16, G17)

Materials & Equipment

  • Similar shape cards (matching pairs)
  • Scale drawing equipment
  • 3D models of similar solids

Lesson 1: Similarity and Scale Factors

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Enlargement Recap

Shape enlarged by SF 3. Lengths ×3. Area ×? (9). Volume ×? (27). Why? (Area = length², Volume = length³).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Similar shapes definition: Same shape, different size. Corresponding angles equal. Corresponding sides in same ratio (proportional).
Length scale factor (k): Ratio of corresponding lengths. k = length on B / length on A. If B larger, k > 1. If B smaller, 0 < k < 1.
Area scale factor: k². Area of B = k² × Area of A.
Volume scale factor: k³. Volume of B = k³ × Volume of A.
Finding k: Given corresponding lengths: k = L₂/L₁. Given areas: k = √(A₂/A₁). Given volumes: k = ∛(V₂/V₁).
Checking similarity: For triangles: AAA (three angles equal) or SSS (sides proportional) or SAS (two sides proportional, included angle equal). Note: AAA proves similarity, NOT congruence.
Worked Example

"Two similar cylinders. Heights 5 cm and 15 cm. Smaller volume 50π cm³. Larger volume?" Answer: k = 15/5 = 3. Volume SF = k³ = 27. Larger volume = 27 × 50π = 1350π cm³.

Worked Example

"Similar triangles. Sides 3, 4, 5 and 6, 8, 10. Area of smaller = 6 cm². Area of larger?" Answer: k = 6/3 = 2. Area SF = 4. Larger area = 4 × 6 = 24 cm². Check: 6-8-10 triangle area = ½×6×8 = 24. ✓

Common Misconception

"Area scale factor = k (same as length)"

Area = length × length. If lengths ×k, area ×k². Volume = length³ → ×k³. This is the most common error!

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Table: k, k², k³ for k=2,3,4,½,⅓. Visual: draw square side 2, square side 4 (k=2). Area 4 vs 16 (×4). Cube 2 vs 4: volume 8 vs 64 (×8).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Negative scale factors (enlargement with flip). Similarity in coordinate geometry: prove triangles similar using gradients. Fractals: self-similarity at different scales (Koch snowflake, Sierpinski triangle).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Scale Factor Chain

k = 2 → k² = 4, k³ = 8. k = 5 → k² = ?, k³ = ?. k = 1/3 → k² = ?, k³ = ?. Given A₁=9, A₂=36 → k=? (2). Given V₁=8, V₂=27 → k=? (1.5).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses k, k², k³ fluently, finds k from areas/volumes, checks triangle similarity
  • Getting there: Finds k from lengths, but uses k instead of k² for area
  • Not yet: Cannot identify corresponding sides or find k

Lesson 2: Similar Triangles and Problem Solving

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Parallel Lines Create Similar Triangles

Triangle with line parallel to base. Two similar triangles (AAA). Identify corresponding sides.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Similar triangles from parallel lines: Line parallel to one side of triangle creates smaller similar triangle (AAA).
Common configurations:
- Triangle with parallel line (most common)
- Two triangles sharing an angle, other angles equal (e.g., vertically opposite + alternate)
- Right triangles with same acute angle
Setting up proportions: Match corresponding sides carefully. Use same order: small/large = small/large.
Overlapping similar triangles: Redraw separately to avoid confusion.
Scale drawings and maps: Scale 1 : n means 1 unit on map = n units real. Length scale factor = n. Area scale factor = n². Map area × n² = real area.
Worked Example

"Triangle ABC. DE ∥ BC. AD = 3, DB = 6, AE = 4. Find EC." Answer: AD/AB = AE/AC. 3/(3+6) = 4/(4+EC). 1/3 = 4/(4+EC). 4+EC = 12. EC = 8.

Worked Example

"Map scale 1:25000. Lake area on map = 4 cm². Actual area?" Answer: Length SF = 25000. Area SF = 25000² = 625,000,000. Actual area = 4 × 625,000,000 = 2,500,000,000 cm² = 250,000 m² = 0.25 km².

Common Misconception

"In similar triangles, corresponding sides are the ones that 'look' the same position"

Corresponding sides are opposite EQUAL ANGLES. Always mark equal angles first, then match sides opposite those angles.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Colour-code equal angles. Redraw overlapping triangles separately. Provide proportion template: ___ / ___ = ___ / ___.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Prove: "Line joining midpoints of two sides is parallel to third side" (similarity). Similarity in circle theorems (intersecting chords). Problems with multiple similar triangles in one diagram (e.g., right triangle altitude to hypotenuse creates 3 similar triangles).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Find the Missing Length

Diagram with similar triangles. Students find one missing length using proportion.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies similar triangles in complex diagrams, sets up correct proportions
  • Getting there: Spots parallel line similarity, struggles with overlapping triangles
  • Not yet: Cannot identify corresponding sides

Lesson 3: 3D Similarity and Advanced Problems

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Similar Solids

Two similar cones. Heights 4 and 10. Radius of smaller = 3. Radius of larger? Surface area ratio? Volume ratio?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Similar solids: All corresponding lengths in ratio k. Includes: radii, heights, slant heights, circumferences.
Surface area: k². Volume: k³.
Mass (if same material): Proportional to volume → k³.
Problems with combinations: Frustum = large cone − small similar cone. Find volume of frustum: V_large − V_small.
Surface area of frustum: Curved SA = π(R+r)l, where l = slant height of frustum. (Or: curved SA of large cone − curved SA of small cone).
Scale models: Architectural models, engineering prototypes. If model length 1:50, model area 1:2500, model volume 1:125000.
Density: If similar objects made of same material, mass ratio = volume ratio = k³.
Worked Example

"Cone height 12 cm, radius 5 cm. Cut parallel to base at height 8 cm from vertex. Frustum volume?" Answer: Small cone height 8, large 12. k = 12/8 = 1.5. Small radius = 5/1.5 = 10/3. V_large = ⅓π×25×12 = 100π. V_small = ⅓π×(100/9)×8 = 800π/27. Frustum = 100π − 800π/27 = 1900π/27 ≈ 221 cm³.

Worked Example

"Two similar statues. Heights 20 cm and 1.5 m. Smaller mass 2 kg. Larger mass?" Answer: k = 150/20 = 7.5. Volume/mass SF = 7.5³ = 421.875. Mass = 2 × 421.875 = 843.75 kg.

Common Misconception

"Surface area of frustum = πRl + πrl (add the two curved surfaces)"

Curved SA of frustum = π(R+r)l where l is slant height of FRUSTUM (not the cones). Total SA of frustum = π(R+r)l + πR² + πr² (if both ends included).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability): Focus on finding k, then k², k³. Use table. Provide frustum as "large minus small" method rather than formula.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted): Similar triangles in coordinate geometry: centroid divides median 2:1. Prove using similar triangles. Three similar right triangles from altitude to hypotenuse. Geometric mean: altitude² = product of hypotenuse segments.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam Question

Past paper similar shapes question (Higher). Include area/volume scale factors and similar triangles.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves frustum problems, map scale problems, mass/density similarity problems
  • Getting there: Handles basic similar solids, struggles with frustum or combined scale factors
  • Not yet: Confuses k, k², k³ for 3D problems

Homework Suggestions

  • Similar shapes worksheet: triangles, 2D shapes, 3D solids, scale factors
  • Frustum problems: cone and pyramid frustums
  • Map scale: "1:50000 map. Field measures 3 cm × 4 cm. Actual area in km²?"
  • Prove: "In a right triangle, the altitude to the hypotenuse divides the triangle into two triangles similar to each other and to the original."
