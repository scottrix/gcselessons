G4: Quadrilaterals & Triangles

Foundation Higher All Boards

Classify and understand properties of special quadrilaterals (square, rectangle, parallelogram, rhombus, trapezium, kite) and triangles (equilateral, isosceles, scalene, right). Use properties to solve problems and prove facts.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Identify and classify triangles by sides and angles
  • Know properties of special quadrilaterals: sides, angles, diagonals, symmetry
  • Use properties to find missing angles and lengths
  • Understand hierarchy: square → rectangle → parallelogram; square → rhombus → parallelogram

Prerequisites

  • Geometric notation (G1)
  • Angle facts (G3)
  • Parallel and perpendicular lines

Materials & Equipment

  • Property summary cards
  • Quadrilateral/triangle cut-outs for folding
  • Rulers, protractors

Lesson 1: Triangles

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Triangle Sort

Students sort triangle cards into groups. Discuss: by sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) and by angles (acute, right, obtuse). Can a triangle be both isosceles and right?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
By sides:
- Equilateral: 3 equal sides, 3 equal angles (60° each). 3 lines symmetry, rotational order 3.
- Isosceles: 2 equal sides, 2 equal base angles. 1 line symmetry.
- Scalene: No equal sides, no equal angles. No symmetry.
By angles:
- Acute: all angles < 90°
- Right: one angle = 90°
- Obtuse: one angle > 90°
Key properties:
- Angle sum = 180° (G3)
- Exterior angle = sum of two opposite interior angles
- Largest angle opposite longest side
- Triangle inequality: sum of two shorter sides > longest side
Right triangles: Pythagoras (G20), trigonometry (G20). Special: 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 (G21).
Worked Example

"Triangle has angles 40°, 70°, x. Classify it." Answer: x = 70° (sum 180°). Two equal angles → isosceles. All angles < 90° → acute. Answer: acute isosceles triangle.

Common Misconception

"An equilateral triangle is not isosceles"

An equilateral triangle IS isosceles (at least 2 equal sides). It's a special case. Similarly, a square is a rectangle, a rhombus, and a parallelogram.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use physical triangle cut-outs. Fold to find lines of symmetry. Colour-code equal sides/angles. Provide classification flowchart.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove base angles of isosceles triangle are equal (construct perpendicular bisector). Explore triangle centres: centroid, circumcentre, incentre, orthocentre. When do they coincide?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Always/Sometimes/Never

"An isosceles triangle has a right angle." (Sometimes). "An equilateral triangle has a 90° angle." (Never). "A right triangle is scalene." (Sometimes — can be isosceles 45-45-90).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Classifies correctly, uses properties to find missing angles/sides
  • Getting there: Knows types but confuses properties (e.g., thinks isosceles has equal sides but not equal angles)
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish triangle types

Lesson 2: Quadrilaterals

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quadrilateral Properties

Quick quiz: "Which quadrilateral has diagonals that bisect at 90°?" (Rhombus, square). "Which has one pair of parallel sides?" (Trapezium).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Square: 4 equal sides, 4 right angles. Diagonals: equal, bisect at 90°, bisect angles. 4 lines symmetry, rotational order 4.
Rectangle: Opposite sides equal/parallel, 4 right angles. Diagonals: equal, bisect each other. 2 lines symmetry, rotational order 2.
Parallelogram: Opposite sides equal/parallel, opposite angles equal. Diagonals: bisect each other. No line symmetry (usually), rotational order 2.
Rhombus: 4 equal sides, opposite sides parallel, opposite angles equal. Diagonals: bisect at 90°, bisect angles. 2 lines symmetry, rotational order 2.
Trapezium (UK) / Trapezoid (US): One pair of parallel sides. Isosceles trapezium: non-parallel sides equal, base angles equal, diagonals equal. 1 line symmetry (if isosceles).
Kite: Two pairs of adjacent equal sides. One pair of opposite equal angles. Diagonals: cross at 90°, one bisects the other. 1 line symmetry.
Hierarchy: Square ⊂ Rectangle ⊂ Parallelogram. Square ⊂ Rhombus ⊂ Parallelogram. Square ⊂ Kite. Isosceles trapezium ⊂ Trapezium.
Worked Example

"Quadrilateral has diagonals that are equal and bisect each other at 90°. What is it?" Answer: Square. (Rectangle: equal, bisect, not 90°. Rhombus: bisect at 90°, not equal. Square: both.)

Common Misconception

"All parallelograms have diagonals that cross at 90°"

Only rhombus and square (special parallelograms). Rectangle and general parallelogram diagonals do NOT cross at 90°.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Property table with tick boxes. Physical shapes to fold and measure. Focus on square, rectangle, parallelogram first. Use "family tree" diagram.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Prove properties using congruent triangles (e.g., parallelogram diagonal bisects → two congruent triangles). Cyclic quadrilaterals (opposite angles sum to 180°) — preview G10. Varignon's theorem: midpoints of any quadrilateral form a parallelogram.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Property Bingo

Teacher reads property (e.g., "diagonals bisect at 90°"). Students mark all shapes with that property. First to complete row wins.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Recalls all properties, uses hierarchy, solves problems with missing info
  • Getting there: Knows square/rectangle but confuses rhombus/parallelogram/kite properties
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish quadrilateral types

Lesson 3: Problem Solving with Properties

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Missing Angle

"Parallelogram has one angle 70°. Find the other three." Answer: Opposite = 70°, adjacent = 110° (co-interior). 70°, 110°, 70°, 110°.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Using properties: Set up equations from angle/side properties.
Algebra in quadrilaterals: Angles as expressions (e.g., parallelogram: x, 2x, x, 2x → 6x = 360° → x = 60°).
Coordinate geometry: Use midpoint, gradient, distance to verify quadrilateral type (G11).
Construction: Construct quadrilaterals given properties (G2).
Proofs: "Prove this quadrilateral is a parallelogram" — show opposite sides parallel, or diagonals bisect, or one pair both parallel and equal.
Worked Example

"Rhombus has side 5 cm and one diagonal 6 cm. Find the other diagonal." Answer: Diagonals bisect at 90°. Half-diagonals: 3 cm and x. Right triangle: 3² + x² = 5² → 9 + x² = 25 → x² = 16 → x = 4. Other diagonal = 8 cm.

Common Misconception

"A quadrilateral with equal diagonals is a rectangle"

Isosceles trapezium also has equal diagonals. Square too. Need more properties (e.g., diagonals bisect each other + equal = rectangle).

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide structured problem templates: "Shape is a ___ because ___". Step-by-step: 1) List known properties, 2) Identify shape, 3) Find missing values.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
"Prove that the quadrilateral formed by joining midpoints of a kite is a rectangle." Coordinate proofs: place vertices at (0,0), (a,0), (b,c), (0,c) for parallelogram. Vector proofs.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Which Shape?

Clues: "My diagonals are perpendicular." "My diagonals are not equal." "I have 2 lines of symmetry." → Rhombus. Students write clues for a shape.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves multi-property problems, uses algebra, begins formal proofs
  • Getting there: Finds missing angles in standard shapes, struggles with algebra or combined properties
  • Not yet: Cannot apply properties to find unknowns

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a quadrilateral family tree poster showing hierarchy and properties
  • Complete classification worksheet with mixed triangle/quadrilateral problems
  • Prove: "The diagonals of a rhombus bisect the interior angles" using congruent triangles
  • Find 5 real-world examples of each quadrilateral type (architecture, logos, nature)
