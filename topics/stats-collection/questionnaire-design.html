Lesson 2: Core Concepts: questionnaire design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on questionnaire design. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A questionnaire is a set of written questions used to collect data from respondents.
Key Fact: Open questions allow free-text answers (e.g. 'What do you think about…?'); they give detailed responses but are hard to analyse.
Key Fact: Closed questions give a fixed set of response options (e.g. tick boxes, Likert scales); they are easy to analyse but limit answers.
Key Fact: Leading questions influence the respondent to answer in a particular way — they must be avoided.
Key Fact: Questions should be clear, unbiased, relevant and not ask two things at once (double-barrelled).
Key Fact: Response options should be exhaustive (cover all possibilities) and mutually exclusive (no overlap).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A student writes this question on a questionnaire: 'How many hours of TV do you watch each week? (a) 0–5, (b) 5–10, (c) 10–15, (d) 15+'. Identify two problems and rewrite the question with improved options. <div class="

Problem 1: The response options overlap — someone who watches exactly 5 hours could choose (a) or (b), and 10 hours could be (b) or (c). Problem 2: The upper limit of option (d) is open-ended ('15+'), which makes analysis of the upper range difficult. Improved version: 'How many hours of TV do you watch each week?' (a) 0–4, (b) 5–9, (c) 10–14, (d) 15–19, (e) 20 or more. The intervals are now non-overlapping and exhaustive, covering all possible values.

Exam Tips: When criticising a question, use the correct term: 'leading', 'double-barrelled', 'overlapping options', 'ambiguous'. | Always suggest an improved version of a flawed question, not just what is wrong. | Closed question response boxes must be non-overlapping and cover all likely answers — add 'Other' if needed. | Pilot study questions are very common — remember to say it is a small-scale trial run. | If asked about response rate, mention both how to maximise it and the problem of non-response bias.
Common Errors: ✗ Writing response options that overlap, e.g. '0–5, 5–10, 10–15' ✓ Use non-overlapping intervals, e.g. '0–4, 5–9, 10–14' or '0 ≤ x ≤ 5, 5 < x ≤ 10'. ✗ Using a leading question such as 'Don't you agree that homework is important?' ✓ Using a neutral question such as 'What is your opinion on the importance of homework?' ✗ Forgetting 'Other' or 'Prefer not to say' on closed questions ✓ Include an 'Other' option or allow respondents to opt out of sensitive questions to ensure options are exhaustive. ✗ Asking sensitive questions at the start of a questionnaire ✓ Place sensitive questions later, after rapport is built, and ensure anonymity to encourage honest responses.
