C13: Gas Volumes

Higher All Boards

Calculate the volumes of gases produced in reactions using the mole ratio and molar volume at RTP.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Use the molar volume of gas at RTP to calculate volumes of gases in reactions
  • Apply gas volume calculations to multi-step problems

Prerequisites

  • Relative Masses and Moles (C10) — moles, Mr
  • Reacting Masses (C11) — balanced equations
  • Scientific calculator

Materials & Equipment

  • Periodic table (printed or wall chart)
  • Scientific calculator
  • Gas syringe for demonstration (if available)

Lesson 1: Molar Volume of Gas

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

How Big Is a Mole of Gas?

One mole of water weighs 18g — about a sip. One mole of iron weighs 56g — about a small nail. But one mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure takes up 24 dm³ — about the size of a large suitcase!

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Molar volume = the volume occupied by one mole of a gas.

At room temperature and pressure (RTP):
1 mole of any gas = 24 dm³ (= 24,000 cm³ = 24 litres)

This is true for ALL gases — hydrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, methane — because gas particles are far apart and the spaces between them are much larger than the particles themselves.

Key equation:
Volume (dm³) = number of moles × 24
or
Volume (cm³) = number of moles × 24,000

In reactions:
The mole ratio from the balanced equation tells us the ratio of gas volumes too.
e.g. 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g)
Volume ratio: 2 : 1 : 2
2 volumes of H₂ react with 1 volume of O₂ to produce 2 volumes of H₂O vapour
Volume of CO₂ from acid + carbonate

What volume of CO₂ is produced at RTP when 5g of calcium carbonate reacts with excess acid?
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
Mr(CaCO₃) = 100
Moles = 5/100 = 0.05 mol
CO₂ ratio = 1:1, so moles of CO₂ = 0.05
Volume = 0.05 × 24 = 1.2 dm³ = 1200 cm³

Volume ratio in reactions

2CO(g) + O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g)
Volume ratio: 2 : 1 : 2
If 6 dm³ of CO reacts with excess O₂, volume of CO₂ produced = 6 dm³ (same ratio).
O₂ used = 3 dm³ (half the CO volume).

At RTP: 1 mol of gas = 24 dm³

Volume (dm³) = n × 24
Volume (cm³) = n × 24,000

Volume ratio = mole ratio (from balanced equation)

e.g. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
1 : 3 : 2 (volume ratio)

Common Misconception

Different gases have different molar volumes because they have different Mr values.

All gases have the same molar volume at the same temperature and pressure (24 dm³ at RTP). This is because gas particles are so far apart that the volume of the particles themselves is negligible compared to the space between them (Avogadro's hypothesis).

Differentiation

Support:
Use the suitcase analogy: one mole of any gas fills about 24 dm³. Show that this is independent of the gas by comparing Mr and volume.
Stretch:
At STP (0°C, 1 atm), 1 mol of gas = 22.4 dm³. Why is this different from RTP? How does temperature affect gas volume? (Charles's Law: V ∝ T)

Plenary (5 minutes)

Calculate: what volume of oxygen is needed to burn 48g of methane completely? (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates gas volumes from moles and uses volume ratios from balanced equations.
  • Getting there: Can calculate volume from moles but makes errors with volume ratios in reactions.
  • Not yet: Cannot use molar volume. Reteach with the suitcase analogy and simple mole-to-volume conversions.

Lesson 2: Gas Volume Calculations

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Conversions

Convert to volume at RTP: 0.5 mol H₂, 2.5 mol CO₂, 0.01 mol O₂. Answers: 12 dm³, 60 dm³, 0.24 dm³.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
This lesson combines gas volume calculations with other quantitative skills:

1. Finding volume from mass:
mass → moles (÷Mr) → volume (×24)

2. Finding mass from volume:
volume → moles (÷24) → mass (×Mr)

3. Finding volume from concentration and volume of solution:
moles = concentration × volume → gas volume = moles × 24

4. Reverse calculations:
Given gas volume, find mass of reactant needed

Key types of exam question:
• What volume of gas is produced from a given mass of reactant?
• What mass of reactant is needed to produce a given volume of gas?
• What volume of gas is collected when a solution of known concentration reacts?
Volume from acid concentration

25 cm³ of 0.2 mol/dm³ HCl reacts with excess CaCO₃. What volume of CO₂ is produced?
Moles of HCl = 0.2 × 0.025 = 0.005 mol
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
CO₂:HCl ratio = 1:2, so moles of CO₂ = 0.005/2 = 0.0025 mol
Volume of CO₂ = 0.0025 × 24 = 0.06 dm³ = 60 cm³

Mass from gas volume

What mass of magnesium is needed to produce 480 cm³ of hydrogen at RTP?
Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂
Volume of H₂ = 480 cm³ = 0.48 dm³
Moles of H₂ = 0.48/24 = 0.02 mol
Mg:H₂ ratio = 1:1, so moles of Mg = 0.02 mol
Mass of Mg = 0.02 × 24 = 0.48g

Gas calculation pathways:

mass → (÷Mr) → moles → (×24) → volume (dm³)
volume (dm³) → (÷24) → moles → (×Mr) → mass

For solutions:
concentration (mol/dm³) × volume (dm³) = moles
moles → (×24) → gas volume (dm³)

Always check units: volume must be in dm³ (not cm³) for concentration; volume must be in dm³ for gas calculations

Common Misconception

You can use the volume in cm³ directly in the formula V = n × 24.

The formula V = n × 24 gives volume in dm³. If you start with cm³, you must convert to dm³ first (÷1000). Similarly, if the answer needs to be in cm³, multiply the dm³ result by 1000.

Differentiation

Support:
Create a 'calculation pathway' poster: mass → moles → volume with arrows showing ÷Mr and ×24. Children can follow the arrows.
Stretch:
A gas has a volume of 2.4 dm³ at RTP and a mass of 4.4g. Identify the gas. (Hint: calculate the molar mass — Mr = mass/moles = 4.4/0.1 = 44 → CO₂)

Plenary (5 minutes)

Two quick calculations: one finding gas volume from mass, one finding mass from gas volume. Check answers.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Confidently converts between mass, moles, and gas volumes in any direction.
  • Getting there: Can do straightforward calculations but makes errors with unit conversions (cm³ vs dm³).
  • Not yet: Cannot link moles to gas volume. Reteach with the 24 dm³ = 1 mole rule.

Homework Suggestions

  • Calculate gas volumes for 10 different reactions
  • Investigate: what volume of CO₂ is produced from different amounts of acid reacting with marble chips?
  • Research: why is 24 dm³ used for RTP instead of 22.4 dm³ (STP)?
