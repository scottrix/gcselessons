Lesson 2: Core Concepts: verse set texts

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: GCSE Latin verse set texts are prescribed by each exam board and change every two years. Check which texts are current for your exam year.
Key Fact: Common verse authors include Virgil (Aeneid), Ovid (Metamorphoses), Catullus (poems), and Horace (Odes and Satires).
Key Fact: Virgil's Aeneid is the national epic of Rome, telling how Aeneas fled Troy and founded the Roman race in Italy. Key themes: pietas, fate, the cost of empire.
Key Fact: Ovid's Metamorphoses tells myths of transformation, often with wit, irony, and playful subversion of epic conventions.
Key Fact: Catullus wrote personal lyric poetry about love, friendship, and social satire. His poems to Lesbia are among the most famous love poetry in any language.
Key Fact: Horace's Odes are polished, philosophical lyrics reflecting on life, death, love, and Roman values with understated elegance.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Compare how Virgil and Ovid approach the theme of power in their verse set texts. (8 marks) <div class="

Virgil and Ovid both engage with the theme of power but from very different perspectives. Virgil's Aeneid presents power as a divine imperative: Aeneas must fulfil his fate (fatum) to found Rome, ordained by Jupiter. Power is legitimate when exercised in service of Rome's destined future, and Aeneas's pietas demands he subordinate personal desires to this mission. However, Virgil does not present this uncritically: Dido's death shows the devastating human cost of imperial destiny, and Aeneas's final killing of Turnus in rage raises questions about whether power corrupts even the most dutiful hero. Ovid's Metamorphoses is far more sceptical. The gods repeatedly abuse their power to exploit mortals: Jupiter pursues Io, Europa, and Callisto; Apollo pursues Daphne; and divine power is shown as arbitrary, selfish, and destructive. Where Virgil's gods direct fate for Rome's glory, Ovid's gods act from personal desire. Ovid also shows transformation itself as an exercise of power, stripping mortals of agency. Common Errors: ✗ The Aeneid is purely a celebration of Augustus and Roman power. ✓ While the Aeneid celebrates Rome's destiny, Virgil also powerfully expresses the cost of that destiny: Dido's death, Turnus's killing, and Aeneas's own suffering all complicate any simple pro-Augustan reading. ✗ Ovid's Metamorphoses is just a collection of entertaining stories. ✓ The Metamorphoses is a sophisticated work that questions power, authority, and the nature of civilisation.
