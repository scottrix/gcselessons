Lesson 2: Core Concepts: subjunctive mood & advanced syntax

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on subjunctive mood & advanced syntax. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The subjunctive mood expresses possibility, purpose, wish, or subordinate action — unlike the indicative, which states facts.
Key Fact: Present subjunctive is formed by changing the stem vowel: 1st conjugation changes -a- to -e- (muset), 2nd changes -e- to -ea- (moneas), 3rd/4th change to -a- (ducamus, audiamus).
Key Fact: Imperfect subjunctive is formed by adding -re to the infinitive: laudarem (I would praise / I might praise).
Key Fact: Purpose clauses: ut + subjunctive = 'in order to' (positive); ne + subjunctive = 'in order not to' (negative). Example: venit ut pater videret (he came in order that his father might see).
Key Fact: Result clauses: ut + subjunctive after tam/ita/tantus/talis = 'so...that'. Example: tam fortis erat ut hostes vinceret (he was so brave that he conquered the enemy).
Key Fact: Indirect commands: after verbs of ordering/asking/persuading + ut/ne + subjunctive. Example: rex imperavit ut milites venirent (the king ordered that the soldiers should come).

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain the main uses of the subjunctive mood in Latin subordinate clauses, giving examples of purpose, result, and cum clauses. (8 marks) <div class="

The subjunctive mood is used in Latin to express actions that are not stated as facts but as possibilities, purposes, wishes, or subordinate ideas. In purpose clauses, ut + subjunctive expresses positive purpose ('in order to') and ne + subjunctive expresses negative purpose ('in order not to'). For example: milites venerunt ut urbem caperent (the soldiers came in order to capture the city). The subjunctive caperent shows this is the purpose of their coming. In result clauses, ut + subjunctive follows signal words such as tam, tantus, or ita in the main clause to express 'so...that'. For example: tam fortis erat Brutus ut hostes solus vinceret (Brutus was so brave that he alone conquered the enemy). The result clause describes the outcome of his bravery. In cum clauses, cum + subjunctive has three possible meanings depending on context. Temporal cum means 'when': cum venisset, omnes laeti erant (when he had come, everyone was happy). Causal cum means 'since': cum laboravisset, defessus est (since he had worked, he was tired).
