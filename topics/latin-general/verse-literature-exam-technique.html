Lesson 2: Core Concepts: verse literature exam technique

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on verse literature exam technique. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Verse literature exams test translation, analysis of poetic features, and evaluation of themes and characterisation — always with reference to the Latin text.
Key Fact: When analysing style, always PEA: Point (name the feature), Evidence (quote the Latin), Analysis (explain the effect on the reader).
Key Fact: Common poetic features to discuss: alliteration, enjambement, elision, caesura, onomatopoeia, hyperbaton, simile, metaphor, and word order.
Key Fact: For evaluation questions on character or theme, structure your answer with an introduction, 3-4 paragraphs each with a Latin quotation, and a conclusion.
Key Fact: When asked about metre, identify the metre (hexameter for epic) and discuss how rhythm contributes to meaning.
Key Fact: Comparison questions: identify similarities AND differences, using connectives like 'whereas', 'similarly', 'in contrast'.

Exam Tips: Prepare a 'style toolkit' for each author before the exam: know their characteristic devices and effects. | In the exam, read the question twice and underline key words. | For translation, work through the Latin methodically: identify the verb, then subjects and objects, then subordinate clauses. | Never write a paragraph without a Latin quotation in it.
Common Errors: ✗ You can discuss themes in general without quoting the Latin text. ✓ Every point about theme or character must be supported by a specific Latin quotation from the set text. General discussion without textual evidence will score very low marks. ✗ Style analysis is about identifying as many devices as possible. ✓ Identifying devices is only step one.
