Lesson 2: Core Concepts: nouns & declensions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on nouns & declensions. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Latin nouns change their endings to show their grammatical function in a sentence; this system is called declension.
Key Fact: There are five declensions, each with a characteristic vowel in the stem: 1st (a-stem), 2nd (o-stem), 3rd (consonant/i-stem), 4th (u-stem), 5th (e-stem).
Key Fact: Every Latin noun has a gender: masculine, feminine, or neuter. Gender must be learned with each noun and does not always match natural sex.
Key Fact: First declension nouns are mostly feminine (e.g. puella, villa, familia). Exceptions like nauta and agricola are masculine.
Key Fact: Second declension nouns are mostly masculine (ending -us) or neuter (ending -um). Notable feminine exceptions include humus and vilicus.
Key Fact: Third declension is the largest and most varied group; it contains nouns of all three genders and has both consonant stems and i-stems.

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on nouns & declensions from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always learn the nominative AND genitive singular of every noun — the genitive is essential for identifying the declension and stem. | When translating, identify the noun's case first: the ending tells you its grammatical function in the sentence. | Watch for neuter nouns: their nominative and accusative look the same, so context determines which case is meant. | Third declension nouns are the most tested — practise recognising i-stems by their genitive plural (-ium) and neuter plural (-ia).
Common Errors: ✗ All first declension nouns are feminine. ✓ Most first declension nouns are feminine, but some are masculine (nauta, agricola, poeta, pirata). Gender must be learned individually. ✗ The stem of a noun is found from the nominative singular. ✓ The stem is found from the genitive singular by removing the genitive ending.
