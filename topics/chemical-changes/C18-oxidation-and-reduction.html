C18: Oxidation and Reduction

Higher All Boards

Define oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen loss/gain and electron transfer, and identify redox reactions.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen loss and gain
  • Define oxidation and reduction in terms of electron transfer (OIL RIG)
  • Identify redox reactions and explain what is oxidised and what is reduced

Prerequisites

  • Electrolysis (C17) — cathode/anode, half-equations
  • Reactivity of Metals (C15) — metal extraction

Materials & Equipment

  • Periodic table (printed or wall chart)
  • Scientific calculator
  • Video: iron(III) oxide reduction with carbon monoxide
  • Printed redox equation worksheets

Lesson 1: Oxidation and Reduction — Oxygen Definition

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Rusting and Burning

Iron rusting and magnesium burning in air both involve reaction with oxygen. What do these two processes have in common? What is different about them?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Two definitions of oxidation and reduction:

Definition 1 (Oxygen-based):
• Oxidation: gain of oxygen
• Reduction: loss of oxygen

Definition 2 (Electron-based):
• Oxidation: loss of electrons
• Reduction: gain of electrons

Mnemonic: OIL RIG
Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons)
Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

Redox reactions always happen together — if one substance is oxidised, another must be reduced. You cannot have one without the other.

Examples using oxygen definition:
• CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
  CuO is reduced (loses oxygen → copper)
  H₂ is oxidised (gains oxygen → water)
• Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
  Fe₂O₃ is reduced (loses oxygen → iron)
  CO is oxidised (gains oxygen → CO₂)
Redox in the blast furnace

Iron(III) oxide is reduced to iron by carbon monoxide:
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
Fe₂O₃ loses oxygen → reduced
CO gains oxygen → oxidised
This is a redox reaction — both happen simultaneously.

Copper oxide reduced by hydrogen

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
CuO loses oxygen → reduced to Cu
H₂ gains oxygen → oxidised to H₂O
Black CuO turns into brown copper metal. Hydrogen is the reducing agent.

OIL RIG:
Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons)
Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

Oxygen-based definition:
Oxidation = gain of oxygen
Reduction = loss of oxygen

Redox = both happen together (one substance oxidised, another reduced)

Common Misconception

Oxidation and reduction can happen on their own.

They always happen together — you cannot oxidise one substance without reducing another. Electrons lost by one substance must be gained by another. They are always paired in a redox reaction.

Differentiation

Support:
Use the 'musical chairs' analogy: electrons must always have somewhere to go. One substance loses them, another gains them.
Stretch:
Research: what is the role of oxygen in combustion? Is burning always oxidation?

Plenary (5 minutes)

In each reaction, identify what is oxidised and what is reduced: Mg + CuO → MgO + Cu, H₂O + C → H₂ + CO.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Defines oxidation and reduction using both definitions, and identifies what is oxidised and reduced in redox reactions.
  • Getting there: Knows the oxygen-based definition but struggles with the electron-based definition.
  • Not yet: Cannot define oxidation or reduction. Reteach with OIL RIG and physical demonstrations.

Lesson 2: Redox and Electron Transfer

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Electron Transfer

In the reaction Na + Cl → NaCl, which atom loses electrons and which gains? What does this tell you about which is oxidised and which is reduced?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Using the electron definition:
• Oxidation: loss of electrons → oxidation number increases (more positive)
• Reduction: gain of electrons → oxidation number decreases (more negative)

Linking the two definitions:
When a substance gains oxygen, it loses electrons (oxidation). When a substance loses oxygen, it gains electrons (reduction). Both definitions describe the same process from different perspectives.

Examples with electron transfer:
• Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (sodium is oxidised — loses an electron)
• Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (chlorine is reduced — gains an electron)
• Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ (magnesium is oxidised — loses 2 electrons)
• Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (copper ion is reduced — gains 2 electrons)

Redox in electrolysis:
• At cathode: reduction always happens (ions gain electrons)
• At anode: oxidation always happens (ions lose electrons)
This is why electrolysis is always a redox process.

Redox in displacement:
• Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
• Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (zinc oxidised — loses electrons)
• Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (copper reduced — gains electrons)
Zinc is the reducing agent (causes reduction). Copper ions are the oxidising agent (cause oxidation).
Displacement as redox

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
Zinc loses electrons → oxidised (reducing agent)
Cu²⁺ gains electrons → reduced (oxidising agent)
This connects to the reactivity series: zinc is more reactive because it loses electrons more easily.

Electrolysis is always redox

At cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (reduction — ions gain electrons)
At anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (oxidation — ions lose electrons)
Every electrolysis reaction involves both oxidation and reduction. The anode is the site of oxidation; the cathode is the site of reduction.

OIL RIG (electron definition):
Oxidation: Loss of electrons (oxidation number increases)
Reduction: Gain of electrons (oxidation number decreases)

Oxidising agent: causes oxidation (is itself reduced — gains electrons)
Reducing agent: causes reduction (is itself oxidised — loses electrons)

Electrolysis: oxidation at anode, reduction at cathode (always redox)

Common Misconception

The oxidising agent is oxidised.

The oxidising agent causes oxidation in another substance, but it is itself reduced (gains electrons). Similarly, the reducing agent causes reduction but is itself oxidised (loses electrons). The names are counterintuitive — think of what they do to the other substance, not what happens to themselves.

Differentiation

Support:
Create a summary table: Reaction | What is oxidised? | What is reduced? | Oxidising agent? | Reducing agent? Fill in together.
Stretch:
Assign oxidation numbers to all atoms in: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Identify which atoms change oxidation number.

Plenary (5 minutes)

For each reaction, identify: (a) what is oxidised, (b) what is reduced, (c) the oxidising agent, (d) the reducing agent. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu, Mg + HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies redox reactions confidently using both definitions, and correctly identifies oxidising/reducing agents.
  • Getting there: Can identify oxidation and reduction but confuses oxidising and reducing agents.
  • Not yet: Cannot apply OIL RIG to reactions. Reteach with step-by-step electron counting.

Homework Suggestions

  • Classify 10 reactions as redox or not redox, and explain your reasoning
  • Create a glossary: oxidation, reduction, redox, oxidising agent, reducing agent, OIL RIG
  • Research: how is hydrogen used as a reducing agent in metallurgy?
