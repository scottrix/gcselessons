Lesson 2: Core Concepts: climate change and pollution

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on climate change and pollution. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Human activities are increasing the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. The main greenhouse gases are carbon dioxide (CO₂) and methane (CH₄) . Water vapour is also a greenhouse gas.
Increased greenhouse gas concentrations are causing global warming — an increase in the average temperature of the Earth. This leads to climate change , with significant consequences.
A carbon footprint is the total amount of CO₂ and other greenhouse gas emissions caused by a person, organisation, event or product, expressed as a CO₂ equivalent.
Reducing carbon footprints involves reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Methods include using renewable energy , improving energy efficiency , carbon capture , and changing agricultural practices .
Burning fossil fuels also releases pollutants that harm the environment and human health: sulfur dioxide , oxides of nitrogen , and particulates (carbon particles/soot).
You need to interpret data on atmospheric CO₂ concentrations over time, calculate percentage changes in greenhouse gas levels, and compare carbon footprints of different activities.

Lesson 3: Application: climate change and pollution

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Answers. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Name two greenhouse gases increased by human activity and state one source of each.

Answer: CO₂ — burning fossil fuels / deforestation. Methane — farming (cattle) / landfill sites.

Q2: Describe three consequences of global warming / climate change.

Answer: (1) Rising sea levels due to thermal expansion and melting ice. (2) More extreme weather — floods, droughts, storms. (3) Habitat loss and species extinction — ecosystems change too quickly for species to adapt.

Q3: Define the term "carbon footprint" and suggest two ways an individual could reduce their carbon footprint.

Answer: A carbon footprint is the total amount of CO₂ and other greenhouse gas emissions caused by a person, organisation or product. Two ways to reduce: use public transport, walk or cycle instead of driving; use renewable energy sources at home.

Q4: Higher Explain how sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen cause acid rain. Describe two effects of acid rain on the environment.

Answer: SO₂ from burning sulfur-containing fossil fuels and NOₓ from high-temperature combustion dissolve in moisture in the atmosphere to form sulfuric acid and nitric acid . These fall as acid rain. Effects: (1) Corrodes buildings and statues. (2) Makes lakes and soil acidic, killing fish and trees.

Q5: Explain what particulates are, how they are produced, and two problems they cause.

Answer: Particulates are tiny solid particles (soot/carbon) produced by incomplete combustion . Problems: (1) Respiratory problems such as asthma. (2) Global dimming by reflecting sunlight back into space.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Extended Answer 6 marks: Explain how human activities are increasing greenhouse gas concentrations, describe three consequences of climate change, and suggest why it is difficult to reduce global emissions. <div class="

Human activities are increasing greenhouse gas concentrations in several ways. Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas) for electricity, heating and transport releases large amounts of CO₂ that was previously locked underground. Deforestation reduces the number of trees available to absorb CO₂ through photosynthesis, and when trees are burned or decompose they release stored carbon as CO₂. Farming produces methane from cattle digestion and rice paddies, while landfill sites produce methane from anaerobic decomposition of organic waste. Three consequences of climate change: rising sea levels (thermal expansion of oceans and melting ice caps), more extreme weather events (droughts, floods, storms), and habitat loss and species extinction (ecosystems change too rapidly for species to adapt). It is difficult to reduce global emissions because: fossil fuels are cheap and well-established as energy sources, making alternatives expensive; there is a lack of international cooperation — not all countries agree on targets or implement them fully; and individuals are reluctant to change their lifestyles (e.g. reduce car use, eat less meat).
