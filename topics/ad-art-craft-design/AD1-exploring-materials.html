AD1: Exploring Materials & Techniques

Foundation Higher All Boards

Explore a wide range of art materials and techniques through hands-on experimentation. Develop understanding of material properties, mark-making, and creative processes essential for portfolio development.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 4
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 60 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA, WJEC
Practical: Yes

Learning Objectives

  • Experiment with at least 6 different art materials and their properties
  • Develop mark-making vocabulary through varied tools and surfaces
  • Understand health and safety requirements for different media
  • Document experiments systematically in a sketchbook
  • Evaluate material choices for expressive effect

Prerequisites

  • Basic drawing skills (line, tone, shape)
  • Understanding of primary/secondary colours
  • Familiarity with sketchbook use

Materials & Equipment

  • Drawing: Graphite pencils (2H-6B), charcoal sticks, compressed charcoal, charcoal pencils, putty erasers, fixative spray
  • Painting: Watercolour set, gouache paints, acrylic paints, brushes (rounds, flats, filberts), palette knives, water pots, palettes
  • Printmaking: Lino blocks, lino tools, printing ink, rollers, baren/printing press, cartridge paper
  • Mixed Media: PVA glue, collage materials (magazines, tissue, fabric), ink, bleach, salt, wax crayons, masking fluid
  • Surfaces: Cartridge paper (A3/A2), watercolour paper, sugar paper, canvas boards, mount board
  • Safety: Aprons, gloves, ventilation for fixative/sprays, wet wipes, hand washing station, non-slip floor covering
  • Space: Large tables with wipeable surfaces, drying rack/area, sink access, good lighting, storage for wet work

Lesson 1: Dry Media Exploration

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Mark-Making Carousel

Set up 4 stations: graphite, charcoal, pastel, pen. Students spend 2 minutes at each creating as many different marks as possible (dots, dashes, hatching, stippling, smudging, pressure variation). Rotate on timer.

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Guide students through systematic exploration of dry media:
  • Graphite: Grading scale (9H-9B), pressure control, blending with tortillon/tissue, eraser drawing
  • Charcoal: Willow vs compressed, expressive gesture, subtractive drawing (toned paper + eraser), fixative safety
  • Pastels: Oil vs chalk, layering, blending, sgraffito (scratching through layers), dust management
  • Pen/Ink: Line weight, cross-hatching, stippling, wash techniques with water-soluble ink
Students create a "material library" page in sketchbook for each medium with labelled samples.

Required Practical: Material Library

Students produce 4 sketchbook pages (one per medium) showing: material name, 6+ distinct marks, tonal gradient, texture experiments, brief annotation on properties and best uses. Health & safety notes for charcoal dust and fixative fumes.

Common Misconception

"Charcoal is just messy pencil"

Charcoal has fundamentally different properties: it's softer, more expressive, allows subtraction (erasing to create light), and requires fixative. It behaves differently on textured vs smooth paper.

Differentiation

Support: Provide mark-making reference sheets. Limit to 2 media per lesson. Use larger paper for motor control.
Stretch: Combine media in one drawing (e.g., charcoal underpainting with pen detail). Research historical uses of each medium.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Quick Study

Using one medium, complete a 10-minute observational study of a simple object (shell, leaf, crumpled paper) focusing on exploiting that medium's unique qualities.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Medium Match

Show 4 artwork images. Students identify the medium used and justify their choice based on visual evidence.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Control of medium, range of marks, tonal control
  • Creativity: Inventive mark-making, willingness to experiment
  • Understanding: Accurate annotations, health & safety awareness
  • Presentation: Organised sketchbook layout, clear labelling

Lesson 2: Wet Media Exploration

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Wet-on-Wet Race

Students wet paper, drop in 3 colours, tilt to blend. Race to create smoothest gradient. Discusses water control, pigment behaviour, paper absorption.

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Systematic wet media exploration:
  • Watercolour: Transparency, wet-on-wet, wet-on-dry, glazing, lifting, salt/bloom effects, masking fluid
  • Gouache: Opacity, re-wetting, layering light over dark, flat colour, poster-style application
  • Acrylic: Fast drying, impasto with palette knife, glazing mediums, texture gels, plastic wrap/card scraping effects
  • Ink/Brush: Calligraphic marks, dilution series, blow painting, salt/crystallisation
Students create "wet media library" sketchbook pages with swatches, technique labels, drying times noted.

Required Practical: Colour & Technique Charts

For each wet medium: 12-colour mixing chart, 6 technique swatches, 1 small study (5x5cm) demonstrating 3+ techniques combined. Annotate: transparency/opacity, drying time, rewettability, lightfastness.

Common Misconception

"Watercolour mistakes can't be fixed"

Lifting (wet or dry), scrubbing, gouache overpainting, masking fluid preservation, and embracing "happy accidents" are all valid watercolour strategies.

Differentiation

Support: Pre-drawn grids for charts. Limited palette (3 colours). Step-by-step technique cards.
Stretch: Create a monochrome study using only one colour + water. Research pigment numbers and lightfastness ratings.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Mood Wash

Choose an emotion word (stormy, serene, anxious, joyful). Create an abstract wash composition using one wet medium to express that mood.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Technique Swap

Students exchange sketchbooks, identify one technique they'd like to try, and write a question for the creator.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Water control, colour mixing accuracy, technique execution
  • Creativity: Inventive technique combinations, expressive colour use
  • Understanding: Clear annotations of processes, drying times, material behaviour
  • Presentation: Clean swatches, organised layout, legible notes

Lesson 3: Printmaking & Mixed Media

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Texture Hunt

Students make rubbings (graphite/wax crayon) of 5 different textures around the room. Discuss: which textures print well? How does pressure affect result?

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Introduce accessible printmaking and mixed media:
  • Monoprinting: Rolling ink on acetate, drawing into ink, pressing paper (subtractive); adding ink, pressing objects (additive)
  • Lino/Relief: Safety cutting (bench hook, cut away from hand), positive/negative space, registration for multi-colour
  • Collage/Mixed Media: Adhesives (PVA, gel medium, spray mount), layering order, integrating drawn/painted elements, bleach/ink reactions
  • Experimental: Cling film textures, bubble wrap printing, cardboard relief, string prints

Required Practical: Print Series

Produce a series of 4 related prints (monoprint or lino) exploring a simple theme (pattern, texture, silhouette). Document: block/process photos, ink consistency notes, registration method, edition numbering (1/4, 2/4...). Include 2 "ghost prints" (second pulls).

Common Misconception

"Printmaking needs a press"

Hand burnishing with a baren, wooden spoon, or even firm hand pressure works for lino and monoprints. Many professional printmakers work without a press.

Differentiation

Support: Pre-cut simple lino shapes. Monoprint only (no cutting). Collaborative large-scale print.
Stretch: Reduction lino (multi-colour from one block). Chine collé (collaging thin paper during printing). Digital/print hybrid.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Mixed Media Response

Combine one print with drawing/painting/collage to develop the image further. Work quickly — respond intuitively.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Process Reflection

Students note: What worked? What surprised? What would you change? Which process suits your ideas best?

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Clean prints, consistent inking, safe cutting, registration
  • Creativity: Image development across series, inventive combinations
  • Understanding: Process documentation, technical vocabulary
  • Presentation: Edition presented professionally, sketchbook documentation

Lesson 4: Material Selection & Personal Response

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Material Speed-Dating

Students rotate through 6 material stations (2 min each), making a quick response to the prompt "growth" using only that material. Rapid decision-making.

Skill Development (25 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Focus on critical selection and justification:
  • Review all experiments — identify personal strengths and preferences
  • Match materials to expressive intentions (e.g., charcoal for drama, watercolour for delicacy, lino for bold graphic)
  • Consider practical constraints: drying time, space, cost, portability
  • Plan a small personal project using 2-3 chosen media

Required Practical: Material Justification Sheet

Create an A3 sheet: 3 chosen media, 3 reasons for each (expressive quality, technical suitability, personal affinity), 1 test piece per medium (10x10cm), health & safety summary, estimated time/cost for a sustained project.

Creative Work (20 minutes)

Mini Personal Project

Using chosen media, create a developed response to "growth" (or student's own theme) demonstrating material understanding. This becomes a portfolio starting point.

Reflection & Presentation (5 minutes)

Gallery Walk

Work displayed. Students leave sticky-note feedback: "Effective use of [medium] because..." and "Consider trying..."

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Competent handling of chosen media
  • Creativity: Personal, thoughtful response to theme
  • Understanding: Clear justification linking material to intention
  • Presentation: Professional justification sheet, resolved mini-outcome

Homework Suggestions

  • Extend material library: experiment with 2 household/unconventional materials (coffee, tea, soil, bleach, expired makeup)
  • Artist research: Find 3 artists known for innovative material use (e.g., Anselm Kiefer, El Anatsui, Tara Donovan). Create analysis page.
  • Sustained drawing: 1-hour observational study using only dry media, focusing on surface quality
  • Material swap: Trade one material with a peer, create a response using their unfamiliar medium
