AD2: Developing Ideas Through Investigation

Foundation Higher All Boards

Learn how to develop meaningful artistic ideas through structured investigation. Explore primary and secondary sources, artist research methods, and strategies for evolving initial concepts into sustained personal enquiries.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 4
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 60 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA, WJEC
Practical: Yes

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish between primary and secondary sources and use both effectively
  • Conduct meaningful artist research that informs practical work (not just biography)
  • Develop a personal line of enquiry from a given or chosen starting point
  • Use mind-mapping, mood boards, and visual analysis to expand ideas
  • Document the investigative process clearly in sketchbook

Prerequisites

  • Basic sketchbook habits (dating, titling, annotating)
  • Experience with at least 3 art materials from AD1
  • Familiarity with formal elements (line, tone, colour, texture, form, space)

Materials & Equipment

  • Research: Internet access, art books, magazines, library access, printer for images
  • Recording: Camera/phone for primary photography, sketchbook (A3 recommended), drawing materials from AD1
  • Presentation: Scissors, glue sticks, double-sided tape, mounting board, washi tape, coloured paper/card
  • Analysis Tools: Highlighters, annotation pens, sticky notes, formal elements prompt cards
  • Space: Wall space for mood boards, good lighting for photography, computer/tablet for digital research
  • Safety: Safe internet use guidelines, copyright awareness for images, cutting tools supervision

Lesson 1: Starting Points & Primary Sources

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Object Interrogation

Place a natural object (shell, seed pod, bone, feather) on each table. Students have 5 minutes to: draw it, photograph it from 3 angles, list 10 descriptive words, write 3 questions about it. Share findings.

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Structure the investigation process:
  • Primary Sources: Direct observation, photography, site visits, handling objects, interviews, own body as subject. Emphasise: YOU create the record.
  • Secondary Sources: Books, internet, galleries, reproductions, artist works, historical documents. Emphasise: SOMEONE ELSE created the record.
  • Quality Criteria: Primary — specific, personal, high-quality images, multiple viewpoints. Secondary — credible sources, high-resolution images, proper attribution.
  • Mind-Mapping: Central theme → branches (visual, conceptual, material, contextual) → sub-branches with specific ideas/artists/questions.

Required Practical: Primary Source Collection

Create a "Primary Source Portfolio" (min 6 pages): 10+ own photographs on theme, 3+ observational drawings (different media), 1+ site visit record (sketches/photos/notes), 1+ object handling record. All dated, located, annotated with formal elements analysis.

Common Misconception

"Research means copying pictures from the internet"

Research is ACTIVE investigation: selecting, analysing, responding, connecting. Copying is passive. Every image must have purpose — what does it SHOW you? What does it MAKE you think?

Differentiation

Support: Provide structured mind-map template. Give specific primary source tasks (e.g., "photograph 5 textures in your kitchen"). Limit secondary sources to 3 pre-vetted artists.
Stretch: Design own primary source brief. Compare how 3 artists approach same theme. Develop a research question to drive investigation.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Idea Seeds

From the object interrogation, each student identifies 3 potential "idea seeds" — directions the work could go. Quick thumbnail sketches for each.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Source Audit

Students categorise their current research: Primary vs Secondary. Count each. Set target: double primary sources by next lesson.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Quality of observational drawings, photographic composition
  • Creativity: Unusual viewpoints, personal connections, divergent thinking
  • Understanding: Clear primary/secondary distinction, purposeful selection
  • Presentation: Organised portfolio, consistent annotation, clear progression

Lesson 2: Artist Research That Informs Practice

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Artist Speed-Analysis

Show 4 artworks (1 min each). Students note: media, formal elements, mood, one question for the artist. Discuss: which artists connect to your theme?

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Transform artist research from biography to practice:
  • Avoid: Birth/death dates, school attended, general style labels without evidence.
  • Do: Formal analysis (composition, colour, mark-making), process investigation (how did they make it?), conceptual links (what ideas? why this subject?), practical response (your experiment using their method).
  • Structure: Image + credit → Formal analysis (annotated) → Process experiment → Personal response → How this influences YOUR work.
  • Comparative: Place 2 artists side by side — similarities/differences in approach to same theme.

Required Practical: Artist Research Pages (×3)

For 3 relevant artists: 1 double-page spread each. Must include: high-quality image with full credit, formal elements analysis (labelled), media/process investigation with your own test strip, 150-word critical commentary, explicit link to your developing ideas.

Common Misconception

"I need to copy the artist's style exactly"

Research is about UNDERSTANDING their decisions, not mimicking their look. Take their PROCESS or CONCEPT, apply to YOUR subject. Synthesis > imitation.

Differentiation

Support: Provide analysis framework (SEE-THINK-WONDER). Pre-select artists with clear process documentation (e.g., YouTube studio visits). Sentence starters for annotation.
Stretch: Contact a living artist with questions. Research exhibition curation. Write comparative essay (500 words) on two artists' approaches.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Process Borrowing

Choose ONE technique/process from an artist studied. Spend 10 minutes applying it to YOUR primary source imagery. Not a copy — a translation.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Influence Map

Draw a diagram: your theme in centre, artists around it, arrows showing what you've taken from each (colour? composition? concept? process?).

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Quality of process experiments, drawing from analysis
  • Creativity: Original synthesis, not pastiche; unexpected connections
  • Understanding: Depth of analysis, clear articulation of influence
  • Presentation: Professional layout, accurate referencing, clear visual hierarchy

Lesson 3: Developing a Line of Enquiry

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Question Storm

In pairs: Student A states their theme. Student B asks "Why?" 5 times. Student A must answer each time. Swap. Generates deeper conceptual grounding.

Skill Development (30 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Move from broad theme to focused enquiry:
  • Theme → Question → Enquiry: "Nature" (too broad) → "How does decay reveal structure?" (question) → "Investigating fungal patterns on fallen fruit through macro photography and printmaking" (enquiry).
  • Enquiry Criteria: Specific, investigable, personally meaningful, allows material exploration, connects to artists, sustainable for 10+ hours.
  • Planning: Break enquiry into 4-6 practical investigations (each 1-2 lessons). Each investigation = question + method + expected outcome.
  • Documentation: Enquiry statement at front of sketchbook. Investigation log: date, question, method, outcome, next step.

Required Practical: Enquiry Proposal

One A3 page: Enquiry statement (50 words), 3 key questions, 4 planned investigations (table: question, method, media, artist link, estimated time), success criteria (what will "good" look like?), risk assessment for materials/processes.

Creative Work (15 minutes)

First Investigation

Begin Investigation 1 from proposal. Practical work — could be drawing series, photo shoot, material tests, maquettes. Document as you go.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Enquiry Check

Partner reads your proposal. Asks: "What's the question?" "Why this media?" "How does Artist X connect?" You answer verbally, then note gaps.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Appropriate media selection, competent execution of Investigation 1
  • Creativity: Original enquiry, inventive investigation design
  • Understanding: Clear logical progression theme→question→enquiry→plan
  • Presentation: Professional proposal document, organised investigation start

Lesson 4: Sustained Investigation & Review

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (5 minutes)

Progress Share

Students display Investigation 1 work. 2-minute gallery walk. Each leaves one "strength" and one "direction" note on 2 different peers' work.

Skill Development (20 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Review and forward planning:
  • Review Cycle: What did Investigation 1 reveal? Answered the question? New questions? Media successful? Need new skills?
  • Iterative Process: Each investigation builds on previous. Show this visually: arrows between pages, "leading to..." annotations.
  • Decision Points: When to persist vs pivot. Evidence-based: "This medium isn't capturing the translucency I need → try watercolour/acetate layering."
  • Portfolio Building: Select strongest work for assessment. Annotate WHY selected. Show journey, not just finals.

Creative Work (30 minutes)

Investigation 2

Execute Investigation 2 from proposal, informed by Investigation 1 review. Teacher circulates for 1:1 tutorials (5 min each) on enquiry progress.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Next Steps Note

Write on a sticky note: "Investigation 2 showed me... Therefore Investigation 3 will..." Stick on next blank sketchbook page.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Developing competence, media control improving
  • Creativity: Ideas evolving, not repeating; responsive to discoveries
  • Understanding: Articulate review, evidence-based decisions, clear trajectory
  • Presentation: Sketchbook shows clear journey, reflective annotations, organised

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete Investigation 2 and begin Investigation 3 independently
  • Visit a gallery/exhibition (virtual or physical) related to enquiry. Create 2-page response.
  • Contact a practitioner (artist, designer, craftsperson) for interview/email Q&A about their process.
  • Create a "research wall" at home — pin up all source material, work in progress, notes. Photograph weekly.
  • Write 300-word reflective journal entry: "How has my thinking changed since starting this enquiry?"
