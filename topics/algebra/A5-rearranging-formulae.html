A5: Rearranging Formulae

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understand and use standard mathematical formulae; rearrange formulae to change the subject

📋 Key Concepts

Changing the subject means rearranging a formula so a different variable is isolated on one side (usually the left).

Golden Rule

Do the same to both sides. Use inverse operations in reverse BIDMAS order to isolate the target variable.

📝 Simple Rearranging (One Step)

Example 1

Rearrange v = u + at to make t the subject

Solution:

v = u + at

v − u = at (subtract u from both sides)

t = (v − u)/a (divide both sides by a)

Example 2

Rearrange A = πr² to make r the subject

Solution:

A = πr²

A/π = r² (divide by π)

r = √(A/π) (square root both sides, r > 0 for radius)

📝 Two or More Steps

Strategy: Deal with terms not containing the target variable first, then isolate the target.
Example 3

Rearrange P = 2(l + w) to make l the subject

Solution:

P = 2(l + w)

P/2 = l + w (divide by 2)

l = P/2 − w (subtract w)

Example 4

Rearrange y = 3x + 5 to make x the subject

Solution:

y = 3x + 5

y − 5 = 3x

x = (y − 5)/3

Example 5

Rearrange v² = u² + 2as to make s the subject

Solution:

v² = u² + 2as

v² − u² = 2as

s = (v² − u²)/2a

📝 Rearranging with Fractions

Method: Multiply both sides by the denominator to eliminate fractions, then rearrange.
Example 6

Rearrange v = u + at/2 to make t the subject

Solution:

v = u + at/2

v − u = at/2

2(v − u) = at (multiply by 2)

t = 2(v − u)/a

Example 7

Rearrange 1/f = 1/u + 1/v to make v the subject

Solution:

1/f = 1/u + 1/v

1/v = 1/f − 1/u

1/v = (u − f)/fu (common denominator)

v = fu/(u − f) (invert both sides)

📝 Rearranging with Brackets

Example 8

Rearrange y = 2(x + 3) to make x the subject

Solution:

y = 2(x + 3)

y/2 = x + 3 (divide by 2)

x = y/2 − 3

Example 9

Rearrange P = 2(l + w) to make w the subject

Solution:

P/2 = l + w

w = P/2 − l

📝 Rearranging with Powers and Roots

Example 10

Rearrange E = ½mv² to make v the subject

Solution:

E = ½mv²

2E = mv² (multiply by 2)

2E/m = v² (divide by m)

v = √(2E/m) (square root, v > 0 for speed)

Example 11

Rearrange T = 2π√(L/g) to make L the subject

Solution:

T = 2π√(L/g)

T/(2π) = √(L/g) (divide by 2π)

T²/(4π²) = L/g (square both sides)

L = gT²/(4π²) (multiply by g)

📝 Subject Appears Twice (Higher)

Example 12

Rearrange ax + b = cx + d to make x the subject

Solution:

ax − cx = d − b (collect x terms on left, constants on right)

x(a − c) = d − b (factorise x)

x = (d − b)/(a − c) (divide by a − c)

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Rearrange v = u + at to make t the subject

Q2: Rearrange A = πr² to make r the subject

Q3: Rearrange P = 2(l + w) to make l the subject

Q4: Rearrange y = 3x + 5 to make x the subject

Q5: Rearrange v² = u² + 2as to make s the subject

✅ Answers

  1. t = (v - u)/a
  2. r = √(A/π)
  3. l = P/2 - w
  4. x = (y - 5)/3
  5. s = (v² - u²)/2a

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Identify the target variable — make it the subject
  • Use inverse operations in reverse BIDMAS order
  • Do the same to both sides at every step
  • With fractions: multiply by denominator first
  • With brackets: divide first (or expand then collect)
  • With powers: square root last (remember ± for x² = a)
  • If subject appears twice: collect terms, factorise, divide
  • Check by substituting numbers into original and rearranged

🧠 Problem-Solving Strategies

Problem-Solving

When rearranging complex formulae: 1) Write the formula. 2) Identify target. 3) Remove fractions (multiply by LCM of denominators). 4) Expand brackets. 5) Collect target terms on one side. 6) Factorise target. 7) Divide. For square roots: isolate root, then square. For powers: isolate power, then root.
Multi-Step Problem

The volume of a cylinder is V = πr²h. (a) Rearrange to make h the subject. (b) Rearrange to make r the subject. (c) A cylinder has volume 500 cm³ and radius 5 cm. Find its height.

Solution: (a) h = V/(πr²). (b) r = √(V/(πh)). (c) h = 500/(π × 25) = 20/π ≈ 6.37 cm.

⚠️ Common Errors

Watch Out!

1. Wrong: v = u + at → t = v - u/a Correct: t = (v - u)/a (brackets needed!)

2. Wrong: √(x²) = x Correct: √(x²) = |x| — for radius/length, positive only

3. Wrong: y = 2(x + 3) → y/2 = x + 3 → x = y/2 - 3 Correct: That IS correct! But some do y = 2x + 3 (forgetting to multiply 3 by 2)

4. Wrong: 1/a + 1/b = 1/(a+b) Correct: 1/a + 1/b = (a+b)/ab

5. Wrong: x² = 9 → x = 3 Correct: x = ±3 (unless context restricts, e.g., length > 0)

✍️ 6-Mark Exam Question

Extended Answer

6 marks: The period of a pendulum is T = 2π√(L/g). (a) Rearrange to make L the subject. (b) Rearrange to make g the subject. (c) On Earth, g = 9.8, T = 2 s. Find L. (d) On the Moon, g = 1.6. For the same L, find T.

(a) L = gT²/(4π²)

(b) g = 4π²L/T²

(c) L = 9.8 × 4 / (4π²) = 9.8/π² ≈ 0.993 m

(d) T = 2π√(0.993/1.6) ≈ 4.95 s

Mark scheme: 2 marks for (a), 2 marks for (b), 1 mark for (c), 1 mark for (d)

📊 AO3: Reason & Interpret

Reasoning and Interpretation

A student rearranges F = ma to make a the subject and writes a = Fm.

(a) Is this correct? Explain.

(b) The student then substitutes F = 20, m = 4 into their formula. What do they get? What should the correct answer be?

(c) Write a similar formula where the subject appears in a denominator, and rearrange it.

Answers: (a) No — should be a = F/m (divide by m, not multiply). (b) Student gets a = 20 × 4 = 80. Correct: a = 20/4 = 5. (c) Example: v = u + at. Rearrange for t: t = (v - u)/a.
