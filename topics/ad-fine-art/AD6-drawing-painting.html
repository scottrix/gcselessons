AD6: Drawing & Painting Techniques

Foundation Higher All Boards

Master core drawing and painting skills through structured workshops. Develop observational accuracy, expressive mark-making, colour theory application, and personal painterly language essential for Fine Art portfolio.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 5
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 60 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA, WJEC
Practical: Yes

Learning Objectives

  • Demonstrate proficiency in observational drawing (line, tone, proportion, perspective)
  • Apply colour theory: mixing, harmony, temperature, limited palettes
  • Explore paint handling: glazing, impasto, scumbling, wet-in-wet, alla prima
  • Develop personal mark-making vocabulary across drawing and painting media
  • Critically reflect on technical choices in relation to expressive intent

Prerequisites

  • Basic drawing experience (AD1 materials exploration)
  • Understanding of colour wheel, primary/secondary/tertiary
  • Sketchbook habit for developmental work

Materials & Equipment

  • Drawing: Graphite (2H-8B), charcoal (willow, compressed, pencil), conté crayons, ink (Indian, acrylic), dip pens, brushes for ink, A2/A1 cartridge paper, sugar paper, toned paper
  • Painting: Acrylics (artist quality primaries + white, burnt umber, Payne's grey), oils (optional, with solvent safety), watercolours, gouache; brushes (hog, synthetic, rounds 0-12, flats, filberts, rigger); palette knives; palettes (stay-wet for acrylic)
  • Surfaces: Stretched canvas, canvas boards, primed MDF, heavy watercolour paper (300gsm+), oil paper
  • Colour Aids: Colour wheel, mixing charts, grey card, Munsell student book (optional)
  • Space: Easels (table/floor), drawing boards, clip lights for directional lighting, wet work storage, solvent-safe ventilation (if oils)
  • Safety: Solvent handling (odourless mineral spirits, no turpentine), rag disposal (metal bin with water), skin protection, ventilation, fire safety for oil-soaked rags

Lesson 1: Observational Drawing Intensive

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Gesture Drawing Series

Set up simple still life. 10 x 1-minute poses (move object slightly each time). Charcoal on newsprint. Focus: capture energy, weight, proportion, not detail. No erasing.

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Structured observational drawing:
  • Sighting & Measuring: Pencil measuring (thumb on pencil), comparative proportions, angles, negative space checking, plumb lines.
  • Tonal Drawing: Identify 5 tones (highlight, light, mid, shadow, reflected light). Build from mid-tone. Charcoal/graphite on toned paper — add highlights with white conté/pencil.
  • Line Quality: Contour drawing (blind, modified), cross-contour, expressive line weight (pressure, speed, tool).
  • Composition: Viewfinder use, thumbnail sketches (4 per setup), rule of thirds, cropping decisions.

Required Practical: Sustained Observational Drawing

2-hour drawing (can span sessions): Complex still life (5+ objects, varied textures, reflective/transparent). A2 size. Full tonal range. Media choice justified. Photograph stages every 30 min. Annotate: decisions, corrections, lighting changes.

Common Misconception

"Good drawing = photographic accuracy"

Good drawing = convincing structure, sensitive observation, expressive mark-making. Photography captures appearance; drawing captures UNDERSTANDING of form.

Differentiation

Support: Simpler still life (3 objects, matte surfaces). Grid method for proportion. Toned paper with white charcoal only.
Stretch: Self-portrait from life (mirror). Multi-viewpoint drawing (Cubist influence). Drawing with non-dominant hand, then refine.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Expressive Response

Same still life, 10 min: draw with emotion word prompt (fragile, aggressive, melancholic, joyful). Media free. Compare to observational version.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Skill Audit

Rate 1-5: Proportion accuracy, Tonal range, Line sensitivity, Composition, Media control. Set 2 targets for next lesson.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Accurate proportion, full tonal range, confident line, measured perspective
  • Creativity: Expressive quality in sustained drawing, inventive response
  • Understanding: Articulate sighting process, tonal logic, compositional reasoning
  • Presentation: Clean drawing, staged photos, clear annotations

Lesson 2: Colour Theory & Mixing Mastery

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Colour Matching Challenge

Teacher shows 5 mixed colours (one at a time). Students mix match in 2 minutes each. Discuss: which were hardest? Why? (Complements, neutrals, subtle temperatures).

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Systematic colour mixing:
  • Primary Systems: RYB (traditional), CMY (process), RGB (light). Pigment reality: no perfect primaries. Split primaries (warm/cool red, yellow, blue).
  • Mixing Charts: 12-hue wheel, value scales (tints/shades/tones), chroma scales, complementary pairs, neutral greys from complements.
  • Colour Relationships: Monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic, tetradic. Temperature (warm/cool) within hue.
  • Limited Palettes: Zorn (ivory black, vermilion, yellow ochre, titanium white), Velázquez, modern primaries. Benefits: harmony, learning, speed.

Required Practical: Colour Reference Portfolio

Create in sketchbook: Split-primary mixing chart (6x6 grid), 5 limited palette charts (5 swatches each), 3 mastercopy colour studies (small, 10x10cm) analysing artist's palette, personal "go-to" mixes page with recipes.

Common Misconception

"Black darkens colours"

Black deadens/cools colours. Darken with complement (red→green, blue→orange, yellow→purple) or darker analogue (blue+burnt umber). Black = colour killer.

Differentiation

Support: Pre-drawn chart templates. Limited to 3 mixing exercises. Colour matching with reference swatches.
Stretch: Mix 50 greys without black. Analyse a masterwork's palette — recreate exact 12-colour palette. Research pigment numbers (PB29, PR108, etc.) and lightfastness.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Mood Palette

Choose emotion. Mix 5-colour palette expressing it. Paint 5x5cm abstract swatches. Annotate: why these colours, temperatures, relationships?

Reflection (5 minutes)

Palette Swap

Exchange palettes with peer. Paint same mood with their colours. Discuss constraints/possibilities.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Accurate mixing, clean charts, understanding pigment behaviour
  • Creativity: Inventive limited palettes, expressive mood palettes
  • Understanding: Articulate colour theory, pigment knowledge, temperature control
  • Presentation: Precise charts, labelled recipes, organised reference

Lesson 3: Paint Handling & Techniques

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Technique Carousel

4 stations (5 min each): Glazing (thin transparent layers), Impasto (thick palette knife), Scumbling (dry brush over dry), Wet-in-wet (alla prima). Rotate. Note characteristics.

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Paint application mastery:
  • Acrylic Techniques: Glazing (gloss medium + 10% paint), impasto (heavy body + modelling paste), scumbling (dry brush, little paint), staining (thin on raw canvas), pouring (fluid acrylics), sgraffito (scratch through wet).
  • Oil Techniques (if used): Fat over lean rule, glazing (stand oil/turps), impasto, scumbling, alla prima (wet-in-wet single session), grisaille underpainting.
  • Watercolour/Gouache: Wet-on-wet, wet-on-dry, glazing, lifting, dry brush, salt/alcohol textures, gouache opacity/rewetting.
  • Brushwork: Load, pressure, direction, speed, edge control (hard/soft/lost). Brush as extension of arm.

Required Practical: Technique Sampler

A2 sheet divided into 12 sections (15x15cm each). Each demonstrates one technique with annotation: medium, tool, ratio, drying time, best use. Include: 3 glazes, 2 impasto, scumbling, wet-in-wet, staining, sgraffito, dry brush, experimental (student invented).

Common Misconception

"Thick paint = expressive, thin paint = tight"

Expression comes from INTENTIONAL mark-making at any thickness. A single precise glaze can be more expressive than slathered impasto if it serves the image. Technique serves idea.

Differentiation

Support: Focus on 3 core techniques (glaze, impasto, scumbling). Step-by-step cards. Larger sampler sections.
Stretch: Invent a new technique. Combine 3+ in one 20x20cm study. Research historical techniques (tempera, fresco, encaustic) and test modern equivalents.

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Technique Fusion

Small study (15x15cm) combining minimum 3 techniques to render a simple form (sphere, cube, fruit). Plan sequence: what dries first?

Reflection (5 minutes)

Technique Journal

Note: Which technique felt most natural? Which surprised you? Which will you use in final work?

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Control of each technique, understanding drying/mechanical properties
  • Creativity: Inventive combinations, personal adaptations
  • Understanding: Clear annotations, technical vocabulary, sequence logic
  • Presentation: Clean sampler, consistent section size, legible notes

Lesson 4: Figure & Portrait - Structure & Expression

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (10 minutes)

Anatomy Quick Studies

Photo reference or model. 5 x 2-min sketches: skeleton landmarks, muscle groups, planes of head, hand structure, foot structure. Anatomical accuracy over beauty.

Skill Development (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Figure/portrait fundamentals:
  • Structure First: Block in big shapes (ribcage, pelvis, skull). Centre lines. Joint alignments. Proportions (head units).
  • Planes of the Head: Loomis/Reilly method. Light logic on planes. Features sit ON planes, not floating.
  • Paint Application: Block average colours per plane. Refine edges. Temperature shifts (warm light/cool shadow or vice versa). Avoid "colouring in" features.
  • Expression: Gesture before likeness. Weight, balance, tension. Eyes: not symbols — spheres in sockets with lids.

Required Practical: Figure/Portrait Study

3-hour study (can span sessions): Clothed figure or portrait from life/photo. A2. Chosen paint media. Demonstrate: structural blocking, plane analysis, colour temperature, edge variety. Process photos at block-in, mid, final. Annotate structural decisions.

Common Misconception

"Paint the eyes first — they're most important"

Eyes LAST. Structure first (skull, planes, light logic). Eyes are small details on a complex form. Paint the socket, lid, brow — the eye emerges.

Differentiation

Support: Pre-drawn structural template. Photo reference with grid. Limited palette (Zorn). Focus on head only.
Stretch: Full figure in environment. Multiple figures. Narrative composition. Experimental paint handling (distortion, abstraction).

Creative Work (10 minutes)

Expressive Portrait

10-min portrait: same model, but paint HOW THEY FEEL (tired, anxious, confident) not just how they look. Colour, mark, distortion serve expression.

Reflection (5 minutes)

Likeness vs Life

Discuss: Can a portrait be "accurate" but dead? Can a "wrong" portrait feel more true? What makes presence?

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Structural accuracy, plane sensitivity, colour temperature, edge control
  • Creativity: Expressive presence, not just likeness
  • Understanding: Anatomical awareness, light logic, paint logic
  • Presentation: Resolved study, process documentation, reflective annotation

Lesson 5: Personal Painterly Language

Duration: 60 minutes

Warm-up (5 minutes)

Artist DNA

List 3 painters whose work you love. For each: 3 words describing their paint handling. What do they share? That's your starting DNA.

Skill Development (20 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Developing personal voice:
  • Synthesis: Combine influences — not copy. "I like Richter's blur + Freud's flesh + Twombly's gesture." How do they coexist?
  • Constraints Breed Style: Self-imposed limits: 3 colours, 1 brush, 30 minutes, palette knife only, non-dominant hand.
  • Series Work: Same subject, 6 ways. Different grounds, tools, palettes, speeds. Patterns emerge = your language.
  • Reflective Practice: After each painting: "What did the paint DO? What did I WANT it to do? Gap = next experiment."

Required Practical: Language Series

Same simple subject (apple, cup, corner of room) painted 4 ways (A5 each): 1. Observational/traditional 2. Expressive/gestural 3. Abstracted/colour-focused 4. Experimental/process-led. 30 min each. Annotate each: intention, discovery, rejection, keeper.

Creative Work (25 minutes)

Language Development

Begin a sustained painting (can continue in own time) using emerging personal language. Scale A3+. Document decisions.

Reflection (10 minutes)

Peer Language Review

In 3s: Present language series. Peers identify: "Your language seems to be... [words]" "I see [influence] in..." "Try pushing..." Student notes feedback.

Assessment Criteria

  • Technical Skill: Competent execution across approaches, media control
  • Creativity: Distinctive emerging voice, risk-taking in experimental piece
  • Understanding: Articulate influences, conscious choices, self-awareness
  • Presentation: Series presented cohesively, insightful annotations

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete sustained painting from Lesson 5
  • Daily 15-min painting sketch (A6) for 2 weeks — build habit, discover language
  • Mastercopy: 2-hour copy of a painting you admire. Focus on PROCESS not result. Annotate every decision.
  • Colour diary: Photograph one interesting colour relationship daily (shadow on wall, fruit bowl, street scene). Annotate.
  • Research one contemporary painter's process (studio visit video, interview). Create analysis page.
