Lesson 2: Core Concepts: victorian novel conventions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on victorian novel conventions. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Effect on Structure: Serialisation required cliffhangers at chapter ends. Each instalment needed a hook to make readers buy the next. This creates episodic, suspenseful pacing.
Moral Purpose: Victorian novels often had explicit moral messages. Dickens used fiction to campaign for social reform. Literature was seen as a way to educate and improve society.
Victorian Values: Novels promoted hard work, charity, self-improvement, and Christian morality. Characters who ignore these values face consequences.
AO2 Conventions Strategy: When analysing a 19th century novel, AO2 (language, form, structure) typically carries 33% of marks. The highest-achieving responses don't just identify conventions — they explain why the writer chose them and what effects they create. Examiners reward analysis of how conventions shape meaning, not labelling of features. For Grade 9, show how writers use, subvert, or combine conventions deliberately to create specific effects on the reader.

Lesson 3: Application: victorian novel conventions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Effect on Structure, Moral Purpose, Victorian Values. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: How did serialised publication affect Victorian novel structure?

Answer: Serialised publication required cliffhangers to maintain reader interest between instalments. This created episodic structure with regular moments of tension. Chapters often end mid-action or with revelations.

Q2: Why did Victorian novels often have moral purposes?

Answer: Victorians saw literature as educational. Novels were expected to teach moral lessons and promote social reform. Authors like Dickens used fiction to campaign against poverty and injustice.

Q3: What are the advantages of first-person narration?

Answer: First-person narration creates intimacy with readers, shows events from a character's perspective, allows for unreliable narration and dramatic irony, and lets characters reflect on their own development.

Q4: How does "Jekyll and Hyde" combine realism and Gothic?

Answer: "Jekyll and Hyde" is set in realistic London with accurate social details (lawyers, servants, streets). But it includes Gothic elements: transformation, horror, duality, secrets. The realistic setting makes the Gothic more unsettling.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 30 marks: How does Stevenson use the conventions of Victorian novel writing to explore the theme of duality in "The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde"? Write about: Stevenson's use of narrative structure; Stevenson's use of Gothic conventions; how Stevenson uses language to present duality. <div class="

Stevenson exploits the Victorian convention of the framed narrative — multiple perspectives gradually revealing truth — to embody the theme of duality itself. The novel's structure is split: Utterson's rational, external perspective gives way to Lanyon's horrified witness account, which finally yields to Jekyll's own confession. This structural fragmentation mirrors the divided self at the novel's heart; just as Jekyll's identity is split across two figures, the narrative is split across multiple narrators, each holding only a partial truth. The reader, like Utterson, must piece together the whole from fragments — experiencing the same struggle for understanding that the duality creates. Stevenson simultaneously uses and subverts Gothic conventions. The foggy London setting and mysterious door are quintessential urban Gothic, creating the atmosphere of dread that Ann Radcliffe defined as terror — "nothing is so oppressive to the imagination." Yet Stevenson subverts Gothic's typical clear moral categories. In Walpole's tradition, good and evil are separate forces; Stevenson collapses them into one body.
