Lesson 2: Core Concepts: shakespeare extract analysis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on shakespeare extract analysis. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Point: You MUST discuss BOTH the extract AND the whole play. Ignoring either will cost you marks.
The Bridge Method: After analysing the extract, add a paragraph that connects to the wider play. Use phrases like: "This moment echoes..." "Earlier in the play..." "This contrasts with..." "Later, we see..."
Extract Analysis Strategy: Spend 5 minutes reading and annotating before writing. Identify 3-4 key quotations to analyse in depth. Roughly 2/3 of your essay should analyse the extract; 1/3 should link to the whole play. Lesson 3: Application: shakespeare extract analysis

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Key Point, The Bridge Method, Extract Analysis Strategy. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What should you look for in a close reading of an extract?

Answer: Look for: key quotations rich in language techniques; structural features (verse/prose, soliloquy); effects on audience; connections to themes and whole play.

Q2: How do you embed a quotation effectively?

Answer: Embed by integrating the quotation into your sentence structure. Instead of "He says 'quote'. This shows...", write "Macbeth's use of 'quote' reveals..." - make the quotation part of your analysis.

Q3: What's the difference between extract analysis and whole-play linking?

Answer: Extract analysis focuses on the specific lines given: word choice, imagery, structure within that passage. Whole-play linking connects to scenes before/after, tracking character development and themes across the entire play.

Q4: How much time should you spend on the extract vs whole play?

Answer: For a 30-mark question: roughly 20 marks for extract analysis (2/3 of essay), 10 marks for whole-play links (1/3 of essay). Time: 20 mins extract, 10 mins whole play.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 30 marks: Starting with this extract from Act 2 Scene 1, explore how Shakespeare presents Macbeth's state of mind before the murder of Duncan. <div class="

Shakespeare presents Macbeth's pre-murder state of mind as a battleground between ambition and conscience, with guilt already fracturing his perception. The interrogative "Is this a dagger which I see before me, the handle toward my hand?" opens with uncertainty — the question reveals a mind unable to distinguish reality from projection, and the detail "the handle toward my hand" suggests the dagger invites him, as if fate itself is beckoning. The oxymoron "dagger of the mind" crystallises this tension: it is simultaneously a weapon (suggesting murderous intent) and a mental fabrication (suggesting psychological torment). The preposition "of" fuses the two — Macbeth's mind has become a weapon against itself. The imagery of "wicked dreams abuse the curtain'd sleep" associates the forthcoming murder with both sorcery and violation — the adjective "wicked" and verb "abuse" frame regicide as a transgressive act that corrupts even the sanctuary of sleep. This foreshadows Macbeth's later recognition that he has "murdered sleep," a structural echo that deepens the meaning: what begins as a hallucinated dagger becomes a permanent psychological wound.
