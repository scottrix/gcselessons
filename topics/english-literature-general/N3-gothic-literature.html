Lesson 2: Core Concepts: gothic literature

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on gothic literature. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Gothic Definition: A genre that combines horror, death, romance and the supernatural. It explores what lies beneath the surface of civilization - our fears, desires and secrets.
Scientific Supernatural: Victorian Gothic often uses science to explain supernatural events. This reflects Victorian anxiety about science going too far - creating horrors through knowledge.
The Secret as Society: Gothic secrets reflect Victorian society's secrets - what respectable families hid behind closed doors. The secret reveals the hypocrisy beneath the surface.
Gothic Analysis Strategy: When writing about Gothic elements, the highest marks come from treating Gothic conventions as meaningful choices, not decorative features. Every Gothic element has an intellectual purpose: to explore anxiety, reveal repression, or challenge rationalism. Examiners reward responses that explain what Gothic conventions allow writers to say that realism alone cannot. For Grade 9, show how Gothic conventions function as a language of the unconscious — expressing fears and desires that Victorian society refused to acknowledge openly.

Lesson 3: Application: gothic literature

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Gothic Definition, Scientific Supernatural, The Secret as Society. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Why were Victorians fascinated by Gothic literature despite their scientific progress?

Answer: Victorian repression forced desires and fears underground. Gothic expressed what couldn't be spoken about openly: fears about science, degeneration, hidden desires. It explored the darker side of human nature that respectable society denied.

Q2: How does Stevenson create terror before horror in "Jekyll and Hyde"?

Answer: Stevenson creates terror through atmosphere: the fog, the mysterious door, Utterson's growing suspicion. We sense danger before seeing it. The horror comes with Hyde's violent acts (Carew's murder). The delay makes the horror more shocking.

Q3: What does the duality of Jekyll and Hyde reveal about Victorian society?

Answer: The duality reveals Victorian hypocrisy. Society demanded respectability but people had hidden desires. Jekyll creates Hyde to enjoy forbidden pleasures while staying respectable. The novel shows this division is unsustainable - the hidden self will emerge.

Q4: How does Stevenson transform London into a Gothic setting?

Answer: London becomes Gothic through fog (obscuring truth), maze-like streets (losing one's way), the contrast between respectable facades and dark alleys, and the hidden door. The modern city becomes as threatening as any Gothic castle.

Error Review

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 30 marks: How does Stevenson use Gothic conventions to explore Victorian fears in "The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde"? Write about: Stevenson's use of setting; Stevenson's use of the doppelganger; how Stevenson uses terror and horror. <div class="

Stevenson transforms London into an urban Gothic landscape, subverting the genre's traditional castles and abbeys to suggest that modern civilisation itself is the site of horror. The "great chocolate-coloured pall" of fog that hangs over the city is not mere atmospheric decoration — it is the visible form of moral obscurity. In a society that prized transparency and respectability, fog becomes a metaphor for everything Victorians could not see about themselves: the desires, violences, and doubleness hidden behind every closed door and drawn curtain. When Utterson sits "treading the measure" of his own fears, the reader is placed in the same position — sensing danger in the familiar, which is precisely the urban Gothic's most unsettling trick. The doppelganger convention is Stevenson's most powerful Gothic device, but he radicalises it. In traditional Gothic from Walpole to Poe, the double is a separate being — a external threat to be defeated. Stevenson makes Jekyll and Hyde the same person, which shifts the horror from outside to inside.
