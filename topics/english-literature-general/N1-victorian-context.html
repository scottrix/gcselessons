Lesson 2: Core Concepts: victorian context

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on victorian context. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Class in "Jekyll and Hyde": Jekyll is respectable middle-class professional; Hyde represents the underclass he secretly joins. Class boundaries are both rigid and permeable.
Effect on Structure: Dickens's novels have cliffhangers at chapter ends because they were originally published in parts. Each installment needed to keep readers hooked.
AO3 Context Strategy: For the 19th century novel question, AO3 is worth 25% of marks (typically 8 out of 30 marks). Spend roughly 5-7 minutes of a 45-minute response on embedding context. Never bolt context on at the end — weave it into your argument. Examiners reward contextual points that illuminate the text, not historical facts listed for their own sake. A Grade 9 response treats context as the lens through which the text's meaning becomes clear, not a separate paragraph tagged on as an afterthought.

Lesson 3: Application: victorian context

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Class in &quot;Jekyll and Hyde&quot;, Effect on Structure, AO3 Context Strategy. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: How did the Industrial Revolution affect Victorian literature?

Answer: Industrial Revolution created urban poverty, class division, and working conditions that novelists critiqued. Dickens exposed poverty; Shelley questioned science; Stevenson explored repression.

Q2: What was the Victorian "crisis of faith"?

Answer: Darwin's evolution theory challenged Biblical creation. Geology showed earth was older than Bible suggested. Science undermined religious certainty, creating doubt and debate.

Q3: Why were Victorians fascinated by Gothic horror?

Answer: Despite scientific progress, Victorians feared what progress might unleash. Gothic expressed anxieties about science, degeneration, and the dark side of respectability. Hyde represents primitive urges beneath civilized surface.

Q4: How did serialised publication affect novel structure?

Answer: Serialised novels needed cliffhangers to keep readers buying installments. Each chapter had to hook readers for the next. This created suspenseful, episodic structure.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 30 marks: How does Dickens use the character of Scrooge to comment on Victorian attitudes to poverty? Write about: how Dickens presents Scrooge's views; how Dickens uses Scrooge to criticise Victorian society; the context of poverty in Victorian England. <div class="

Dickens constructs Scrooge as a personification of Malthusian economic thinking — the Victorian belief that poverty was inevitable and even necessary. When Scrooge declares that the poor should go to "the treadmill and the Poor Law," he echoes the language of the 1834 Poor Law Amendment Act, which made workhouse conditions deliberately harsh to deter reliance on state support. Dickens's contemporary readers would have recognised this as the dominant attitude of the middle classes, making Scrooge not an aberration but a representative figure. The specificity of Dickens's reference — naming actual legislation — transforms Scrooge from miserly caricature into political critique. However, Dickens does not merely condemn; he shows how this ideology dehumanises its adherent. Scrooge's refusal to donate to charity is presented through the metaphor of cold: he is "hard and sharp as flint" and carries his "own low temperature" with him. This pathological coldness suggests that Victorian capitalist values do not merely harm the poor — they corrupt the wealthy themselves, stripping them of empathy and connection.
