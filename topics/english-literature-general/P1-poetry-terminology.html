Lesson 2: Core Concepts: poetry terminology

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on poetry terminology. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Why write a sonnet?: Traditionally about love. Using sonnet form signals: this is serious, this is about love, this connects to poetic tradition.
Golden Rule: Naming a technique scores zero marks. You must explain its EFFECT.
Top Tip: In poetry analysis questions, AO2 (language, form, structure) typically carries 30-36 marks. Name the technique, quote precisely, then explain the effect — this three-step sequence is the minimum for each analytical point. Aim for 3-4 developed technique discussions in a 30-mark response.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: What is enjambment and what effect can it create?

Answer: Enjambment is when a sentence runs over the end of a line without punctuation. Effects: creates forward momentum, links ideas, reflects continuous action or thought, sounds natural and conversational.

Lesson 3: Application: poetry terminology

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Why write a sonnet?, Golden Rule, Top Tip. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What is enjambment and what effect can it create?

Answer: Enjambment is when a sentence runs over the end of a line without punctuation. Effects: creates forward momentum, links ideas, reflects continuous action or thought, sounds natural and conversational.

Q2: What's the difference between a caesura and an end-stopped line?

Answer: Caesura is a pause WITHIN a line (usually with punctuation). End-stopped lines have punctuation AT the end. Caesura slows within the line; end-stopped lines complete the thought before the next.

Q3: What are the features of a Shakespearean sonnet?

Answer: Shakespearean sonnet: 14 lines, iambic pentameter, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG rhyme scheme. Three quatrains present problem; final couplet provides resolution or twist.

Q4: What is the effect of sibilance?

Answer: Sibilance (repeated 's' sounds) can create: soft, seductive effect; sinister, snake-like quality; whispering or hushing sound; emphasis on certain words through the hissing sound.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: poetry terminology

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on poetry terminology from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often name a technique without explaining its effect. Wrong: "The poet uses enjambment on line 5." Correct: "The enjambment on line 5 creates a sense of forward momentum, reflecting the speaker's restless energy." Students often confuse simile and metaphor. Wrong: "This is a metaphor because it compares two things using 'like'." Correct: "A simile uses 'like' or 'as' to compare; a metaphor states the comparison directly without those words." Students often describe what a technique is instead of what it does. Wrong: "Iambic pentameter is five iambs per line." Correct: "The iambic pentameter mirrors a heartbeat, reinforcing the poem's theme of intimate, living emotion."
