Lesson 2: Core Concepts: shakespearean dramatic techniques

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on shakespearean dramatic techniques. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Audience Complicity: Dramatic irony makes the audience feel involved - we know secrets, we anticipate outcomes, we're emotionally invested.
Why Off-Stage?: Some violence too extreme for stage; imagination more powerful; focuses on psychological aftermath rather than physical act.
Banquo as Foil: He shows what Macbeth could have been - the alternative path. His murder marks Macbeth's descent into true tyranny.
Dramatic Techniques Strategy: Focus on what the AUDIENCE experiences. Ask: "What does the audience know that characters don't? What can the audience see that characters can't? How does Shakespeare control what we feel and when?" Analyse dramatic irony, soliloquies, entrances/exits, and on-stage vs off-stage action. Always link technique to audience effect.

Lesson 3: Application: shakespearean dramatic techniques

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Audience Complicity, Why Off-Stage?, Banquo as Foil. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What is dramatic irony and give an example from "Macbeth".

Answer: Dramatic irony is when the audience knows more than characters. Example: Duncan praises Macbeth's loyalty while we know he plans murder. Creates tension and makes audience complicit.

Q2: How does Shakespeare use the supernatural to create tension?

Answer: Witches establish evil from the opening; prophecies drive plot; ambiguity creates irony; ghost and hallucinations show psychological horror. Supernatural creates atmosphere and motivates action.

Q3: What is the function of Banquo as a foil to Macbeth?

Answer: Banquo contrasts Macbeth - both receive prophecies, but Banquo resists temptation. He shows what Macbeth could have been. His murder marks Macbeth's descent into tyranny.

Q4: Why does Shakespeare have Duncan's murder happen off-stage?

Answer: Off-stage murder lets audience imagine worst; focuses on psychological aftermath not gore; shows consequence through characters' reactions; some violence too extreme for Elizabethan stage.

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 25 marks: "How does Shakespeare use dramatic irony to engage the audience in Macbeth?" <div class="

Shakespeare weaponises dramatic irony throughout Macbeth to position the audience as complicit witnesses, creating an engagement that is both intellectual and viscerally uncomfortable. When Duncan arrives at Inverness and praises its "pleasant seat," the irony operates on multiple levels: we know Macbeth plans regicide, making Duncan's trust painfully misguided; for a Jacobean audience who believed in the Divine Right of Kings, the irony carries spiritual weight — Duncan praises the very man who will violate God's order. The effect is not merely suspense but moral discomfort: the audience is forced to watch without intervening, becoming passively complicit in the unfolding atrocity. The witches' prophecies generate sustained dramatic irony through ambiguity — the audience gradually perceives the double meanings ("none of woman born shall harm Macbeth") that Macbeth blindly accepts at face value, positioning us as interpreters who understand what the tragic hero cannot. This creates dramatic tension that is simultaneously intellectual (we piece together the truth) and emotional (we foresee destruction we cannot prevent).
