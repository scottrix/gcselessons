Always Write About Effect on Audience: Identifying a dramatic technique earns minimal marks. Explaining its effect on the audience is where the marks are. Every time you mention lighting, sound, stage directions, or structure, you must answer: What does the audience see, hear, or feel? How does this shape their understanding of character or theme?

Lesson 3: Application: modern dramatic techniques

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: The Doorbell in &quot;An Inspector Calls&quot;, &quot;An Inspector Calls&quot;, Always Write About Effect on Audience. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: What is the effect of the single setting in "An Inspector Calls"?

Answer: Single setting (dining room) creates claustrophobia - characters cannot physically escape the Inspector. It symbolises their trapped, privileged world. Unity of place intensifies the interrogation and prevents evasion.

Q2: How does Priestley use dramatic irony with Birling's speeches?

Answer: Birling makes confident predictions ("Titanic is unsinkable", "no war") that 1945 audiences know are false. This undermines his authority, shows capitalist complacency as foolish, and positions audience as superior witnesses.

Q3: What is real time structure and why does it matter?

Answer: Real time means performance time matches story time. It creates urgency, intensity, and makes audience experience events simultaneously with characters. In "An Inspector Calls", it traps everyone in the moment.

Q4: How does the Inspector control dialogue?

Answer: The Inspector uses questions to control, interrupts to dominate, uses short declarative sentences for authority, and employs silence to force confession. His dialogue style shows his power over the family.

Error Review

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response 30 marks: How does Priestley use dramatic techniques to present the Inspector's power in "An Inspector Calls"? <div class="

Priestley constructs the Inspector as an unstoppable theatrical force through cumulative dramatic techniques that leave the Birlings — and the audience — with no refuge. The lighting shift from "pink and intimate" to "brighter and harder" at his arrival immediately reconfigures the stage from safe domestic space to interrogation room: the audience, like the Birlings, is stripped of comfort and forced into the harsh light of scrutiny. The Inspector's dialogue is Priestley's most precise instrument of power. He controls every exchange through questions — "I'm asking questions" — cutting through Birling's blustering interruptions with short, declarative sentences that demand compliance. When Birling attempts to assert authority by name-dropping "I was an alderman for years," the Inspector simply continues his questioning, theatrically rendering social status irrelevant. On stage, the audience watches the family's hierarchy dissolve as the working-class Inspector commands the room. The real-time structure intensifies this power.
