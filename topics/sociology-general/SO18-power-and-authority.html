Lesson 2: Core Concepts: power, authority & the state

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on power, authority & the state. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Power: the ability to make others do what you want. Lukes identified three dimensions: decision-making, agenda-setting, and ideological power.
Key Fact: Weber's three types of authority: Traditional (custom - monarchy), Charismatic (personal qualities - MLK), Rational-legal (rules and laws - elected officials). Most modern authority is rational-legal.
Key Fact: The state: institutions that govern (government, parliament, police, courts). Marxists: the state serves ruling class interests. Functionalists: the state serves collective interests. Pluralists: the state balances competing interests.
Key Fact: Power elites: Mills argued power is concentrated in a small group of corporate leaders, military chiefs and politicians. In the UK, the 'establishment' (Eton, Oxbridge) dominates politics, media and business.
Key Fact: Democracy and power: formal equality through elections but real power is unequal: wealthy fund parties, media owners shape opinion, corporations influence policy, and voter turnout has declined.
Sociology Exam Tips: When discussing power, always: distinguish power from authority, use Weber's typology, and evaluate whether power is democratically distributed.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe Weber's three types of authority and evaluate their usefulness.

Answer: Traditional: custom (monarchy). Charismatic: personality (MLK). Rational-legal: rules (elected officials). Evaluation: useful but some leaders combine types. Charismatic brings change but is unstable; rational-legal is stable but may resist change.

Lesson 3: Application: power, authority & the state

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Sociology Exam Tips, UK Context, UK Context. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe Weber's three types of authority and evaluate their usefulness.

Answer: Traditional: custom (monarchy). Charismatic: personality (MLK). Rational-legal: rules (elected officials). Evaluation: useful but some leaders combine types. Charismatic brings change but is unstable; rational-legal is stable but may resist change.

Q2: Compare Marxist and Functionalist views of the state.

Answer: Marxist: state serves ruling class (protects property, maintains capitalist order). Functionalist: state serves everyone (maintains order, provides services). Evidence supports Marxist view but the state also provides welfare that benefits the working class.

Q3: Evaluate whether democracy ensures that power is equally distributed.

Answer: Democracy gives formal equality (one vote) but real power is unequal: wealth funds parties, media shapes opinion, lobbyists influence policy. Marxists: democracy is a sham. Pluralists: democracy works through competing groups. Conclusion: some distribution but significant inequalities remain.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: power, authority & the state

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the view that power in modern Britain is held by a small elite rather than democratically distributed. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: present elite theory evidence (Mills, establishment networks); present democratic counter-evidence (suffrage, free press, unions); evaluate: formal democracy exists but is undermined by economic inequality; conclude: real power is disproportionately held by a small elite. Reform could make democracy more genuine.

Exam Tips: Weber's 3 types: Traditional (custom), Charismatic (personality), Rational-legal (rules). | Marxist: state = tool of ruling class. Functionalist: state = serves everyone. | Democracy = formal equality but REAL power is unequal (money, media, lobbying).
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: In a democracy, power is equally distributed because everyone has one vote. Correct: While everyone has one vote, real political power is very unequal: wealthy donors, media owners, corporate lobbyists, and establishment networks dominate. Lukes' third dimension shows the most powerful influence is shaping what people WANT. Voting equality is necessary but not sufficient for genuine democratic power.
