Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the role of education

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the role of education. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Functionalist view: education serves vital functions for society. Durkheim: education transmits society's norms and values (social solidarity) and teaches specialist skills for the division of labour. Parsons: education is the main agent of secondary socialisation and a bridge between family and wider society; it promotes meritocracy (everyone has equal opportunity to succeed based on ability and effort).
Key Fact: Marxist view: education serves capitalism. Althusser: education is an Ideological State Apparatus (ISA) that transmits ruling class ideology, making inequality seem natural and inevitable. Bowles and Gintis: the correspondence principle - schools mirror the workplace through the hidden curriculum (hierarchy, competition, obedience to authority, punctuality) preparing workers for capitalist exploitation.
Key Fact: Feminist view: education is patriarchal. Historically: girls were steered towards domestic subjects and away from science/maths. Kelly: the curriculum is male-dominated (textbooks feature men, examples from male experiences). Modern challenge: despite girls outperforming boys, women still face a glass ceiling in careers and are underrepresented in STEM subjects.
Key Fact: The hidden curriculum: the unwritten, unofficial lessons that schools teach - obedience to authority, competition, conformity, acceptance of hierarchy, punctuality. Functionalists see this as positive (teaches social norms); Marxists see it as negative (prepares workers for exploitation).
Key Fact: Vocational education and training: provides work-based skills. Functionalists argue it meets the economy's need for skilled workers. Marxists argue it creates a compliant, low-paid workforce and reproduces class inequality (working-class students are steered towards vocational courses, middle-class towards academic).
Sociology Exam Tips: When evaluating the role of education, always compare perspectives, use named sociologists and concepts, and provide evidence for whether education serves all groups equally or reproduces inequality.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Compare Functionalist and Marxist views of the role of education.

Answer: Functionalism: education transmits norms (Durkheim), allocates roles by merit (Parsons), provides social mobility. Marxism: education transmits ruling class ideology (Althusser), corresponds to workplace hierarchy (Bowles & Gintis), reproduces class inequality. Key difference: Functionalism sees education serving everyone; Marxism sees it serving the ruling class.

Lesson 3: Application: the role of education

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Sociology Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Compare Functionalist and Marxist views of the role of education.

Answer: Functionalism: education transmits norms (Durkheim), allocates roles by merit (Parsons), provides social mobility. Marxism: education transmits ruling class ideology (Althusser), corresponds to workplace hierarchy (Bowles & Gintis), reproduces class inequality. Key difference: Functionalism sees education serving everyone; Marxism sees it serving the ruling class.

Q2: Explain what the hidden curriculum is and evaluate its importance in education.

Answer: The hidden curriculum teaches: obedience (follow rules without questioning), hierarchy (teachers have authority over pupils), competition (grades, rankings), punctuality and attendance. Functionalists: this socialises young people for society. Marxists: this produces compliant workers for capitalism. Evaluation: the hidden curriculum exists but its effects vary - some students resist and question authority (e.g. student protests).

Q3: Evaluate whether the education system is meritocratic.

Answer: Meritocracy means equal opportunity based on ability and effort. Evidence for: comprehensive schools aim to give all children the same education; exam results are (mostly) blind-marked. Evidence against: private schools give huge advantages (7% of pupils, 74% of judges); cultural capital (Bourdieu) gives middle-class children an advantage; streaming and labelling disadvantage working-class and minority ethnic students. Conclusion: the education system CLAIMS to be meritocratic but structural inequalities undermine this claim.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: the role of education

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the view that the education system serves the interests of the ruling class. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: present Marxist evidence (Althusser's ISA, Bowles & Gintis' correspondence principle, hidden curriculum, cultural capital); present Functionalist counter-evidence (education provides social solidarity and skills, enables some social mobility); evaluate using evidence on class inequality in achievement (working-class underachievement); conclude: the education system does serve ruling class interests by reproducing class inequality, but it also provides genuine opportunities for some working-class students. The system is not purely ideological - it has dual functions: maintaining inequality AND enabling some social mobility. The question is whether the balance favours reproduction or mobility.

Exam Tips: Functionalism: education = SOCIAL SOLIDARITY + MERITOCRACY. Marxism: education = IDEOLOGICAL CONTROL + CORRESPONDENCE PRINCIPLE. | Hidden curriculum: obedience + hierarchy + competition + punctuality. Functionalists say POSITIVE. Marxists say NEGATIVE. | Meritocracy: claimed but NOT fully achieved - class, gender and ethnicity affect outcomes.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: The education system gives everyone an equal chance to succeed regardless of their background. Correct: While the education system claims to be meritocratic, evidence shows significant inequalities: private school pupils are disproportionately represented in top universities and professions; middle-class children benefit from cultural capital (Bourdieu); working-class and some ethnic minority students are disadvantaged by streaming and labelling; and the hidden curriculum reproduces class, gender and ethnic inequalities. Equal opportunity in principle does not mean equal opportunity in practice.
