Lesson 2: Core Concepts: research design & ethics

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on research design & ethics. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Sampling: random (equal chance, unbiased but hard), systematic (every nth, simple but may bias), stratified (proportional, representative but complex), opportunity (available, quick but biased), volunteer (self-selected, motivated but unrepresentative).
Key Fact: Reliability: consistency - repeat study, same results. Improved by: standardised procedures, clear operationalisation. Validity: accuracy - measures what it claims. Improved by: ecological validity, triangulation.
Key Fact: Operationalisation: defining concepts precisely so they can be measured. Example: 'social class' must be operationalised as specific categories. Good operationalisation improves both reliability and validity.
Key Fact: Ethical principles (BSA): informed consent, confidentiality, right to withdraw, protection from harm, avoidance of deception, sensitivity to participants' values and culture.
Key Fact: Positivists prefer quantitative methods (reliability). Interpretivists prefer qualitative methods (validity). Both are important: reliable but invalid data is consistently wrong; valid but unreliable is accidentally right.
Sociology Exam Tips: When designing research, always: choose methods appropriate to the question, address reliability and validity, and consider ethics before starting.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe different sampling methods and evaluate when each would be most appropriate.

Answer: Random: most representative but hard. Stratified: ensures diversity. Opportunity: quick but biased. Choice depends on: resources, need for representativeness, access.

Lesson 3: Application: research design & ethics

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Sociology Exam Tips, UK Context, UK Context. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe different sampling methods and evaluate when each would be most appropriate.

Answer: Random: most representative but hard. Stratified: ensures diversity. Opportunity: quick but biased. Choice depends on: resources, need for representativeness, access.

Q2: Explain the difference between reliability and validity.

Answer: Reliability = consistency (repeat, same results). Validity = accuracy (measures what it claims). Both needed: reliable but invalid = consistently wrong; valid but unreliable = accidentally right.

Q3: A sociologist wants to study domestic violence. Evaluate the ethical issues.

Answer: Ethical issues: informed consent (victims may be too distressed), confidentiality (identifying abusers risks victims), protection from harm (discussing abuse may re-traumatise), right to withdraw, sensitivity.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: research design & ethics

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the importance of ethical guidelines in sociological research. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: describe BSA principles; argue they are essential (protect participants, maintain trust); argue they may be restrictive (some research needs deception); conclude: guidelines are essential but should be proportionate. Ethics committees provide appropriate oversight.

Exam Tips: Sampling: random (unbiased, hard) vs opportunity (easy, biased). | Reliability = CONSISTENCY. Validity = ACCURACY. Both needed. | BSA Ethics: consent + confidentiality + protection + withdrawal + avoid deception + debrief.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: If participants give informed consent, there are no ethical issues. Correct: Informed consent is necessary but not sufficient: power imbalances can undermine genuine consent; unexpected issues may arise; confidentiality may be hard to guarantee. Ethical research requires ongoing reflection, not just a signed form.
