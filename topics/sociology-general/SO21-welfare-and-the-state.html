Lesson 2: Core Concepts: welfare, poverty & the state

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on welfare, poverty & the state. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The welfare state (1945): based on Beveridge Report (1942) which identified five 'giant evils': Want, Disease, Ignorance, Squalor, Idleness. Key services: NHS, state education, social security, council housing.
Key Fact: Universal vs means-tested benefits: universal (available to all - NHS, state education) vs means-tested (only for those below a certain income - Universal Credit). Means-testing targets resources but creates the poverty trap.
Key Fact: New Right critique (Murray): welfare creates a 'dependency culture' where people rely on benefits. The 'underclass' is trapped in welfare dependency. Evaluation: 68% of families in poverty have at least one working adult; most benefit claimants are there temporarily.
Key Fact: Marxist view: welfare maintains capitalism (reserve army of labour, legitimises the system, controls the poor through conditionality and sanctions).
Key Fact: Poverty: relative poverty (below 60% of median) affects 22%. The Poverty and Social Exclusion survey found 14 million people cannot afford basic necessities. Causes: structural (unemployment, low wages, housing costs) vs individual (New Right: laziness, poor choices).
Sociology Exam Tips: When discussing welfare and poverty, always: distinguish structural from individual explanations and use evidence (in-work poverty statistics).

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe the key features of the welfare state and evaluate different views on its role.

Answer: The welfare state provides NHS, education, social security, housing. Functionalists: safety net. Marxists: maintains capitalism. New Right: creates dependency. Feminists: supports women's caring role. Each perspective reveals different aspects.

Lesson 3: Application: welfare, poverty & the state

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Sociology Exam Tips, UK Context, UK Context. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe the key features of the welfare state and evaluate different views on its role.

Answer: The welfare state provides NHS, education, social security, housing. Functionalists: safety net. Marxists: maintains capitalism. New Right: creates dependency. Feminists: supports women's caring role. Each perspective reveals different aspects.

Q2: Evaluate the New Right view that welfare creates a dependency culture.

Answer: Murray: welfare removes incentive to work, creating an underclass. Evaluation: 68% of poor families have someone working (in-work poverty is the real problem); most benefit use is temporary; benefits are too low for comfortable dependency. The thesis stigmatises the poor.

Q3: Assess whether poverty is caused by structural factors or individual behaviour.

Answer: Structural: unemployment, low wages, inadequate benefits, housing costs. Individual: laziness, poor choices (New Right). Evidence: structural factors are far more significant - most poor people are in work or seeking work. Structural solutions (living wage, affordable housing) work better.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: welfare, poverty & the state

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the view that the welfare state has failed to reduce poverty in modern Britain. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: describe the welfare state; present evidence it HAS reduced poverty (prevents destitution, NHS improves health); present evidence it HASN'T (22% in relative poverty, in-work poverty growing); explain why: structural inequality; conclude: the welfare state has prevented absolute poverty but not eliminated relative poverty because it addresses symptoms not causes. A living wage and affordable housing would reduce poverty more effectively.

Exam Tips: Beveridge's 5 evils: Want, Disease, Ignorance, Squalor, Idleness. Welfare = NHS + education + social security + housing. | New Right: DEPENDENCY CULTURE (Murray). Counter: 68% of poor families have someone WORKING. | Poverty: STRUCTURAL (low wages, unemployment) > INDIVIDUAL (poor choices). Structural solutions work better.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: People are poor because they don't work hard enough and choose to rely on benefits. Correct: 68% of working-age families in poverty have at least one person in work. Poverty is primarily caused by low wages, high housing costs, inadequate benefits, and zero-hours contracts. The dependency narrative blames individuals while ignoring structural conditions. Most benefit claimants would prefer a living wage.
