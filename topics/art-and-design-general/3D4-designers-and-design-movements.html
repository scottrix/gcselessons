Lesson 2: Core Concepts: designers and design movements

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on designers and design movements. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Key movements: Bauhaus (form follows function, industrial production), Art Deco (geometric, luxurious), Memphis (playful, postmodern), De Stijl (abstract, primary colours).
Key Fact: Understanding design context helps you make informed choices — every design has cultural, social and technological influences.
Key Fact: Form follows function means the shape of an object should relate to its purpose — but this principle has been both followed and deliberately challenged.
Key Fact: Your research should INFORM your own design decisions — understand WHY designers made their choices before adapting their approaches.
Three-Dimensional Design Exam Tips: Name SPECIFIC designers, objects and buildings. Explain CONTEXT — what was happening in society. Analyse the FORM-FUNCTION relationship. Show how the movement's philosophy influences YOUR design choices, not just their visual style.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring designers and design movements. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to designers and design movements, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: designers and design movements

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Three-Dimensional Design Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring designers and design movements. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to designers and design movements, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with designers and design movements. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with designers and design movements. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: designers and design movements

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Research two 3D design movements and compare how they approach the relationship between form and function. Show how your research has informed your own design work. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: name specific designers and objects/spaces; analyse the cultural context of each movement; compare their approaches to form and function; show clear connections between researched movements and personal design decisions; use specialist vocabulary. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Design movements are just art history — they have nothing to do with making products or spaces. Correct: Design movements directly shaped how things are made and used. Bauhaus created the template for modern industrial design. Art Deco influenced architecture and consumer products. Memphis challenged the assumption that design must be serious. Understanding movements helps you make intentional design choices.
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):

  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of designers and design movements
  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in designers and design movements?"
