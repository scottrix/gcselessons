Lesson 2: Core Concepts: materials and manufacturing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on materials and manufacturing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Material categories: woods (hardwood, softwood, manufactured), metals (ferrous, non-ferrous), polymers (thermoplastic, thermoset), ceramics.
Key Fact: Manufacturing processes: subtractive (CNC, laser cutting), additive (3D printing), forming (vacuum forming, injection moulding).
Key Fact: Material properties determine suitability: strength, weight, flexibility, durability, cost, sustainability and aesthetic qualities.
Key Fact: Joining methods: permanent (welding, adhesive, rivets) and temporary (screws, bolts, nuts) — choose based on function and disassembly needs.
Three-Dimensional Design Exam Tips: Always justify material choices with SPECIFIC PROPERTIES. Know the difference between thermoplastic and thermoset. Understand which manufacturing process suits which quantity (one-off vs batch vs mass). Consider sustainability in material selection.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring materials and manufacturing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to materials and manufacturing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: materials and manufacturing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Three-Dimensional Design Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring materials and manufacturing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to materials and manufacturing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with materials and manufacturing. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with materials and manufacturing. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: materials and manufacturing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Select a product and analyse its material and manufacturing choices. Explain why each material was chosen and suggest more sustainable alternatives where possible. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify specific materials and their properties; explain which manufacturing process was used and why; suggest sustainable alternatives with rationale; use specialist vocabulary throughout; show understanding of how material choice affects function, aesthetics and sustainability. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: 3D printing can replace all other manufacturing methods. Correct: 3D printing is excellent for prototyping and complex geometries, but has limitations: slow for mass production, limited material range, and weaker structural properties than moulded or machined parts. Each manufacturing method has strengths — choose the RIGHT process for the task.
