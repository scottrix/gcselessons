Lesson 2: Core Concepts: textile designers and traditions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on textile designers and traditions. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Textile traditions carry deep cultural meaning — African wax prints, Indian block printing, Japanese shibori, Scandinavian knitting each tell stories of place and identity.
Key Fact: Contemporary textile designers draw on tradition while innovating — understanding history enriches your own creative decisions.
Key Fact: Sustainability and ethics in textiles: consider material sourcing, dye pollution, fast fashion, fair trade and waste reduction.
Key Fact: Your research should INSPIRE your own work — adapt traditional techniques for contemporary purposes, do not just reproduce them.
Textile Design Exam Tips: Always research the CULTURAL CONTEXT — who made this tradition and why? Understand the TECHNIQUE before adapting it. Show how you DEVELOPED from the tradition, not just copied it. Be culturally sensitive — acknowledge the tradition's origins.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring textile designers and traditions. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to textile designers and traditions, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Lesson 3: Application: textile designers and traditions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Textile Design Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring textile designers and traditions. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to textile designers and traditions, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with textile designers and traditions. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with textile designers and traditions. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: textile designers and traditions

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Research a textile tradition from a specific culture. Understand its history, techniques and cultural significance. Create a personal textile piece inspired by this research, adapting the tradition for your own contemporary purpose. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate thorough research into the textile tradition's cultural context; understand the technique and its significance; create a personal piece that ADAPTS rather than copies; annotate with specific connections between tradition and personal work. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Researching textile traditions just means copying traditional patterns into your work. Correct: AQA requires you to DEVELOP ideas informed by sources — understand the CULTURAL CONTEXT and TECHNIQUE behind a tradition, then use that understanding to inspire your OWN personal response. Copying without understanding scores poorly on AO1 and AO4.
