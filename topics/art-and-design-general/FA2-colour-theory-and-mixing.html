Lesson 2: Core Concepts: colour theory and mixing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on colour theory and mixing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The colour wheel organises hues: primary (red, yellow, blue), secondary (orange, green, purple), tertiary (red-orange, blue-green etc).
Key Fact: Complementary colours sit opposite on the wheel and create maximum contrast and visual vibration when placed together.
Key Fact: Colour has emotional and psychological impact — warm colours (reds, oranges) feel energetic; cool colours (blues, greens) feel calm.
Key Fact: Tints (add white), shades (add black) and tones (add grey) expand your palette beyond pure hues.
Fine Art Exam Tips: Demonstrate understanding of colour relationships, not just colour mixing. Show experiments with different colour schemes. Annotate swatches with colour theory language. Explain WHY you chose a particular palette, not just HOW you mixed it.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring colour theory and mixing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to colour theory and mixing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: colour theory and mixing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Fine Art Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring colour theory and mixing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to colour theory and mixing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with colour theory and mixing. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with colour theory and mixing. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: colour theory and mixing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Select an artwork that uses colour expressively. Analyse the colour relationships and explain how the artist creates mood and meaning through colour. How has this informed your own colour choices? <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify specific colour schemes (complementary, analogous, triadic); analyse the emotional impact of colour choices; reference colour theory terminology; compare colour use across different artists; make thoughtful connections to personal work. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: A shade is when you add white to a colour. Correct: A TINT is hue + white (lighter); a SHADE is hue + black (darker); a TONE is hue + grey (muted/desaturated). These are three distinct colour modifications, not the same thing.
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):

  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of colour theory and mixing
  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in colour theory and mixing?"
