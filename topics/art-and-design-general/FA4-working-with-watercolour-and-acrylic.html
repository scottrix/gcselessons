Lesson 2: Core Concepts: working with watercolour and acrylic

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on working with watercolour and acrylic. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Watercolour relies on the white of the paper for luminosity — work from light to dark, preserving highlights.
Key Fact: Acrylic is versatile: thin it for washes or apply thickly (impasto) for texture; it dries quickly so you can layer rapidly.
Key Fact: Wash techniques include flat wash (even tone), graded wash (light to dark), and wet-on-wet (blended, soft edges).
Key Fact: Glazing (thin transparent layers over dried paint) builds depth and richness in both watercolour and acrylic.
Fine Art Exam Tips: Show PROCESS, not just outcome — include test strips, colour wheels, wash experiments. Annotate with technique names and explanations. Compare the same subject rendered in different paint media.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring working with watercolour and acrylic. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to working with watercolour and acrylic, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: working with watercolour and acrylic

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Fine Art Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring working with watercolour and acrylic. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to working with watercolour and acrylic, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with working with watercolour and acrylic. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with working with watercolour and acrylic. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: working with watercolour and acrylic

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Create a series of paint experiments showing at least four different watercolour or acrylic techniques. Annotate each sample explaining the process, its effect and when you might use it. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate a wide RANGE of techniques; show technical control and understanding; annotate with precise specialist vocabulary; evaluate which techniques are most effective for different purposes; connect technique choices to artists studied. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Keep adding paint and scrubbing to get darker tones in watercolour. Correct: Watercolour's beauty is its transparency — build tone with thin GLAZES, not thick layers. Overworking creates muddy, dull areas. Plan highlights in advance — you cannot easily restore them once lost.
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):

  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of working with watercolour and acrylic
  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in working with watercolour and acrylic?"

Plenary (5 minutes)

Assessment Criteria
