Lesson 2: Core Concepts: camera controls and exposure

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on camera controls and exposure. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The exposure triangle: aperture (f-stop), shutter speed and ISO work together — changing one affects the others.
Key Fact: Aperture controls depth of field: wide aperture (f/1.8) = shallow DoF; narrow aperture (f/16) = deep DoF.
Key Fact: Shutter speed controls motion: fast (1/1000s) freezes action; slow (1/15s) creates blur and sense of movement.
Key Fact: ISO controls sensor sensitivity: low ISO (100) = clean image; high ISO (3200+) = noisy/grainy but usable in low light.
Photography Exam Tips: Show your camera SETTINGS for every image (aperture, shutter speed, ISO). Demonstrate UNDERSTANDING of the exposure triangle — not just what each setting does, but how they interact. Explain the CREATIVE REASON for each technical choice.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring camera controls and exposure. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to camera controls and exposure, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Lesson 3: Application: camera controls and exposure

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring camera controls and exposure. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to camera controls and exposure, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with camera controls and exposure. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with camera controls and exposure. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: camera controls and exposure

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Photograph the same subject using three different aperture settings and three different shutter speeds. Create a contact sheet showing all exposures. Annotate the creative effect of each setting. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate understanding of the exposure triangle; show deliberate use of aperture for depth of field control; show deliberate use of shutter speed for motion control; annotate with technical vocabulary (f-stop, DoF, motion blur, ISO noise); explain creative INTENT behind each technical choice. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Auto mode will give you the best exposure every time. Correct: Auto mode makes AVERAGE exposure decisions that may not suit your creative intention. Manual control lets you CHOOSE: shallow or deep depth of field? Frozen or blurred motion? Clean or grainy aesthetic? Understanding the exposure triangle gives you creative control auto mode cannot provide.
