Lesson 2: Core Concepts: architectural and interior design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on architectural and interior design. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Spatial planning considers how people move through and use spaces — function, flow and atmosphere are key.
Key Fact: Scale drawing uses set ratios (1:50, 1:100) to represent spaces accurately; orthographic projection shows plan, elevation and section.
Key Fact: Architectural models communicate 3D ideas that drawings cannot — they reveal spatial relationships, light and material qualities.
Key Fact: Responding to site means understanding the context: existing structures, landscape, climate, culture and community.
Three-Dimensional Design Exam Tips: Produce drawings at a STATED SCALE (e.g. 1:50). Show at least PLAN and ELEVATION. Build a physical MODEL — photograph it. Explain how your design responds to its SPECIFIC site. Consider human scale — annotate dimensions.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring architectural and interior design. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to architectural and interior design, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: architectural and interior design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Three-Dimensional Design Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring architectural and interior design. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to architectural and interior design, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with architectural and interior design. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with architectural and interior design. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: architectural and interior design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Design a small space (e.g. a pavilion, studio or room) for a specific site and purpose. Show plans, elevations, a scale model and a written rationale explaining how your design responds to its context. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: produce accurate scale drawings; build a refined architectural model; explain how the design responds to site and context; consider lighting, materials and atmosphere; annotate with specialist vocabulary (plan, elevation, section, spatial flow); evaluate the design's effectiveness. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Architectural design is just about drawing nice-looking buildings. Correct: Architectural design must address FUNCTION (how spaces are used), STRUCTURE (how the building stands up), CONTEXT (how it responds to its site and community), SUSTAINABILITY (energy efficiency, materials) and ATMOSPHERE (how it feels to be there) — not just appearance.

  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in architectural and interior design?"
