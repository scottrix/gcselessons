Lesson 2: Core Concepts: formal elements line colour tone shape form texture pattern

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on formal elements line colour tone shape form texture pattern. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Line: defines edges, creates movement, suggests direction. Colour: communicates emotion, creates emphasis, establishes mood. Tone: creates form, depth and atmosphere through light and dark.
Key Fact: Shape (2D) and form (3D): geometric shapes feel man-made; organic shapes feel natural. Texture: actual (real surface) vs visual (implied through mark-making).
Key Fact: Pattern: the repetition of a motif — can be regular (predictable) or irregular (organic, varied).
Key Fact: Formal elements are the BUILDING BLOCKS of visual language — understanding them gives you the vocabulary to analyse and create effectively.
Art, Craft and Design Exam Tips: Create YOUR OWN examples for each element — not copied illustrations. Annotate with FORMAL ELEMENTS vocabulary. Show how the same element can communicate different things in different contexts. Use this vocabulary in ALL your annotations across the portfolio.

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate clear understanding of each formal element; create distinctive, personal examples (not generic); annotate with specialist vocabulary; explain how each element can communicate meaning; compare how different artists use the same element; show how understanding elements has informed personal work. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: The formal elements are just theory — they do not matter when you are actually making art. Correct: The formal elements are the VOCABULARY of visual language — without understanding them, you cannot make intentional creative choices or analyse work effectively. Every artist uses formal elements whether they know it or not; understanding them lets you use them PURPOSEFULLY rather than accidentally.
