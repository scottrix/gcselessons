Lesson 2: Core Concepts: branding and corporate identity

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on branding and corporate identity. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A logo must work at ALL scales — from a billboard to a favicon; simplicity and clarity are key.
Key Fact: Brand guidelines document: logo usage rules, colour palette (Pantone, CMYK, RGB), typography, imagery style, spacing requirements.
Key Fact: Consistency across all applications builds recognition — stationery, packaging, digital, signage must all speak the same visual language.
Key Fact: Rebranding involves updating an existing identity — it must balance familiarity with freshness.
Graphic Communication Exam Tips: Test your logo at postage-stamp size. Show it in COLOUR and BLACK-AND-WHITE. Apply it to at least 3 different items. Create simple brand guidelines (colours, fonts, spacing).

Lesson 3: Application: branding and corporate identity

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Graphic Communication Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring branding and corporate identity. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to branding and corporate identity, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with branding and corporate identity. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with branding and corporate identity. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: branding and corporate identity

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Create a complete brand identity for a fictional company. Design the logo, choose colours and typography, and apply the identity across at least three different media. Include brand guidelines documentation. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: design a simple, scalable, memorable logo; define clear brand guidelines; apply the identity consistently across media; annotate every decision with rationale; show the logo working at different scales and in monochrome. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: A good logo just needs to look great on a screen. Correct: A logo must work across ALL applications — business card, website, signage, uniform, packaging, social media. It must be legible at tiny sizes and reproduced in colour AND black-and-white. Test your logo at multiple scales.
