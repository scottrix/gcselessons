Lesson 2: Core Concepts: packaging and print design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on packaging and print design. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Packaging nets are 2D templates that fold into 3D containers — understanding tabs, glue flaps and die-cuts is essential.
Key Fact: Sustainable packaging considers material choice, minimal waste, recyclability and environmental impact.
Key Fact: Print processes differ: offset litho for high-volume, digital for short runs and variable data, screen for speciality finishes.
Key Fact: Information graphics use visual design to communicate data clearly — charts, diagrams, maps and infographics.
Graphic Communication Exam Tips: Show the FLAT NET and the ASSEMBLED package. Test that your net actually folds — make a prototype. Consider sustainability — annotate material choices. Include all required information (barcode, contents, warnings).

Lesson 3: Application: packaging and print design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Graphic Communication Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring packaging and print design. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to packaging and print design, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with packaging and print design. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with packaging and print design. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: packaging and print design

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Design packaging for a product. Create the net, consider materials and sustainability, and design the surface graphics. Build a prototype and annotate your design decisions. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: design a functional net that folds correctly; choose appropriate materials with sustainability considered; create effective surface graphics with clear information hierarchy; build a working prototype. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Packaging design is just about making the box look attractive. Correct: Packaging design must consider: PROTECTION (does it keep the product safe?), FUNCTION (does it open/close properly?), SUSTAINABILITY (materials, waste, recyclability), LEGISLATION (barcodes, ingredients, safety warnings), and aesthetics.
