Lesson 2: Core Concepts: composition and framing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on composition and framing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Composition in photography is about what you INCLUDE and EXCLUDE from the frame — every element should have a purpose.
Key Fact: The rule of thirds places subjects at intersection points for dynamic balance; centre placement creates formality or stillness.
Key Fact: Leading lines guide the viewer's eye through the image — roads, fences, rivers, shadows all work as leading lines.
Key Fact: Viewpoint changes meaning: low angle = power; high angle = vulnerability; eye level = neutrality; dutch angle = tension.
Photography Exam Tips: Show MULTIPLE compositions of the same subject — prove you considered alternatives. Annotate with COMPOSITIONAL vocabulary. Explain the MEANING behind your framing choices. Consider what you EXCLUDED as well as what you included.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring composition and framing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to composition and framing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: composition and framing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Photography Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring composition and framing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to composition and framing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with composition and framing. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with composition and framing. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: composition and framing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Photograph the same subject from five different viewpoints and using at least three compositional techniques. Create a contact sheet and annotate which composition is most effective and why. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate a range of compositional techniques; show deliberate viewpoint choices; annotate with compositional vocabulary (rule of thirds, leading lines, negative space); explain how composition creates MEANING, not just looks nice; evaluate which approach best serves the intention. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Good photography is about having an expensive camera, not about composition. Correct: Composition is MORE important than equipment. A well-composed photo on a phone will always be stronger than a poorly composed photo on an expensive camera. The decisions about what to include, how to frame and where to place elements are what make a photograph communicate effectively.
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):
  • Synoptic links: explain how composition and framing connects to another Art and Design topic you have studied
  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of composition and framing
  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in composition and framing?"

Plenary (5 minutes)

Assessment Criteria
  • Got it: Confident explanation + correct worked examples
  • Getting there: Main points OK, needs support with detail
  • Not yet: Confused on key concepts - re-run Lesson 2

Homework & Consolidation

  • Extension: Explain composition and framing to someone else in your own words (10 mins)
