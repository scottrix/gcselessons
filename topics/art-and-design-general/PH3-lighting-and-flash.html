Lesson 2: Core Concepts: lighting and flash

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on lighting and flash. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Light direction dramatically changes a subject: front light = flat, even; side light = texture and depth; back light = rim lighting or silhouette.
Key Fact: Light quality: hard light (direct sun, bare flash) creates sharp shadows; soft light (overcast, softbox) creates gentle gradients.
Key Fact: The golden hour (just after sunrise/before sunset) produces warm, directional, soft light — ideal for many subjects.
Key Fact: Flash can fill shadows, freeze motion or be the primary light source — but on-camera flash creates flat, unflattering light.
Photography Exam Tips: Show the LIGHT SETUP for each photograph — include diagrams if possible. Annotate with LIGHTING vocabulary (key light, fill, rim, hard, soft). Explain the MOOD created by each lighting approach. Compare natural and artificial light results.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring lighting and flash. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to lighting and flash, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: lighting and flash

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Photography Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring lighting and flash. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to lighting and flash, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with lighting and flash. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with lighting and flash. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: lighting and flash

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Photograph the same subject using at least three different lighting setups (e.g. natural side light, backlit silhouette, diffused window light). Annotate each describing the light quality, direction and mood created. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate understanding of light direction and quality; use at least three different lighting approaches; annotate with specialist vocabulary (hard/soft light, colour temperature, rim lighting, key light, fill); explain how lighting choices create mood and meaning. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: You need expensive studio lights to take good photographs. Correct: Natural light is often the BEST light source — window light, golden hour and open shade all produce beautiful, professional-quality results without any equipment. Understanding light DIRECTION and QUALITY is far more important than owning expensive lighting gear.
