Lesson 2: Core Concepts: fabric printing and dyeing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on fabric printing and dyeing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Block printing uses carved stamps applied to fabric; screen printing forces ink through a mesh stencil — both can create repeat patterns.
Key Fact: Batik applies wax as a resist before dyeing — the wax blocks dye, creating patterns where the wax is removed.
Key Fact: Tie-dye and shibori create patterns through resist: binding, folding, twisting or compressing fabric before dyeing.
Key Fact: Repeat patterns use a single motif repeated systematically — half-drop, brick, mirror and random repeats create different visual effects.
Textile Design Exam Tips: Show the BLOCK/SCREEN alongside the printed fabric. Document the FULL PROCESS — preparation, printing, fixing, washing. Annotate with technique names and specialist vocabulary.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Create a series of experimental studies exploring fabric printing and dyeing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to fabric printing and dyeing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: fabric printing and dyeing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Textile Design Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Create a series of experimental studies exploring fabric printing and dyeing. Annotate your process.

Answer: A range of experimental studies showing different approaches to fabric printing and dyeing, each annotated with media, technique and purpose. Show development from initial experiments to refined outcomes.

Q2: Research an artist or designer who works with fabric printing and dyeing. Write a critical analysis of their approach and explain how it has informed your own work.

Answer: A critical analysis naming a specific artist/designer, using specialist vocabulary, explaining their context and approach, and making clear connections to personal work.

Q3: Evaluate the effectiveness of your experiments with fabric printing and dyeing. Which approaches were most successful and why?

Answer: A reflective evaluation identifying successful and less successful experiments, explaining WHY using technical and conceptual language, and showing how evaluation led to further refinement.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: fabric printing and dyeing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Create printed and dyed fabric samples using at least two different techniques. Design a repeat pattern and print it. Annotate your process explaining your design decisions, technical choices and results. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: demonstrate competent use of at least two techniques; show understanding of repeat pattern design; annotate with precise specialist vocabulary (motif, repeat system, registration, resist); evaluate which technique best serves their intention. Mark scheme: AO1 (develop through investigation) + AO2 (refine through experimentation) + AO3 (record ideas and observations) + AO4 (present personal response) = total marks

Exam Tips: Address ALL four Assessment Objectives — AO1 (develop), AO2 (refine), AO3 (record), AO4 (present) | Show the FULL creative journey — from initial research to final outcome | Annotate your work using specialist vocabulary (not just "I like it") | Draw from primary sources wherever possible — real objects, not photos | Experiment with a RANGE of media and techniques before selecting | Make clear connections between artist research and your own developing work
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes with this topic. Wrong: Fabric printing is just stamping paint onto fabric — it is simple and does not need planning. Correct: Successful fabric printing requires careful planning: design the motif, consider the repeat system, choose appropriate fabric (natural fibres for dye, tight weave for screen printing), mix the right consistency of ink/dye, and plan registration for multi-colour prints.
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):

  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of fabric printing and dyeing
  • Critical: "What are the limitations of the models used in fabric printing and dyeing?"
