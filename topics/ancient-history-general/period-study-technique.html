Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Period Study (Paper 1) is worth 50% of the GCSE and lasts 1 hour 30 minutes
Key Fact: It focuses on narrative, causation, and change and continuity across a broad chronological period
Key Fact: You must know the key events in chronological order — timeline knowledge is essential
Key Fact: Narrative questions ask you to tell the story of events — use chronological structure
Key Fact: Causation questions require you to explain why something happened, identifying multiple causes
Key Fact: Change and continuity questions ask you to identify what changed and what stayed the same over a period

In 480 BCE, King Xerxes launched a massive invasion of Greece, determined to avenge his father Darius's defeat at Marathon a decade earlier. Xerxes assembled the largest army and navy the ancient world had seen, crossing the Hellespont on a bridge of boats. The Greeks, led by Sparta, initially attempted to hold the Persians at the pass of Thermopylae. King Leonidas and 300 Spartans, along with several thousand other Greeks, held the narrow pass for three days in August 480 BCE, inflicting heavy casualties. However, a local Greek named Ephialtes betrayed a mountain path, allowing the Persians to outflank the defenders. Leonidas and his Spartans fought to the death. With Thermopylae lost, the Greek army retreated and the Persians sacked Athens, which had been evacuated. The decisive turning point came at the naval Battle of Salamis in September 480 BCE. The Athenian general Themistocles lured the Persian fleet into the narrow straits near Salamis, where the larger Persian ships could not manoeuvre effectively. Exam Tips: For narrative questions, use dates as structural markers: 'In 490 BCE...', 'By 480 BCE...' | In causation answers, group causes into long-term and short-term, then identify which was most significant and why | For change and continuity, create a before-and-after comparison in your plan | Don't just write about one event in isolation — show how events connect across the period | Use the wording of the question to frame every paragraph — keep linking back to it
Common Errors: ✗ Writing about events in random order in a narrative question ✓ Always use chronological order — the examiner needs to see you understand the sequence of events and how one led to another ✗ Only identifying one cause for a causation question ✓ Identify multiple causes (long-term and short-term) and explain how they combined to produce the outcome ✗ Only discussing change without continuity (or vice versa) ✓ A change and continuity question requires both — explicitly state what changed AND what stayed the same ✗ Focusing on one small part of the period instead of the whole period ✓ The Period Study tests breadth of knowledge — make sure your answer covers the full chronological range of the
