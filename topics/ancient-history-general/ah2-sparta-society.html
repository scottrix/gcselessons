Lesson 2: Core Concepts: spartan society &amp; the helots

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on spartan society &amp; the helots. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Spartan society was organised around military preparedness; full citizens (homoioi/equals) were professional soldiers supported by helot labour
Key Fact: The agoge was the rigorous state education system that trained Spartan boys from age 7 in discipline, endurance and warfare
Key Fact: Helots were the enslaved population of Messenia who farmed the land; they vastly outnumbered citizens and were constantly controlled by fear
Key Fact: The krypteia was a secret police of young Spartans who terrorised and killed helots to prevent rebellion
Key Fact: Spartan government: two hereditary kings (military and religious roles), five ephors (overseers with wide powers), Gerousia (council of 28 elders) and the apella (citizen assembly with limited power)
Key Fact: Spartan women had more freedom than in other Greek cities: they owned property, trained physically and managed households while men were in barracks

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on spartan society &amp; the helots from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Agoge = state-controlled military training from age 7 | Helots: always explain WHY Spartans were brutal (numerical superiority, fear of revolt) | Government: 2 kings + 5 ephors + Gerousia + apella — know each role | Spartan women: unusual freedom compared to other Greek women
Common Errors: ✗ Sparta was a democracy ✓ Sparta was a mixed constitution: monarchy (two kings), oligarchy (Gerousia and ephors) and limited democracy (apella). It was far from democratic — the citizen assembly had no power to debate or propose ✗ Helots were slaves like those in Athens ✓ Helots were state-owned serfs tied to the land, not privately owned chattel slaves. They could not be bought or sold individually and lived in family groups on the kleros (land allotment) ✗ All Spartan boys became full citizens ✓ Only those who successfully completed the agoge and were elected to a mess (syssitia) became homoioi. Some failed and became inferior hypomeiones (inferiors)
