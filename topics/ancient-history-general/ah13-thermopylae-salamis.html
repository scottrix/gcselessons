Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Xerxes invaded Greece in 480 BC with a massive army and fleet, aiming to conquer all of Greece
Key Fact: At Thermopylae, 300 Spartans under King Leonidas held the narrow pass for three days against the Persian army
Key Fact: The Greek position was betrayed when Ephialtes showed the Persians a mountain path to outflank the Greeks
Key Fact: Leonidas dismissed most troops but the 300 Spartans and 700 Thespians fought to the death — a symbol of heroic sacrifice
Key Fact: At Salamis, Themistocles lured the Persian fleet into narrow waters where the Greeks' superior tactics won a decisive naval victory
Key Fact: The Delphic oracle's 'wooden wall' prophecy was interpreted by Themistocles as meaning the Athenian navy, not the Acropolis walls

Exam Tips: Thermopylae = heroic sacrifice but a defeat; Salamis = strategic victory | Themistocles: interpreted oracle, built navy, tricked Xerxes at Salamis | Ephialtes = traitor; Leonidas = hero who fought to the death | Narrow terrain was key at both battles: pass at Thermopylae, strait at Salamis
Common Errors: ✗ Only 300 Spartans fought at Thermopylae ✓ There were 300 Spartans but also approximately 700 Thespians who chose to stay and fight to the death, plus helots and other allies. The total Greek force at the start was about 7,000 ✗ Xerxes won the war after Thermopylae ✓ Although Thermopylae was a Persian victory, Salamis (480 BC) was the decisive battle that turned the war in Greece's favour. Plataea (479 BC) completed the Greek victory ✗ The Spartans were the only brave Greeks ✓ The 700 Thespians who chose to stay and die with the Spartans showed equal courage. Herodotus also notes that other Greeks fought bravely throughout the wars; the Spartan-focused narrative reflects later propaganda
