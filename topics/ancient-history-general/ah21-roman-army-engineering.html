Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the roman army &amp; engineering

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the roman army &amp; engineering. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Roman legion was the basic military unit of about 5,000 men, divided into 10 cohorts of 6 centuries of 80 men each
Key Fact: Legionaries carried a pilum (javelin) and gladius (short sword); the pilum's soft iron shank bent on impact, making shields unusable
Key Fact: Roman engineering: roads (over 400,000 km of roads connecting the empire), aqueducts (gravity-fed water supply), and fortifications like Hadrian's Wall
Key Fact: Hadrian's Wall (122 AD) was a 117 km defensive wall across northern Britain, marking the northwest frontier of the empire
Key Fact: Roman camps (castra) were built to a standard plan every night on campaign, with gates, roads and defences
Key Fact: The army was also an engineering force: building roads, bridges, siege works and infrastructure wherever they went

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on the roman army &amp; engineering from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Legion = 5,000 citizen infantry; auxiliaries = non-citizen specialists | Pilum = bending javelin that disabled shields | Roads = military control + trade + communication | Army = soldiers AND engineers; they built the empire's infrastructure
Common Errors: ✗ Roman legions were the entire army ✓ Legionaries (citizen heavy infantry) were only part of the army. Auxiliaries (non-citizens) provided cavalry, archers and specialist troops, often equalling or exceeding legionary numbers. The navy was also separate ✗ Hadrian's Wall successfully kept all barbarians out ✓ The Wall controlled and monitored movement rather than being an impenetrable barrier. Gates (milecastles) allowed controlled trade and passage. Its primary function was border management and demonstrating Roman power, not total exclusion ✗ Roman soldiers only fought ✓ Legionaries spent much of their time on engineering and construction: building roads, forts, aqueducts and infrastructur
