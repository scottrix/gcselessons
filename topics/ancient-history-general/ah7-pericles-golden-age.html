Lesson 2: Core Concepts: pericles &amp; the golden age of athens

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on pericles &amp; the golden age of athens. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Pericles dominated Athenian politics for over 30 years (c. 461-429 BC), elected strategos repeatedly
Key Fact: The Athenian building programme: Parthenon, Propylaea, Erechtheion on the Acropolis, funded by Delian League tribute
Key Fact: Pericles' Funeral Oration (431 BC, recorded by Thucydides) celebrated Athenian democracy, freedom and cultural superiority
Key Fact: The Peloponnesian War (431-404 BC) between Athens and Sparta devastated Greece and ended the Athenian Golden Age
Key Fact: The plague of Athens (430 BC) killed a third of the population, including Pericles himself in 429 BC
Key Fact: Thucydides is our main source: he wrote a contemporary history of the Peloponnesian War, valued for its accuracy and analysis

Exam Tips: Pericles = strategos repeatedly elected, not a tyrant or king | Building programme = funded by tribute, controversial with allies | Funeral Oration = Thucydides' version, idealised, not a verbatim transcript | Peloponnesian War: Spartan fear of Athens + Athenian imperialism
Common Errors: ✗ Pericles was a king or tyrant ✓ Pericles was an elected strategos who dominated Athenian politics through persuasion in the assembly. He had no constitutional authority beyond any other general; his power came from his oratory, reputation and repeated election ✗ The Funeral Oration is Pericles' exact words ✓ Thucydides wrote the speech decades later and admitted he composed speeches that expressed what was appropriate for the occasion. It represents Thucydides' interpretation of Pericles' views and Athenian ideals, not a verbatim record ✗ The plague caused Athens to lose the Peloponnesian War ✓ The plague was devastating but Athens continued fighting for another 25 years after it.
