Lesson 4: Exam Practice: command words &amp; question types

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain why understanding command words is essential for success in OCR J198 Ancient History (8 marks) <div class="

Understanding command words is essential because they determine the type of response the examiner expects. For example, 'describe' requires factual detail about what happened, while 'explain' demands reasons and causes. Misreading a command word can lead to a low mark even if the historical knowledge is strong — describing when asked to explain, or giving a one-sided answer to a 'how far' question, are common errors. Furthermore, OCR J198 has two papers with different emphases: the Period Study focuses on narrative and causation, while the Depth Study requires source analysis. Each paper uses command words that reflect these different skills, so understanding them ensures the candidate applies the correct approach. Ultimately, command words are the key to unlocking what the question demands, making them the most important first step in any exam answer.

Exam Tips: Always circle or underline the command word before you start writing — it determines your entire approach | For 'how far' questions, plan both sides before writing so your argument is balanced | Short-answer questions: one point per mark is a good rule of thumb | Extended answers need 3-4 developed paragraphs plus a conclusion | Don't just describe when the question asks you to explain — always link back to reasons and significance
Common Errors: ✗ Describing events when the question says 'explain why' ✓ Focus on causes and reasons, not just what happened — use 'because', 'due to', 'as a result of' ✗ Giving a one-sided answer to a 'how far' question ✓ Always present both sides of the argument before making your judgement ✗ Writing everything you know about a topic regardless of the command word ✓ Tailor your answer to the specific command word — description, explanation and evaluation require different approaches ✗ Ignoring the command word and writing a generic answer ✓ The command word is your instruction — it tells you exactly what the examiner wants to see
