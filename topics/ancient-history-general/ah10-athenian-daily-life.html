Lesson 2: Core Concepts: athenian daily life &amp; society

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on athenian daily life &amp; society. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The oikos (household) was the basic social unit: the male head (kyrios) controlled the family, property and dependants
Key Fact: Athenian women lived largely secluded lives in the women's quarters (gynaikeion); they could not vote, own property or conduct business independently
Key Fact: The symposium was an elite male drinking party featuring conversation, music and entertainment; it was central to aristocratic social and political networking
Key Fact: Slavery was fundamental to the Athenian economy: slaves worked in homes, workshops, mines and fields; they had no legal rights
Key Fact: Education (paideia) for boys focused on grammar, music and physical training; girls received little or no formal education
Key Fact: Hetairai (courtesans) were educated women who attended symposia and had more freedom than citizen wives

Exam Tips: Oikos = basic unit, kyrios = male head | Women: secluded, no vote, no property, kyrios represents them | Symposium = elite male networking, NOT just a party | Slavery = fundamental to Athenian economy and citizen leisure
Common Errors: ✗ All women in Athens had the same restricted life ✓ Citizen wives were secluded, but hetairai (courtesans) had education and social freedom. Foreign women (metics) had different restrictions. Slaves had no freedom regardless of gender ✗ Greek women did nothing but weave ✓ While weaving was a primary duty, citizen women also managed the household budget, supervised slaves, raised children, performed religious duties and managed the household when the kyrios was absent ✗ Slavery in Athens was the same as modern slavery ✓ While equally immoral, Athenian slavery differed: some slaves were Greeks from other cities, some could earn money, skilled slaves had better conditions, and domestic slaves
