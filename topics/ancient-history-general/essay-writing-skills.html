Lesson 2: Core Concepts: essay writing skills

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on essay writing skills. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A good essay has a clear structure: introduction, 3-4 developed paragraphs, and a conclusion
Key Fact: The introduction should define key terms, show understanding of the question, and indicate your line of argument
Key Fact: Use the PEEL structure for paragraphs: Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link back to the question
Key Fact: Each paragraph should focus on one main point that directly answers the question
Key Fact: Evidence must be specific — named people, events, dates, and sources score highest marks
Key Fact: Explanation means showing how your evidence supports your argument, not just listing facts

Spartan military strength was a significant reason for its dominance in the Peloponnese, but it was not the only factor — its political system and diplomatic strategies were also crucial. The Spartan agoge produced the most feared hoplite army in Greece. Their discipline at battles like Plataea in 479 BCE demonstrated overwhelming military capability, and this reputation alone deterred many rivals from challenging Spartan authority. The Peloponnesian League, however, was equally important. By binding neighbouring states into a network of alliances, Sparta secured loyalty without constant military action. States like Corinth and Megara contributed forces and paid tribute, extending Spartan influence far beyond its own resources. Furthermore, the Spartan political system provided remarkable stability. The dual kingship, ephors and gerousia created a balanced constitution that prevented the kind of internal strife seen in other Greek cities. This stability allowed consistent long-term foreign policy.
