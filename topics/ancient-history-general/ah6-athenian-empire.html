Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the athenian empire (delian league)

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the athenian empire (delian league). Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Delian League was founded in 478 BC as a defensive alliance against Persia, with Athens as leader (hegemon)
Key Fact: Member states contributed ships or money (phoros/tribute) stored on the island of Delos at the League's treasury
Key Fact: Athens gradually transformed the League into an empire: moved the treasury to Athens (454 BC), forced members to use Athenian coinage and courts
Key Fact: States that tried to leave (e.g. Naxos, Thasos) were forcibly prevented; the League became an instrument of Athenian control
Key Fact: Athens used tribute to fund its navy and building programme (Parthenon), effectively spending allied money on itself
Key Fact: The Erythrae decree shows Athens directly controlling member cities' legal and political affairs

Exam Tips: Delian League = alliance (478 BC) then empire (by 450s BC) | Moving treasury to Athens (454 BC) = key turning point | Naxos and Thasos = examples of Athens crushing revolts | Tribute funded the Parthenon and Athenian navy
Common Errors: ✗ The Delian League was always an Athenian empire ✓ The League began as a genuine voluntary alliance against Persia. The transformation into empire was gradual, through a series of steps over about 25 years (478-454 BC) ✗ All League members paid tribute in money ✓ Some members contributed ships instead of money. Over time, most states chose to pay money rather than provide ships, which suited Athens because it kept the navy under Athenian control ✗ Athens controlled the League through elected representatives ✓ Athens controlled the League through military force, not representative institutions. There was no League assembly or council where allies could vote on policy
