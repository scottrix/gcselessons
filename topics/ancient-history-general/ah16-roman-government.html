Lesson 2: Core Concepts: roman government &amp; the constitution

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on roman government &amp; the constitution. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Republic was a mixed constitution with monarchical (consuls), aristocratic (Senate) and democratic (assemblies) elements
Key Fact: Two consuls were elected annually with imperium (power of command); they could veto each other (intercessio)
Key Fact: The Senate was the most powerful body: 300 patrician elders who controlled foreign policy, finances and state religion
Key Fact: The comitia centuriata (centuriate assembly) elected consuls and declared war; voting was weighted by wealth
Key Fact: Tribunes of the plebs (10 annually elected plebeian officers) could veto any magistrate's action and summon the Senate
Key Fact: In emergencies, a dictator could be appointed for six months with supreme power

Exam Tips: Mixed constitution: monarchy (consuls) + aristocracy (Senate) + democracy (assemblies) | Senate = most powerful long-term; Consuls = executive; Tribunes = plebeian protection | Veto power was the key check: consuls veto each other, tribunes veto anyone | Dictator = 6-month emergency power, then must resign
Common Errors: ✗ The Roman Republic was a democracy like Athens ✓ Rome was a mixed constitution with strong aristocratic elements. The Senate (unelected patricians) held more real power than the assemblies. Voting in the comitia centuriata was heavily weighted toward the wealthy, unlike Athens' direct democracy ✗ The Senate made laws in the Republic ✓ The Senate technically only issued advisory decrees (senatus consulta), not laws. Laws were passed by the assemblies. However, in practice, the Senate's authority meant its decrees were almost always obeyed ✗ The dictatorship was like modern dictatorship ✓ The Roman dictator was a constitutional office with strict limits: maximum six months, appointed only in
