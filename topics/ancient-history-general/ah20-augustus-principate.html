Lesson 2: Core Concepts: augustus &amp; the principate

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on augustus &amp; the principate. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Octavian (later Augustus) was Caesar's adopted heir who defeated Mark Antony at Actium (31 BC) to become Rome's sole ruler
Key Fact: The Second Triumvirate (43 BC): Octavian, Antony and Lepidus divided the Roman world; it ended in civil war between Octavian and Antony
Key Fact: Augustus established the Principate: he held supreme power but maintained Republican forms and titles (Princeps = First Citizen)
Key Fact: The constitutional settlement (27 BC): Augustus 'restored the Republic' but kept consulship, tribunician power and control of key provinces
Key Fact: The Pax Romana (Roman Peace) brought stability, prosperity and cultural flourishing across the empire
Key Fact: Augustus reformed the army, morality, religion and building programme; he boasted he 'found Rome brick and left it marble'

Exam Tips: Actium = 31 BC, Octavian defeats Antony | Principate = monarchy disguised as Republic (Princeps = First Citizen) | Constitutional settlement (27 BC): 'restored Republic' but kept real power | Pax Romana = 200 years of stability after decades of civil war
Common Errors: ✗ Augustus abolished the Republic ✓ Augustus carefully preserved Republican institutions (Senate, consulships, elections, tribunes). He held power through these offices rather than creating new ones. The Republic existed in form, but Augustus dominated it in practice ✗ Augustus called himself emperor ✓ Augustus never used the title 'emperor' (imperator was a military acclamation). He preferred Princeps (First Citizen), suggesting primacy among equals rather than rule over subjects ✗ The Pax Romana meant no wars at all ✓ The Pax Romana meant no major civil wars between Romans.
