Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the ionian revolt (499-494 bc)

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the ionian revolt (499-494 bc). Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Ionian Revolt (499-494 BC) was a rebellion of Greek city-states in Asia Minor against Persian rule
Key Fact: Aristagoras, tyrant of Miletus, initiated the revolt after a failed attack on Naxos left him fearing Persian punishment
Key Fact: Athens sent 20 ships and Eretria sent 5 to support the Ionians — this enraged King Darius
Key Fact: The burning of Sardis (498 BC) was the revolt's greatest success but provoked a devastating Persian response
Key Fact: The revolt was crushed at the Battle of Lade (494 BC); Miletus was sacked and its people enslaved
Key Fact: Herodotus is our main source; he presents the revolt as the beginning of the chain of events leading to the Persian Wars

Exam Tips: Ionian Revolt = trigger for the Persian Wars | Aristagoras: failed Naxos attack then started revolt to save himself | Athens sent 20 ships, Eretria sent 5 — this specifically enraged Darius | Lade (494 BC) = decisive Persian naval victory ending the revolt
Common Errors: ✗ The Ionian Revolt was a spontaneous popular uprising ✓ The revolt was initiated by Aristagoras for personal reasons (fear of punishment after the Naxos failure). He cynically resigned as tyrant and declared democracy to gain popular support, but the revolt's origins were self-serving ✗ Athens supported the revolt out of pure idealism ✓ Athens had practical motives: shared Ionian identity, commercial interests and the desire to weaken Persian influence in the Aegean. Their 20 ships represented a modest commitment, not a full military effort ✗ The Persians were easily defeated in the revolt ✓ The Persians ultimately crushed the revolt decisively. The early success at Sardis was the exception
