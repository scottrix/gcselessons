Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the punic wars &amp; hannibal

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the punic wars &amp; hannibal. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The three Punic Wars (264-146 BC) were fought between Rome and Carthage for control of the Western Mediterranean
Key Fact: First Punic War (264-241 BC): Rome built its first navy and won Sicily as its first province
Key Fact: Second Punic War (218-201 BC): Hannibal crossed the Alps with elephants, won at Cannae (216 BC) but was eventually defeated by Scipio at Zama (202 BC)
Key Fact: The Battle of Cannae was Rome's worst military defeat: perhaps 50,000-70,000 Romans died in a single day
Key Fact: Third Punic War (149-146 BC): Carthage was destroyed; the city was burned and its territory became a Roman province
Key Fact: Cato the Elder ended every speech with 'Carthago delenda est' (Carthage must be destroyed)

Exam Tips: Three Punic Wars: 264-241, 218-201, 149-146 BC | Cannae = Rome's worst defeat; Zama = Scipio defeats Hannibal | Hannibal's failure: couldn't take Rome, allies stayed loyal, Carthage didn't reinforce | Aftermath: Rome = Mediterranean superpower, but social tensions grew
Common Errors: ✗ Hannibal conquered Rome ✓ Hannibal never captured Rome. He campaigned in Italy for 16 years, winning battles but never taking the city itself. Rome's walls, resilient allies and ability to raise new armies prevented his ultimate success ✗ The Third Punic War was a fair fight ✓ By 149 BC, Carthage had been disarmed and was no real threat. The war was essentially an unprovoked Roman aggression driven by fear and greed, as Cato's obsessive 'Carthago delenda est' rhetoric demonstrates ✗ Rome won the Punic Wars easily ✓ Rome suffered catastrophic defeats: at Cannae alone, more Romans died in one day than in any other battle in Western history.
