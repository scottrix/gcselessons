Lesson 2: Core Concepts: greek city-states (poleis)

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on greek city-states (poleis). Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: A polis (city-state) was the fundamental political unit of ancient Greece, typically consisting of a city and its surrounding countryside
Key Fact: Each polis was self-governing with its own laws, customs, constitution and patron deity
Key Fact: Citizens (free adult males) had political rights and duties; women, slaves and metics (foreign residents) were excluded from citizenship
Key Fact: The acropolis (high city) was the religious and defensive centre; the agora was the marketplace and civic meeting place
Key Fact: Greek colonisation from the 8th century BC spread poleis across the Mediterranean and Black Sea
Key Fact: The two most powerful poleis were Athens (democratic) and Sparta (oligarchic/militaristic)

Exam Tips: Polis is the key concept — always define it precisely | Citizenship = rights AND duties (military service, political participation) | Athens vs Sparta is a classic comparison question | Colonisation: driven by land shortage and overpopulation, not just trade
Common Errors: ✗ All residents of a polis were citizens ✓ Only free adult males whose parents were both citizens held full citizenship; women, slaves and metics were excluded from political life ✗ Greek city-states were united under one government ✓ Each polis was completely independent with its own laws, government and foreign policy; there was no unified Greek state ✗ The agora was only a marketplace ✓ The agora was also the political, social and religious heart of the polis, used for assemblies, law courts, festivals and public announcements
