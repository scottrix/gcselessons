Key Fact: Romulus and Remus founded Rome after being suckled by a she-wolf — Romulus killed Remus over the city walls
Key Fact: Aeneas fled Troy and journeyed to Italy, becoming the ancestor of the Roman people according to Virgil
Key Fact: Foundation myths gave cities divine authority and a heroic past that legitimised their power and identity

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain the importance of foundation myths to the ancient Greeks and Romans. (12 marks) <div class="

Foundation myths were crucial to both Greeks and Romans because they provided identity, legitimacy and unity. In Athens, the myth of Theseus established the city's political structure: his synoecism of Attica legitimised Athenian dominance over the surrounding region, and the claim of autochthony gave Athenians a unique status among Greeks. The Theseus myth also provided a heroic ancestor who could rival Heracles, giving Athens cultural prestige. In Rome, the myth of Romulus and Remus explained the city's origins and values: Romulus's establishment of laws and religious practices set the pattern for Roman pietas, and even his killing of Remus carried a message about the primacy of the state over family loyalty. The Aeneas myth, developed fully by Virgil under Augustus, connected Rome to the heroic age of Troy, giving it a pedigree equal to Greece while asserting a distinct Roman identity based on pietas and duty. Both cultures used foundation myths to unify diverse populations under a single origin story, legitimise territorial claims, and justify their political systems. Without such myths, neither Athens nor Rome would have had the shared narrative that bound their citizens toget

Exam Tips: Always explain WHY a foundation myth was important, not just WHAT happened in the story | Connect foundation myths to political and social functions — identity, legitimacy, unity | The Theseus myth is particularly important for OCR J280 — know its details thoroughly | Compare Greek and Roman foundation myths to show how cultures used myth differently | Remember that foundation myths evolved over time to serve changing political needs
Common Errors: ✗ Treating foundation myths as historical facts ✓ Foundation myths blend legend, symbolism and possible historical memory — they reveal cultural values, not literal history ✗ Only narrating the myth without explaining its significance ✓ Always explain why the myth mattered to the people who told it — its political, social or religious function ✗ Confusing the Greek and Roman versions of the Aeneas story ✓ Homer mentions Aeneas briefly; Virgil's Aeneid is the full Roman version — always specify which source you are using ✗ Forgetting that foundation myths served different purposes at different times ✓ Myths were adapted and reinterpreted — e.g., the Theseus myth was expanded during the Atheni
