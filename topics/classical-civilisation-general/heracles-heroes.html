Lesson 2: Core Concepts: heracles and heroes

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on heracles and heroes. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Heracles (Hercules in Roman) was the greatest Greek hero, son of Zeus and the mortal Alcmene
Key Fact: Hera hated Heracles because he was proof of Zeus's infidelity — she sent two snakes to kill him in his cradle
Key Fact: Heracles was driven mad by Hera and killed his wife Megara and their children
Key Fact: The Twelve Labours were his punishment and path to redemption, set by King Eurystheus of Tiryns
Key Fact: Key Labours include: Nemean Lion, Lernaean Hydra, Ceryneian Hind, Erymanthian Boar, Augean Stables, Stymphalian Birds, Cretan Bull, Mares of Diomedes, Belt of Hippolyta, Cattle of Geryon, Apples of the Hesperides, and Cerberus
Key Fact: Heracles represents the hero who suffers greatly but achieves glory through endurance and strength

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain why Heracles was considered the greatest of the Greek heroes. (12 marks) <div class="

Heracles was considered the greatest Greek hero because his deeds, suffering and eventual deification surpassed all other heroes. His Twelve Labours were the most extraordinary feats in Greek mythology: he defeated monsters that no other mortal could overcome, including the Nemean Lion whose skin was impervious to weapons, the many-headed Hydra, and Cerberus the guard-dog of the Underworld itself. These Labours took him to the very edges of the world and beyond, symbolising a reach no other hero achieved. His suffering was also unmatched — Hera's persecution drove him to murder his own family, he was forced into servitude under the inferior Eurystheus, and he ultimately died in agony from the poisoned blood of Nessus. Yet Heracles endured all of this, embodying the Greek concept of arete (excellence) through sheer perseverance. His deification was unique among heroes: he alone was granted full immortality and a place on Olympus, marrying Hebe and becoming the gatekeeper of the gods. Other heroes like Perseus and Theseus achieved great deeds but did not suffer as profoundly or attain godhood. Exam Tips: When discussing Heracles, always connect his story to the wider concept of the Greek hero | Use specific Labours as evidence — don't just say 'he completed tasks' | The theme of suffering is central to Heracles — examiners want to see this recognised | Compare Heracles with other heroes if the question allows it | Remember that Heracles' story has a moral dimension: great deeds come at great personal cost
Common Errors: ✗ Calling Heracles 'Hercules' in a Greek context ✓ Hercules is the Roman name — use Heracles for Greek topics in OCR J280 ✗ Treating the Labours as just a list of adventures ✓ The Labours were penance for murder, symbolising redemption through suffering — their moral purpose is essential ✗ Forgetting Hera's role in Heracles' suffering ✓ Hera's persecution drives the entire narrative — her hatred causes both his madness and his Labours ✗ Describing heroes as simply 'brave' ✓ Greek heroes were defined by semi-divine birth, extraordinary deeds, extreme suffering, and facing death — courage alone is insufficient
