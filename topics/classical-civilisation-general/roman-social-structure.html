Extended question: Full-Mark Response How far was Roman society built on the patron-client system? (15 marks) <div class="

Roman society was fundamentally built on the patron-client system, as it provided the social glue that connected all levels of the hierarchy. At every level, from the poorest freedman to the emperor himself, Romans were bound by networks of mutual obligation. A senator's power depended on the number of clients who supported him; a plebeian's survival might depend on his patron's protection and generosity. The system permeated politics (clients voted as their patron directed), economics (patrons provided loans and business opportunities) and law (patrons represented clients in court). Even the emperor was described as the ultimate patron of the Roman people, distributing grain and games to his citizen-clients. However, the patron-client system alone did not structure Roman society. Legal status created rigid boundaries: the distinction between citizen and non-citizen, free and slave, patrician and plebeian was defined by law, not patronage. The family (familia) under the paterfamilias was an independent power structure. Military service created loyalty bonds separate from patronage.
