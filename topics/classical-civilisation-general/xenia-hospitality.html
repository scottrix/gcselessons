Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Xenia was the sacred code of hospitality between guest and host, protected by Zeus Xenios
Key Fact: A good host provided food, drink, a bath, rest and a gift before asking the guest's identity
Key Fact: A good guest showed respect, did not outstay their welcome and did not make excessive demands
Key Fact: The Phaeacians (King Alcinous and Queen Arete) demonstrate ideal xenia — they host Odysseus generously
Key Fact: The Cyclops Polyphemus violates xenia completely — he eats his guests instead of feeding them
Key Fact: The suitors in Odysseus's palace abuse xenia by consuming his resources and courting his wife

Extended question: Full-Mark Response How far does the Odyssey show that xenia was the most important social value in the Greek world? (15 marks) <div class="

The Odyssey presents xenia as the most important social value in the Greek world, though it also shows that other values like loyalty, courage and family were essential. Xenia structures almost every episode of the poem: Odysseus's reception by the Phaeacians, Circe, Aeolus and Eumaeus shows good xenia, while Polyphemus and the Laestrygonians show its violation. The suitors' abuse of xenia is the central conflict of the second half of the poem — their crime is primarily against hospitality, not against Odysseus personally. The fact that their punishment is death shows that violating xenia was a capital offence in the moral world of the Odyssey. Furthermore, xenia is divinely protected by Zeus Xenios, giving it a religious authority that no other social custom enjoys. The contrast between Eumaeus's generous xenia (despite his poverty) and the suitors' exploitation (despite their wealth) shows that hospitality reveals true character. However, other values are also crucial in the Odyssey. Loyalty (Penelope's faithfulness, Eumaeus's devotion) is equally central. Courage and cunning enable survival. Family bonds drive Odysseus's entire journey. Without loyalty, xenia would be meaningles

Exam Tips: Always give specific examples of good and bad xenia from the Odyssey | The contrast between the Phaeacians and the Cyclops is the clearest comparison | The suitors are the most important example of xenia violation — they are the main antagonists | Explain WHY xenia mattered socially, not just that it existed | Zeus Xenios is the divine enforcer — always mention the religious dimension
Common Errors: ✗ Describing xenia as just 'being polite to guests' ✓ Xenia was a sacred religious obligation enforced by Zeus — violating it was a sin, not just bad manners ✗ Only discussing the host's obligations ✓ Xenia required obligations from both sides — guests must also behave respectfully and not abuse their welcome ✗ Confusing the Cyclops episode with the suitors when discussing xenia violation ✓ The Cyclops is the extreme monster who eats guests; the suitors are civilised Greeks who abuse hospitality — both violate xenia but in very different ways ✗ Forgetting that Eumaeus shows the best xenia despite being poor ✓ Eumaeus's humble xenia contrasts with the suitors' wealthy abuse — Homer shows that
