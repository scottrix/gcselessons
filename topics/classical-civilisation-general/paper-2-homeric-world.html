Extended question: Full-Mark Response How far is the Odyssey a poem about homecoming rather than adventure? (15 marks) <div class="

The Odyssey is fundamentally about homecoming (nostos) rather than adventure, though the adventures are essential to developing its themes. Homecoming is the poem's central purpose — every episode, character and theme serves Odysseus's return to Ithaca. The opening lines declare the subject as the man who strove to win his friends' return. Penelope's faithfulness, Telemachus's journey and the suitors' occupation all revolve around Odysseus's absence. Even the adventures serve homecoming: each monster tests whether his desire for nostos can overcome the temptations of forgetfulness, comfort and knowledge. The second half, set entirely in Ithaca, is purely about homecoming — the disguise, planning and violent reclamation. However, the adventures are not merely obstacles — they are what makes Odysseus the hero who deserves to return. Without the Cyclops, Circe and the Underworld, his homecoming would lack the depth of suffering and demonstration of arete that make it meaningful. The adventures also allow Homer to explore themes that homecoming alone could not: civilisation, heroism and the relationship between mortals and gods.
