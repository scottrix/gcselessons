B12: Pathogens and Disease

Foundation Higher All Boards

Learn about the different types of pathogens — bacteria, viruses, fungi, and protists — and how they cause disease in humans.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define pathogen and describe the four main types
  • Explain how pathogens cause disease by damaging cells or producing toxins
  • Describe the structure of bacteria and viruses
  • Explain how pathogens enter and spread through the body

Prerequisites

  • Basic knowledge of microorganisms from B1 and B5

Materials & Equipment

  • Microscope images of different pathogen types
  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Structure diagrams of bacteria and viruses
  • Card sort activity on pathogen types

Lesson 1: Types of Pathogen

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Disease Brainstorm

Write "Pathogens" on the board. Students list as many diseases as they can in one minute. Discuss which are caused by different types of pathogen.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
A pathogen is any microorganism that causes disease. Four main types:
  • Bacteria: single-celled prokaryotic organisms. Very small (0.2-5 µm). Some have plasmids (extra circular DNA). Some have flagella for movement. Many are harmless; some cause disease by multiplying rapidly in the body and/or producing toxins (poisons). Examples: Salmonella (food poisoning), Streptococcus (throat infections), Mycobacterium tuberculosis (TB).
  • Viruses: much smaller than bacteria (20-300 nm). NOT cells — they are genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside a protein coat. They cannot reproduce on their own — they invade host cells and hijack the cell's machinery to make copies of themselves. This damages or kills the host cell. Examples: Influenza virus (flu), HIV (AIDS), SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19).
  • Fungi: can be single-celled (yeasts) or multicellular (molds). Some cause disease by growing on body surfaces (skin, lungs). Examples: Candida albicans (thrush), Trichophyton (athlete's foot).
  • Protists (Protoctists): single-celled eukaryotic organisms. Some cause disease. Usually spread by vectors (insects). Example: Plasmodium (malaria, spread by mosquitoes).
How pathogens cause disease:
  • Damage host cells directly (e.g. viruses bursting out of cells)
  • Produce toxins that damage cells or interfere with body functions
  • Trigger immune responses that cause symptoms (e.g. inflammation, fever)
Worked Example

"Give two ways that bacteria cause disease." Answer: (1) By multiplying rapidly in the body and physically damaging tissues. (2) By producing toxins (poisons) that damage cells and disrupt normal body functions.

Common Misconception

"Viruses are the smallest living things"

Viruses are not considered living things by many biologists because they cannot reproduce on their own, do not carry out metabolism, and are not made of cells. They are essentially genetic material in a protein coat — they need a host cell to replicate.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide a card sort: match pathogen type to example disease. Focus on four key examples: bacteria (food poisoning), virus (flu), fungus (athlete's foot), protist (malaria).
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research how viruses differ from other pathogens — discuss whether viruses are "alive." Investigate prions (infectious proteins) as an additional type of pathogen.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Four Types

Write four disease names on the board. Students classify each by pathogen type. Quick discussion of answers.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe all four pathogen types with structures and examples
  • Getting there: Can name the four types but confuses structures or examples
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish between pathogen types

Lesson 2: How Pathogens Spread and Infect

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Germ Theory

Ask: "How do you think people caught diseases before they knew about germs?" Discuss historical beliefs (bad air, miasma) and how germ theory changed our understanding.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
How pathogens enter the body:
  • Respiratory tract: inhaling droplets from coughs/sneezes (flu, COVID-19, TB)
  • Digestive tract: swallowing contaminated food or water (Salmonella, cholera)
  • Skin: entering through cuts or abrasions (tetanus, athlete's foot)
  • Genital tract: sexual contact (HIV, chlamydia)
  • Vectors: injected by biting insects (malaria via mosquitoes, sleeping sickness via tsetse flies)
How pathogens spread between people:
  • Direct contact (touching, kissing, sexual contact)
  • Droplet spread (coughing, sneezing — droplets travel ~1 metre)
  • Airborne (tiny droplet nuclei that remain suspended in air for hours)
  • Waterborne (contaminated water supplies)
  • Faecal-oral (poor hygiene after using the toilet)
  • Vectors (insects that carry pathogens from one person to another)
Pathogen examples in detail:
  • Bacteria (Salmonella): enters via mouth in contaminated food → multiplies in the gut → produces toxins → causes inflammation of the gut lining → symptoms: vomiting, diarrhoea, stomach cramps.
  • Virus (Influenza): enters via respiratory tract → invades epithelial cells lining the airways → hijacks cell machinery to make copies → cells burst, releasing new viruses → triggers immune response → symptoms: fever, aches, runny nose.
  • Protist (Plasmodium/malaria): injected by mosquito bite → travels to liver → multiplies → enters red blood cells → destroys them → symptoms: fever, chills, anaemia, can be fatal.
Worked Example

"Explain how the influenza virus causes disease." Answer: (1) Virus enters the respiratory tract via droplets from an infected person. (2) Virus attaches to and invades epithelial cells lining the airways. (3) Inside the cell, viral RNA is used to make new virus particles. (4) The host cell bursts (lyses), releasing thousands of new viruses. (5) The immune system responds, causing inflammation and symptoms (fever, aches). (6) New viruses spread to adjacent cells and to other people via coughing.

Common Misconception

"Antibiotics work against all pathogens"

Antibiotics only work against bacteria. They have no effect on viruses, fungi, or protists. Using antibiotics for viral infections is ineffective and contributes to antibiotic resistance.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide a template for describing how one pathogen causes disease: enters through → travels to → multiplies in → damages by → symptoms include. Complete for one example together.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research the difference between endemic, epidemic, and pandemic with examples. Discuss why some diseases (e.g. malaria) are restricted to certain geographical areas.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Entry Points

Name a disease and students must state how the pathogen enters the body. Rapid-fire round with 6-8 diseases.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe how pathogens enter the body and cause disease with specific examples
  • Getting there: Knows how pathogens enter but cannot describe the disease process
  • Not yet: Cannot describe how pathogens spread or cause disease

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a comparison table of the four pathogen types: structure, size, reproduction, examples of diseases
  • Research one pandemic in history (e.g. Spanish flu, Black Death, COVID-19) and write a 200-word summary
  • Design an infographic showing how one pathogen enters, spreads, and causes disease
  • Write a newspaper report about a fictional disease outbreak, describing how it spreads and what precautions people should take
