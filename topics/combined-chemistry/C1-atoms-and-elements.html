Lesson 2: Core Concepts: c1 atoms and elements

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on c1 atoms and elements. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Atom: The smallest particle of an element that can still be recognised as that element. Atoms are the building blocks of all matter.
Element: A substance made up of only one type of atom. Each element has a unique atomic number and is listed on the Periodic Table.
Compound: A substance made from two or more elements that are chemically combined in fixed proportions. Compounds can only be separated into their elements by chemical reactions.
Mixture: Two or more substances that are mixed together but are not chemically combined. Mixtures can be separated by physical methods such as filtration, distillation or chromatography.
Chemical symbol: A one- or two-letter abbreviation used to represent an element (e.g. O for oxygen, Na for sodium).
Law of conservation of mass: No atoms are created or destroyed in a chemical reaction, so the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the equation.

Lesson 3: Application: c1 atoms and elements

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Atom, Element, Compound. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Foundation Define the term compound and give one example.

Answer: A compound is a substance made from two or more elements that are chemically combined in fixed proportions. Example: water (H₂O) is a compound of hydrogen and oxygen.

Q2: Foundation Explain the difference between a mixture and a compound.

Answer: A compound has elements chemically combined in fixed proportions and can only be separated by chemical reactions. A mixture has substances not chemically combined, with variable proportions, and can be separated by physical methods (filtration, distillation, etc.).

Q3: Foundation Balance the following equation: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

Answer: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Count: Left = H: 4, O: 2; Right = H: 4, O: 2. Balanced.

Q4: Higher Write a balanced symbol equation with state symbols for the reaction of calcium with oxygen to form calcium oxide, CaO.

Answer: 2Ca(s) + O₂(g) → 2CaO(s). Calcium is a solid metal, oxygen is a gas, and calcium oxide is a solid.

Q5: Higher Balance the following equation: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃

Answer: 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃. Check: Left = Al: 4, O: 6; Right = Al: 4, O: 6. Balanced.

Q6: Foundation Give the chemical symbols for: sodium, potassium, iron, chlorine, calcium.

Answer: Sodium = Na, Potassium = K, Iron = Fe, Chlorine = Cl, Calcium = Ca. (Na and K come from their Latin names: natrium and kalium.)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Extended Answer 6 marks: Explain the differences between elements, compounds and mixtures. <div class="

An element is a substance made of only one type of atom and cannot be broken down chemically (e.g. Fe, O₂). A compound is a substance made from two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions; it can only be separated into elements by chemical reactions (e.g. H₂O always has 2 H : 1 O). A mixture contains two or more substances not chemically combined, so the substances retain their individual properties and proportions can vary; mixtures can be separated by physical methods such as filtration, distillation or chromatography. Mark scheme: 1 mark for each correct definition (element, compound, mixture — up to 3); 1 mark for stating compounds have fixed proportions; 1 mark for stating mixtures have variable proportions; 1 mark for correct separation methods for each; 1 mark for using examples.

Exam Tips: Learn the definition of compound precisely: examiners look for "chemically combined" and "fixed proportions" - both must be mentioned for full marks | When asked about the difference between mixtures and compounds, always state at least two differences for full marks | Never change subscripts in a formula when balancing - only add numbers in front of formulae | Always check your balanced equation by counting atoms of each element on both sides | State symbols: remember (aq) means dissolved in water, not just "watery" - it is distinct from (l) | Common mistake: writing Na for sodium is correct, but writing NA or nA is wrong - symbols have a capital letter first, lowercase second
Common Errors: Watch Out! Atoms and molecules are the same thing. Wrong: atoms and molecules are the same Correct: molecules contain two or more atoms chemically bonded together; atoms are the smallest unit of an element Compounds can be separated by physical methods. Wrong: compounds can be separated physically Correct: compounds can only be separated by chemical reactions that break the chemical bonds

Diagram B shows two types of atoms joined together in fixed groups. Diagram C shows two types of atoms mixed together but not joined. Question: Classify each substance as an element, compound or mixture. Explain your reasoning for each. Answer: A = element (one type of atom only); B = compound (two types of atom chemically bonded in fixed groups); C = mixture (two types of atom not chemically combined, randomly arranged).
