PE28: Arousal, Anxiety, and Stress Management

Foundation Higher All Boards

Drive theory, inverted-U, catastrophe theory; relaxation techniques

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the key concepts and principles of arousal, anxiety, and stress management
  • Apply knowledge of arousal, anxiety, and stress management to real-world engineering contexts
  • Analyse and evaluate arousal, anxiety, and stress management scenarios and data
  • Develop practical skills related to arousal, anxiety, and stress management

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of physical education concepts
  • Ability to follow safety procedures in practical settings

Materials & Equipment

  • Workshop PPE: safety glasses, steel-toe boots, ear protection
  • Relevant tools and equipment for practical demonstrations
  • Material samples for handling and testing
  • Technical drawing equipment (drawing board, set squares, compass)
  • Measuring instruments (vernier calipers, micrometers, steel rules)
  • Access to workshop machinery (lathe, mill, drill press) as required
  • Risk assessment templates and COSHH sheets

  • Safety equipment (PPE, first aid)

Method Outline

  1. Risk assessment and safety briefing
  2. Equipment setup and calibration
  3. Data collection following standardised protocol
  4. Repeat measurements for reliability
  5. Data analysis and graphical representation
  6. Evaluation of method and sources of error
  7. Conclusion linking to theory

Safety Considerations

  • Complete risk assessment before starting
  • Appropriate PPE must be worn throughout
  • Supervision required for all practical work
  • Emergency procedures understood by all participants

Assessment Criteria (Practical)

  • Competent: Safely completes procedure, collects valid data, draws valid conclusions
  • Developing: Completes with minor errors, data mostly valid, conclusions partially supported
  • Not yet: Safety concerns, incomplete data, or invalid conclusions
