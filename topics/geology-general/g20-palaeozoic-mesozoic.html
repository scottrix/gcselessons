Lesson 2: Core Concepts: palaeozoic &amp; mesozoic eras

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on palaeozoic &amp; mesozoic eras. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Palaeozoic Era (541-252 Ma): Cambrian explosion of hard-bodied life, Silurian marine life, Devonian age of fish and early land plants, Carboniferous coal forests, Permian mass extinction
Key Fact: The Cambrian explosion (~541 Ma): rapid diversification of life with hard shells and skeletons, making fossilisation much more common
Key Fact: The Carboniferous (359-299 Ma): warm, humid climate; vast swamp forests of ferns, horsetails and clubmosses formed today's coal deposits
Key Fact: The Permian-Triassic mass extinction (~252 Ma): the 'Great Dying' eliminated ~96% of marine species, caused by massive volcanism (Siberian Traps) and climate change
Key Fact: The Mesozoic Era (252-66 Ma): Triassic (recovery and early dinosaurs), Jurassic (age of dinosaurs and ammonites), Cretaceous (flowering plants, T-rex)
Key Fact: Ammonites are key Mesozoic index fossils: they evolved rapidly and were globally distributed, allowing precise correlation of Jurassic and Cretaceous rocks

Exam-Style Question

Exam Tips: Cambrian explosion = hard shells appear, fossil record suddenly rich | Carboniferous = coal from tropical swamp forests | Permian-Triassic = biggest extinction, Siberian Traps volcanism | Ammonites = key Mesozoic index fossils, rapid evolution, wide distribution
Common Errors: ✗ The Cambrian explosion was the origin of life ✓ Life existed for ~3 billion years before the Cambrian; the 'explosion' was the rapid evolution of hard-bodied organisms, which greatly increased fossilisation potential ✗ Coal formed from dinosaurs ✓ Coal formed from compressed ancient plant material (ferns, horsetails, clubmosses) in Carboniferous swamps, millions of years before dinosaurs existed ✗ The dinosaurs were killed by the Permian extinction ✓ Dinosaurs went extinct at the Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) boundary ~66 Ma; the Permian extinction (~252 Ma) occurred before dinosaurs evolved
