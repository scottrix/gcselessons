Lesson 2: Core Concepts: tenses: present, perfect and imperfect

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Regular verbs: drop -en and add: -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en. Irregular verbs (sein, haben, werden) must be memorised. Some verbs have vowel changes (e→i, e→ie) in du/er/sie forms: sprechen → du sprichst, er spricht; sehen → du siehst, er sieht.
Exam Tip: With sein verbs, the past participle does NOT add a ge- prefix if the verb ends in -ieren: studieren → ich habe studiert (NOT gestudiert). Irregular past participles must be memorised: gehen → gegangen, schreiben → geschrieben, trinken → getrunken.
Key Fact: Regular verbs: drop -en, add -te endings: ich machte, du machtest, er machte, wir machten, ihr machtet, sie machten. The imperfect is essential for reading comprehension in the exam.
German Exam Tips — Tenses: Present, Perfect and Imperfect: 1. For Tenses: Present, Perfect and Imperfect, use a range of vocabulary and complex grammatical structures to access higher marks 2. Check verb tenses, agreements and word order carefully in your written work 3. In translation tasks, translate meaning rather than word-for-word 4. When writing about Tenses: Present, Perfect and Imperfect, include opinions and justifications, not just descriptions 5. For listening and reading, focus on key words and cognates to extract meaning

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Writing task: Write about forming and using the present, perfect (perfekt) and imperfect (präteritum) tenses; when to use each. <div class="

A grade 9 answer will: use at least three tenses accurately; include complex structures (subordinate clauses, modal verbs, separable verbs); show precise grammar (cases, word order, adjective endings); express and justify opinions; include cultural knowledge about DACH countries; use a wide range of vocabulary; be well-organised with connecting words (außerdem, jedoch, trotzdem, zusammenfassend). Mark scheme: Content (relevance and detail) + Quality of language (accuracy and range) = total marks

Exam Tips: Use a range of tenses (present, past, future) to access higher marks | Include opinions with reasons (ich finde... weil... / meiner Meinung nach...) | Check case endings (nominative, accusative, dative) before finishing | Get word order right: verb-second in main clauses, verb-at-end after dass/weil/wenn | Use complex structures: subordinate clauses, modal verbs, separable verbs | Include cultural references to German-speaking countries (DACH)
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often forget word order in subordinate clauses. Wrong: Weil ich krank bin, ich bleibe zu Hause. Correct: Weil ich krank bin, bleibe ich zu Hause (in the MAIN clause after a subordinate clause, the verb comes FIRST, then the subject).
