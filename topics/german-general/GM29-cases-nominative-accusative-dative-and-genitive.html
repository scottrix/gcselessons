Lesson 2: Core Concepts: cases: nominative, accusative, dative and genitive

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: German has four cases that show the role of a noun in a sentence. Each case affects the articles (der/die/das), adjectives and pronouns.
Exam Tip: Two-way prepositions: movement towards = ACCUSATIVE (Wohin?); location/position = DATIVE (Wo?). "Ich gehe in die Stadt" (acc — movement) vs "Ich bin in der Stadt" (dat — location).
German Exam Tips — Cases: Nominative, Accusative, Dative and Genitive: 1. For Cases: Nominative, Accusative, Dative and Genitive, use a range of vocabulary and complex grammatical structures to access higher marks 2. Check verb tenses, agreements and word order carefully in your written work 3. In translation tasks, translate meaning rather than word-for-word 4. When writing about Cases: Nominative, Accusative, Dative and Genitive, include opinions and justifications, not just descriptions 5. For listening and reading, focus on key words and cognates to extract meaning

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Writing task: Write about the four german cases; when to use each; definite and indefinite articles in all cases; prepositions taking specific cases. <div class="

A grade 9 answer will: use at least three tenses accurately; include complex structures (subordinate clauses, modal verbs, separable verbs); show precise grammar (cases, word order, adjective endings); express and justify opinions; include cultural knowledge about DACH countries; use a wide range of vocabulary; be well-organised with connecting words (außerdem, jedoch, trotzdem, zusammenfassend). Mark scheme: Content (relevance and detail) + Quality of language (accuracy and range) = total marks

Exam Tips: Use a range of tenses (present, past, future) to access higher marks | Include opinions with reasons (ich finde... weil... / meiner Meinung nach...) | Check case endings (nominative, accusative, dative) before finishing | Get word order right: verb-second in main clauses, verb-at-end after dass/weil/wenn | Use complex structures: subordinate clauses, modal verbs, separable verbs | Include cultural references to German-speaking countries (DACH)
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often forget word order in subordinate clauses. Wrong: Weil ich krank bin, ich bleibe zu Hause. Correct: Weil ich krank bin, bleibe ich zu Hause (in the MAIN clause after a subordinate clause, the verb comes FIRST, then the subject).
