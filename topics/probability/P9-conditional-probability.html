P9: Conditional Probability

Higher Only All Boards

Understand and calculate conditional probability P(A|B), use the formula P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B), solve problems using tree diagrams, two-way tables, and Venn diagrams, and distinguish conditional probability from independent events.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Higher Only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define conditional probability: P(A|B) = probability of A given that B has occurred
  • Use the formula: P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B), provided P(B) > 0
  • Calculate conditional probabilities from tree diagrams, two-way tables, and Venn diagrams
  • Understand the relationship: P(A∩B) = P(A|B) × P(B) = P(B|A) × P(A)
  • Recognise independence: P(A|B) = P(A) and P(B|A) = P(B)
  • Solve "reverse conditional" problems (Bayes' theorem context)
  • Apply conditional probability to real-world contexts (medical testing, quality control)

Prerequisites

  • All Foundation probability topics (P1-P8)
  • Tree diagrams for dependent events (P8)
  • Two-way tables and Venn diagrams
  • Algebraic manipulation of fractions and equations
  • Set notation: ∩ (intersection), ∪ (union), ' (complement)

Materials & Equipment

  • Tree diagram templates (pre-drawn for conditional problems)
  • Two-way table and Venn diagram templates
  • Medical testing / screening scenario cards
  • Mini whiteboards for formula practice
  • Calculators

Lesson 1: Conditional Probability Formula and Two-Way Tables

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Given That...

Class survey: 30 students. 18 study French, 15 study Spanish, 8 study both. A student is chosen at random. Given they study French, what's the probability they also study Spanish? Students discuss: "Given French" → restrict to 18 French students. 8 of them study Spanish → 8/18 = 4/9.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Conditional Probability Definition: P(A|B) = "Probability of A given B" = probability that event A occurs, given that we know event B has already occurred. The sample space is reduced to just the outcomes in B.
Formula: P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B), provided P(B) > 0.
  • P(A∩B) = probability of both A and B occurring
  • P(B) = probability of B occurring (the "given" condition)
  • Think: "Out of all B outcomes, what fraction are also A?"
From Two-Way Tables:
  • P(A|B) = (Frequency of A and B) / (Total Frequency of B)
  • Use ROW total or COLUMN total as denominator depending on condition
  • Example: "Given Male" → use Male row total as denominator
From Venn Diagrams:
  • P(A|B) = (Number in overlap) / (Number in B circle)
  • Visual: Shade B circle, then see what fraction of shaded region is also in A
Key Relationships:
  • P(A∩B) = P(A|B) × P(B) = P(B|A) × P(A)
  • P(A|B) = P(A) ⇔ A and B are independent
  • P(A|B) + P(A'|B) = 1 (complement still works in conditional space)
Worked Example

Two-way table: 100 people surveyed on Smoker (Yes/No) and Lung Disease (Yes/No).
| | Disease Yes | Disease No | Total |
| Smoker | 15 | 25 | 40 |
| Non-smoker | 5 | 55 | 60 |
| Total | 20 | 80 | 100 |
Find:
(a) P(Disease | Smoker)
(b) P(Smoker | Disease)
(c) P(Disease | Non-smoker)
(d) Are Smoking and Disease independent?

Answers:
(a) P(D|S) = 15/40 = 0.375
(b) P(S|D) = 15/20 = 0.75
(c) P(D|NS) = 5/60 = 1/12 ≈ 0.083
(d) P(D) = 20/100 = 0.2. P(D|S) = 0.375 ≠ 0.2. NOT independent. Smoking increases disease probability.

Common Misconception: P(A|B) = P(B|A). "Probability of disease given smoker = probability of smoker given disease." Correction: These are DIFFERENT. P(D|S) = 15/40 = 0.375. P(S|D) = 15/20 = 0.75. The denominators are different (total smokers vs total with disease). Only equal if P(A) = P(B).

Differentiation

Support:
Provide two-way tables with all totals. Use the phrase "Out of the [condition] group, how many [event]?" Colour-code the condition row/column. Practice identifying denominator.
Stretch:
Algebraic two-way table: "Total = 200. P(A) = 0.6. P(B|A) = 0.4. P(B|A') = 0.2. Complete the table and find P(A|B)." Prove: P(A|B) = P(A) ⇔ P(A∩B) = P(A)P(B) ⇔ independence.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Formula Practice

Quick-fire: Given P(A∩B) and P(B), find P(A|B).
1. P(A∩B)=0.2, P(B)=0.5 → 0.4
2. P(A∩B)=3/20, P(B)=1/4 → 3/5
3. P(A∩B)=0.12, P(B)=0.3 → 0.4

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Uses formula correctly; extracts data from two-way tables and Venn diagrams; understands denominator shift
  • Getting there: Uses formula but confuses which probability goes in denominator; struggles with Venn diagrams
  • Not yet: Thinks P(A|B) = P(A∩B); cannot identify conditional from wording

Lesson 2: Conditional Probability from Tree Diagrams

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Tree Diagram Conditional

Show a completed tree diagram for: Bag A (3R,2B), Bag B (1R,4B). Choose bag at random, then draw counter. End probabilities: Bag A & R = 0.3, Bag A & B = 0.2, Bag B & R = 0.1, Bag B & B = 0.4. Find P(Bag A | Red).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Conditional Probability from Tree Diagrams:
  • P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B)
  • P(A and B) = probability at end of branch where both A and B occur
  • P(B) = SUM of end probabilities of all branches where B occurs
  • Visual: Highlight all paths where B occurs. The conditional probability is the fraction of that highlighted probability that also has A.
Common Tree Diagram Conditional Problems:
  • "Given the second counter is Red, find probability the first was Blue" (Reverse conditional)
  • "Given at least one Red, find probability both are Red"
  • Medical testing: "Given positive test, find probability actually has disease"
Reverse Conditional (Bayes' Theorem Context):
  • P(Cause | Effect) = P(Effect | Cause) × P(Cause) / P(Effect)
  • P(Effect) = P(Effect | Cause)P(Cause) + P(Effect | No Cause)P(No Cause) (Law of Total Probability)
  • Tree diagrams naturally show this structure
Independence Check on Tree:
  • If second-stage probabilities are IDENTICAL regardless of first branch → Independent
  • If they DIFFER → Dependent, and conditional probability ≠ unconditional
Worked Example

A factory has two machines: M1 produces 60% of items, M2 produces 40%. M1 defect rate = 2%, M2 defect rate = 5%. An item is chosen at random.
(a) Draw a tree diagram.
(b) Find P(Defective).
(c) Given the item is defective, find P(it came from M1).
(d) Given the item is not defective, find P(it came from M2).

Answers:
(a) First branches: M1 (0.6), M2 (0.4). From M1: D (0.02), ND (0.98). From M2: D (0.05), ND (0.95).
(b) P(D) = P(M1∩D) + P(M2∩D) = (0.6×0.02) + (0.4×0.05) = 0.012 + 0.02 = 0.032
(c) P(M1|D) = P(M1∩D) / P(D) = 0.012 / 0.032 = 12/32 = 3/8 = 0.375
(d) P(ND) = 1 - 0.032 = 0.968. P(M2∩ND) = 0.4×0.95 = 0.38. P(M2|ND) = 0.38 / 0.968 = 380/968 = 95/242 ≈ 0.393

Common Misconception: P(M1|D) = P(D|M1). "Probability it came from M1 given defective = defect rate of M1." Correction: P(D|M1) = 0.02. P(M1|D) = 0.375. Very different! P(M1|D) depends on how much each machine produces AND their defect rates. This is the "base rate fallacy" — ignoring the prior probability (production proportion).

Differentiation

Support:
Provide tree diagram with structure. Colour-code: "Given B" paths in one colour. Use a table: End Outcome | Probability | B? | A and B? Then sum columns.
Stretch:
Three-stage tree with conditional: "Bag 1 (3R,2B), Bag 2 (4R,1B). Choose bag, draw counter, note colour, REPLACE, draw again. Given both draws Red, which bag was more likely?" Derive Bayes' theorem formula from tree: P(A|B) = P(B|A)P(A) / [P(B|A)P(A) + P(B|A')P(A')].

Plenary (5 minutes)

Interpret the Result

Medical test: Disease prevalence 1%. Test sensitivity 99% (P(Pos|Dis)=0.99). Specificity 95% (P(Neg|NoDis)=0.95). P(Dis|Pos) = ? Students calculate: ≈ 0.167. Discuss: "Positive test but only 16.7% chance of disease? Why?" (Low prevalence, false positives outnumber true positives).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates conditional from tree diagrams; solves reverse conditional (Bayes); interprets results in context
  • Getting there: Calculates P(A and B) and P(B) from tree but struggles with reverse conditional
  • Not yet: Cannot identify correct branches for P(B); uses wrong denominator

Lesson 3: Problem Solving and Venn Diagrams

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Venn Diagram Conditional
Draw Venn: A (12), B (15), Overlap (5), Outside (8). Total 40. Find P(A|B) = 5/15 = 1/3. Find P(B|A) = 5/12.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Conditional Probability from Venn Diagrams:
  • Draw Venn with probabilities (not just frequencies)
  • P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B)
  • On Venn: P(B) = entire B circle. P(A∩B) = overlap.
  • Can solve for missing values using: P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B)
Algebraic Conditional Problems:
  • Given P(A), P(B), P(A|B), find P(A∩B), P(A∪B), P(A'∩B'), etc.
  • Use P(A∩B) = P(A|B) × P(B)
  • Check: P(A∩B) ≤ min(P(A), P(B))
Problem-Solving Strategy:
  1. Identify events A and B
  2. Identify what is given (condition) and what is asked
  3. Choose representation: Tree, Two-way table, Venn, or Formula
  4. Extract/calculate P(A∩B) and P(B)
  5. Apply P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B)
  6. Check answer is between 0 and 1
  7. Interpret in context
Exam Question Types:
  • Direct calculation from table/tree/Venn
  • "Given that..." wording
  • Reverse conditional (Bayes)
  • Show independence / dependence
  • Algebraic: find x given conditional probabilities
Worked Example

Events A and B: P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, P(A|B) = 0.3.
(a) Find P(A∩B).
(b) Find P(A∪B).
(c) Find P(A|B').
(d) Are A and B independent?

Answers:
(a) P(A∩B) = P(A|B) × P(B) = 0.3 × 0.5 = 0.15
(b) P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B) = 0.4 + 0.5 - 0.15 = 0.75
(c) P(A∩B') = P(A) - P(A∩B) = 0.4 - 0.15 = 0.25. P(B') = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5. P(A|B') = 0.25/0.5 = 0.5
(d) P(A|B) = 0.3 ≠ P(A) = 0.4. NOT independent.

Common Misconception: P(A|B') = P(A|B). "Probability of A given not B is the same as given B." Correction: Usually different. P(A|B') = P(A∩B')/P(B'). Need to calculate P(A∩B') = P(A) - P(A∩B).

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a structured template: "Event A = ..., Event B = ... Given: ... = ... Asked: P(...|...) = ? Formula: P(A|B) = ... / ... = ...". Use decimal probabilities throughout.
Stretch:
"P(A) = 0.6, P(B|A) = 0.7, P(B|A') = 0.2. Find P(A|B)." (Bayes). "Three events A,B,C mutually exclusive exhaustive. P(A)=0.2, P(B)=0.5. P(D|A)=0.1, P(D|B)=0.4, P(D|C)=0.6. Find P(A|D)." (Generalised Bayes).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam Question Practice

Students attempt a 5-mark conditional probability question (past paper). Self-assess against mark scheme focusing on: correct formula, correct identification of P(A∩B) and P(B), correct arithmetic, answer in context.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves all problem types (direct, reverse, algebraic); chooses efficient representation; checks independence correctly
  • Getting there: Solves direct problems from tables/trees; struggles with algebraic or reverse conditional
  • Not yet: Confuses P(A|B) with P(A∩B); cannot set up formula

Homework Suggestions

  • Conditional probability worksheet: 5 two-way table, 5 tree diagram, 5 Venn diagram, 3 algebraic problems.
  • Medical testing scenario: "Disease prevalence 0.5%. Test sensitivity 98%, specificity 97%. Calculate P(Disease|Positive). Explain why result might be surprising."
  • Past paper questions: 6 conditional probability questions (15-20 marks total).
  • Create a "Conditional Probability" revision card with: formula, tree diagram method, two-way table method, Venn diagram method, independence check, common pitfalls.
