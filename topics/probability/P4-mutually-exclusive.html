P4: Mutually Exclusive Events

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understand mutually exclusive and exhaustive events, use the addition rule for mutually exclusive events, and solve problems involving complementary probabilities.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define mutually exclusive events and exhaustive events
  • Identify whether pairs of events are mutually exclusive
  • Use the addition rule: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) for mutually exclusive events
  • Understand that for exhaustive events, probabilities sum to 1
  • Solve problems using P(A) + P(A') = 1
  • Distinguish between mutually exclusive and independent events

Prerequisites

  • Probability scale and notation (P3)
  • Sample spaces and equally likely outcomes (P1, P3)
  • Basic fraction/decimal/percentage arithmetic
  • Set notation basics: union (∪), intersection (∩), complement (')

Materials & Equipment

  • Venn diagram templates (blank and pre-drawn)
  • Event cards for sorting activity (mutually exclusive / not mutually exclusive)
  • Dice, coins, spinners for practical demonstration
  • Mini whiteboards

Lesson 1: Mutually Exclusive and Exhaustive Events

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Can Both Happen?

For each pair, can both events happen simultaneously?
1. Roll a die: A = "even number", B = "number > 4"
2. Draw a card: A = "heart", B = "king"
3. Flip a coin: A = "heads", B = "tails"
4. Choose a student: A = "wears glasses", B = "has brown hair"
Discuss: Which pairs are mutually exclusive?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Mutually Exclusive Events: Two (or more) events that CANNOT happen at the same time. They have no outcomes in common. Intersection is empty: A ∩ B = ∅. Examples: Heads and Tails on one coin flip; rolling a 3 and rolling a 5 on one die roll.
NOT Mutually Exclusive: Events that CAN happen together. Example: "Heart" and "King" — the King of Hearts satisfies both.
Exhaustive Events: A set of events that covers ALL possible outcomes. At least one of the events MUST happen. The union is the entire sample space: A ∪ B ∪ C ... = S. For exhaustive events, the sum of their probabilities = 1.
Mutually Exclusive AND Exhaustive: A complete partition of the sample space. Examples: {Heads, Tails} for a coin; {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} for a die; {Red, Blue, Green} for a 3-colour spinner.
Addition Rule for Mutually Exclusive Events:
If A and B are mutually exclusive: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
In words: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)
This extends to more than two mutually exclusive events: P(A or B or C) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C)
Important: This rule ONLY works for mutually exclusive events. If events are not mutually exclusive, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B) (Higher only, covered later).
Worked Example

A bag contains 5 red, 3 blue, 2 green, and 4 yellow counters. One counter is drawn at random.
(a) Are "Red" and "Blue" mutually exclusive? Are they exhaustive?
(b) Find P(Red or Blue).
(c) Find P(not Green).
(d) The events "Red", "Blue", "Green", "Yellow" are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Verify their probabilities sum to 1.

Answers:
(a) Red and Blue are mutually exclusive (can't be both colours). They are NOT exhaustive (could be Green or Yellow).
(b) P(Red or Blue) = P(Red) + P(Blue) = 5/14 + 3/14 = 8/14 = 4/7
(c) P(not Green) = 1 - P(Green) = 1 - 2/14 = 12/14 = 6/7
(d) 5/14 + 3/14 + 2/14 + 4/14 = 14/14 = 1 ✓

Common Misconception: "Mutually exclusive means the same as independent." Correction: They are DIFFERENT concepts. Mutually exclusive = cannot happen together (P(A and B) = 0). Independent = one happening doesn't affect the probability of the other (P(A and B) = P(A)×P(B)). Mutually exclusive events with non-zero probability are NEVER independent (if A happens, B definitely didn't, so probability of B changes to 0).

Differentiation

Support:
Use Venn diagrams with no overlap for mutually exclusive events. Colour-code mutually exclusive pairs. Provide a decision flowchart: "Can both happen? Yes → Not mutually exclusive. No → Mutually exclusive."
Stretch:
Prove: If A and B are mutually exclusive and both have non-zero probability, they cannot be independent. Explore: "A and B are exhaustive. P(A) = 0.6. P(B) = 0.5. Is this possible?" (No, sum > 1). "A, B, C are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. P(A) = 2x, P(B) = 3x, P(C) = 4x. Find x and each probability."

Plenary (5 minutes)

True or False

Students hold up T/F cards:
1. "If P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.4, then A and B could be mutually exclusive." (True)
2. "If A and B are mutually exclusive, P(A and B) = 0." (True)
3. "If A and B are mutually exclusive, P(A or B) = P(A) × P(B)." (False — that's for independent)
4. "Rolling a prime and rolling an even number on a die are mutually exclusive." (False — 2 is both)

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Correctly identifies mutually exclusive/exhaustive events; applies addition rule; uses complement rule; distinguishes from independence
  • Getting there: Understands definitions but struggles to apply addition rule in context; confuses mutually exclusive with independent
  • Not yet: Cannot identify mutually exclusive events; adds probabilities incorrectly (e.g. adds for non-mutually-exclusive events)

Lesson 2: Problem Solving with Mutually Exclusive Events

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Missing Probability

Events A, B, C are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. P(A) = 0.2, P(B) = 0.5. Find P(C).
Events X, Y are mutually exclusive. P(X) = 0.3, P(X or Y) = 0.7. Find P(Y).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Problem-Solving Strategies:
  1. Identify if events are mutually exclusive (given or deduce from context)
  2. Identify if events are exhaustive (often implied by "only possible outcomes")
  3. Use P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) for mutually exclusive
  4. Use P(A) + P(A') = 1 for complements
  5. Use sum = 1 for exhaustive sets
  6. Set up equations for algebraic probabilities
Common Problem Types:
  • Missing probability: Given some probabilities of mutually exclusive exhaustive events, find the rest
  • Algebraic probabilities: Probabilities given as expressions in x; use sum = 1 to solve
  • Two-way tables / Frequency trees: Extract probabilities from data
  • Comparing likelihoods: Which event is more likely? By how much?
Key Phrases in Questions:
  • "A and B cannot both happen" → mutually exclusive
  • "Only possible outcomes are..." → exhaustive
  • "A or B or both" → NOT mutually exclusive (Higher: use inclusion-exclusion)
  • "At least one" → complement of "none"
Worked Example

A biased 4-sided spinner has sections A, B, C, D. P(A) = 0.2, P(B) = 0.3, P(C) = 2x, P(D) = x.
(a) Explain why P(A) + P(B) + P(C) + P(D) = 1.
(b) Find x.
(c) Find P(C) and P(D).
(d) The spinner is spun twice. Find P(both spins land on C).

Answers:
(a) The events are mutually exclusive and exhaustive (spinner must land on exactly one section).
(b) 0.2 + 0.3 + 2x + x = 1 → 0.5 + 3x = 1 → 3x = 0.5 → x = 1/6 ≈ 0.167
(c) P(C) = 2x = 1/3 ≈ 0.333, P(D) = 1/6 ≈ 0.167
(d) Spins are independent. P(C and C) = P(C) × P(C) = (1/3)² = 1/9 ≈ 0.111

Common Misconception: Using addition rule for non-mutually-exclusive events. "P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)" always. Correction: Only for mutually exclusive! If events can happen together, adding double-counts the overlap. Example: P(Heart or King) = 13/52 + 4/52 = 17/52 is WRONG (King of Hearts counted twice). Correct: 13/52 + 4/52 - 1/52 = 16/52.

Differentiation

Support:
Provide structured worksheet: "Step 1: Are they mutually exclusive? Step 2: Which rule? Step 3: Calculate." Use numerical probabilities first, then introduce algebraic.
Stretch:
Multi-step problems: "A bag contains red, blue, green counters. P(Red) = 0.4. P(Blue) = 2 × P(Green). Find all probabilities." Venn diagram problems with 3 mutually exclusive events. "Design a spinner with 5 sections where probabilities are in ratio 1:2:3:4:5."

Plenary (5 minutes)

Exam Question Practice

Students attempt a past paper question on mutually exclusive events in timed conditions (5 min). Then self-mark using a mark scheme.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Solves multi-step problems; sets up and solves algebraic probability equations; extracts probabilities from tables
  • Getting there: Solves straightforward problems but struggles with algebraic or multi-step
  • Not yet: Cannot set up equation from "sum = 1"; misapplies addition rule

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete worksheet: Identify mutually exclusive pairs from 15 scenarios (coin/die/cards/bags).
  • Algebraic probability problems: 5 questions where probabilities are expressions in x.
  • Create a Venn diagram poster showing: mutually exclusive events (no overlap), exhaustive events (cover whole space), both, neither.
  • Past paper questions on mutually exclusive events (5-8 marks worth).
