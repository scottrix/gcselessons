P3: Probability Scale

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understand the probability scale from 0 to 1, express probabilities as fractions, decimals, and percentages, and use language of likelihood to describe events.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the probability scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain)
  • Express probabilities as fractions, decimals, and percentages
  • Convert between different forms of probability
  • Use correct probability notation: P(A) for probability of event A
  • Place events on a probability scale using descriptive language
  • Understand that probabilities cannot be less than 0 or greater than 1

Prerequisites

  • Fractions, decimals, and percentages equivalence (Number topic)
  • Basic probability vocabulary (from P1): outcome, event, sample space
  • Ability to simplify fractions

Materials & Equipment

  • Probability scale number lines (0 to 1) for student use
  • Event cards with descriptions (e.g. "It will rain tomorrow", "A flipped coin lands on heads")
  • Dice, coins, spinners for practical probability
  • Mini whiteboards for conversions practice

Lesson 1: The Probability Scale and Notation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Likelihood Line

Draw a line on the board labelled "Impossible" at left, "Certain" at right. Ask students to place these events: "The sun rises tomorrow", "A fair die shows 7", "You roll an even number on a fair die", "It snows in the Sahara in July". Discuss placements.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Probability Scale:
  • Probability is a number between 0 and 1 inclusive
  • 0 = Impossible (event cannot happen)
  • 1 = Certain (event will definitely happen)
  • 0.5 = Even chance / Equally likely
  • Between 0 and 0.5 = Unlikely
  • Between 0.5 and 1 = Likely
Probability Notation:
  • P(A) = probability of event A occurring
  • P(A') or P(not A) = probability of event A NOT occurring
  • P(A) + P(A') = 1
Forms of Probability:
  • Fraction: e.g. 1/4, 3/8 (most common in theoretical probability)
  • Decimal: e.g. 0.25, 0.375 (useful for calculations and comparison)
  • Percentage: e.g. 25%, 37.5% (common in everyday contexts)
Converting:
  • Fraction → Decimal: divide numerator by denominator
  • Decimal → Percentage: multiply by 100
  • Percentage → Fraction: write over 100 and simplify
Key Rule: For equally likely outcomes, P(A) = (Number of favourable outcomes) / (Total number of possible outcomes)
Worked Example

A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue, and 6 green counters. One counter is drawn at random.
(a) Find P(Red) as a fraction, decimal, and percentage.
(b) Find P(not Blue) as a fraction.
(c) Mark P(Red), P(Blue), P(Green) on a probability scale.

Answers:
(a) Total = 15. P(Red) = 4/15 = 0.266... = 26.7% (or 26 2/3%)
(b) P(not Blue) = 1 - P(Blue) = 1 - 5/15 = 10/15 = 2/3
(c) P(Red) ≈ 0.27 (unlikely), P(Blue) = 0.33 (unlikely), P(Green) = 0.4 (unlikely, close to even chance)

Common Misconception: "Probability can be written as '1 in 4' or '3 out of 10' instead of a fraction." Correction: "1 in 4" is a ratio, not a probability. Probability must be a single number between 0 and 1. Write as 1/4, 0.25, or 25%. Also, probabilities cannot be greater than 1 (e.g. 1.5 or 150% is impossible).

Differentiation

Support:
Provide a probability scale with labelled benchmarks (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1). Use a conversion table: Fraction → Decimal → Percentage. Start with denominators 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100.
Stretch:
Ask: "If P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.6, can A and B be mutually exclusive? Can they be independent?" Explore probabilities expressed as algebraic fractions (e.g. P(A) = x/(x+5)).

Plenary (5 minutes)

Scale Placement

Give 5 events with probabilities: 0, 1/8, 0.45, 7/10, 1. Students place them on a blank probability scale and label with both the value and the descriptive word (impossible, very unlikely, unlikely, likely, certain).

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Fluently converts between fraction, decimal, percentage; uses P(A) notation; places events correctly on scale; knows P(A)+P(A')=1
  • Getting there: Can convert with a calculator but struggles with mental conversions; knows scale endpoints
  • Not yet: Thinks probability can be >1 or <0; cannot convert between forms; confuses "1 in 4" with 1/4

Lesson 2: Calculating and Comparing Probabilities

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Conversions

Rapid fire on mini-whiteboards: Convert to decimals: 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/5, 2/5, 1/8, 3/8, 1/3, 2/3. Convert to percentages: 0.2, 0.75, 0.125, 0.6. Convert to fractions: 60%, 12.5%, 33.3%, 80%.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Calculating Probabilities for Equally Likely Outcomes:
  • List the sample space systematically
  • Count total outcomes (n)
  • Count favourable outcomes for the event (r)
  • P(Event) = r/n
  • Simplify fraction, then convert if needed
Complementary Events:
  • P(A') = 1 - P(A)
  • Useful when it's easier to count what you DON'T want
  • Example: P(at least one 6 in two dice rolls) = 1 - P(no sixes)
Comparing Probabilities:
  • Convert all to same form (usually decimals) for easy comparison
  • Order from least likely to most likely
  • Use inequality symbols: P(A) < P(B) means A is less likely than B
Probability from Frequency Tables / Relative Frequency:
  • P(Event) ≈ Relative Frequency = Frequency of Event / Total Trials
  • This is an ESTIMATE of the true probability
  • More trials → better estimate
Worked Example

Two fair 4-sided spinners (numbered 1-4) are spun. The scores are added.
(a) List all possible totals in a sample space diagram.
(b) Find P(Total = 5) as a fraction and decimal.
(c) Find P(Total ≥ 6) as a percentage.
(d) Which is more likely: total of 3 or total of 7?

Answers:
(a) Sample space: 16 outcomes. Totals range from 2 to 8.
(b) P(Total=5) = 4/16 = 1/4 = 0.25
(c) P(Total≥6) = P(6,7,8) = (3+2+1)/16 = 6/16 = 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
(d) P(Total=3) = 2/16 = 1/8 = 0.125; P(Total=7) = 2/16 = 0.125. Equally likely.

Common Misconception: "When adding probabilities, you add the numerators and denominators." (e.g. 1/4 + 1/2 = 2/6) Correction: Only add probabilities for mutually exclusive events (covered in P4), and you must have a common denominator. 1/4 + 1/2 = 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4. Never add denominators!

Differentiation

Support:
Provide sample space diagram templates. Use two-way tables for two-dice/spinner problems. Colour-code favourable outcomes. Give a conversion reference sheet.
Stretch:
Algebraic probability: "A bag contains x red counters and (x+3) blue counters. P(Red) = 0.4. Find x." Investigate non-standard dice (e.g. Sicherman dice) that have same sum distribution as normal dice but different face numbers.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Odd One Out

Show four probabilities: 0.25, 1/4, 25%, 0.3. Three are equivalent, one is different. Students identify the odd one out and explain why. Repeat with different sets.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Calculates probabilities from sample spaces; uses complement rule; compares probabilities; converts fluently
  • Getting there: Calculates simple probabilities but struggles with complement or comparison; needs calculator for conversions
  • Not yet: Cannot list sample space systematically; adds probabilities incorrectly; cannot convert between forms

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete a probability conversion worksheet (30 conversions between fractions, decimals, percentages).
  • Create a "Probability Scale Poster" with 10 real-life events placed correctly on a 0-1 scale with fractions, decimals, percentages, and descriptive words.
  • Calculate probabilities for: (a) drawing a vowel from the letters of "MATHEMATICS", (b) rolling a prime number on a 12-sided die, (c) choosing a multiple of 3 from 1-30.
  • Write 5 events that are: impossible, very unlikely, unlikely, even chance, likely, very likely, certain.
