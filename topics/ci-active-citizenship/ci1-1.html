CI1.1: What is Active Citizenship?

Foundation Higher All Boards

Definition, participation types, volunteering, campaigning, community action, skills

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 4
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR

Lesson 1: Knowledge and Understanding

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Concept Map

Students create a mind map of what they already know about What is Active Citizenship?. Add to it throughout the lesson.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Cover essential knowledge for What is Active Citizenship?:
  • Key definitions, concepts, and terminology
  • Legal frameworks, institutions, and processes
  • Rights, responsibilities, and duties
  • Current statistics and case examples
Case Study

Examine a real citizenship case study related to What is Active Citizenship?. Identify the issues, stakeholders, legal framework, and outcomes.

Common Misconception

"Citizenship is just about knowing the law"

Citizenship involves active participation, critical thinking, and taking informed action — not just passive knowledge of legal structures.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide a glossary of key terms. Use simplified case studies. Focus on core concepts rather than complex legal details.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Analyse competing interpretations of citizenship concepts. Compare UK approach with other democracies. Consider philosophical underpinnings.

Plenary (5 minutes)

3-2-1 Summary

3 key facts, 2 questions, 1 real-world example.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Accurate knowledge of key concepts, institutions, and processes
  • Getting there: Good knowledge with minor gaps or confusions
  • Not yet: Significant gaps in core knowledge

Lesson 2: Skills - Campaigns and Legal Processes

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Skill Audit

Students rate their confidence in: research, communication, teamwork, planning, evaluation. Set personal targets.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Develop citizenship skills through What is Active Citizenship?:
  • Research skills: finding reliable sources, evaluating evidence, identifying bias
  • Campaign planning: SMART objectives, stakeholder mapping, timeline, resources
  • Legal process understanding: how laws are made, how to use legal channels, rights in practice
  • Communication: persuasive writing, public speaking, digital advocacy, petition writing
Campaign Simulation

In groups, plan a mini-campaign on an issue related to What is Active Citizenship?. Assign roles. Create a 2-minute pitch.

Extended Campaign Plan

Assessed task: Produce a detailed campaign plan including: issue analysis, objectives, target audience, methods, timeline, success criteria, risk assessment, evaluation strategy.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide campaign planning template with prompts. Work on a whole-class campaign. Focus on one skill at a time.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Require evidence of primary research (survey, interview). Analyse a real campaign's strategy and effectiveness. Consider counter-campaigns and opposition.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Peer Feedback

Groups swap campaign plans. Use WWW/EBI (What Went Well / Even Better If) feedback sheet.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Comprehensive plan with clear objectives, realistic methods, evidence of research
  • Getting there: Good plan with some gaps in research or evaluation
  • Not yet: Plan lacks focus, objectives unclear, minimal research

Lesson 3: Active Citizenship in Action

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Inspiration Gallery

Show examples of successful youth citizenship projects. Students identify what made them effective.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Put What is Active Citizenship? into action:
  • Planning and executing a citizenship action (or detailed simulation)
  • Engaging with decision-makers: MPs, councillors, school leaders, community groups
  • Using democratic processes: petitions, consultations, elections, tribunals
  • Reflecting on impact and personal development
Action Planning

Students finalise their individual or group action plan for What is Active Citizenship?. Set milestones.

Extended Reflection

Reflective account: "Evaluate the effectiveness of your citizenship action. What did you achieve? What would you do differently? How has this developed your skills as an active citizen?"

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide reflection sentence starters. Focus on one small achievable action. Use structured reflection template.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Require measurable impact evidence. Connect to broader democratic theory. Consider sustainability and legacy of action.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Commitment Card

Students write one citizenship action they commit to taking in the next month. Share with accountability partner.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Takes meaningful action, reflects critically, demonstrates skill development
  • Getting there: Participates in action, reflects with support
  • Not yet: Passive participation, minimal reflection

Homework Suggestions

  • Create summary notes or flashcards for key concepts and studies
  • Complete practice exam questions on this topic
  • Research one key study or theorist in more depth
  • Prepare a mind map linking this topic to other areas of the specification
