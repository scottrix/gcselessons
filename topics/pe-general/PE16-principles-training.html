Lesson 2: Core Concepts: principles of training

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on principles of training. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

The five principles of training are known as SPORT: S pecificity, P rogressive Overload, O verload, R eversibility, T edium
Definition: Training should be matched to the needs of the sport and the individual. The training must be relevant to the activities and components of fitness required by the performer's sport.
Definition: Gradually increasing the demands of training over time so the body continues to adapt and improve. If training stays the same, the body will plateau and no further improvements will occur.
Definition: Making the body work harder than normal to cause adaptation. The body must be stressed beyond its current capability for training adaptations to occur.
Definition: Training adaptations are lost if training stops or is significantly reduced. "Use it or lose it." The rate of reversal is roughly proportional to the rate of gain - detraining occurs faster than training.
Definition: Training must be varied to prevent boredom and maintain motivation. If the same routine is repeated day after day, performers may lose interest and stop training.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the principle of specificity and give a sporting example.

Answer: Specificity means training should be matched to the needs of the sport and the individual. Training must focus on the specific fitness components and movements required. Example: a sprinter should train for speed and power (sprint drills, plyometrics, weight training), not cardiovascular endurance through long slow distance running.

Lesson 3: Application: principles of training

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: PE Exam Tips — Principles of Training. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain the principle of specificity and give a sporting example.

Answer: Specificity means training should be matched to the needs of the sport and the individual. Training must focus on the specific fitness components and movements required. Example: a sprinter should train for speed and power (sprint drills, plyometrics, weight training), not cardiovascular endurance through long slow distance running.

Q2: What is progressive overload and why is it necessary?

Answer: Progressive overload means gradually increasing the demands of training over time so the body continues to adapt. It is necessary because if training remains the same, the body adapts to the stimulus and reaches a plateau where no further improvements occur. By progressively increasing the load, the body is forced to keep adapting.

Q3: Explain the principle of reversibility and how it affects an injured athlete.

Answer: Reversibility means training adaptations are lost when training stops or is significantly reduced ("use it or lose it"). An injured athlete who cannot train will lose fitness: cardiovascular endurance declines within 2-3 weeks, muscle atrophy begins within 1-2 weeks, and skill levels deteriorate. They must gradually rebuild fitness when returning from injury rather than resuming at their previous level.

Q4: Define each letter of the FITT principle.

Answer: F = Frequency (how often you train); I = Intensity (how hard you train); T = Time (how long you train); T = Type (what kind of training you do).

Q5: How could a weightlifter apply the FITT principle to progressively overload their training?

Answer: Frequency: increase from 3 to 4 sessions per week. Intensity: increase the weight on the bar (e.g. from 80kg to 85kg on bench press). Time: increase the duration of sessions or add more sets. Type: vary exercises to target different muscle groups or change from heavy strength work to power-focused work.

Q6: Explain the difference between SPORT and FITT.

Answer: SPORT describes the overall principles of training (Specificity, Progressive Overload, Overload, Reversibility, Tedium) - the WHY and WHAT. FITT describes how to apply progressive overload in practice (Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type) - the HOW. FITT is the practical method for implementing the SPORT principle of progressive overload.
