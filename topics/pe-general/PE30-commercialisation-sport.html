Lesson 2: Core Concepts: commercialisation of sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on commercialisation of sport. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Commercialisation: The involvement of businesses and corporations in sport for financial gain. Sport is treated as a product to be marketed, sponsored and sold. Commercialisation has transformed modern sport from amateur participation into a multi-billion-pound global industry.
The Golden Triangle: The interdependent relationship between sport, media and sponsorship. Each element supports and depends on the other two, creating a cycle of mutual benefit that drives commercialisation.
Balanced View: Commercialisation has transformed sport, bringing enormous financial investment and global exposure. However, it has also changed the nature of sport, prioritising profit over tradition and creating inequalities between commercially attractive and unattractive sports. The challenge is maximising the benefits while managing the negative effects.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe the golden triangle and explain how each element depends on the other two.

Answer: The golden triangle is the interdependent relationship between sport, media and sponsorship. Sport provides content and audiences for the media. Media provides exposure and a platform for sponsors to display their brands. Sponsors provide the funding that sport needs to operate and develop. Each element depends on the other two: without media, sponsors wouldn't invest because their brand wouldn't be seen; without sponsors, sport can't fund itself; without sport, media has no compelling content to attract viewers.

Lesson 3: Application: commercialisation of sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Commercialisation, The Golden Triangle, Balanced View. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe the golden triangle and explain how each element depends on the other two.

Answer: The golden triangle is the interdependent relationship between sport, media and sponsorship. Sport provides content and audiences for the media. Media provides exposure and a platform for sponsors to display their brands. Sponsors provide the funding that sport needs to operate and develop. Each element depends on the other two: without media, sponsors wouldn't invest because their brand wouldn't be seen; without sponsors, sport can't fund itself; without sport, media has no compelling content to attract viewers.

Q2: Explain two positive and two negative impacts of commercialisation on performers.

Answer: Positive impacts on performers: (1) Higher wages and prize money enable professional careers and financial security, allowing full-time dedication to training. (2) Access to the best equipment and facilities funded by sponsorship improves performance. Negative impacts: (1) Pressure to perform for sponsors creates stress - sponsorship can be withdrawn after poor performance or injury. (2) Media intrusion into private lives and constant scrutiny from social media can affect mental health and wellbeing.

Q3: How has social media changed the relationship between sport and sponsorship?

Answer: Social media has changed the sport-sponsorship relationship by: (1) Allowing performers to build personal brands directly with fans, making them more valuable to sponsors based on their own social media following rather than just their sporting success. (2) Enabling sponsors to reach audiences through athletes' social media posts, not just traditional TV advertising. (3) Creating new revenue streams through sponsored posts and influencer partnerships. (4) Making the relationship more immediate and interactive, but also more fragile as any controversy spreads rapidly online.

Q4: Explain why commercialisation can be both beneficial and harmful for spectators.

Answer: Beneficial for spectators: More live sport is available through TV, streaming and social media than ever before; the quality of performance is higher due to professionalisation; stadiums have better facilities funded by commercial revenue. Harmful for spectators: Subscription TV is expensive, pricing some fans out of watching their sport; ticket prices have risen significantly; kickoff times are changed to suit TV schedules, not fans; commercial breaks interrupt the viewing experience.

Q5: Describe three types of sponsorship in sport, giving a specific example of each.

Answer: Financial sponsorship: Direct monetary payment, e.g. Emirates Airlines sponsoring Arsenal's stadium and shirt for £200m. Equipment sponsorship: Providing kit and sport-specific equipment, e.g. Adidas supplying the kit for the British Olympic team. Facility sponsorship: Naming rights and venue funding, e.g. Etihad Airways sponsoring Manchester City's Etihad Stadium.

Q6: "Commercialisation has ruined sport." To what extent do you agree with this statement? Give reasoned arguments for both sides.

Answer: Agree: Commercialisation has changed rules and traditions for TV (e.g. T20 cricket), fixture schedules prioritise TV over fans, ticket prices exclude traditional supporters, the gap between rich and poor sports widens, and financial incentives threaten integrity (e.g. match-fixing risks). Disagree: Commercialisation has funded professional careers, built world-class facilities, increased participation through higher-profile sport, and enabled global audiences to access sport. Without commercial funding, most professional sport would not exist. Conclusion: Commercialisation has both positive and negative effects, and its impact depends on how well the balance between profit and sporting integrity is managed.

Exam Tips: Always explain the golden triangle as a three-way relationship, not just sport + money | When discussing impacts, address specific stakeholder groups separately | Give both positive AND negative impacts for balanced evaluation | Use real examples of sponsorship deals to demonstrate knowledge | Remember that social media is now a major part of the media landscape | For evaluation questions, present both sides then give a reasoned conclusion
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often describe the golden triangle as just "sport, money, TV." Wrong: "The golden triangle is sport, money and TV." Correct: "The golden triangle is the interdependent relationship between sport, media and sponsorship. Each element supports and depends on the other two - sport provides content for media, media provides exposure for sponsors, and sponsors provide funding for sport." Students often focus only on positive impacts. Wrong: "Commercialisation is good because it gives players more money." Correct: "Commercialisation benefits performers through higher wages, but negatively impacts them through increased media pressure and potential loss of autonomy over sponsorsh
