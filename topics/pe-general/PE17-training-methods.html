Lesson 2: Core Concepts: training methods

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on training methods. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: A series of exercises performed one after another at different stations. Each station targets a different muscle group or fitness component, with a set time or number of reps at each station, followed by a short rest before moving to the next.
Definition: Exercising at a steady, moderate intensity for a sustained period without rest breaks. The heart rate is maintained at 60-80% of maximum heart rate throughout the session.
Definition: "Speed play" - a form of continuous training where the intensity varies throughout the session. The performer alternates between fast and slow running (or other activities) over varying terrain, without structured rest periods.
Definition: Alternating periods of high-intensity exercise with periods of rest or low-intensity recovery. HIIT (High Intensity Interval Training) involves very intense bursts (85-95% max HR) with short recovery periods.
Definition: Using resistance (free weights, machines, body weight) to develop muscular strength, endurance and hypertrophy. The programme can be adapted by changing reps, sets, weight and rest periods.
Definition: Exercises that involve rapid, powerful movements using the stretch-shortening cycle of muscles. The muscle is stretched (eccentric contraction) then immediately contracted (concentric contraction) to produce an explosive movement.

Lesson 3: Application: training methods

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: PE Exam Tips — Training Methods. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe circuit training and explain two advantages and two disadvantages.

Answer: Circuit training involves performing a series of exercises at different stations, one after another, with a short rest between stations. Advantages: targets multiple fitness components; reduces tedium through variety. Disadvantages: requires equipment and space; may not develop one specific component enough for elite performers.

Q2: What is Fartlek training and which two energy systems does it develop?

Answer: Fartlek training ("speed play") is a form of continuous training where the intensity varies - alternating between fast and slow running over varied terrain. It develops both the aerobic system (during slower sections) and the anaerobic system (during sprints and high-intensity bursts).

Q3: Explain the difference between continuous training and interval training.

Answer: Continuous training involves exercising at a steady, moderate intensity for a sustained period without rest (e.g. a 30-minute jog at 70% max HR). Interval training alternates periods of high-intensity exercise with periods of rest or low-intensity recovery (e.g. 8 × 400m sprints with 2-minute recovery). Continuous is purely aerobic; interval develops both aerobic and anaerobic fitness.

Q4: Which training method is most appropriate for developing power? Explain why.

Answer: Plyometric training is most appropriate for developing power. It uses the stretch-shortening cycle of muscles, where a rapid eccentric stretch is followed immediately by an explosive concentric contraction. This develops the ability to produce maximal force in the shortest time (power = strength × speed). Examples include box jumps and depth jumps.

Q5: A footballer wants to improve their cardiovascular endurance. Which two training methods would you recommend and why?

Answer: Fartlek training and interval training. Fartlek replicates the variable pace of football (jogging, sprinting, walking) and develops both aerobic and anaerobic fitness. Interval training with short, high-intensity bursts and recovery periods mimics the intermittent nature of football and improves both cardiovascular endurance and the ability to recover quickly between efforts.

Q6: Describe plyometric training and explain why it carries a high risk of injury.

Answer: Plyometric training involves rapid, powerful movements using the stretch-shortening cycle (e.g. box jumps, depth jumps, bounding). It carries a high risk of injury because the explosive movements place enormous stress on joints (especially knees and ankles) and the eccentric loading on muscles can cause micro-tears. It requires a foundation of strength and good technique to perform safely.
