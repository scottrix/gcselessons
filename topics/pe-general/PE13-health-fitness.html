Lesson 2: Core Concepts: health and fitness

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on health and fitness. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

WHO Definition: The World Health Organisation defines health as "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity."
Important: Health is NOT just the absence of illness. A person can be free from disease but still have poor mental or social health. True health requires well-being in all three areas.
Definition: Fitness is "the ability to meet the demands of the environment" or "the ability to meet the demands of a physical task." It refers to how well the body can cope with the physical challenges it faces.
Key Concept: Health and fitness are related but they are NOT the same thing. A person can be fit but not healthy, or healthy but not fit. Being fit can contribute to good health, but it does not guarantee it.
Regular exercise improves both health and fitness, but they are improved through different mechanisms:
Yes.: A person can be physically fit enough to perform a task even while having a health condition. For example, athletes with asthma (like runner Paula Radcliffe) can be extremely fit for their sport while managing a long-term health condition. However, ill health typically makes it harder to achieve and maintain fitness.
TermMeaningExample
Fit AND healthyThe person can meet the physical demands of their activity AND has complete physical, mental and social well-beingA recreational runner who trains regularly, eats well, has good social connections and positive mental health
Fit but NOT healthyThe person can meet physical demands but has health issues in one or more dimensionsAn elite athlete with an eating disorder (mental health issue); a footballer playing through injury; an athlete with depression
Healthy but NOT fitThe person is free from disease and has good well-being, but cannot meet the physical demands of specific tasksA person who is disease-free and happy but leads a sedentary lifestyle and cannot run for a bus without becoming breathless
Neither fit nor healthyThe person has health problems and cannot meet physical demandsA person with chronic disease who is physically inactive and socially isolated

Lesson 3: Application: health and fitness

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: WHO Definition, Key Concept, Yes.. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: State the WHO definition of health.

Answer: "A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity."

Q2: Define fitness.

Answer: Fitness is the ability to meet the demands of the environment / the ability to meet the demands of a physical task.

Q3: Explain how a person can be fit but not healthy, giving a specific example.

Answer: A person can be physically capable of performing at a high level (fit) but still have health problems. For example, an elite marathon runner may be extremely fit for their sport but may suffer from an eating disorder (poor mental health) or compete through injury (poor physical health). Fitness relates only to physical capacity, while health encompasses physical, mental and social well-being.

Q4: How can ill health decrease fitness?

Answer: Ill health can decrease fitness because: disease reduces the body's capacity for physical work; illness may prevent training and cause de-training; conditions like flu reduce cardiovascular efficiency, muscle strength and energy levels; chronic conditions limit the ability to exercise regularly.

Q5: Describe the three dimensions of health and give an indicator for each.

Answer: Physical health - body functions efficiently without disease (indicator: healthy body weight, good energy levels). Mental health - psychological well-being (indicator: positive self-esteem, ability to cope with stress). Social health - positive relationships and interactions (indicator: supportive friendships, community involvement).

Q6: Explain why fitness does not guarantee good health.

Answer: Fitness refers only to the physical capacity to meet task demands. Health encompasses physical, mental and social well-being. A fit person can still develop mental health issues (e.g. depression, anxiety), social health problems (e.g. isolation despite athletic success), or physical conditions unrelated to fitness (e.g. asthma, diabetes). Additionally, extreme training for fitness can sometimes harm health (e.g. overtraining, eating disorders, injuries).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on health and fitness from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: You MUST learn the WHO definition of health word-for-word - it is frequently tested | Health has three dimensions: physical, mental and social - always mention all three | Health ≠ "not being ill" - the WHO definition specifically states it is more than absence of disease | Fitness is about meeting the demands of the ENVIRONMENT or TASK - use the exact wording | Be ready to explain the relationship between health and fitness with examples | Know that fitness is task-specific - a fit marathon runner may not be fit for weightlifting
