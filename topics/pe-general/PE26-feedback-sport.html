Lesson 2: Core Concepts: feedback in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on feedback in sport. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Feedback: Information received by a performer about the outcome of their action or the quality of their performance. Feedback is essential for learning and improvement as it completes the information processing loop.
Positive Feedback: Feedback that focuses on what the performer did correctly. It reinforces good technique and encourages the performer to repeat successful actions.
Negative Feedback: Feedback that identifies what the performer did incorrectly. It highlights errors and suggests corrections to improve future performance. It is NOT criticism - it is constructive information about what went wrong.
Exam Tip: "Negative feedback" does NOT mean telling someone they are bad. It means identifying what went wrong and providing constructive correction. If a coach just says "that was terrible" without explaining how to improve, this is criticism, not negative feedback.
Knowledge of Results (KR): Feedback about the outcome of a performance. It tells the performer what happened as a result of their action - whether they were successful or not.
Knowledge of Performance (KP): Feedback about the quality and technique of a performance. It tells the performer how well they executed the movement, regardless of the outcome.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the difference between knowledge of results and knowledge of performance, using a sporting example.

Answer: Knowledge of results (KR) is feedback about the outcome of performance, while knowledge of performance (KP) is feedback about the quality of technique. Example: In golf, KR is knowing the ball landed in the bunker (the result), while KP is the coach telling the player their weight transfer was late and they came over the top of the ball (the technique). KR tells you what happened; KP tells you why it happened and how to fix it.

Lesson 3: Application: feedback in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Feedback, Positive Feedback, Negative Feedback. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain the difference between knowledge of results and knowledge of performance, using a sporting example.

Answer: Knowledge of results (KR) is feedback about the outcome of performance, while knowledge of performance (KP) is feedback about the quality of technique. Example: In golf, KR is knowing the ball landed in the bunker (the result), while KP is the coach telling the player their weight transfer was late and they came over the top of the ball (the technique). KR tells you what happened; KP tells you why it happened and how to fix it.

Q2: Why is positive feedback particularly important for beginners?

Answer: Positive feedback is important for beginners because it builds their confidence and motivation, which are fragile in the early stages of learning. When beginners receive encouragement for what they do correctly, they are more likely to persist and continue practising. Without positive feedback, beginners may become discouraged by their errors and give up. Positive reinforcement also helps establish correct motor programmes by encouraging repetition of successful technique.

Q3: Describe the difference between extrinsic and intrinsic feedback and explain when each is most effective.

Answer: Extrinsic feedback comes from outside the performer (coach, video, scoreboard), while intrinsic feedback comes from within the performer (kinaesthesia, proprioception, feel). Extrinsic feedback is most effective for beginners who lack the experience to self-assess, and during skill acquisition when the performer needs external information about their technique. Intrinsic feedback is most effective for experienced performers who can self-correct, and during competition when no coach input is available.

Q4: A basketball player misses a free throw. Give an example of negative feedback a coach could give. How does this differ from criticism?

Answer: Negative feedback: "Your elbow was flared out to the side - keep it tucked under the ball and aligned with the basket." This is constructive negative feedback because it identifies the specific error and provides a clear correction. Criticism would be "That was a terrible shot" - this tells the player nothing about what went wrong or how to improve.

Q5: Explain why an elite performer relies more on intrinsic feedback than extrinsic feedback during competition.

Answer: Elite performers rely more on intrinsic feedback during competition because: coaches cannot provide feedback during play; elite performers have highly developed kinaesthetic awareness and proprioception from years of practice; they can feel whether a technique was executed correctly and self-correct for the next attempt; they have well-established motor programmes that provide internal feedback about movement quality. Extrinsic feedback is more useful in training sessions for analysis.

Q6: A coach tells a swimmer "your hand entry was too wide on that lap." Identify the type(s) of feedback being used and explain why it is appropriate for this performer.

Answer: The coach's comment "your hand entry was too wide" is: (1) Knowledge of Performance - it addresses the technique/process, not the outcome; (2) Negative feedback - it identifies an error; (3) Extrinsic feedback - it comes from the coach, not the swimmer's own senses. This is appropriate because swimmers cannot easily see their own hand entry during a stroke, so extrinsic KP from a coach is essential for identifying and correcting technical faults that the performer cannot detect through intrinsic feedback alone.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: feedback in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on feedback in sport from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Know all six types of feedback: positive, negative, KR, KP, extrinsic, intrinsic | Always use sporting examples to illustrate each type | Remember: negative feedback ≠ criticism. Negative feedback is constructive and corrective | KR = outcome/result; KP = technique/process - this is a very common exam distinction | Link feedback type to the stage of learning (beginner vs elite) | Feedback types can overlap - e.g. a comment can be both extrinsic AND negative AND KP
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse negative feedback with criticism. Wrong: "Negative feedback is when the coach tells you you're rubbish." Correct: "Negative feedback constructively identifies what went wrong and how to correct it, such as 'your elbow was out of position - tuck it in next time.'" Students often confuse KR and KP. Wrong: "KR is about your technique." Correct: "KR is about the result/outcome (did the ball go in?). KP is about the technique/process (was your foot placement correct?)."
