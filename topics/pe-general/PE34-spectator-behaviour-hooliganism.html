Lesson 2: Core Concepts: spectator behaviour and hooliganism

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on spectator behaviour and hooliganism. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Spectators: People who watch sporting events, either at the venue in person or through media coverage. Spectators are a vital part of the sporting experience, providing atmosphere, revenue and motivation for performers.
Hooliganism: Disorderly, aggressive and violent behaviour by spectators at sporting events, most commonly associated with football. Hooliganism involves organised groups of fans who engage in fighting, vandalism and intimidation, often planning confrontations in advance.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain three positive effects of spectators on sport.

Answer: Three positive effects: (1) Atmosphere - spectators create excitement through singing, chanting and organised support, making the event memorable for performers and fans. (2) Revenue - ticket sales, merchandise and food/drink generate essential income for clubs and venues, funding facilities and development. (3) Home advantage - home crowd support motivates performers and can intimidate opponents, contributing to the statistical advantage that home teams enjoy in most sports.

Lesson 3: Application: spectator behaviour and hooliganism

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Spectators, Hooliganism. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain three positive effects of spectators on sport.

Answer: Three positive effects: (1) Atmosphere - spectators create excitement through singing, chanting and organised support, making the event memorable for performers and fans. (2) Revenue - ticket sales, merchandise and food/drink generate essential income for clubs and venues, funding facilities and development. (3) Home advantage - home crowd support motivates performers and can intimidate opponents, contributing to the statistical advantage that home teams enjoy in most sports.

Q2: Describe three causes of hooliganism and explain how each contributes to spectator disorder.

Answer: Three causes: (1) Rivalries - long-standing hatred between clubs (e.g. local derbies) fuels animosity between fan groups, leading to planned confrontations. (2) Alcohol - reduces inhibitions and increases aggression, making intoxicated spectators more likely to engage in violent behaviour. (3) Gang culture - organised firms provide identity and belonging; leaders plan violence through social media, and gang mentality encourages individuals to behave aggressively as part of a group.

Q3: Explain how banning orders work and evaluate their effectiveness in reducing hooliganism.

Answer: Banning orders are court-issued legal orders that prevent known hooligans from attending football matches. Individuals must surrender their passports during international tournaments and report to police stations during match times. Breaching a banning order is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment. They are effective because they remove known troublemakers from the crowd, significantly reducing the risk of organised disorder. However, they are limited because they can only be applied to individuals who have already been identified and convicted, and new offenders may emerge. They work best in combination with other strategies like CCTV and segregation.

Q4: Why are high-risk football matches sometimes given early kick-off times? Explain the reasoning behind this strategy.

Answer: High-risk matches are given early kick-off times (12:30 or 13:30) to reduce the time available for pre-match drinking. When matches kick off early, there is less opportunity for fans to consume large amounts of alcohol in pubs before the game, which reduces the aggression and loss of inhibition associated with intoxication. Early kick-offs also reduce the time available for rival groups to organise confrontations and travel to meeting points. This strategy is moderately effective as it reduces one of the key triggers for disorder.

Q5: Describe three negative effects of spectator behaviour on sport and suggest how one of these could be addressed.

Answer: Three negative effects: (1) Violence between rival fans endangers safety and can cause serious injury. (2) Property damage to stadiums and surrounding areas costs money and resources. (3) Racist and discriminatory abuse towards players and officials creates a hostile environment. Property damage can be addressed through CCTV surveillance that identifies vandals for prosecution, combined with banning orders that remove repeat offenders from future events. The threat of identification and punishment acts as a deterrent.

Q6: Explain how the Taylor Report changed football stadiums in England and why these changes reduced hooliganism.

Answer: The Taylor Report (1990), commissioned after the Hillsborough disaster, recommended that all stadiums in the top two English divisions become all-seater. This eliminated standing terraces where crowd surging could occur and where crowd density was difficult to control. All-seater stadiums reduced hooliganism because: every spectator has an allocated seat, making identification easier; there is no standing area where crowds can gather and build group mentality; the controlled seating environment makes it harder for disorder to spread; and the overall atmosphere is more civilised, attracting families and discouraging those seeking confrontation.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on spectator behaviour and hooliganism from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always balance positive and negative effects of spectators - examiners want both | Know three causes of hooliganism and at least three prevention strategies | Explain HOW each strategy works, not just what it is | Banning orders = legal orders, not just "being banned" - use the correct term | Alcohol restrictions: specific rule = no alcohol in view of the pitch in English football | The Taylor Report and all-seater stadiums are key historical context for football
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often say alcohol is banned at football. Wrong: "Alcohol is banned at all football matches." Correct: "Alcohol cannot be consumed in view of the pitch at English football league matches, but it can be consumed in concourse areas. The restriction applies to the seating/playing area, not the entire venue." Students often describe hooliganism without explaining causes. Wrong: "Hooliganism is when fans fight." Correct: "Hooliganism is organised disorder and violence by spectators, caused by factors including rivalries, alcohol, gang culture and media influence. It is not spontaneous - it is often planned in advance through social media and encrypted messaging."
