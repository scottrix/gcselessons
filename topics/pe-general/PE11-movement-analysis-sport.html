Lesson 2: Core Concepts: movement analysis in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on movement analysis in sport. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Shoulder joint: Ball-and-socket joint allowing a wide range of movement including flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation and circumduction.
Elbow joint: Hinge joint allowing only flexion and extension.
Hip joint: Ball-and-socket joint allowing flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation and circumduction.
Knee joint: Hinge joint (modified) allowing primarily flexion and extension, with slight rotation when flexed.
Abduction: = moving a limb away from the midline of the body. Adduction = moving a limb towards the midline of the body.
Ankle joint: Hinge joint allowing plantar flexion (pointing foot down) and dorsiflexion (pulling foot up).
TermMeaningExample
Flexion at shoulderMoving the arm forwards and upwardsAnterior deltoids, pectorals
Extension at shoulderMoving the arm backwards and downwardsLatissimus dorsi, posterior deltoids
Abduction at shoulderMoving the arm away from the body sidewaysDeltoids (middle fibres)
Adduction at shoulderMoving the arm towards the body from the sidePectorals, latissimus dorsi
Flexion at elbowBending the elbow, decreasing the angleBiceps (agonist), brachialis
Extension at elbowStraightening the elbow, increasing the angleTriceps (agonist)
Flexion at hipLifting the leg forwards and upwardsIliopsoas (hip flexors), rectus femoris

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Identify the joint, movement and agonist muscle when a cricketer bowls the ball forward.

Answer: Joint: Shoulder (ball-and-socket). Movement: Flexion (the arm swings forwards and upwards). Agonist: Anterior deltoids and pectorals.

Lesson 3: Application: movement analysis in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Shoulder joint, Elbow joint, Hip joint. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Identify the joint, movement and agonist muscle when a cricketer bowls the ball forward.

Answer: Joint: Shoulder (ball-and-socket). Movement: Flexion (the arm swings forwards and upwards). Agonist: Anterior deltoids and pectorals.

Q2: Describe the movement at the knee during the upward phase of a squat and name the agonist muscle.

Answer: Movement: Extension at the knee (the angle increases as the leg straightens). Agonist: Quadriceps contract concentrically.

Q3: What movement occurs at the shoulder during the arm-raising phase of a star jump? Name the agonist muscle.

Answer: Movement: Abduction at the shoulder (the arms move away from the midline of the body). Agonist: Deltoids (middle fibres).

Q4: Explain the difference between plantar flexion and dorsiflexion, giving a sporting example of each.

Answer: Plantar flexion is pointing the foot downward (e.g. rising onto toes in a sprint start, using the gastrocnemius). Dorsiflexion is pulling the foot upward towards the shin (e.g. lifting the foot when walking uphill, using the tibialis anterior).

Q5: Describe the movement at the hip when a footballer kicks a ball and name the agonist and antagonist muscles.

Answer: Movement: Flexion at the hip (the leg swings forwards). Agonist: Iliopsoas (hip flexors). Antagonist: Gluteals (relax to allow the hip to flex).

Q6: During a press-up, describe the movement at the elbow when pushing up and state the agonist muscle.

Answer: Movement: Extension at the elbow (the arm straightens). Agonist: Triceps contracts concentrically to push the body upwards.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: movement analysis in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on movement analysis in sport from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always give: joint → movement → muscle, in that order | Use the correct joint name (shoulder, elbow, hip, knee, ankle) - don't say "arm" or "leg" | Use precise movement terms: flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, plantar flexion/dorsiflexion | Name the specific muscle as the agonist - don't just say "the muscle" | Practise analysing a range of sporting actions - this is a very common 4-6 mark question | Remember: hinge joints (elbow, knee, ankle) only allow flexion/extension; ball-and-socket (shoulder, hip) allow all movements
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think second class levers are the most common in the body. Wrong: Second class levers are the most common in the body Correct: Third class levers are the most common in the human body (e.g. biceps curl, hamstring kick). They favour range of movement and speed over force. Students often think the effort is always at the muscle. Wrong: The effort is always at the muscle Correct: The effort is where the muscle inserts on the bone, not where the muscle belly is. The insertion is usually close to the joint (fulcrum), creating a third class lever. Students often think planes and axes are the same thing. Wrong: Planes and axes are the same thing Correct: A plane is a flat
