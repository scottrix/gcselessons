Lesson 2: Core Concepts: guidance in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on guidance in sport. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Guidance: The help and direction given to a performer to assist them in learning and developing skills. Coaches use different types of guidance to support performers at different stages of learning and for different types of skill.
Visual Guidance: Showing the performer how to perform a skill through demonstrations, videos, or diagrams. The performer watches and copies the correct technique.
Verbal Guidance: Explaining how to perform a skill using spoken instructions, coaching points, or cue words. The performer listens and interprets the information.
Manual Guidance: The coach physically moves, supports or guides the performer's body to help them experience the correct movement. This provides kinaesthetic awareness of the skill.
Important: Manual guidance should be gradually reduced (faded) as the performer gains confidence and ability. If the coach continues to provide physical support for too long, the performer becomes dependent and cannot perform the skill independently.
Mechanical Guidance: Using equipment, devices or apparatus to support the performer and help them experience the correct movement. The equipment guides or constrains the movement.
TermMeaningExample
VisualDemonstration, video, diagramsBeginners learning new skills
VerbalInstructions, cues, coaching pointsExperienced performers for fine-tuning
ManualPhysical support and guidanceBeginners for dangerous/complex skills
MechanicalEquipment and devicesBeginners for safety and consistency

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe the four types of guidance used in sport and give a sporting example of each.

Answer: Visual guidance: showing a skill through demonstration, e.g. a coach demonstrating a tennis serve. Verbal guidance: explaining a skill through instructions or cue words, e.g. "keep your eye on the ball" in cricket. Manual guidance: physically moving or supporting the performer, e.g. a coach guiding a beginner's arm through a javelin throw technique. Mechanical guidance: using equipment to support the performer, e.g. a trampolining harness for practising somersaults.

Lesson 3: Application: guidance in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Guidance, Visual Guidance, Verbal Guidance. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe the four types of guidance used in sport and give a sporting example of each.

Answer: Visual guidance: showing a skill through demonstration, e.g. a coach demonstrating a tennis serve. Verbal guidance: explaining a skill through instructions or cue words, e.g. "keep your eye on the ball" in cricket. Manual guidance: physically moving or supporting the performer, e.g. a coach guiding a beginner's arm through a javelin throw technique. Mechanical guidance: using equipment to support the performer, e.g. a trampolining harness for practising somersaults.

Q2: Explain why visual guidance is particularly effective for beginners in the cognitive stage of learning.

Answer: Visual guidance is most effective for beginners because they have no prior experience of the skill and need to see what the correct technique looks like. A demonstration provides a clear mental image of the movement that the beginner can copy. Beginners in the cognitive stage learn best by watching and imitating, as they do not yet have the motor programmes stored in long-term memory to understand verbal instructions alone.

Q3: What is the main disadvantage of manual guidance and how can a coach address this?

Answer: The main disadvantage of manual guidance is that the performer may become over-reliant on the physical support and struggle to perform the skill independently. A coach can address this by gradually reducing (fading) the manual support as the performer gains confidence and ability, eventually removing it completely so the performer can execute the skill unassisted.

Q4: A swimming coach uses a pull buoy with a beginner. Name the type of guidance and explain why it is appropriate.

Answer: This is mechanical guidance. The pull buoy is a piece of equipment that supports the swimmer's legs, keeping them buoyant. It is appropriate for a beginner because it allows them to focus on their arm pull technique without having to coordinate the leg kick simultaneously. It also provides safety by preventing the swimmer from sinking, building confidence in the water.

Q5: Explain why verbal guidance is more effective for an elite performer than for a complete beginner.

Answer: Verbal guidance is more effective for elite performers because they already have well-established motor programmes and understand the basic technique. They only need small, specific verbal cues to fine-tune their performance (e.g. "hinge wrists earlier"). For a complete beginner, verbal guidance alone is often insufficient because they have no mental picture of the skill and may misunderstand or become overloaded by instructions without a visual reference.

Q6: Compare manual and mechanical guidance, explaining one similarity and two differences.

Answer: Similarity: Both manual and mechanical guidance provide physical support to help the performer experience the correct movement safely. Differences: (1) Manual guidance involves human physical contact (coach's hands), while mechanical guidance uses equipment/devices. (2) Manual guidance can be adjusted in real-time by the coach, whereas mechanical guidance provides consistent but less adaptable support.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: guidance in sport

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Exam-Style Question

Attempt a past-paper style question on guidance in sport from the exam board past paper finder (see Resources), then mark it against the scheme.

Exam Tips: Always give a sporting example when describing each type of guidance | Link guidance type to the stage of learning - beginners vs elite | Remember to discuss both advantages and disadvantages for each type | Use the term "fading" when discussing how manual/mechanical guidance should be gradually removed | Verbal guidance for beginners = short cue words only; for elite = detailed technical adjustments | Manual and mechanical both address safety and kinaesthetic awareness but differ in delivery method
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often confuse manual and mechanical guidance. Wrong: "Manual guidance is when you use a harness." Correct: "Manual guidance is when the coach physically moves or supports the performer's body with their hands. Mechanical guidance uses equipment or devices such as harnesses, floats or belts." Students often say guidance should be used independently rather than combined. Wrong: "A coach should only use visual guidance for beginners." Correct: "Coaches often combine types of guidance - for example, demonstrating a skill (visual) while giving key coaching points (verbal) to reinforce learning."
