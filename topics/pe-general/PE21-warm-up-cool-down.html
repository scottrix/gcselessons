Lesson 2: Core Concepts: warm up and cool down

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on warm up and cool down. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Definition: A warm up is a period of light exercise that prepares the body for more strenuous activity. It gradually increases heart rate, body temperature and blood flow to working muscles.
Exam Tip: Dynamic stretching is recommended for warm ups. Static stretching is more appropriate for cool downs. Make sure you know the difference.
Definition: A cool down is a period of light exercise after strenuous activity that allows the body to gradually return to its resting state. It helps remove waste products and speeds up recovery.
EPOC (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption): The elevated oxygen intake after exercise that helps repay the oxygen debt and supports the breakdown of lactic acid. An active cool down supports EPOC more effectively than complete rest.

Lesson 3: Application: warm up and cool down

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Exam Tip, PE Exam Tips — Warm Up and Cool Down. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Name the four phases of a warm up in the correct order.

Answer: Pulse raiser, stretching, skill practice, mental preparation.

Q2: Explain how a pulse raiser reduces the risk of injury during exercise.

Answer: The pulse raiser gradually increases heart rate and body temperature. Warmer muscles are more pliable and elastic, reducing the risk of tears and strains. Increased blood flow delivers more oxygen to muscles and synovial fluid is released to lubricate joints.

Q3: Why is dynamic stretching preferred over static stretching in a warm up?

Answer: Dynamic stretching involves moving muscles through their range of motion, mimicking the movements of the upcoming activity. This actively prepares the neuromuscular system. Static stretching can actually reduce muscle power output and is better suited to cool downs.

Q4: Describe the role of the cool down in removing lactic acid from the body.

Answer: The cool down maintains an elevated heart rate and continued blood flow. This circulating blood transports lactic acid from the muscles to the liver, where it is converted back to pyruvic acid and then either used for energy or converted to glycogen for storage.

Q5: Explain what is meant by blood pooling and how a cool down prevents it.

Answer: Blood pooling occurs when exercise stops suddenly and blood collects in the lower extremities due to gravity, as the muscle pump is no longer active. This reduces blood return to the heart and brain, causing dizziness or fainting. A cool down maintains gentle muscular contraction, keeping the muscle pump active and blood returning to the heart.

Q6: How does mental preparation as part of a warm up benefit a competitive performer?

Answer: Mental preparation helps the performer focus on the task, improves concentration, builds confidence through visualisation, and ensures the performer is psychologically ready to compete at their best. It can also help manage pre-competition anxiety.
