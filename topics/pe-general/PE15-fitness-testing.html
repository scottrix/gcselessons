Lesson 2: Core Concepts: fitness testing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on fitness testing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Reasons for fitness testing: Identify strengths and weaknesses - find out which components of fitness need improvement Monitor progress - compare test results over time to see if training is working Set baseline scores - establish starting points for setting targets and designing training programmes Compare against normative data - see how results compare to national averages for age and gender Provide motivation - give performers targets to aim for and a sense of achievement Assess suitability for a sport - determine if fitness levels meet the demands of a specific activity
Normative data: is a set of reference values (averages) that allow you to compare your test results against typical scores for your age and gender. Without normative data, a test score is just a number with no context.

Lesson 3: Application: fitness testing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Reasons for fitness testing, Normative data, Limitations. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Which fitness test measures agility and briefly describe its protocol.

Answer: The Illinois Agility Run measures agility. The participant runs through a course of cones in a 10m × 5m grid, including changes of direction, slalom sections and acceleration. The time taken to complete the course is measured.

Q2: Name the test used to measure cardiovascular endurance and describe how it works.

Answer: The Multi-Stage Fitness Test (Beep Test) measures cardiovascular endurance. Participants run 20m shuttles in time with audio beeps that get progressively faster. They continue until they cannot reach the line on two consecutive beeps. The level and shuttle reached is the score.

Q3: Explain three reasons why fitness testing is carried out.

Answer: (1) Identify strengths and weaknesses in different components of fitness. (2) Monitor progress over time to see if a training programme is effective. (3) Set baseline scores at the start of a programme to establish starting points for goal setting. (Other valid: compare against normative data, provide motivation, assess suitability for sport.)

Q4: Describe three limitations of fitness testing.

Answer: (1) Lack of specificity - most tests don't replicate the exact demands of a particular sport. (2) Practice effect - participants may improve just by becoming familiar with the test, not from genuine fitness gains. (3) Motivation - results depend on effort; low motivation produces artificially low scores. (Other valid: environmental factors, measurement error, not sport-specific, one-off testing.)

Q5: Which test would you use to measure each of the following: power, reaction time, flexibility?

Answer: Power = Vertical Jump Test (Sargent Jump); Reaction time = Ruler Drop Test; Flexibility = Sit and Reach Test.

Q6: Explain what normative data is and why it is important when interpreting fitness test results.

Answer: Normative data is a set of reference values (averages) for different age groups and genders. It is important because it provides context for test results - without it, a score is just a number. Normative data allows you to determine whether a score is above average, average or below average for a particular population.

Exam-Style Question

Exam Tips: You MUST know which test measures which component - this is a very common matching question | Learn the basic protocol for each test (you don't need exact cone positions, but know the general procedure) | Be ready to explain limitations - "not sport-specific" is the most important limitation | Normative data gives CONTEXT to scores - always mention this when discussing test interpretation | The Multi-Stage Fitness Test is the most commonly tested - know it in detail | Understand the difference between validity (does it measure what it claims?) and reliability (is it consistent?)
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often think fitness and health are the same thing. Wrong: Fitness and health are the same thing Correct: Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being (WHO). Fitness is the ability to meet the demands of the environment. You can be fit but unhealthy (e.g. a marathon runner with flu). Students often think static stretching is the best warm-up. Wrong: Static stretching is the best warm-up Correct: Current evidence suggests dynamic stretching is more effective in warm-ups as it mimics sporting movements.
