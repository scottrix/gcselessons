B20: Aerobic Respiration

Foundation Higher All Boards

Learn the aerobic respiration equation, where it occurs in cells, and how energy released is used by organisms for life processes.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Write the word and balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration
  • Explain where respiration occurs in cells
  • Describe the uses of energy released during respiration
  • Explain how metabolic rate varies with activity, temperature, and age

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of mitochondria as the site of respiration (B1)
  • Knowledge of the photosynthesis equation for comparison (B18)

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Diagram of a mitochondrion
  • Heart rate measuring equipment (pulse oximeter or manual pulse check)

Lesson 1: The Respiration Equation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Think About It

Ask: "Why do you breathe harder when you exercise?" Students think and share. Introduce the idea that exercise requires more energy, which comes from respiration, which requires more oxygen.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Aerobic respiration: the chemical process in which glucose is broken down in the presence of oxygen to release energy, carbon dioxide, and water. Word equation: Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water (with energy released) Balanced symbol equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (with energy released) Key points:
  • Occurs in ALL living cells (plants and animals)
  • Takes place in the mitochondria
  • Releases energy from glucose — this energy is used for life processes
  • The energy is initially transferred to a carrier molecule called ATP (adenosine triphosphate)
  • ATP is the "energy currency" of cells — it can be used directly for cellular work
Uses of the energy released:
  • Muscle contraction (movement)
  • Active transport of substances across cell membranes
  • Building larger molecules from smaller ones (e.g. protein synthesis)
  • Maintaining body temperature in mammals and birds (endotherms)
  • Cell division (mitosis)
  • Sending nerve impulses
Metabolic rate: the rate at which chemical reactions occur in the body. Affected by:
  • Exercise (increases rate — more energy needed)
  • Body temperature (higher temperature → faster reactions)
  • Age (children have higher metabolic rate than adults)
  • Gender (males typically have higher metabolic rate due to more muscle mass)
  • Amount of thyroid hormone (increases metabolic rate)
Worked Example

"Write the word equation for aerobic respiration and name the organelle where it occurs." Answer: Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water (energy released). It occurs in the mitochondria.

Common Misconception

"Respiration is the same as breathing"

Breathing (ventilation) is the physical process of moving air in and out of the lungs. Respiration is a chemical process that occurs in cells to release energy. Breathing provides the oxygen needed for aerobic respiration and removes the carbon dioxide produced, but they are completely different processes.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Provide the equation with blanks to fill in. Focus on the key concept: "respiration releases energy from food." List four uses of energy.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research ATP in more detail: what is its structure, how is it formed, and how does it release energy? Discuss the role of mitochondria in detail.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Equation Check

Students write the respiration equation from memory. Discuss any errors. Then list five uses of the energy released.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can write the equation accurately and list uses of energy with explanations
  • Getting there: Can write the equation but cannot list uses of energy
  • Not yet: Cannot write the respiration equation or distinguish it from breathing

Lesson 2: Respiration and Exercise

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Pulse Rate

Students measure their resting heart rate. Then do 30 star jumps and measure again. Record the difference. Ask: "Why did your heart rate increase?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
During exercise:
  • Muscles contract more frequently → need more energy → rate of respiration increases
  • More oxygen is needed → breathing rate and depth increase to get more O₂ into the blood
  • More CO₂ is produced → detected by receptors in the brain → breathing rate increases to remove it
  • Heart rate increases to transport O₂ and CO₂ faster between lungs and muscles
Recovery:
  • After exercise, breathing and heart rate remain elevated for a period
  • This is to repay the "oxygen debt" — the extra oxygen needed to:
    • Convert lactic acid (from anaerobic respiration) back to glucose
    • Restore ATP and creatine phosphate stores
    • Replenish myoglobin (oxygen-storing protein in muscles)
Fitness and the heart:
  • Regular exercise strengthens the heart muscle → more efficient pumping → lower resting heart rate
  • Increased stroke volume (more blood pumped per beat)
  • Better circulation → faster delivery of O₂ and nutrients to muscles
  • Reduced risk of cardiovascular disease
Worked Example

"Explain why your breathing rate increases during exercise." Answer: (1) During exercise, muscles contract more frequently, requiring more energy. (2) More energy is released by aerobic respiration, which requires more oxygen. (3) More carbon dioxide is produced as a waste product. (4) Receptors in the brain detect the increased CO₂ and send signals to increase breathing rate. (5) This brings in more O₂ and removes more CO₂.

Common Misconception

"You breathe harder because you need more oxygen"

While you do need more oxygen, the primary stimulus for increased breathing during exercise is actually the increase in carbon dioxide levels in the blood. Receptors in the brain detect rising CO₂ and trigger faster breathing to remove it. If CO₂ builds up, blood becomes more acidic, which is dangerous.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on the basic chain: exercise → more energy needed → more respiration → more O₂ needed → breathing rate increases. Provide a simple diagram showing this chain.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research the concept of VO₂ max and how it relates to fitness. Investigate how athletes train to improve their aerobic capacity. Discuss the role of myoglobin vs haemoglobin.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Chain of Events

Students write the chain of events from exercise to increased breathing rate in five steps. Share and compare.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain the body's response to exercise with clear biological reasoning
  • Getting there: Knows that breathing increases during exercise but cannot explain why
  • Not yet: Cannot explain the body's response to exercise

Homework Suggestions

  • Write the aerobic respiration equation from memory five times
  • Measure your resting heart rate each morning for a week and look for patterns
  • Research how the body responds to exercise at different intensities (walking vs sprinting)
  • Create a poster showing how the heart, lungs, and muscles work together during exercise
