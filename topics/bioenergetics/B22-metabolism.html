B22: Metabolism

Higher All Boards

Understand metabolism as the sum of all chemical reactions in living organisms, including the roles of enzymes in anabolism and catabolism.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Higher only
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Define metabolism and distinguish between anabolism and catabolism
  • Describe the metabolic roles of carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins
  • Explain how the body controls metabolic rates
  • Understand the role of the liver in metabolism

Prerequisites

  • Knowledge of aerobic and anaerobic respiration (B20, B21)
  • Understanding of enzymes (B7)
  • Basic knowledge of digestion and absorption (B7)

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Metabolism concept map template
  • Printed diagrams of metabolic pathways (simplified)

Lesson 1: What is Metabolism?

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Chemical Factory

Ask: "Your body is carrying out thousands of chemical reactions right now. What are some of them?" Students brainstorm: breaking down food, making new cells, repairing wounds, producing hormones. Introduce metabolism.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Metabolism: the sum of all chemical reactions that take place in living organisms to maintain life. Two types of metabolic reactions:
  • Anabolism: building larger molecules from smaller ones. Requires energy (from ATP). Examples:
    • Amino acids → proteins (protein synthesis)
    • Glucose → starch (for storage in plants)
    • Glucose → cellulose (for cell walls in plants)
    • Glucose → lipids (for energy storage)
    • Nucleotides → DNA (replication)
  • Catabolism: breaking down larger molecules into smaller ones. Releases energy. Examples:
    • Glucose → CO₂ + H₂O (aerobic respiration)
    • Starch → glucose (during digestion)
    • Proteins → amino acids (during digestion)
    • Lipids → fatty acids + glycerol (during digestion)
Metabolism of key nutrients:
  • Carbohydrates: digested to glucose → used for respiration (energy) or converted to starch/glycogen for storage. Excess glucose is converted to fat (lipogenesis).
  • Lipids (fats): digested to fatty acids + glycerol → used for energy storage, cell membranes, insulation. Can be broken down for energy in long-duration exercise.
  • Proteins: digested to amino acids → used for protein synthesis (making enzymes, structural proteins like collagen, haemoglobin). Excess amino acids cannot be stored — they are deaminated in the liver (nitrogen removed as urea, excreted in urine).
The liver's role in metabolism:
  • Deamination of excess amino acids (removing nitrogen)
  • Detoxification of alcohol and drugs
  • Storage of glucose as glycogen (and release when needed)
  • Production of bile for fat digestion
Worked Example

"Classify each reaction as anabolic or catabolic: (1) amino acids joining to make a protein, (2) starch being broken down to glucose during digestion, (3) glucose being converted to glycogen in the liver." Answer: (1) Anabolic — building a larger molecule from smaller ones. (2) Catabolic — breaking down a larger molecule into smaller ones. (3) Anabolic — building a storage molecule from glucose.

Common Misconception

"Metabolism only refers to breaking down food"

Metabolism includes BOTH building up (anabolism) and breaking down (catabolism) reactions. It is the sum of all chemical reactions — not just digestion. Building new cells, making hormones, and synthesising enzymes are all metabolic processes.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Simplify: "Anabolism = building up (needs energy). Catabolism = breaking down (releases energy)." Provide four examples and students classify each.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Create a concept map showing how the main metabolic pathways are connected: digestion → absorption → transport → respiration → anabolism → catabolism. Show how the same glucose molecule can follow different pathways.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Sort

Give 8 metabolic reactions. Students classify as anabolic or catabolic. Speed challenge.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can define metabolism, distinguish anabolism and catabolism, and give examples of each
  • Getting there: Can define metabolism but confuses anabolism and catabolism
  • Not yet: Cannot define metabolism or distinguish the two types

Lesson 2: Metabolic Rate and the Liver

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Metabolic Comparison

Ask: "A bodybuilder and a person who doesn't exercise eat the same number of calories per day. Why might the bodybuilder not gain weight?" Discuss muscle being metabolically active tissue.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Metabolic rate: the speed at which chemical reactions occur in the body. Factors affecting metabolic rate:
  • Exercise: increases metabolic rate during and after exercise (muscles need energy to contract and to repair)
  • Body temperature: higher temperature → faster enzyme-controlled reactions → higher metabolic rate
  • Thyroid hormones: the thyroid gland produces thyroxine, which controls metabolic rate. Too much (hyperthyroidism) → very high metabolic rate, weight loss, rapid heartbeat. Too little (hypothyroidism) → low metabolic rate, weight gain, fatigue.
  • Gender: males typically have a higher metabolic rate because they tend to have more muscle mass. Muscle tissue is metabolically more active than fat tissue.
  • Age: children and teenagers have higher metabolic rates because they are growing (anabolic reactions). Metabolic rate decreases with age.
  • Genetics: some people inherit a naturally higher or lower metabolic rate.
The liver in metabolism:
  • Deamination: amino acids contain nitrogen which must be removed before they can be used for energy or converted to glucose/fat. The liver removes the nitrogen group (deamination), producing ammonia (toxic), which is quickly converted to urea (less toxic) and excreted by the kidneys.
  • Detoxification: the liver breaks down alcohol and drugs using enzymes (e.g. ethanol → ethanal → ethanoic acid → CO₂ + H₂O). Chronic alcohol use damages liver cells (fatty liver → hepatitis → cirrhosis).
  • Glycogenesis and glycogenolysis: the liver converts excess glucose to glycogen for storage (glycogenesis) and breaks glycogen back down to glucose when blood sugar drops (glycogenolysis). This helps maintain blood glucose levels.
Worked Example

"Explain why a male bodybuilder typically has a higher metabolic rate than a sedentary female of the same age." Answer: (1) Males typically have more muscle mass than females (due to testosterone). (2) Muscle tissue is more metabolically active than fat tissue — it requires more energy to maintain. (3) The bodybuilder has even more muscle mass due to training. (4) More muscle → more metabolic reactions → higher metabolic rate.

Common Misconception

"Metabolism slows down when you eat less food"

When you drastically reduce food intake (starvation or very low calorie diets), the body may actually slow down its metabolic rate to conserve energy. This is a survival mechanism — the body tries to use less energy when it detects a shortage of food.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on three factors: exercise, temperature, hormones. For each, describe the effect. Simplify liver functions: "The liver removes nitrogen from amino acids and breaks down alcohol."
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research anabolic steroids in sport — how do they affect metabolism? Discuss the ethical and health implications. Investigate how the liver regenerates after damage.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Factor Effect

Give a factor, students state whether metabolic rate increases or decreases. Quick-fire round with 6 factors.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can explain factors affecting metabolic rate and describe the liver's metabolic roles
  • Getting there: Can name factors but cannot explain how they affect metabolic rate
  • Not yet: Cannot describe factors affecting metabolic rate or the liver's role

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a concept map of metabolism showing the links between digestion, respiration, photosynthesis, and protein synthesis
  • Research the effects of starvation on the body and explain the metabolic changes that occur
  • Write a comparison of anabolism and catabolism with at least three examples of each
  • Investigate how the liver processes alcohol — how long does it take to metabolise one unit?
