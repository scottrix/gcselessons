Lesson 2: Core Concepts: economic growth

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on economic growth. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Economic growth is an increase in the value of goods and services produced in an economy over time, usually measured by GDP (Gross Domestic Product).
Key Fact: Real GDP adjusts for inflation (uses constant prices), giving a true picture of whether output actually increased. GDP per capita = GDP / population, indicating average living standards.
Key Fact: Causes of growth: increase in quantity/quality of factors of production, technological progress, increased investment, improved education and training, and population growth.
Key Fact: Benefits of growth: higher incomes, improved living standards, lower unemployment, increased tax revenue (for public services), and greater consumer choice.
Key Fact: Costs of growth: inflation (if demand outstrips supply), environmental damage (pollution, resource depletion), increased inequality (growth may benefit the rich more), and opportunity cost of investment (less consumption now for more later).
Economics Exam Tips: When evaluating economic growth, use the QQQ framework: Quantity of output (has GDP risen?), Quality of growth (is it sustainable and equitable?), Quality of life (do people's lives actually improve?). Growth that damages the environment or increases inequality may not improve overall welfare.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the difference between GDP, real GDP, and GDP per capita.

Answer: GDP = total value of all goods and services produced in a country in a year. Real GDP = GDP adjusted for inflation (using constant prices so we can compare across years). GDP per capita = GDP divided by population, giving average output per person — a better indicator of average living standards than total GDP.

Lesson 3: Application: economic growth

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Economics Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain the difference between GDP, real GDP, and GDP per capita.

Answer: GDP = total value of all goods and services produced in a country in a year. Real GDP = GDP adjusted for inflation (using constant prices so we can compare across years). GDP per capita = GDP divided by population, giving average output per person — a better indicator of average living standards than total GDP.

Q2: Describe three benefits and two costs of economic growth.

Answer: Benefits: (1) Higher incomes and consumption. (2) Lower unemployment as firms hire more workers. (3) More tax revenue for government to spend on public services. Costs: (1) Environmental damage (pollution, resource depletion). (2) Inequality — growth may benefit higher earners disproportionately.

Q3: Evaluate whether economic growth always improves living standards.

Answer: Growth improves average living standards (higher GDP per capita), but averages can hide inequality — the richest may capture most gains while the poorest see little improvement. Also, growth can cause: environmental damage (reducing quality of life), longer working hours (less leisure), and congestion in cities. Living standards depend on distribution, environment, and work-life balance, not just income. Sustainable growth that benefits all is better than rapid growth concentrated at the top.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: economic growth

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate whether the UK government should pursue economic growth as its primary objective. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: argue for growth (higher incomes, lower unemployment, more tax revenue for public services, global competitiveness); argue against as sole objective (environmental damage, inequality, growth can coexist with poverty, well-being depends on more than income); consider conflicts (growth vs sustainability, growth vs low inflation, growth vs equality); conclude: growth is important but should not be pursued at any cost — sustainable and inclusive growth that benefits all and respects environmental limits is preferable to growth at any cost.

Exam Tips: Always specify REAL GDP (adjusted for inflation) when discussing whether the economy has actually grown. | GDP per capita is a better measure of living standards than total GDP — India has higher GDP than Switzerland but much lower GDP per capita. | When evaluating growth, consider BOTH economic AND non-economic factors (environment, wellbeing).
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Economic growth always improves a country's standard of living. Correct: Growth increases average income but doesn't guarantee improved living standards for all: inequality may widen (the rich benefit most); environmental damage reduces quality of life (pollution, congestion); growth may come from longer working hours rather than productivity gains; and GDP doesn't measure unpaid work, leisure, or health. Bhutan measures 'Gross National Happiness' alongside GDP precisely because GDP alone is an incomplete measure of welfare.
