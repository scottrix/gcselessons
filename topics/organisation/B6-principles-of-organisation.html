B6: Principles of Organisation

Foundation Higher All Boards

Understand the levels of organisation in living organisms, from cells to organ systems, and how the major organ systems work together.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the levels of organisation: cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organisms
  • Give examples of tissues, organs, and organ systems
  • Understand that organ systems work together to maintain life
  • Describe the functions of the major organ systems in the human body

Prerequisites

  • Knowledge of cell structure and specialised cells (B1, B4)

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Printed diagrams of organ systems
  • Index cards for matching activities
  • Large poster paper and coloured pens (for group work)

Lesson 1: Levels of Organisation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Build-Up

Write on the board: one cell → group of similar cells → group of different tissues working together → several organs working together. Ask students to fill in the levels: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Define each level clearly:
  • Cell: the basic structural and functional unit of life (e.g. muscle cell, nerve cell)
  • Tissue: a group of similar cells working together to perform a function (e.g. muscle tissue contracts, epithelial tissue covers surfaces, connective tissue supports, nervous tissue transmits impulses)
  • Organ: a structure made of two or more tissue types working together (e.g. the heart contains cardiac muscle tissue, connective tissue, and nervous tissue)
  • Organ system: a group of organs working together for a particular function (e.g. the digestive system includes the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, pancreas)
  • Organism: a living thing made of organ systems working together
Four types of animal tissue:
  1. Epithelial tissue — covers surfaces (skin, lining of gut)
  2. Connective tissue — supports and connects (bone, blood, fat)
  3. Muscle tissue — contracts to cause movement (skeletal, cardiac, smooth)
  4. Nervous tissue — transmits electrical impulses (brain, spinal cord, nerves)
Example walkthrough: muscle cell → muscle tissue → heart (organ) → circulatory system (organ system) → human (organism).
Worked Example

"Name the organ system that includes the lungs, trachea, and diaphragm. Give its function." Answer: The respiratory system. Its function is gas exchange — taking in oxygen and removing carbon dioxide from the body.

Common Misconception

"An organ is made of only one type of tissue"

Most organs contain multiple tissue types. The heart contains cardiac muscle (to pump), connective tissue (valves, structural support), and nervous tissue (to coordinate heartbeat). The stomach has epithelial, muscle, and glandular tissues.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Use a simple hierarchy: draw a muscle cell, then a group of them as muscle tissue, then the heart as an organ. Focus on the four levels with one clear example chain.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Map all the major organ systems and show how they interconnect. For example, how does the digestive system supply nutrients to the muscular system? How does the circulatory system connect everything?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Chain Game

One student starts with a cell type (e.g. "red blood cell"). The next student names the tissue (blood is a connective tissue). The next names an organ (the heart). The next names the organ system (circulatory). Continue until they reach organism level.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe all levels of organisation with accurate examples
  • Getting there: Knows the levels but cannot give examples for each
  • Not yet: Cannot sequence the levels of organisation

Lesson 2: Organ Systems Working Together

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Quiz

Name as many organ systems as you can in one minute. Review as a class and list them on the board.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Major organ systems to cover:
  • Digestive system: breaks down food and absorbs nutrients (mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, pancreas)
  • Circulatory system: transports substances around the body (heart, blood vessels, blood)
  • Respiratory system: gas exchange (lungs, trachea, bronchi, diaphragm)
  • Nervous system: coordinates responses (brain, spinal cord, nerves)
  • Endocrine system: hormonal coordination (pituitary gland, thyroid, adrenal glands, pancreas, ovaries/testes)
  • Reproductive system: produces offspring (ovaries/testes, uterus, penis)
  • Muscular system: movement (skeletal muscles attached to bones)
  • Skeletal system: support and protection (bones, joints, cartilage)
  • Excretory system: removes waste (kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra)
  • Immune system: defends against pathogens (white blood cells, lymph nodes, antibodies)
Key concept: organ systems do NOT work in isolation. They are interdependent. Example: during exercise, the muscular system needs more oxygen → the respiratory system increases breathing rate → the circulatory system pumps faster → the nervous system coordinates these responses.
Worked Example

"When you eat a meal, which organ systems are involved?" Answer: Digestive system (breaks down food and absorbs nutrients), circulatory system (transports absorbed nutrients), nervous system (controls digestion through peristalsis and enzyme secretion), endocrine system (releases hormones like insulin). This shows how systems work together.

Common Misconception

"The digestive system only includes the stomach and intestines"

The digestive system includes the mouth (where digestion begins), oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (where most absorption happens), large intestine, liver (produces bile), and pancreas (produces digestive enzymes). The whole tract from mouth to anus is involved.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on three main systems: digestive, circulatory, and respiratory. Provide a diagram with organs to label and match to the correct system. Use a matching card activity.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research the immune system in more detail. Create a flowchart showing how the body responds to a wound: clotting, inflammation, white blood cell action, repair. Show how multiple systems are involved.

Plenary (5 minutes)

System Spotter

Show a diagram of the human body with all major organs labelled. Students write which organ system each organ belongs to. Quick fire round — hands up for each correct answer.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can name the major organ systems, their organs, and explain how they work together
  • Getting there: Can name some organ systems but cannot explain interdependence
  • Not yet: Cannot name organ systems or match organs to systems

Homework Suggestions

  • Create an organ system poster with diagrams of each system and labels for key organs
  • Write a paragraph explaining how at least three organ systems work together during exercise
  • Research the function of the liver and create a mind map of its roles
  • Complete a matching worksheet: organs to organ systems
