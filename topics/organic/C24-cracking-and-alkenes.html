C24: Cracking and Alkenes

Foundation All Boards

Explain why cracking is needed, describe catalytic and steam cracking, and describe the properties of alkenes.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why cracking is necessary and describe catalytic and steam cracking
  • Describe the properties of alkenes and the test for unsaturation

Prerequisites

  • Hydrocarbons and Crude Oil (C23) — alkanes, fractional distillation

Lesson 1: Cracking

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Supply and Demand

Fractional distillation produces more long-chain fractions (like bitumen and heavy oil) than we need, but not enough short-chain fractions (like petrol). How can we solve this problem?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Cracking is the process of breaking long-chain alkanes into shorter, more useful molecules. It is needed because:
• Fractional distillation produces too many long-chain fractions (not enough demand)
• We need more short-chain fractions (higher demand — petrol, alkenes for plastics)

Types of cracking:

1. Catalytic cracking:
• Uses a zeolite catalyst (aluminium oxide/silicon dioxide)
• Temperature: ~450°C
• Pressure: low pressure
• Produces alkenes (useful for making plastics) and some branched-chain alkanes (used in petrol — high octane rating)

2. Steam cracking:
• Uses steam (no catalyst)
• Temperature: ~800°C (very high — needs more energy)
• Pressure: low pressure
• Produces mainly alkenes (ethene, propene — used to make polymers/plastics)

What cracking produces:
• Short-chain alkanes (e.g. petrol components)
• Alkenes (e.g. ethene, propene — used to make plastics via polymerisation)

Example of cracking:
C₁₅H₃₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₇H₁₄
(long-chain alkane → shorter alkane + alkene)

The alkene product has a double bond (C=C). This is what makes alkenes unsaturated and chemically more reactive than alkanes.
Why we need cracking

We use about 50% of crude oil as petrol and diesel (short-chain). But fractional distillation only produces about 25% petrol/diesel. Cracking converts the unwanted long-chain fractions into the short-chain ones we need. It also produces alkenes for making plastics.

Cracking equation

C₁₅H₃₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₇H₁₄
Long-chain alkane (pentadecane) → shorter alkane (octane, used in petrol) + alkene (heptene, used for making plastics). Notice the total number of carbon and hydrogen atoms is the same on both sides.

Cracking: long-chain alkane → shorter alkane + alkene

Catalytic cracking: zeolite catalyst, ~450°C, low pressure
→ alkanes + branched alkanes + alkenes

Steam cracking: steam, ~800°C, low pressure
→ mainly alkenes

General: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ → CₘH₂ₘ₊₂ + CₚH₂ₚ (where n = m + p)

Common Misconception

Cracking breaks bonds and creates new atoms.

Cracking breaks C-C bonds only — the total number of carbon and hydrogen atoms stays the same. No atoms are created or destroyed. A long chain is simply broken into two shorter chains.

Differentiation

Support:
Use a piece of string (long chain). Cut it in the middle (cracking). You now have two shorter pieces. The total length is the same — it's just divided differently.
Stretch:
Research: why are branched-chain alkanes (from catalytic cracking) better for petrol than straight-chain alkanes? What is 'octane rating'?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Why is cracking needed? Name two types of cracking. What are the products of cracking?

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Explains why cracking is needed, describes both types, and writes cracking equations.
  • Getting there: Knows cracking produces alkanes + alkenes but cannot always write equations.
  • Not yet: Cannot explain why cracking is needed. Reteach with the supply/demand argument and string model.

Lesson 2: Alkenes and the Test for Unsaturation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Double Bond

Alkanes have only single bonds. Cracking produces molecules with C=C double bonds. What is different about a double bond compared to a single bond? Why does this make alkenes more reactive?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one C=C double bond.
General formula: CₙH₂ₙ (for alkenes with one double bond)

First four alkenes:
• Ethene C₂H₄ (making ethanol, plastics)
• Propene C₃H₆ (making poly(propene), propanol)
• Butene C₄H₈ (making polymers)
• Pentene C₅H₁₀ (chemical feedstock)

Properties of alkenes:
• More reactive than alkanes (C=C double bond is a site of reactivity — weaker π bond breaks easily)
• Burn in excess oxygen (like alkanes) → CO₂ + H₂O
• Undergo addition reactions — the double bond breaks and atoms add across it

Addition reactions:
• With hydrogen: C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ (ethene → ethane)
• With bromine: C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (ethene → 1,2-dibromoethane)
• With water: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (ethene → ethanol — industrial method)
• Polymerisation: many ethene molecules join → poly(ethene)

Test for unsaturation (bromine water test):
• Add bromine water (orange/brown) to the sample
• Alkane: no reaction → stays orange/brown
• Alkene: decolourises bromine water → turns colourless
• The bromine adds across the C=C double bond
Bromine water test

Alkene + Br₂ → colourless dibromoalkane. The orange bromine water turns colourless. This is a specific test for C=C double bonds (unsaturation). Alkanes do not react — the bromine water stays orange.

Ethene hydration

C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH. Ethene (from steam cracking) reacts with water (steam) in the presence of phosphoric acid catalyst to make ethanol. This is the main industrial method for making ethanol — it's faster and cheaper than fermentation.

Alkene general formula: CₙH₂ₙ

Test for unsaturation: bromine water
Alkene + Br₂ → dibromoalkane (colourless)
Alkane + Br₂ → no reaction (stays orange)

Addition reactions:
C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ (hydrogenation)
C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (bromination)
C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (hydration)
nC₂H₄ → (C₂H₄)ₙ (polymerisation)

Common Misconception

Alkenes and alkanes have similar reactivity.

Alkenes are much more reactive than alkanes because of the C=C double bond. The π bond in the double bond is weaker and more exposed, making it a site where other molecules can easily add across. Alkanes have only strong single bonds and are relatively unreactive.

Differentiation

Support:
Use molecular model kits to build ethene and ethane. Show the double bond clearly. Use bromine water (if safe to do so) to test hexane vs hexene.
Stretch:
Write the equation for the polymerisation of propene to make poly(propene). Draw the repeating unit.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Name the test for unsaturation and describe what happens. Write an equation for the reaction of ethene with bromine.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Describes alkenes, the bromine test, and addition reactions confidently.
  • Getting there: Knows the bromine test but cannot write addition reaction equations.
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish alkanes from alkenes. Reteach with molecular models and the bromine test.

Homework Suggestions

  • Draw displayed formulae for the first five alkenes
  • Write equations for three addition reactions of propene
  • Research: what is hydrogenation? How is it used to make margarine from vegetable oil?
