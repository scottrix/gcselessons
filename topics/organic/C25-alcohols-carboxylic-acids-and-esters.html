C25: Alcohols, Carboxylic Acids and Esters

Foundation All Boards

Describe the homologous series of alcohols and carboxylic acids, their properties, and how esters are formed.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 3
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the structure and properties of alcohols and carboxylic acids
  • Explain how ethanol is made by fermentation and hydration
  • Describe how esters are formed and their uses

Prerequisites

  • Hydrocarbons and Crude Oil (C23) — alkanes
  • Cracking and Alkenes (C24) — addition reactions

Lesson 1: Alcohols

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Ethanol

Ethanol is the alcohol in drinks. It's also used as a fuel, a solvent, and in hand sanitiser. What do all these uses have in common? What properties does ethanol have?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Alcohols are a homologous series with the functional group -OH (hydroxyl group).
General formula for monohydric alcohols: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH

First four alcohols:
• Methanol CH₃OH — fuel, solvent (toxic)
• Ethanol C₂H₅OH — drinks, fuel, solvent, hand sanitiser
• Propanol C₃H₇OH — solvent, chemical feedstock
• Butanol C₄H₉OH — fuel, solvent

Properties of alcohols:
• Flammable (burn in oxygen → CO₂ + H₂O) — used as fuels
• Miscible with water (the -OH group forms hydrogen bonds with water)
• Solvents for many organic substances
• React with carboxylic acids to form esters
• Boiling points higher than corresponding alkanes (hydrogen bonding)

Making ethanol — two methods:

1. Fermentation (biological method):
• Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide
• C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
• Uses yeast enzymes
• Temperature: ~37°C (body temperature — yeast works best)
• No oxygen needed (anaerobic)
• Produces ethanol up to about 15% concentration (above this, yeast dies)
• Batch process — slow but uses renewable resources

2. Hydration of ethene (industrial method):
• Ethene + water → ethanol
• C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH
• Uses phosphoric acid catalyst
• Temperature: ~300°C, pressure: 60 atm
• Continuous process — fast but uses ethene from crude oil (non-renewable)
• Produces pure ethanol (useful for industrial purposes)
Fermentation of glucose

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂. Yeast enzymes break down glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide. This is how beer, wine, and spirits are made. The CO₂ is what makes beer fizzy. Fermentation is done in sealed containers (no oxygen) at about 37°C.

Ethanol purification

Fermentation produces dilute ethanol (about 15%). To make stronger alcoholic drinks, the ethanol is purified by distillation (ethanol boils at 78°C, water at 100°C). Fractional distillation separates them.

Alcohol general formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH

Fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
(enzyme catalyst, ~37°C, no O₂)

Hydration: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH
(phosphoric acid catalyst, ~300°C, 60 atm)

Common Misconception

Fermentation and hydration are the same process.

Fermentation uses glucose (from plants) and yeast enzymes — it is biological and renewable. Hydration uses ethene (from crude oil) and an acid catalyst — it is chemical and non-renewable. They produce the same product (ethanol) by completely different methods.

Differentiation

Support:
Compare the two methods side by side: raw materials, conditions, speed, renewable/non-renewable, purity of product.
Stretch:
Research: what is bioethanol? How is it used as a fuel? What are the advantages and disadvantages?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Compare fermentation and hydration: raw materials, conditions, catalyst, type of process, renewable or not.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Describes both methods of making ethanol, their conditions, and compares their advantages.
  • Getting there: Knows both methods but cannot always write the equations or compare conditions.
  • Not yet: Cannot distinguish fermentation from hydration. Reteach with the comparison table.

Lesson 2: Carboxylic Acids

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Vinegar

Vinegar is a dilute solution of ethanoic acid. It's acidic. But it's not dangerous like hydrochloric acid. Why is ethanoic acid different from hydrochloric acid?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Carboxylic acids are a homologous series with the functional group -COOH (carboxyl group).
General formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH

First four carboxylic acids:
• Methanoic acid HCOOH — ant stings, leather tanning
• Ethanoic acid CH₃COOH — vinegar (5% solution), used in food industry
• Propanoic acid C₂H₅COOH — food preservation
• Butanoic acid C₃H₇COOH — rancid butter smell

Properties of carboxylic acids:
• Weak acids (only partially dissociate in water — pH 3-5 for dilute solutions)
• React with metals → salt + hydrogen (like other acids)
• React with bases → salt + water (neutralisation)
• React with carbonates → salt + water + CO₂
• React with alcohols → ester + water (esterification)
• Ethanoic acid is the main acid in vinegar

Weak vs strong acids:
Carboxylic acids are weak acids — they only partially dissociate in water. Most molecules stay as whole molecules, only a few release H⁺ ions. This is why they are less corrosive than strong acids like HCl or H₂SO₄.
Ethanoic acid reactions

CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O (neutralisation — forms sodium ethanoate)
2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂ (reaction with metal — forms magnesium ethanoate)
2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ (reaction with carbonate — fizzes!)

Weak acid in vinegar

Vinegar is about 5% ethanoic acid. When you add it to baking soda (sodium carbonate), it fizzes because CO₂ is produced: 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂. This is a safe, kitchen-friendly acid-base reaction.

Carboxylic acid general formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH

Functional group: -COOH (carboxyl)

Reactions (like other acids):
Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen
Acid + base → salt + water
Acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂

Carboxylic acids are weak acids (partial dissociation)

Common Misconception

Carboxylic acids are strong acids.

Carboxylic acids are weak acids — they only partially dissociate in water. Only a small proportion of molecules release H⁺ ions. This is why they are less corrosive and have a higher pH than strong acids like HCl at the same concentration.

Differentiation

Support:
Use the 'open door' analogy for weak vs strong acids (from C16). Carboxylic acids = few doors open. Hydrochloric acid = all doors open.
Stretch:
Write the equation for the reaction of propanoic acid with sodium hydroxide. What salt is formed?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Write equations for ethanoic acid reacting with: (a) sodium, (b) sodium hydroxide, (c) calcium carbonate.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Describes carboxylic acids, writes equations for their reactions, and explains why they are weak acids.
  • Getting there: Knows carboxylic acids are weak but cannot always write equations.
  • Not yet: Cannot describe carboxylic acids. Reteach using vinegar and baking soda demonstration.

Lesson 3: Esters

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Fruity Smells

Bananas, pineapples, and strawberries all smell distinctive. These smells come from esters — chemicals made from carboxylic acids and alcohols. How might you make an ester?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Esters are formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst (usually concentrated sulfuric acid).

Esterification reaction:
Carboxylic acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water
(this is a condensation reaction — water is removed)

Example: ethyl ethanoate
Ethanoic acid + Ethanol → Ethyl ethanoate + Water
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

Naming esters:
The first part of the name comes from the alcohol (alkyl group).
The second part comes from the carboxylic acid (carboxylate).
e.g. Ethyl ethanoate = ethanol + ethanoic acid
    Methyl propanoate = methanol + propanoic acid

Properties and uses of esters:
• Sweet, fruity smells — used in artificial flavourings and perfumes
• Insoluble in water — used in solvents (e.g. nail varnish remover)
• Used to make polyesters (synthetic fibres for clothes)
• Used in polishes and coatings

Hydrolysis of esters:
Esters can be broken down by heating with water and acid or alkali (hydrolysis). This reverses esterification.
Ethyl ethanoate + NaOH → sodium ethanoate + ethanol
Making ethyl ethanoate

Mix ethanoic acid with ethanol. Add a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid as a catalyst. Heat gently under reflux for about 10 minutes. The ester is separated (it floats on top because it's insoluble in water). It has a sweet, fruity smell.

Esters in food and perfume

Ethyl butanoate smells like pineapple. Octyl ethanoate smells like orange. Pentyl ethanoate smells like banana. These esters are used as artificial flavourings and in perfumes because of their pleasant fruity smells.

Esterification:
Carboxylic acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water
(acid catalyst, heat)

Ethanoic acid + Ethanol → Ethyl ethanoate + Water
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

Ester name = [alcohol part] + [acid part]
Ethyl ethanoate = ethanol + ethanoic acid

Common Misconception

Esters are acids.

Esters are not acids — they are a different functional group (–COO–). They are neutral compounds with sweet, fruity smells. They are made FROM carboxylic acids and alcohols, but they do not behave like acids.

Differentiation

Support:
Use a table: Alcohol + Carboxylic acid → Ester name + Use. Fill in together for several examples.
Stretch:
What ester would be formed from methanol and propanoic acid? Name it and draw its structure. What would it smell like?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Name the ester formed from: (a) ethanol + ethanoic acid, (b) methanol + propanoic acid, (c) propanol + methanoic acid. What are esters used for?

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Names and writes equations for esterification, and describes the uses of esters.
  • Getting there: Can name simple esters but cannot always write the equation.
  • Not yet: Cannot name esters. Reteach with the naming rule and several worked examples.

Homework Suggestions

  • Complete the table: name, formula, functional group for 5 alcohols and 5 carboxylic acids
  • Write the esterification equation for propanoic acid + ethanol. Name the ester.
  • Research: what are biodiesel and bioethanol? How are they made?
