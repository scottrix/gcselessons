Lesson 2: Core Concepts: global issues & sustainability

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on global issues & sustainability. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Climate change is one of the greatest global challenges, caused by greenhouse gas emissions from burning fossil fuels. The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.
Key Fact: The UK has committed to net zero carbon emissions by 2050 — the first major economy to do so. This requires transforming energy, transport, and industry. The Climate Change Committee monitors progress.
Key Fact: Extreme poverty has declined globally but inequality persists both between and within nations. The richest 1% own nearly half the world's wealth. Global cooperation on trade, aid, and debt relief is essential.
Key Fact: The UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) set 17 targets for a better world by 2030, including: no poverty, zero hunger, quality education, gender equality, clean water, climate action, and reduced inequalities.
Key Fact: Global health crises like COVID-19 demonstrate the need for international cooperation — viruses do not respect borders, and vaccine distribution requires global coordination.
Key Fact: Conflicts and refugees are global issues: over 100 million people are forcibly displaced worldwide. The UNHCR coordinates refugee protection, but host countries bear most responsibility.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the view that global citizenship requires individuals to take action on climate change. [12 marks] <div class="

Global citizenship implies individuals have responsibilities beyond their own nation, and climate change is the defining global challenge. As global citizens, individuals should take action because everyday choices — energy use, travel, diet, consumption — directly contribute to carbon emissions. Individual action demonstrates commitment and can influence others. Democratic participation is also individual action: citizens can vote for parties with strong climate policies, join environmental pressure groups, and petition government. These individual political actions can drive systemic change that personal lifestyle changes alone cannot achieve. However, individual action has clear limitations. About 70% of global emissions come from just 100 companies. One person's recycling is insignificant compared to industrial emissions. Placing responsibility on individuals can distract from the need for government regulation and corporate accountability. In conclusion, while individual action is necessary to demonstrate commitment and build political demand for change, it is not sufficient on its own. Effective global citizenship on climate change requires both personal action and political

Exam Tips: Always link sustainability to the three pillars: economic, social, environmental | Use specific SDG numbers and names when referencing them (e.g. SDG 13: Climate Action) | When evaluating climate action, consider both mitigation (reducing emissions) and adaptation (preparing for effects)
Common Errors: ✗ Sustainability is only about the environment. ✓ Sustainability has three pillars: environmental protection, social inclusion, and economic development. All three must be balanced for true sustainability. ✗ Individual actions cannot make any difference to global issues. ✓ While individual actions alone cannot solve global issues, collective individual action can drive market changes and influence government policy. Every social movement starts with individuals. ✗ The UK is already doing enough to meet its net zero target. ✓ The Climate Change Committee has warned the UK is not on track, with insufficient progress on home insulation, transport electrification, and agricultural emissions.
