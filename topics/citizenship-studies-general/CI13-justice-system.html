Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the justice system

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the justice system. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The criminal justice system involves: police (investigate), CPS (prosecute), courts (try), sentencing (punish), probation (supervise in community), prison (custodial sentences).
Key Fact: The police are responsible for preventing and detecting crime, maintaining public order, and protecting life and property. Police officers have powers of arrest, stop and search, and detention.
Key Fact: The Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) reviews evidence gathered by the police and decides whether to charge — applying the Full Code Test (sufficient evidence + public interest).
Key Fact: Trial by jury is available for serious criminal offences ('indictable offences') in the Crown Court. The jury of 12 citizens decides the verdict; the judge decides the sentence.
Key Fact: Sentencing aims: retribution (punishment), deterrence (discouraging reoffending and others), rehabilitation (reforming the offender), and protection of the public.
Key Fact: Prison serves to protect the public and punish serious offenders, but the UK has one of the highest prison populations in Western Europe and reoffending rates are around 46% within one year.

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the effectiveness of the UK justice system in achieving fairness. [12 marks] <div class="

The UK justice system has strengths in achieving fairness. Trial by jury allows ordinary citizens to decide verdicts, protecting against state oppression. The rule of law ensures all are equal before the courts, and judicial independence means judges cannot be influenced by government. Legal aid was designed to ensure access to justice regardless of wealth. However, significant fairness issues remain. Legal aid cuts since 2012 have denied many people access to legal representation, particularly in civil cases. This means wealthy individuals and corporations have an advantage. Ethnic minorities are disproportionately represented in the criminal justice system — they are more likely to be stopped, arrested, and receive harsher sentences. The prison population is high but reoffending rates remain around 46%, suggesting the system fails at rehabilitation. Youth justice has improved with a focus on rehabilitation over punishment for young offenders, but concerns remain about the age of criminal responsibility (10 in England and Wales, one of the lowest in Europe). Common Errors: ✗ The police decide whether someone is charged with a crime. ✓ The police investigate and can arrest, but the CPS decides whether to bring criminal charges based on the Full Code Test (evidence + public interest). ✗ Restorative justice means the offender gets a lighter sentence. ✓ Restorative justice is a process that can run alongside the justice system. It does not replace sentencing but can be taken into account by the court. ✗ Juries decide the sentence in criminal cases. ✓ Juries decide the verdict (guilty or not guilty). The judge decides the sentence based on sentencing guidelines and the circumstances of the case.
