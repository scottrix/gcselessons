Lesson 2: Core Concepts: evaluating citizenship action

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on evaluating citizenship action. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Evaluation means assessing what worked, what didn't, and why — it is the most important part of citizenship action and worth the most marks in the exam.
Key Fact: Evaluate by comparing outcomes against original aims: Did you achieve what you set out to do? How do you know? What evidence supports your assessment?
Key Fact: Consider both intended outcomes (what you planned) and unintended outcomes (unexpected results, positive or negative).
Key Fact: Use quantitative evidence (numbers: petition signatures, survey responses, attendance figures) and qualitative evidence (quotes, testimonials, observations, feedback).
Key Fact: Evaluate your methods: Were petitions, social media, or meetings the most effective? Why? Would different methods have worked better?
Key Fact: Assess impact on stakeholders: How were different groups affected? Did the action benefit some more than others? Were any groups excluded?

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the citizenship action you have carried out. In your answer you should refer to the research you undertook, the strategies you chose, the action you took and the impact of the action. [24 marks] <div class="

My citizenship action aimed to improve recycling facilities in my school, where a survey showed 78% of students wanted better options. My research included surveying 150 students, interviewing the site manager, and reviewing the school's current recycling provision. My strategy was to combine a petition with a presentation to the school council and a social media campaign. I chose these methods because they involved different stakeholders and created multiple routes to influence the headteacher. The petition collected 210 signatures in two weeks, exceeding my target of 150. The social media campaign reached 500 views on Instagram but engagement was low — only 30 likes and 5 shares. This showed that while awareness was raised, it did not translate into active support. Presenting to the school council was effective — they voted to support the proposal, giving it legitimacy. The headteacher agreed to fund three new recycling bins and a poster campaign, partially meeting my aim. However, my action had limitations. Exam Tips: AQA Paper 2 Section B is worth 24 marks — most marks are for evaluation, not description | Always use specific evidence from your action, not vague generalisations | Honest reflection scores higher than exaggerated claims of success
Common Errors: ✗ Evaluation means just describing what you did. ✓ Description is only the starting point. Evaluation requires analysis — explaining why things happened, assessing effectiveness, and reflecting on learning. ✗ If your action didn't achieve its aims, you will lose marks. ✓ Marks are for the quality of evaluation, not whether the action succeeded. An honest evaluation of a partially successful action that analyses reasons will score highly. ✗ You only need to evaluate whether you achieved your aims. ✓ You should also evaluate: your choice of methods, impact on stakeholders, unintended outcomes, what you would do differently, and what you learned about citizenship.
