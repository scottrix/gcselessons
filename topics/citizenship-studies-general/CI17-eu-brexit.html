Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the eu & brexit

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The EU began as the European Coal and Steel Community in 1951 and evolved into a political and economic union of 27 member states cooperating on trade, law, and policy.
Key Fact: The UK joined the European Communities in 1973. A 1975 referendum confirmed membership with 67% voting to stay in. Membership gave access to the single market and freedom of movement.
Key Fact: The 2016 EU referendum result: 51.9% voted Leave, 48.1% voted Remain. Turnout was 72.2%. England and Wales voted Leave; Scotland and Northern Ireland voted Remain.
Key Fact: Key Leave arguments: regaining parliamentary sovereignty (making all UK laws in the UK), controlling immigration (ending freedom of movement), making independent trade deals, and saving EU budget contributions (£17.4bn gross in 2019).
Key Fact: Key Remain arguments: economic benefits of the single market (access to 450 million consumers), freedom to live and work across the EU, cooperation on security and the environment, and the EU's role in maintaining peace in Europe.
Key Fact: Article 50 of the Lisbon Treaty triggered the formal leaving process on 29 March 2017. The UK left the EU on 31 January 2020 and the transition period ended on 31 December 2020.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the impact of Brexit on the UK's relationship with Europe. [12 marks] <div class="

Brexit fundamentally changed the UK's relationship with Europe. The UK left the EU single market and customs union, creating new trade barriers. Businesses face customs checks, paperwork, and tariffs on some goods, increasing costs and reducing trade with the EU — previously the UK's largest trading partner. Freedom of movement ended, meaning UK citizens can no longer automatically live and work in the EU. This has affected industries like hospitality, agriculture, and health that relied on EU workers. University participation in Erasmus+ was also lost. The Northern Ireland Protocol created a unique situation where Northern Ireland effectively remains in the EU single market for goods to avoid a hard border with the Republic. The Windsor Framework eased some tensions but the issue remains politically sensitive. However, the UK retains connections through NATO, the Council of Europe, and bilateral relationships. The Trade and Cooperation Agreement preserves tariff-free trade for most goods, and the UK has joined CPTPP in the Indo-Pacific. Overall, Brexit has made the UK-EU relationship more distant and complex, with real economic and social costs, but the two sides continue to coope

Exam Tips: Always present both sides of the Brexit debate — it was closely divided (51.9% vs 48.1%) | Distinguish between the EU (27 member states) and Europe (geographic continent) — they are not the same | Use correct terminology: 'single market' not 'common market', 'freedom of movement' not 'open borders'
Common Errors: ✗ The UK was forced to join the EU. ✓ The UK chose to join the European Communities in 1973, and this was confirmed by a 1975 referendum where 67% voted to stay in. ✗ Brexit means the UK has no relationship with the EU. ✓ The UK and EU have a Trade and Cooperation Agreement and continue to trade and cooperate, though on different terms than before. ✗ The EU makes all the laws for member states. ✓ EU law applies in specific areas where member states have agreed to cooperate. Member states still make most domestic laws independently, though EU law takes priority in areas it covers.
