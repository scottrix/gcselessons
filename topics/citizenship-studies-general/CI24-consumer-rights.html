Lesson 2: Core Concepts: consumer rights

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on consumer rights. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Consumer Rights Act 2015 is the main law protecting consumers. It applies to goods, services, and digital content purchased in the UK.
Key Fact: Three key rights for goods: they must be as described (match their description), fit for purpose (do what the buyer reasonably expects), and of satisfactory quality (meet the standard a reasonable person would accept).
Key Fact: If goods are faulty: within 30 days — full refund; after 30 days but within 6 months — repair or replacement (retailer chooses); if repair/replacement fails — refund (with deduction for use after 6 months).
Key Fact: Services must be performed with reasonable care and skill, at a reasonable price, and within a reasonable time. If not, the consumer is entitled to a repeat performance or price reduction.
Key Fact: Online shoppers have additional rights: 14-day cancellation period for most goods and services (distance selling regulations), and the trader pays return postage for faulty goods.
Key Fact: The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) enforces consumer protection law and can take businesses to court for unfair practices.

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the effectiveness of consumer protection in the UK. [12 marks] <div class="

UK consumer protection has significant strengths. The Consumer Rights Act 2015 provides clear rights: goods must be as described, fit for purpose, and of satisfactory quality. Within 30 days, consumers are entitled to a full refund. Online shoppers have additional protections including a 14-day cancellation period. However, effectiveness depends on consumers knowing their rights. Research suggests many consumers, especially young people, are unaware of their rights. The burden of complaining falls on the individual, which can be time-consuming and stressful. Enforcement is uneven. Trading Standards budgets have been cut significantly, reducing capacity to investigate rogue traders. Large corporations can resist claims with their legal resources, while individuals may give up. The CMA has limited resources relative to the market's scale. Nevertheless, consumer protection is generally effective for those who know their rights. Comparison websites and review platforms give consumers more information than ever before. Overall, while the legal framework is strong, practical effectiveness is limited by awareness, enforcement resources, and the unequal power between individual consumers a

Exam Tips: Use the exact legal terms: 'satisfactory quality', 'fit for purpose', 'as described' | When evaluating consumer rights, consider whether citizens actually know and use their rights | Link consumer rights to broader citizenship themes like accountability and fairness
Common Errors: ✗ You can get a full refund for faulty goods at any time. ✓ A full refund is only available within 30 days. After that, you are entitled to a repair or replacement first, and a refund only if those fail (with a deduction for use after 6 months). ✗ Consumer rights only apply to in-store purchases. ✓ Online purchases have even stronger protections, including a 14-day cancellation period and the trader paying return postage for faulty goods. ✗ Caveat emptor ('buyer beware') is the law in the UK. ✓ UK law protects consumers significantly. Sellers have legal obligations regarding quality, description, and fitness for purpose — the buyer is not solely responsible.
