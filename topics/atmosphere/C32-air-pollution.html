C32: Air Pollution

Foundation All Boards

Describe the main air pollutants, their sources, effects, and how they can be reduced.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the main air pollutants and their sources
  • Describe the effects of air pollution on health and the environment
  • Evaluate methods for reducing air pollution

Prerequisites

  • Greenhouse Gases and Climate Change (C31) — fossil fuels, emissions
  • Reactions of Acids (C16) — acid reactions

Lesson 1: Sources and Effects of Air Pollution

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Smog

Some cities have smog (smoke + fog). London had deadly smogs in the 1950s that killed thousands. What causes smog? What gases are in it?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Main air pollutants and their sources:

1. Carbon monoxide (CO):
• Source: incomplete combustion of fuels (cars, heaters, fires)
• Effects: colourless, odourless, toxic — binds to haemoglobin in blood, preventing oxygen transport. Can cause death in enclosed spaces.

2. Sulfur dioxide (SO₂):
• Source: burning fossil fuels containing sulfur (coal, oil)
• Effects: respiratory problems, contributes to acid rain
• S + O₂ → SO₂

3. Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ — mainly NO and NO₂):
• Source: high-temperature combustion in car engines and power stations. N₂ + O₂ → 2NO (at high temperatures)
• Effects: respiratory problems, contributes to acid rain, contributes to photochemical smog

4. Particulates (soot, PM2.5, PM10):
• Source: incomplete combustion, diesel engines, industrial processes
• Effects: respiratory and cardiovascular problems, reduces visibility (smog)

5. Unburnt hydrocarbons:
• Source: incomplete combustion in car engines
• Effects: contribute to photochemical smog

Acid rain:
• SO₂ and NO₂ react with water in the atmosphere
• SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ (sulfurous acid) → further oxidation to H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid)
• 2NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ + HNO₂ (nitric/nitrous acids)
• pH of normal rain: ~5.6 (slightly acidic due to CO₂)
• pH of acid rain: as low as 4.2
• Effects: damages buildings (especially limestone/marble), kills fish in lakes, damages trees and crops
Carbon monoxide poisoning

CO is dangerous because it binds to haemoglobin 200× more strongly than oxygen. Even small amounts of CO in the air can be deadly. Symptoms: headache, dizziness, confusion, death. This is why CO detectors are essential in homes with gas appliances.

Acid rain damage

Acid rain dissolves calcium carbonate in buildings: CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂. The calcium sulfate washes away, eroding statues and buildings. The Taj Mahal, Greek temples, and cathedrals are all damaged by acid rain.

Air pollutants:
• CO: incomplete combustion, toxic (binds to haemoglobin)
• SO₂: burning sulfur in fuels, acid rain
• NOₓ: high-temp combustion, acid rain, smog
• Particulates: incomplete combustion, health problems

Acid rain: SO₂ + H₂O → acid; NO₂ + H₂O → acid
Normal rain pH: ~5.6; Acid rain pH: as low as 4.2

Common Misconception

CO₂ is an air pollutant.

CO₂ is a greenhouse gas but is not classified as an air pollutant at normal concentrations. It is a natural part of the atmosphere and is essential for photosynthesis. The problem is the enhanced greenhouse effect from too much CO₂, not direct toxicity or pollution.

Differentiation

Support:
Create a table: Pollutant | Source | Effects | How to reduce. Fill in together.
Stretch:
Research: what is photochemical smog? How does it form? Which cities are most affected?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Name three air pollutants, their sources, and one effect of each.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Identifies main pollutants, sources, effects, and describes acid rain formation.
  • Getting there: Knows some pollutants but cannot describe all their effects.
  • Not yet: Cannot name air pollutants. Reteach with the summary table.

Lesson 2: Reducing Air Pollution

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Cleaner Air

London's air is much cleaner now than in the 1950s. What changed? What laws and technologies helped?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Methods of reducing air pollution:

1. Catalytic converters (in cars):
• fitted to exhaust systems of petrol cars
• Contain platinum/rhodium catalyst
• Convert CO → CO₂, NOₓ → N₂, unburnt hydrocarbons → CO₂ + H₂O
• 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂
• Cannot be used on diesel cars (sulfur poisons the catalyst)

2. Desulfurisation (removing SO₂ from power station emissions):
• Flue gas desulfurisation: SO₂ + CaCO₃ → CaSO₃ + CO₂
• Or: SO₂ + 2CaCO₃ + ½O₂ → 2CaSO₄ + 2CO₂ (gypsum produced — useful for plasterboard!)
• Removes up to 95% of SO₂ from flue gases

3. Low-sulfur fuels:
• Using fuels with less sulfur reduces SO₂ emissions
• Ultra-low sulfur diesel: <10 ppm sulfur

4. Electric vehicles:
• No exhaust emissions at point of use
• Electricity may still come from fossil fuels (indirect pollution)
• If powered by renewables, zero emissions overall

5. Particle filters (diesel cars):
• Diesel particulate filters (DPF) trap soot particles
• Must be periodically 'regenerated' (burn off trapped particles)

6. Regulations and legislation:
• Clean Air Acts restrict what can be burned and where
• EU/UK emission standards (Euro 6 for vehicles)
• London ULEZ (Ultra Low Emission Zone) — charges polluting vehicles
• Taxes on high-emission vehicles
Catalytic converter

2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂. The catalyst provides a surface where CO and NO molecules adsorb, rearrange, and desorb as CO₂ and N₂. The catalyst is not used up — it can process millions of molecules. This is why cars need to run for a few minutes before the catalyst reaches working temperature.

Desulfurisation

Power stations spray limestone slurry into flue gases: SO₂ + CaCO₃ → CaSO₃ + CO₂. The calcium sulfite is oxidised to calcium sulfate (gypsum): 2CaSO₃ + O₂ → 2CaSO₄. Gypsum is used to make plasterboard — turning a waste product into something useful!

Reducing air pollution:
• Catalytic converter: 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂
• Desulfurisation: SO₂ + CaCO₃ → CaSO₃ + CO₂
• Low-sulfur fuels
• Electric vehicles
• Particle filters
• Legislation (Clean Air Acts, ULEZ)

Common Misconception

Catalytic converters remove all pollutants from car exhaust.

Catalytic converters reduce CO, NOₓ and unburnt hydrocarbons, but they do NOT remove CO₂ (a greenhouse gas) or all particulates. Electric cars produce no exhaust emissions at all. Diesel cars need separate particulate filters.

Differentiation

Support:
Create a comparison table: pollution source → pollutant → reduction method. Fill in together.
Stretch:
Evaluate: should all cities introduce ULEZ-style zones? Give scientific and economic arguments.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Name two methods of reducing SO₂ emissions and two methods of reducing CO from car exhausts.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Describes methods for reducing each pollutant and explains how they work.
  • Getting there: Knows some methods but cannot explain the chemistry behind them.
  • Not yet: Cannot describe pollution reduction methods. Reteach with the comparison table.

Homework Suggestions

  • Research your local air quality index — what pollutants are measured?
  • Compare petrol and diesel engines: which produces more NOₓ? More particulates? Why?
  • Design a poster explaining acid rain: formation, effects, and prevention
