C30: Earth's Early Atmosphere

Foundation All Boards

Describe the composition of Earth's early atmosphere and explain how it changed over millions of years.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 1
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the composition of Earth's early atmosphere
  • Explain how the atmosphere changed over time
  • Describe the origin of life and the evidence for it

Prerequisites

  • Atoms and Elements (C1) — elements, compounds

Lesson: Earth's Early Atmosphere

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

What's in the Air?

The air today is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon, 0.04% CO₂. Was it always like this? What was the atmosphere like billions of years ago?

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Earth's early atmosphere (about 4 billion years ago) was very different from today:

Composition of early atmosphere:
• Mostly carbon dioxide (CO₂)
• Some water vapour (H₂O)
• Small amounts of methane (CH₄) and ammonia (NH₃)
• Very little or no oxygen (O₂)
• Very little or no nitrogen (N₂)

How the atmosphere changed:

1. Volcanic eruptions:
• Volcanoes released CO₂, H₂O, CH₄, NH₃ from inside the Earth
• Water vapour condensed → formed the oceans

2. Photosynthesis by plants/algae:
• Early plants and algae evolved and began photosynthesis
• 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
• This used up CO₂ and produced O₂
• Oxygen gradually built up in the atmosphere

3. Dissolving CO₂ in oceans:
• CO₂ dissolved in seawater → removed from atmosphere
• Formed carbonates (limestone) → locked away in rocks

4. Death of organisms:
• Dead organisms buried under sediment → became fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas)
• This locked away carbon that was originally in the atmosphere as CO₂

The result:
• CO₂ decreased dramatically (from ~95% to 0.04%)
• O₂ increased dramatically (from ~0% to 21%)
• N₂ increased (from volcanic sources and bacterial processes)
• Current atmosphere: 78% N₂, 21% O₂, 0.9% Ar, 0.04% CO₂

Evidence for early atmosphere:
• Fossil evidence shows early life was anaerobic (didn't need oxygen)
• Isotope ratios in rocks show ancient atmospheric composition
• The Miller-Urey experiment (1953) showed that amino acids can form from simple gases (CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, H₂) with electrical sparks — suggesting how life's building blocks may have formed
The Great Oxidation Event

About 2.4 billion years ago, photosynthetic cyanobacteria had been producing oxygen for hundreds of millions of years. Eventually, the oxygen built up faster than it was used up. This 'Great Oxidation Event' dramatically changed the atmosphere and allowed more complex life to evolve.

From CO₂ to O₂

Early atmosphere: ~95% CO₂, ~0% O₂. Today: ~0.04% CO₂, ~21% O₂. This massive change was caused by: (1) photosynthesis producing O₂, (2) CO₂ dissolving in oceans, (3) CO₂ locked away in fossil fuels and rocks. The process took billions of years.

Early atmosphere: CO₂ + H₂O + CH₄ + NH₃ (no O₂)

Change over time:
• Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
• CO₂ dissolved in oceans → carbonates
• Dead organisms → fossil fuels (locked away carbon)

Current atmosphere: 78% N₂, 21% O₂, 0.9% Ar, 0.04% CO₂

Common Misconception

The early atmosphere had lots of oxygen.

The early atmosphere had very little or no oxygen. Oxygen only built up after photosynthetic organisms (like cyanobacteria) evolved and started producing it. The Great Oxidation Event (~2.4 billion years ago) was when oxygen first became significant in the atmosphere.

Differentiation

Support:
Create a timeline on the wall: 4.5 billion years ago (early atmosphere) → 3.5 billion years ago (first life) → 2.4 billion years ago (oxygen appears) → today. Add key events at each stage.
Stretch:
Research the Miller-Urey experiment. What gases did they use? What did they find? What does this suggest about the origin of life?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Describe how Earth's atmosphere changed from its early composition to today. What were the main processes involved?

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Describes the early atmosphere composition, explains how it changed, and links photosynthesis to oxygen production.
  • Getting there: Knows the early atmosphere had CO₂ and no O₂ but cannot explain the full process of change.
  • Not yet: Cannot describe the early atmosphere. Reteach with the timeline and key processes.

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a labelled diagram showing how Earth's atmosphere changed over time
  • Research: what is the Miller-Urey experiment? What did it show?
  • Compare the early atmosphere with today's atmosphere in a table
