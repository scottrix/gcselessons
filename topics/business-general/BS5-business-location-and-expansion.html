Lesson 2: Core Concepts: business location and expansion

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on business location and expansion. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Location factors: proximity to customers/market, transport links, availability of suitable labour, cost of premises, competition, government incentives (grants, enterprise zones).
Key Fact: Business plan: a document setting out the business idea, market research, financial forecasts, marketing strategy. Essential for securing finance from banks or investors. Shows the business has thought through its strategy.
Key Fact: Organic growth: expanding from within - opening new outlets, increasing product range, growing online sales. Slow but controlled, less risky than external growth.
Key Fact: External growth: mergers (two firms agree to join) and takeovers (one firm buys another). Faster growth but risky: cultural clash, high cost, integration problems. Franchising: rapid expansion with lower capital risk.
Key Fact: Economies of scale: as a business grows, average cost per unit falls (bulk buying, technical efficiency, financial economies, marketing economies). Diseconomies of scale: beyond a certain size, average costs rise (poor communication, coordination problems, low morale).
Business Exam Tips: For expansion questions: 1) Identify the growth method, 2) Explain how it works, 3) Give advantages and disadvantages, 4) Consider the business's specific circumstances (size, industry, resources), 5) Recommend the most appropriate method with justification.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain three factors a business should consider when choosing a location for a new factory.

Answer: 1) Proximity to raw materials/suppliers (reduces transport costs for heavy/bulky inputs). 2) Transport links (access to motorways, ports, rail for distribution). 3) Availability of labour (skills in the local area, wage costs, unemployment levels). Other factors: cost of land, government grants, proximity to customers.

Lesson 3: Application: business location and expansion

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Business Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain three factors a business should consider when choosing a location for a new factory.

Answer: 1) Proximity to raw materials/suppliers (reduces transport costs for heavy/bulky inputs). 2) Transport links (access to motorways, ports, rail for distribution). 3) Availability of labour (skills in the local area, wage costs, unemployment levels). Other factors: cost of land, government grants, proximity to customers.

Q2: Compare organic growth and external growth as ways for a business to expand.

Answer: Organic growth: slow, controlled, funded from profits or borrowing, lower risk, management retains full control. External growth: rapid expansion through merger/takeover, high cost, risk of cultural clash and integration problems, but achieves instant market share increase. Organic is safer for risk-averse businesses; external suits businesses wanting rapid market dominance.

Q3: Explain what is meant by economies of scale and give three examples.

Answer: Economies of scale = as output increases, the average cost per unit falls. Examples: 1) Purchasing economies (bulk buying at discounted prices). 2) Technical economies (using larger, more efficient machinery). 3) Financial economies (large firms can borrow at lower interest rates). 4) Marketing economies (spreading advertising costs over more units).

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: business location and expansion

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A successful local bakery wants to expand nationally. Evaluate franchising versus opening company-owned stores as growth strategies. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: evaluate franchising (rapid expansion with lower capital risk, franchisees invest their own money, motivated owner-operators, but less quality control, shares profits, brand risk if franchisee performs poorly); evaluate company-owned stores (full control, all profits retained, consistent quality, but high capital cost, slower growth, all risk on the business); conclude: franchising suits rapid national expansion with limited capital, but the bakery must invest in strong training and quality systems to protect its brand.

Exam Tips: Always link location choice to the TYPE of business (retail needs customers; manufacturing needs transport) | Distinguish between mergers (agreed) and takeovers (hostile) | Explain economies of scale with the 'average cost per unit falls' formula - be precise
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Bigger is always better for a business because economies of scale mean lower costs. Correct: Economies of scale reduce average costs up to a point, but diseconomies of scale can set in when a business becomes too large: communication breaks down, decisions take longer, staff feel disconnected and morale falls, coordination costs increase. Many large businesses actually split into smaller units to avoid diseconomies. The optimal size depends on the industry - some industries (utilities, car manufacturing) benefit from huge scale; others (creative industries, personal services) work better small.
