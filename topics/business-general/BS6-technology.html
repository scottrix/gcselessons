Lesson 2: Core Concepts: technology in business

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on technology in business. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: E-commerce allows businesses to sell online 24/7, reaching a global market with lower overhead costs than a physical shop.
Key Fact: Digital communication (email, video conferencing, social media) speeds up decision-making and enables remote/flexible working.
Key Fact: Automation and robotics increase productivity and consistency but reduce unskilled jobs and require high capital investment.
Key Fact: EPOS (Electronic Point of Sale) tracks sales in real time, manages stock levels automatically, and provides data for marketing decisions.
Key Fact: Data security is critical: businesses must comply with GDPR, protect customer data from cyberattacks, and have backup systems.
Business Exam Tips: When discussing technology's impact, use the CED framework: Cost (investment vs savings), Efficiency (productivity gains), Disruption (effects on workers, customers, competitors). Always consider short-term costs versus long-term benefits.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two ways e-commerce has changed how businesses sell to customers.

Answer: E-commerce enables 24/7 trading without shop opening hours, reaches customers worldwide (not just local), reduces costs of rent and staff for physical premises, but requires website maintenance, delivery logistics, and faces online competition.

Lesson 3: Application: technology in business

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Business Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two ways e-commerce has changed how businesses sell to customers.

Answer: E-commerce enables 24/7 trading without shop opening hours, reaches customers worldwide (not just local), reduces costs of rent and staff for physical premises, but requires website maintenance, delivery logistics, and faces online competition.

Q2: Analyse the impact of automation on a manufacturing business and its workers.

Answer: Positive impacts: higher output, consistent quality, lower long-term labour costs, 24/7 production. Negative impacts: high initial investment, redundancy and morale issues for displaced workers, need for skilled technicians, potential breakdowns halting production.

Q3: Evaluate whether a small retail business should invest in an online store.

Answer: Arguments for: wider customer reach, lower overheads, trend towards online shopping (25%+ of UK retail), ability to compete with larger firms. Arguments against: website development costs, delivery management, returns handling, cybersecurity risks, existing loyal local customers. Conclusion depends on financial resources and market research - most businesses now need some online presence to remain competitive.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: technology in business

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate whether a traditional bookshop should invest in an e-commerce website. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify financial factors (website costs £2,000-£10,000, ongoing hosting, potential revenue increase); customer factors (convenience, wider reach, but some prefer browsing); competitive factors (Amazon dominance, differentiation through events/advice); operational factors (delivery logistics, stock management, returns). Conclude with a justified view: a hybrid model may work best - online presence to capture wider market while maintaining the physical shop's unique selling point of personal service and community.

Exam Tips: When evaluating technology, always consider both the benefits AND the costs/risks - no technology is purely positive. | Use stakeholder analysis: technology may benefit owners (lower costs) but harm workers (job losses). | Link technology to competitiveness: businesses that fail to adapt risk losing market share.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Technology always benefits businesses because it reduces costs. Correct: Technology can increase costs initially (equipment, training, maintenance) and may alienate customers who prefer personal service. Some technologies become obsolete quickly, requiring further investment. The key question is whether benefits outweigh costs in the specific business context.
