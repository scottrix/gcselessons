Lesson 2: Core Concepts: aims and objectives

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on aims and objectives. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Business aims: the general long-term goals of a business - e.g. 'to become the UK's leading online retailer'. Business objectives: specific, measurable targets that help achieve the aim - e.g. 'increase online sales by 15% in 2024'.
Key Fact: Common objectives: survival (especially for new businesses), profit maximisation (maximise difference between revenue and costs), growth (increase size, revenue, market share), customer satisfaction (retain customers, positive reviews), social/ethical objectives (fair trade, reducing carbon footprint).
Key Fact: SMART objectives: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. E.g. 'Increase market share from 10% to 15% within 2 years' - not just 'grow the business'.
Key Fact: Objectives change over time: a new business aims for survival; once established, it shifts to growth and profit maximisation; a mature business may focus on market share or ethical objectives.
Key Fact: Conflicting stakeholder objectives: owners want profit, employees want higher wages, customers want lower prices, local community wants less pollution. Businesses must balance these.
Business Exam Tips: For aims and objectives questions: 1) State the aim (general direction), 2) Write SMART objectives (specific targets), 3) Explain why these are appropriate for the business's stage, 4) Consider potential conflicts between stakeholders.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain the difference between a business aim and a business objective, giving an example of each.

Answer: An aim is a general, long-term goal - e.g. 'to become the most popular cafe in the city'. An objective is a specific, measurable target that supports the aim - e.g. 'to achieve a 20% market share of city centre coffee sales within 12 months'. Aims give direction; objectives provide measurable milestones.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Lesson 3: Application: aims and objectives

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Business Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain the difference between a business aim and a business objective, giving an example of each.

Answer: An aim is a general, long-term goal - e.g. 'to become the most popular cafe in the city'. An objective is a specific, measurable target that supports the aim - e.g. 'to achieve a 20% market share of city centre coffee sales within 12 months'. Aims give direction; objectives provide measurable milestones.

Q2: Write a SMART objective for a new coffee shop that has been trading for 6 months.

Answer: 'Increase monthly revenue from 8,000 GBP to 12,000 GBP within 6 months by introducing a loyalty scheme and extending opening hours to 7pm.' This is Specific (increase revenue), Measurable (from 8k to 12k), Achievable (through specific actions), Relevant (supports growth), Time-bound (within 6 months).

Q3: Explain why a business's objectives might change as it moves from start-up to an established company.

Answer: Start-up: survival is the priority - covering costs, building a customer base, establishing the brand. Established: survival is assured, so objectives shift to profit maximisation, growth, increasing market share. Mature: may focus on ethical objectives or diversification as competition increases. Objectives evolve as circumstances change.

Error Review

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: aims and objectives

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response A social enterprise that sells fair-trade products aims to both make a profit and support developing-world farmers. Evaluate the potential conflict between these two objectives and suggest how the business could balance them. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: explain the conflict (fair-trade means higher purchase prices, reducing profit margins; profit focus might push towards cheaper, non-fair-trade suppliers); evaluate solutions (premium pricing for ethically-conscious customers, cost savings elsewhere in operations, marketing the ethical story to justify higher prices); conclude that the objectives can be compatible if the target market values fair-trade enough to pay a premium, but the business must carefully manage costs to remain viable.

Exam Tips: Always write objectives in SMART format - examiners check for all five elements | Link objectives to the business's stage of development (new = survival, established = growth) | Consider stakeholder conflict when discussing objectives - owners vs employees vs customers
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Profit maximisation is the only objective that matters because all businesses exist solely to make money for their owners. Correct: While profit is essential for survival, many businesses pursue other objectives simultaneously: social enterprises prioritise social impact; small business owners may value work-life balance over maximum profit; public sector organisations aim to provide services, not maximise profit. Even profit-focused businesses need customer satisfaction and ethical behaviour to sustain long-term profitability.
