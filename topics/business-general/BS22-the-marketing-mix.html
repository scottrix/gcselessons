Lesson 2: Core Concepts: the marketing mix

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on the marketing mix. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The marketing mix (4Ps) combines Product, Price, Place, and Promotion into a coherent strategy. All elements must be consistent - a premium product at a budget price with limited availability and cheap advertising creates confusion.
Key Fact: Product: what is being sold, including features, quality, design, branding, packaging, and after-sales service. It must meet customer needs and be differentiated from competitors.
Key Fact: Price: must reflect the product's positioning and target market's willingness to pay. Premium products need premium prices; budget products need competitive prices. Pricing must also cover costs and generate profit.
Key Fact: Place: how and where the product is sold. Luxury goods are sold in exclusive outlets; everyday products need wide distribution. Online channels have changed place strategy for most businesses.
Key Fact: Promotion: how customers learn about the product. Must reach the target market through appropriate channels and communicate the right message aligned with the product's positioning.
Business Exam Tips: When evaluating the marketing mix, use the CCC framework: Consistency (do all 4Ps send the same message?), Customer focus (does the mix serve the target market's needs?), Competitive advantage (does the mix differentiate from rivals?). A strong marketing mix is coherent, customer-centred, and competitive.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain why all four elements of the marketing mix must be consistent, using an example.

Answer: Consistency is essential because mixed signals confuse customers and damage the brand. Example: if a restaurant positions itself as fine dining (product: high-quality food), but charges fast-food prices (price), operates from a takeaway outlet (place), and advertises on budget billboards (promotion), customers won't know what to expect. The mix must align: fine dining = premium price, smart location, and sophisticated promotion.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Lesson 3: Application: the marketing mix

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Business Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain why all four elements of the marketing mix must be consistent, using an example.

Answer: Consistency is essential because mixed signals confuse customers and damage the brand. Example: if a restaurant positions itself as fine dining (product: high-quality food), but charges fast-food prices (price), operates from a takeaway outlet (place), and advertises on budget billboards (promotion), customers won't know what to expect. The mix must align: fine dining = premium price, smart location, and sophisticated promotion.

Q2: Describe how the marketing mix might differ for a budget airline compared to a luxury airline.

Answer: Budget airline: product = basic seat, no frills, charged for extras; price = low, competitive; place = online booking, secondary airports; promotion = price-led advertising, sales promotions, social media deals. Luxury airline: product = spacious seats, gourmet food, lounge access; price = premium; place = major airports, travel agents, direct booking; promotion = prestige advertising, partnerships with luxury brands, exclusive loyalty programmes.

Q3: Evaluate whether a business should adapt its marketing mix when entering a foreign market.

Answer: Arguments for adaptation: different cultures have different preferences (McDonald's offers vegetarian-only outlets in India), local income levels require different pricing, distribution systems vary by country, and local media habits differ. Arguments against: maintaining a consistent global brand is valuable (Coca-Cola is recognisable everywhere), adaptation increases costs (new recipes, packaging, campaigns), and some products are genuinely universal. Conclusion: some adaptation is usually necessary (especially product and promotion), but the core brand identity should remain consistent. The degree of adaptation depends on cultural distance and the product type.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: the marketing mix

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate the marketing mix of a new energy drink aimed at 16-24 year-old students, and suggest improvements. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: analyse the current mix (product: energy drink with caffeine and vitamins; price: competitive at £1.50; place: convenience stores and vending machines; promotion: social media ads); evaluate consistency (product and promotion align with youth market, but is the price right for students on budgets? Are convenience stores the best place when students spend time on campus?); suggest improvements: product (sugar-free version for health-conscious students), price (multi-pack discounts, student loyalty scheme), place (university campus vending, online subscription delivery), promotion (university ambassador programme, fresher's fair sampling, TikTok challenges); conclude: the basic mix is sound but needs refinement to truly penetrate the student market - campus-based distribution and student-specific promotions would significantly improve effectiveness.

Exam Tips: Always check that all 4Ps are ALIGNED in your answer - examiners look for this specifically. | When discussing the marketing mix, use the word 'coherent' or 'consistent' to show AO3 analysis. | Don't treat the 4Ps as independent - a change in one affects the others (e.g. raising price may require improved product quality or more premium promotion).
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: A good product will sell itself, so the other 3Ps don't matter as much. Correct: Even the best product will fail without the right marketing mix support. A brilliant product at the wrong price (too high for the market), unavailable in the right places, or unknown to the target customers will not sell. Betamax was technically superior to VHS but lost because VHS had better distribution (place) and lower prices. The marketing mix is called a 'mix' because all elements must work together - no single element can compensate for weaknesses in the others.
