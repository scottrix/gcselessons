Lesson 2: Core Concepts: globalisation and legislation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on globalisation and legislation. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Globalisation is the increasing interconnection of world economies through trade, investment, and technology - it means businesses can operate internationally more easily.
Key Fact: Multinational corporations (MNCs) operate in multiple countries: they benefit from lower labour costs and new markets but face cultural differences and complex regulations.
Key Fact: Employment law protects workers: National Minimum Wage, Working Time Regulations, Health and Safety at Work Act, Equality Act (preventing discrimination).
Key Fact: Consumer protection legislation (Consumer Rights Act 2015) ensures products are as described, fit for purpose, and of satisfactory quality - giving consumers the right to refunds, repairs, or replacements.
Key Fact: Legislation creates compliance costs for businesses (training, monitoring, documentation) but also sets minimum standards that protect stakeholders and create a level playing field.
Business Exam Tips: When evaluating legislation, use the PEST framework: Political (government policy direction), Economic (compliance costs vs productivity gains), Social (worker and consumer protection), Technological (how laws adapt to new tech). Always conclude by weighing protection benefits against compliance costs.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two benefits and two drawbacks of globalisation for a UK business.

Answer: Benefits: access to larger markets (billions of potential customers), lower production costs (manufacturing in countries with cheaper labour), economies of scale. Drawbacks: increased competition from foreign firms, cultural and language barriers, risk of supply chain disruption (e.g. political instability), potential reputational damage from overseas practices.

Lesson 3: Application: globalisation and legislation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Business Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two benefits and two drawbacks of globalisation for a UK business.

Answer: Benefits: access to larger markets (billions of potential customers), lower production costs (manufacturing in countries with cheaper labour), economies of scale. Drawbacks: increased competition from foreign firms, cultural and language barriers, risk of supply chain disruption (e.g. political instability), potential reputational damage from overseas practices.

Q2: Describe how the Consumer Rights Act 2015 protects customers and affects businesses.

Answer: The Act gives consumers the right to: goods that match their description, are fit for purpose, and are of satisfactory quality. If not, consumers can claim a full refund within 30 days, or a repair/replacement after that. Businesses must: ensure quality control, provide accurate descriptions, train staff on returns policies, and maintain records - increasing costs but also building trust and reducing disputes.

Q3: Evaluate whether employment legislation does more harm than good to businesses.

Answer: Arguments for: protects vulnerable workers from exploitation, reduces inequality, improves health and safety (fewer accidents), creates a motivated workforce, ensures fair competition (no race to the bottom on wages). Arguments against: increases costs (higher wages, admin, training), reduces flexibility, may make UK businesses less competitive than those in countries with weaker laws, can discourage hiring. Conclusion: while compliance costs exist, the benefits of a protected, motivated workforce and consumer trust outweigh the costs - businesses that treat workers well often see higher productivity and lower staff turnover.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: globalisation and legislation

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate whether a UK small business should start exporting to the EU after Brexit. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: consider opportunities (access to 450M consumers, potential for growth, diversification of risk); challenges (new customs procedures, tariffs, regulatory divergence, currency risk); resources needed (export documentation, logistics, market research, language skills); and conclude with conditions: the business should export if it has a unique product, financial reserves to manage initial costs, and has researched target markets - but may be better starting with just one EU country rather than the entire bloc.

Exam Tips: When discussing legislation, always identify WHO it protects and WHAT it costs businesses. | Globalisation questions need a balanced answer: consider both UK businesses expanding abroad AND foreign businesses entering UK markets. | Use specific legislation names (Consumer Rights Act, Equality Act) to show AO1 knowledge.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Globalisation only benefits large multinational corporations. Correct: Globalisation also benefits small businesses through: access to international markets via e-commerce, cheaper imported raw materials, technology that enables remote work and global collaboration, and cultural diversity that inspires new products. Even a sole trader can sell worldwide through platforms like Etsy or Amazon. However, small businesses may struggle to compete with MNCs on price and brand recognition.
