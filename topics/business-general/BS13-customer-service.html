Lesson 2: Core Concepts: customer service

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on customer service. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Good customer service creates competitive advantage: it builds loyalty, generates repeat business, and encourages positive word-of-mouth recommendations (free marketing).
Key Fact: Methods of delivering customer service: well-trained staff, clear returns policies, efficient complaint handling, after-sales support (warranties, helplines), personalised communication, and multichannel support (phone, email, chat, social media).
Key Fact: Monitoring customer service: customer feedback surveys, mystery shoppers, complaint analysis, online reviews, and Net Promoter Score (NPS).
Key Fact: Handling complaints effectively: listen, acknowledge, resolve quickly, and learn from the complaint to prevent recurrence. A well-handled complaint can increase customer loyalty.
Key Fact: Poor customer service costs businesses: lost sales, negative reviews, damaged reputation, and higher marketing costs to replace lost customers (acquiring a new customer costs 5-7 times more than retaining one).
Business Exam Tips: When evaluating customer service investments, use the RACE framework: Retention (how many customers stay?), Acquisition (does service attract new customers through referrals?), Cost (what does poor service cost in complaints and lost sales?), Efficiency (does good service reduce complaints and returns?). Good customer service is an investment, not a cost.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Explain two ways good customer service can benefit a business.

Answer: Good customer service (1) builds customer loyalty - satisfied customers return and spend more over time (customer lifetime value), and (2) generates word-of-mouth recommendations - happy customers tell others, providing free credible advertising that is more trusted than paid marketing.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Lesson 3: Application: customer service

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Business Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Explain two ways good customer service can benefit a business.

Answer: Good customer service (1) builds customer loyalty - satisfied customers return and spend more over time (customer lifetime value), and (2) generates word-of-mouth recommendations - happy customers tell others, providing free credible advertising that is more trusted than paid marketing.

Q2: Describe how a business can monitor the quality of its customer service.

Answer: Monitoring methods: (1) Customer feedback surveys (online, in-store, post-purchase) - quantitative data on satisfaction scores and qualitative comments. (2) Mystery shoppers - trained evaluators pose as customers and rate service against specific criteria (greeting, product knowledge, wait times). (3) Complaint analysis - tracking number, type, and resolution time of complaints to identify recurring problems. (4) Online review monitoring - tracking ratings on Trustpilot, Google, and social media.

Q3: Evaluate whether investing in customer service training is more important than investing in marketing.

Answer: Customer service training benefits: loyal customers who spend more over time, fewer complaints reducing costs, positive word-of-mouth reducing need for advertising, motivated staff who feel competent. Marketing benefits: reaches new customers, builds brand awareness, drives immediate sales. Argument: customer service training is more important long-term because retained customers are more profitable than constantly acquiring new ones (5-7x cheaper to retain). However, marketing is needed to attract customers in the first place. Conclusion: both are essential, but a business with limited budget should prioritise customer service - excellent service becomes marketing through recommendations.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: customer service

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Evaluate whether an online retailer should invest more in chatbot technology or human customer service advisors. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: analyse chatbots (24/7 availability, handles common queries instantly, low cost per interaction, consistent responses, but cannot handle complex/emotional issues, can frustrate customers with limited understanding); human advisors (empathy and judgement for complex issues, can de-escalate complaints, build relationships, but limited availability, higher cost, variable quality depending on training/mood); conclude: a hybrid approach is optimal - chatbots for routine queries (order tracking, returns policy, FAQs) freeing humans for complex and emotional interactions, giving customers the right support at the right time while controlling costs.

Exam Tips: Always link customer service to financial outcomes: loyalty = repeat sales = revenue stability. | When discussing complaints, remember: a well-handled complaint can actually IMPROVE customer loyalty (the 'service recovery paradox'). | Use the 'cost of acquisition vs retention' statistic: it costs 5-7 times more to gain a new customer than keep an existing one.
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Handling customer complaints well means always giving the customer what they want, even if it's unreasonable. Correct: Good complaint handling means resolving the issue fairly and professionally, not simply giving in to every demand. A business should: listen to understand the problem, acknowledge the customer's feelings, offer a fair solution within company policy (refund, repair, replacement, or apology), and learn from the complaint. Giving customers everything they demand could be financially unsustainable and unfair to other customers. The goal is a reasonable resolution that maintains the relationship.
