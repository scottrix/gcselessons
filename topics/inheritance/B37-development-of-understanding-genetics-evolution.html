B37: The Development of Understanding of Genetics and Evolution

Foundation Higher All Boards

Trace the history of scientific discoveries in genetics and evolution, from early ideas to modern understanding.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the contributions of key scientists to our understanding of genetics
  • Explain how understanding of genetics has developed over time
  • Understand the historical development of the theory of evolution
  • Evaluate how scientific understanding changes with new evidence

Prerequisites

  • Basic understanding of genetics (B31-B32)
  • Understanding of evolution (B33)

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Timeline of key discoveries
  • Information cards on key scientists

Lesson 1: History of Genetics

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Question

Ask: "Before we knew about DNA, how did people explain why children looked like their parents?" Discuss early ideas about inheritance (blending theory, etc.).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Key scientists and discoveries in genetics:
  • Aristotle (384-322 BC): proposed that inheritance was a blend of contributions from both parents (blending theory). This was incorrect but was the dominant idea for centuries.
  • Gregor Mendel (1822-1884): Austrian monk who studied pea plants. Discovered that characteristics are inherited as discrete units (what we now call genes). Found the principles of segregation and independent assortment. Published in 1866 but was ignored until 1900.
  • Walter Sutton and Theodore Boveri (1902-1903): independently proposed that chromosomes carry genetic material (the Boveri-Sutton chromosome theory).
  • Thomas Hunt Morgan (1910): proved that genes are located on chromosomes using fruit fly (Drosophila) experiments.
  • Frederick Griffith (1928): discovered the "transforming principle" — something from dead bacteria could transform living bacteria. This was later identified as DNA.
  • Watson and Crick (1953): discovered the double helix structure of DNA, building on X-ray crystallography work by Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins.
  • Human Genome Project (1990-2003): mapped the entire human genome — identifying all 20,000-25,000 genes.
The history shows how scientific understanding develops: ideas are proposed, tested, and sometimes overturned when new evidence emerges. Science is a cumulative process.
Worked Example

"Explain how Mendel's work contributed to our understanding of genetics." Answer: Mendel crossed pea plants with different characteristics and observed the ratios of offspring. He discovered that characteristics are inherited as discrete units (genes) that are either dominant or recessive. He found that traits do not blend but are passed on as distinct units. His work was not recognised until 1900 when it was rediscovered and confirmed.

Common Misconception

"Mendel discovered DNA"

Mendel discovered the principles of inheritance (dominant/recessive genes, segregation) through breeding experiments in the 1860s. He did not know about DNA or chromosomes. DNA was not identified as the genetic material until the 1940s-50s, and its structure was not discovered until 1953.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on key names: Mendel (pea plants, dominant/recessive), Watson and Crick (DNA structure), Human Genome Project (mapped all genes). Provide a simple timeline to complete.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research Rosalind Franklin's contribution to discovering DNA structure. Why was she not included in the Nobel Prize? Discuss the ethics of scientific credit and recognition.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Timeline

Students place 5 key discoveries in chronological order on a timeline. Quick check.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe key contributions to genetics and explain how understanding developed
  • Getting there: Can name key scientists but cannot explain their contributions
  • Not yet: Cannot describe the history of genetics

Lesson 2: History of Evolution

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Early Ideas

Ask: "Before Darwin, how did people explain why there were so many different species?" Discuss ideas like special creation and the Great Chain of Being.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Key figures in the history of evolution:
  • Charles Darwin (1809-1882):
    • Sailed on HMS Beagle (1831-1836) and observed finches on the Galápagos Islands
    • Noted that finches on different islands had different beak shapes suited to different foods
    • Developed the theory of evolution by natural selection
    • Published "On the Origin of Species" in 1859
    • Theory was controversial because it challenged religious beliefs about creation
  • Alfred Russel Wallace (1823-1913):
    • Independently developed a theory of evolution by natural selection
    • His work prompted Darwin to publish his own theory
    • Contributed to the concept of speciation and warned about the dangers of extinction
  • Thomas Malthus (1766-1834):
    • Economist who wrote about population growth
    • Argued that food supply grows arithmetically while population grows geometrically
    • This inspired Darwin to think about competition and survival
  • Lamarck (1744-1829):
    • Proposed that organisms could pass on characteristics acquired during their lifetime
    • Example: giraffes stretched their necks to reach high leaves, and this longer neck was passed to offspring
    • This theory was WRONG — acquired characteristics are not inherited (unless epigenetic)
Evidence supporting Darwin's theory:
  • Fossil record shows gradual change over time
  • Anatomy: homologous structures suggest common ancestry
  • Antibiotic resistance: direct observation of evolution
  • DNA comparison: closely related species have more similar DNA
Worked Example

"Explain how Darwin's observations on the Galápagos Islands contributed to his theory of natural selection." Answer: Darwin observed that finches on different islands had different beak shapes. Each beak was suited to the food available on that island (e.g. hard seeds, insects, cactus). He reasoned that finches with beaks suited to the available food survived and reproduced more successfully. Over time, populations on different islands became different species. This supported his theory of natural selection.

Common Misconception

"Darwin discovered evolution"

The idea that species change over time existed before Darwin (e.g. Lamarck, Erasmus Darwin). Charles Darwin's specific contribution was the mechanism: natural selection. He proposed HOW evolution occurs, not just that it does.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on: "Darwin observed finches, noticed variation, proposed natural selection. Lamarck was wrong about acquired traits. Wallace also had the idea." Provide a simple comparison of Darwin and Lamarck.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research why Darwin delayed publishing for 20 years. How did the voyage of HMS Beagle shape his thinking? Compare Darwin's and Wallace's contributions.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Key Figure

Students write one sentence about the contribution of Darwin, Wallace, and Lamarck. Quick share.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe the historical development of evolutionary theory
  • Getting there: Knows Darwin proposed natural selection but cannot explain the context
  • Not yet: Cannot describe the history of evolutionary theory

Homework Suggestions

  • Create a timeline of key discoveries in genetics and evolution with dates and scientists
  • Write a biography of one key scientist (Mendel, Darwin, Watson, or Franklin)
  • Compare Darwin's theory with Lamarck's theory — which was correct and why?
  • Research how DNA evidence has confirmed evolutionary relationships between species
