B35: Cloning

Foundation Higher All Boards

Explore the different methods of cloning including embryo transfer, adult cell cloning, and plant cloning, and evaluate the ethical implications.

Lesson Overview

Estimated Lessons: 2
Tier: Foundation and Higher
Duration: 50 minutes per lesson
Exam Boards: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA

Learning Objectives

  • Describe embryo transplant cloning and adult cell cloning (somatic cell nuclear transfer)
  • Explain plant cloning techniques
  • Evaluate the benefits and risks of cloning
  • Understand the difference between reproductive and therapeutic cloning

Prerequisites

  • Understanding of cell division (B2)
  • Knowledge of reproduction and meiosis (B34)

Materials & Equipment

  • Whiteboard and pens
  • Diagrams of cloning processes
  • Worksheets on ethical issues

Lesson 1: Animal Cloning

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Dolly the Sheep

Ask: "In 1996, scientists created Dolly the Sheep — the first mammal cloned from an adult cell. What do you think was the point of doing this? Was it ethical?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Methods of animal cloning: 1. Embryo transplant (twinning):
  1. An embryo at an early stage of development is split into two (or more) separate cells
  2. Each cell contains the same genetic material (clones)
  3. The separated cells are implanted into different surrogate mothers
  4. Each mother gives birth to a genetically identical offspring
  • Used in farming to produce animals with desirable characteristics
  • Limited — can only produce a small number of clones from one embryo
2. Adult cell cloning (Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer — SCNT):
  1. Take a body (somatic) cell from the adult animal to be cloned
  2. Take an egg cell from another animal and remove its nucleus (enucleation)
  3. Insert the nucleus from the somatic cell into the enucleated egg cell
  4. Give the egg cell an electric shock to stimulate it to divide
  5. The developing embryo is implanted into a surrogate mother
  6. The surrogate gives birth to a genetically identical clone of the somatic cell donor
  • This is how Dolly the Sheep was created
  • Can produce clones of adult animals
  • Low success rate — many attempts fail
Therapeutic cloning (Higher only):
  • Uses the same technique as SCNT but the embryo is NOT implanted into a surrogate
  • Instead, stem cells are harvested from the embryo at the blastocyst stage
  • Stem cells can be differentiated into specific cell types (e.g. heart cells, nerve cells)
  • Potential uses: replacing damaged tissue, treating diseases (e.g. Parkinson's, diabetes), testing drugs
  • No immunological rejection because the cells are genetically identical to the patient
Worked Example

"Describe the process of adult cell cloning (SCNT)." Answer: (1) Take a somatic (body) cell from the donor animal. (2) Remove the nucleus from an egg cell from another animal. (3) Insert the somatic cell nucleus into the enucleated egg cell. (4) Stimulate the egg with an electric shock. (5) The egg divides to form an embryo. (6) Implant the embryo into a surrogate mother. (7) The surrogate gives birth to a clone of the somatic cell donor.

Common Misconception

"Cloned animals are exactly identical to the original"

Cloned animals have the same nuclear DNA as the donor, but mitochondrial DNA comes from the egg cell donor. Also, environmental factors, epigenetics, and random developmental differences mean clones are not 100% identical in appearance or behaviour.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on: "Cloning makes genetically identical copies. Two methods: split an embryo, or put an adult cell nucleus into an egg cell." Provide step-by-step diagrams.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Research therapeutic cloning in more depth. How close are we to growing replacement organs? What is the difference between embryonic stem cells and adult stem cells?

Plenary (5 minutes)

Two Methods

Students name the two methods of animal cloning and write one key difference between them.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe both methods of animal cloning
  • Getting there: Can describe one method but not the other
  • Not yet: Cannot describe any cloning method

Lesson 2: Plant Cloning and Ethical Issues

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Garden Cloning

Ask: "Have you ever taken a cutting from a plant? That's a form of cloning! What other ways can plants be cloned?"

Main Content (35 minutes)

Teaching Notes:
Plant cloning:
  • Cuttings: a piece of stem, root, or leaf is cut off and placed in moist compost. It grows roots and develops into a new plant. Genetically identical to the parent.
  • Tissue culture (micropropagation):
    1. A small piece of plant tissue (explant) is removed
    2. Placed in a sterile container with nutrient gel containing plant hormones
    3. The cells divide to form a mass of undifferentiated cells called a callus
    4. Hormones are adjusted to stimulate root and shoot growth
    5. Plantlets develop and are transferred to soil
    Thousands of identical plants can be produced from a tiny piece of tissue.
  • Runners: strawberry plants naturally produce runners (horizontal stems) that root and form new plants — a natural form of cloning
Benefits of plant cloning:
  • Produce large numbers of identical plants quickly
  • Plants are true to type — same quality, disease resistance, yield
  • Can clone rare or endangered species
  • Produce disease-free plants (by using tissue culture from disease-free tissue)
Ethical issues of animal cloning:
  • Welfare of cloned animals — high failure rate, health problems
  • Cloning endangered or extinct species — should we? Could we?
  • Human reproductive cloning — should it be allowed?
  • Therapeutic cloning — destroying embryos for stem cells raises ethical concerns
  • Economic implications — cloned livestock for farming
Worked Example

"Give two benefits and one ethical concern of cloning." Benefits: (1) Large numbers of identical plants/animals with desirable characteristics can be produced quickly. (2) Rare or endangered species could be preserved. Ethical concern: Cloned animals may suffer health problems and the welfare of surrogates used in the process may be compromised.

Common Misconception

"Therapeutic cloning involves creating a clone baby"

Therapeutic cloning does NOT create a cloned organism. The embryo is only allowed to develop to the blastocyst stage (a few days), then stem cells are harvested. The embryo is never implanted into a surrogate. The purpose is to produce stem cells for medical treatments, not to clone a person.

Differentiation

Support (SEN/Lower Ability):
Focus on plant cloning: "Cuttings and tissue culture make identical plants." Ethical issues: "Cloning raises questions about animal welfare and whether we should clone humans." Provide a pros/cons list.
Stretch (Higher/Gifted):
Debate: "Should human reproductive cloning be legalised?" Research international laws on cloning. Investigate why Dolly the Sheep had health problems and died early.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Quick Write

Students write one benefit and one ethical concern of cloning on mini-whiteboards. Discuss as a class.

Assessment Criteria

  • Got it: Can describe plant cloning methods and evaluate ethical issues of cloning
  • Getting there: Can describe plant cloning but cannot evaluate ethical issues
  • Not yet: Cannot describe cloning methods or evaluate

Homework Suggestions

  • Research Dolly the Sheep: how was she created, why was it significant, what problems did she have?
  • Create a poster comparing the three methods of cloning (embryo, adult cell, plant tissue culture)
  • Write a balanced argument for or against therapeutic cloning
  • Investigate one real-world application of plant tissue culture (e.g. disease-free banana plants)
