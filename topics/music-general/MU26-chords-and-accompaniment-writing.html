Lesson 2: Core Concepts: chords and accompaniment writing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on chords and accompaniment writing. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Common pop progressions: I-V-vi-IV (four-chord loop), I-IV-V (rock/blues), ii-V-I (jazz cadence) — each creates a different harmonic character.
Key Fact: Accompaniment styles define genre: Alberti bass (Classical), riff (rock/pop), broken chords (Romantic), ostinato (minimalist), pedal/drone (folk/traditional).
Key Fact: Harmonic rhythm (how often chords change) affects momentum: fast harmonic rhythm creates drive; slow harmonic rhythm creates stasis and calm.
Key Fact: Dominant seventh chords (V7) create tension that resolves to the tonic — they are essential for cadences and key-defining progressions.
Music Exam Tips: Choose genre-appropriate progressions. Match accompaniment style to genre. Use cadences intentionally. Notate chord symbols. Annotate harmonic decisions.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe how chords and accompaniment writing are used in a piece of music you have studied. Use musical vocabulary.

Answer: A detailed description using precise musical vocabulary (e.g. specific terms from the AQA elements list), referencing a named piece with accurate observations about how the elements are used and their effect.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Your student teaches the key points back to you without looking. Fill any gaps immediately.

Lesson 3: Application: chords and accompaniment writing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Music Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe how chords and accompaniment writing are used in a piece of music you have studied. Use musical vocabulary.

Answer: A detailed description using precise musical vocabulary (e.g. specific terms from the AQA elements list), referencing a named piece with accurate observations about how the elements are used and their effect.

Q2: Compare how two different pieces use chords and accompaniment writing. Explain the effect in each case.

Answer: A comparison of two pieces identifying similarities and differences in how chords and accompaniment writing are used, explaining the different effects created in each musical context, using comparative language.

Q3: How could you use chords and accompaniment writing in your own composition or performance? Explain your choices.

Answer: A practical application showing understanding of how chords and accompaniment writing can be manipulated in composition or performance, with specific examples of choices made and their intended effect.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Error Review

Review any questions answered incorrectly. Identify whether the error was knowledge, method, or reading the question.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: chords and accompaniment writing

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Compose a chord progression and accompaniment pattern for a melody you have written. Annotate explaining your harmonic choices and how the accompaniment style serves the genre. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: use genre-appropriate chord progressions; choose accompaniment patterns that match the style; show understanding of cadences; use chord symbols correctly; annotate with precise harmonic vocabulary; demonstrate coherent harmonic direction. Mark scheme: AO3 (analyse and evaluate) + AO4 (knowledge and understanding) = total marks for listening; AO1 (perform) + AO2 (compose) for coursework components

Exam Tips: Always use PRECISE musical vocabulary — not vague descriptions like it sounds nice | Reference SPECIFIC musical elements when analysing any piece | For listening questions: identify elements FIRST, then explain their effect | For study pieces: know the context, structure and key features in detail | For performing: focus on accuracy, expression and interpretation | For composing: show development of ideas, not just repetition
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: You just put chords over the melody and it sounds fine. Correct: Chord choices and accompaniment patterns must be INTENTIONAL and GENRE-APPROPRIATE. Classical styles use functional harmony with cadences; popular styles use stock progressions and riffs; folk styles use drones and modal harmony. The examiner assesses whether your harmonic choices serve your compositional intention.
Stretch & Challenge (Grade 8-9):

  • Real-world: research one real-world use or example of chords and accompaniment writing
