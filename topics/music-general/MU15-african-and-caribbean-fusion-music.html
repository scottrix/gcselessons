Lesson 2: Core Concepts: african and caribbean fusion music

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on african and caribbean fusion music. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Polyrhythm (multiple conflicting rhythmic patterns simultaneously) is central to African music — it creates complex, layered rhythmic textures.
Key Fact: Caribbean genres each have distinct rhythmic signatures: reggae (off-beat skank guitar, one-drop drumming), ska (fast off-beat), calypso (syncopated melody).
Key Fact: Cyclic structures repeat musical patterns — unlike Western through-composed forms, African music often builds intensity through cyclical repetition and layering.
Key Fact: Afrobeat (Fela Kuti) combines West African highlife with jazz, funk and Yoruba music — it demonstrates how fusion creates entirely new genres.
Music Exam Tips: Name rhythmic devices precisely (polyrhythm, cross-rhythm, skank, off-beat). Explain cyclic structure. Identify specific Caribbean genre features. Compare fusion approaches.

Practice (10 minutes)

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: african and caribbean fusion music

Duration: 50 minutes

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Compare the rhythmic features of an African drumming piece with a reggae track. Identify at least four rhythmic terms from the AQA list and explain their effect. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify polyrhythm and cross-rhythm; name Caribbean rhythmic devices (skank, one-drop, off-beat); explain cyclic structure; compare different fusion approaches; use precise AQA vocabulary from the elements list. Mark scheme: AO3 (analyse and evaluate) + AO4 (knowledge and understanding) = total marks for listening; AO1 (perform) + AO2 (compose) for coursework components

Exam Tips: Always use PRECISE musical vocabulary — not vague descriptions like it sounds nice | Reference SPECIFIC musical elements when analysing any piece | For listening questions: identify elements FIRST, then explain their effect | For study pieces: know the context, structure and key features in detail | For performing: focus on accuracy, expression and interpretation | For composing: show development of ideas, not just repetition
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: African and Caribbean music is just drumming with no structure. Correct: African and Caribbean music has sophisticated structures: polyrhythm, cyclic patterns, call and response, and specific rhythmic cells (clave, tumbao, skank). These are identifiable, nameable features using AQA vocabulary. The exam expects precise analytical terminology.
