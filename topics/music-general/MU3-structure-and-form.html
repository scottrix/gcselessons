Lesson 2: Core Concepts: structure and form

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on structure and form. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Binary form has two sections (A-B); ternary has three (A-B-A) — the return of A in ternary creates a satisfying sense of completion.
Key Fact: Rondo form features a recurring refrain (A) alternating with contrasting episodes (A-B-A-C-A-D-A) — common in Classical concertos.
Key Fact: Sonata form has three sections: exposition (two contrasting themes), development (themes manipulated), recapitulation (themes return in tonic).
Key Fact: Popular music structures: verse-chorus (alternating storytelling and hook), 12-bar blues (I-IV-V chord pattern), intro-outro-break (framing and variation).
Music Exam Tips: Always name the STRUCTURE using correct terminology. Explain how sections RELATE to each other. For sonata form, identify the key relationships. For popular music, identify verse-chorus-break patterns.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe how structure and form are used in a piece of music you have studied. Use musical vocabulary.

Answer: A detailed description using precise musical vocabulary (e.g. specific terms from the AQA elements list), referencing a named piece with accurate observations about how the elements are used and their effect.

Plenary (5 minutes)

Explain Back

Lesson 3: Application: structure and form

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Music Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe how structure and form are used in a piece of music you have studied. Use musical vocabulary.

Answer: A detailed description using precise musical vocabulary (e.g. specific terms from the AQA elements list), referencing a named piece with accurate observations about how the elements are used and their effect.

Q2: Compare how two different pieces use structure and form. Explain the effect in each case.

Answer: A comparison of two pieces identifying similarities and differences in how structure and form are used, explaining the different effects created in each musical context, using comparative language.

Q3: How could you use structure and form in your own composition or performance? Explain your choices.

Answer: A practical application showing understanding of how structure and form can be manipulated in composition or performance, with specific examples of choices made and their intended effect.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: structure and form

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Compare the structure of a Classical movement (e.g. sonata form or rondo) with a popular song structure. Explain how each structure serves its musical purpose. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify structures by name using precise terminology; explain HOW each section functions within the whole; compare how different structures create different musical journeys; use structural vocabulary accurately (exposition, development, recapitulation, refrain, episode). Mark scheme: AO3 (analyse and evaluate) + AO4 (knowledge and understanding) = total marks for listening; AO1 (perform) + AO2 (compose) for coursework components

Exam Tips: Always use PRECISE musical vocabulary — not vague descriptions like it sounds nice | Reference SPECIFIC musical elements when analysing any piece | For listening questions: identify elements FIRST, then explain their effect | For study pieces: know the context, structure and key features in detail | For performing: focus on accuracy, expression and interpretation | For composing: show development of ideas, not just repetition
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Structure just means the order of sections in a piece. Correct: Structure is about how musical ideas are ORGANISED and DEVELOPED over time — not just a list of sections. Understanding why a composer chose a particular form helps you explain the music's meaning. Sonata form is not just three sections — it is a dramatic argument between two keys.
