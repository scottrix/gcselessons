Lesson 2: Core Concepts: ensemble performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on ensemble performance. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: Ensemble performance assesses your ability to LISTEN and RESPOND to other musicians — not just play your part accurately.
Key Fact: Balance means adjusting your volume so each part is heard appropriately; tuning means matching pitch with others continuously.
Key Fact: Following a leader (conductor or lead player) requires watching for cues, tempo changes and dynamic adjustments in real time.
Key Fact: Chamber music (string quartet, wind quintet) demands the highest level of ensemble skill — every player must lead and follow simultaneously.
Music Exam Tips: Demonstrate ensemble AWARENESS (listening, balancing, following). Prepare both individually AND as a group. Explain role within the ensemble. Show understanding of AO1 ensemble criteria.

Practice (10 minutes)

Q: Describe how ensemble performance are used in a piece of music you have studied. Use musical vocabulary.

Answer: A detailed description using precise musical vocabulary (e.g. specific terms from the AQA elements list), referencing a named piece with accurate observations about how the elements are used and their effect.

Lesson 3: Application: ensemble performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Quick Recall

Recall the key terms: Music Exam Tips. Define each in one sentence.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Parent/Teacher Guide: Let your student attempt each question alone first, then compare with the model answer. Award method marks for correct working even if the final answer is wrong.

Q1: Describe how ensemble performance are used in a piece of music you have studied. Use musical vocabulary.

Answer: A detailed description using precise musical vocabulary (e.g. specific terms from the AQA elements list), referencing a named piece with accurate observations about how the elements are used and their effect.

Q2: Compare how two different pieces use ensemble performance. Explain the effect in each case.

Answer: A comparison of two pieces identifying similarities and differences in how ensemble performance are used, explaining the different effects created in each musical context, using comparative language.

Q3: How could you use ensemble performance in your own composition or performance? Explain your choices.

Answer: A practical application showing understanding of how ensemble performance can be manipulated in composition or performance, with specific examples of choices made and their intended effect.

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: ensemble performance

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain the skills required for effective ensemble performance. Describe how you would prepare for an ensemble performance, addressing both individual and group rehearsal strategies. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: identify specific ensemble skills (listening, balancing, following); detail both individual and group preparation; explain how to develop ensemble awareness; show understanding of different ensemble types; demonstrate understanding of AO1 criteria for ensemble. Mark scheme: AO3 (analyse and evaluate) + AO4 (knowledge and understanding) = total marks for listening; AO1 (perform) + AO2 (compose) for coursework components

Exam Tips: Always use PRECISE musical vocabulary — not vague descriptions like it sounds nice | Reference SPECIFIC musical elements when analysing any piece | For listening questions: identify elements FIRST, then explain their effect | For study pieces: know the context, structure and key features in detail | For performing: focus on accuracy, expression and interpretation | For composing: show development of ideas, not just repetition
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Ensemble performance is just playing your part while others play theirs. Correct: Ensemble marks specifically assess INTERACTION: listening, balancing, following, leading, and responding to other performers. Playing your part correctly but ignoring others scores poorly. The examiner assesses how well you function as part of a musical team.
