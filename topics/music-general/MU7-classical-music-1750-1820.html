Lesson 2: Core Concepts: classical music 1750-1820

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Review Previous Lesson

Quick recap: write 3 key points from Lesson 1 on classical music 1750-1820. Check them against the notes below.

Main Content (35 minutes)

Key Fact: The Classical period prioritised clarity, balance and elegance — homophonic texture dominates, with clear melody above chordal accompaniment.
Key Fact: Sonata form became the primary structure for first movements: exposition (two contrasting themes in different keys), development, recapitulation.
Key Fact: The Classical orchestra standardised: strings formed the core, with woodwind (flute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon), horns, trumpets and timpani added.
Key Fact: Alberti bass is a broken-chord accompaniment pattern (low-high-middle-high) typical of Classical keyboard music — it provides harmonic and rhythmic support.
Music Exam Tips: Know sonata form structure: exposition, development, recapitulation. Identify key relationships between themes. Explain how Classical conventions create balance and drama.

Practice (10 minutes)

Lesson 4: Exam Practice: classical music 1750-1820

Duration: 50 minutes

Starter Activity (5 minutes)

Command Words

Review what these command words require: state (one point), describe (say what happens), explain (say why), compare (both sides), evaluate (judgement).

Main Content (35 minutes)

Extended Answer

Extended question: Full-Mark Response Explain how sonata form works and how it creates a dramatic musical argument. Use examples from Beethoven's Symphony No. 1. <div class="

A grade 9 response will: describe all three sections of sonata form with key relationships; explain how the first and second themes contrast; show how Beethoven's treatment is innovative; use structural and harmonic vocabulary precisely. Mark scheme: AO3 (analyse and evaluate) + AO4 (knowledge and understanding) = total marks for listening; AO1 (perform) + AO2 (compose) for coursework components

Exam Tips: Always use PRECISE musical vocabulary — not vague descriptions like it sounds nice | Reference SPECIFIC musical elements when analysing any piece | For listening questions: identify elements FIRST, then explain their effect | For study pieces: know the context, structure and key features in detail | For performing: focus on accuracy, expression and interpretation | For composing: show development of ideas, not just repetition
Common Errors: Watch Out! Students often make mistakes here. Wrong: Classical music is just Mozart and piano music. Correct: The Classical period (1750-1820) saw the development of the symphony, string quartet and piano sonata as major genres. It is characterised by sonata form, balanced phrasing, homophonic texture and the expanding orchestra. Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven each contributed distinctively to these developments.
